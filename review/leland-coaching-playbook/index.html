Prime Directive: They Talk, You Ask

This is coaching, not consulting. The value isn't in what Boubacar knows — it's in what the coachee works out for themselves with the right questions in front of them. Every structural choice below (GROW, the question bank, the listening-ratio target) exists to enforce this one discipline.

The test for every question you're about to ask: does this open a door, or does it hand them an answer wearing a question mark? "Have you thought about updating your LinkedIn headline?" is advice in disguise. "What's your LinkedIn headline doing for you right now?" is a real question.

Session Structure: GROW GENERAL

GROW (Goal → Reality → Options → Will) is John Whitmore's coaching framework — not a title confirmed in his Audible library, used here as named, well-established methodology so the session always has a shape without him having to design one live.

PhaseMinutes (60-min session)What it's for
Goal~8What does THIS session need to produce? Not their life goal — today's goal.
Reality~15Where are they actually starting from? Surface the real obstacle, not the presenting one.
Options~25What could they do? Generate options before evaluating any of them. This is the hands-on/skills-based bulk of Leland's ask — build or practice something real here.
Will~12What will they actually do, by when, and how will they know it worked?

Reserve the last 3-5 minutes outside this table for closing (see Question Bank → Closing).

Question Bank by Session Phase

Mostly open-ended. Pull from here rather than improvising — a stacked or leading question is the fastest way to end up talking more than they do.

Opening / Rapport (first 5-10 min)

  • "Before we dive in — how are you doing, really, with everything since December?"
  • "What's been the hardest part of the last few weeks?"
  • "What made you say yes to this session specifically?"
  • "What have you already tried on this?"

Discovery / Reality

  • "Walk me through what a typical day looks like for you right now."
  • "What's working? What isn't?"
  • "What do you think is actually in your way here — not what you assume I'll say, but what you actually believe?"
  • "If a friend were in your exact situation, what would you tell them?" (Externalizes self-knowledge they already have.)
  • "What's the story you're telling yourself about why this is hard?"

Goal-Setting

  • "If this hour is a win, what does that look like when we're done?"
  • "What would need to be true in 30 days for you to feel like this is moving?"
  • "Of everything you could work on today, what matters most right now?"

Obstacle-Surfacing

  • "What's stopping you from doing that already?"
  • "Is that a skills gap, a confidence gap, or something else?"
  • "What's the worst that happens if you try and it doesn't work?"
  • "Whose voice is that — yours, or someone else's?" (Bungay Stanier's AWE question adapted: "And what else?" keeps this line open past the first answer.)

Action-Planning

  • "What's the smallest version of this you could do before our next session?"
  • "What would make this easy instead of hard?"
  • "What do you need from me to make this doable — not from me generally, from me specifically?" (The Coaching Habit's Foundation Question, general methodology.)
  • "On a scale of 1-10, how likely are you to actually do this? What would move it up one point?"

Closing

  • "What's the one thing you're taking away from today?"
  • "What are you going to do the moment we hang up?"
  • "What do you want me to check in on next time?"
  • "What haven't we talked about that we should have?"

Listening Ratio: 70/30 or Better GENERAL

Target: the coachee speaks at least 70% of the session. Concrete tells you're over your share:

  • You answer your own question. If 3+ seconds of silence makes you jump in with a suggestion, that's not helping — it's rescuing them from the productive discomfort of thinking.
  • You stack questions. "What's blocking you, and have you thought about reaching out to your old manager, or maybe updating your resume first?" — three questions, they can only answer the last one they heard.
  • You fill silence too fast. A pause after a real question is often them actually thinking. Count to 5 in your head before you say anything else.
  • You give the answer before they've struggled with it. If you find yourself explaining how to do the thing instead of asking what they think the thing is, stop and reroute to a question.
  • Your sentences are longer than theirs. A rough live check: if your responses are running longer than their answers, you've flipped the ratio.

Exception: the Options phase in GROW is legitimately more hands-on (Leland explicitly wants "skills-based" work — building something together, not just talking about it). Even there, let them drive the keyboard/the draft/the decision; you narrate and ask, they do.

Verizon-Specific Adaptations (from Ella + the 3 guides)

What's different about this cohort vs. a general career-transition client, per Ella's email and the Leland guide pages read in full today:

  • Fresh layoff, not a chosen transition. Ella's own words: "be sensitive to where each person is at." Some have already landed, some are still searching — don't assume either. Open by finding out which one you're actually talking to, before running any framework.
  • 2 hours total, not an ongoing relationship. There is no "next month we'll circle back" — whatever you build has to survive without you. Bias every Will/action-item toward something they can execute alone.
  • Hands-on and skills-based is the explicit brief (Verizon Expert Guide): two tracks — either they build a portfolio-worthy AI project, or they get oriented on function-specific AI tools if they're feeling overwhelmed. Ask which track fits early (their prior AI experience + what they actually want out of the hour), don't assume.
  • Positive and motivating tone, recognizing layoff stress is a named coaching principle in the guide itself, not just general courtesy — the guide explicitly calls it out.
  • If they're newly placed, not searching: the First-90-Days guide's 30/60/90 framework applies instead — Listen & Learn (days 1-30) → Start Contributing (31-60) → Take Ownership (61-90). Useful reframe if the session drifts toward "how do I look good fast" rather than job search.
  • If they're still searching: the AI Job Search guide's four phases (Targeting → Resume/LinkedIn → Outreach → Interview Prep) is the practical menu — ask where they are on that chain rather than starting from scratch. Its own coaching insight, worth repeating to them directly: "AI-only messages... get ignored" — whatever they draft with AI in the session, they still need to edit it into their own voice before sending.
  • Deliverable, not just conversation. The guide's own success metric is "increased AI confidence and immediately applicable skills," with a working project documented for interview discussions as the concrete output. End every session with something that exists outside the call — a doc, a draft, a list — not just a good conversation.

Between-Session AI Homework (the 2 companion guides)

These two Leland guides are not live-session material — they're structured, copy-paste AI prompts (for Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini) meant for the coachee to run on their own between sessions. Treat them as the concrete homework you hand off at the "Will" phase, separate from the GROW question bank above, which is for the 1:1 itself. Point them to whichever guide matches where they are: still searching → AI Job Search; just landed something → First 90 Days.

Guide A — "Use AI in Your Job Search" (still searching)

apps.joinleland.com/guides/verizon-ai-job-search — 4 phases, 11 total prompts:

  • Phase 1 — Targeting (3 prompts): surface competitive job titles + adjacent roles from their background; build a structured "criteria card" (function/culture/growth/comp, must-have vs. nice-to-have vs. dealbreaker) via an interview-style prompt; score and rank actual job postings against that card.
  • Phase 2 — Resume & LinkedIn (2 prompts): resume-vs-posting gap analysis (keyword match, skills gap, weakest-bullet rewrites, match score /100); LinkedIn profile audit (Headline/About/Experience/Skills each scored 1-5 with rewrites below a 4).
  • Phase 3 — Outreach (2 prompts): drafts cold/warm outreach that self-critiques for sounding generic or AI-written; generates 6-8 tailored informational-interview questions ending in a natural referral ask.
  • Phase 4 — Interview Prep (4 prompts): polishes a "tell me about yourself" into a 150-200 word Present→Past→Future answer; builds STAR stories from a rough draft + target competency; runs an AI mock behavioral interview with 1-5 scoring per answer; generates a realistic take-home assessment and evaluates their approach against what a top candidate would do differently.

Guide B — "First 90 Days in a New Role" (just landed)

apps.joinleland.com/guides/verizon-first-90-days — 3 prompts:

  • 30/60/90 day plan: Days 1-30 (listen & learn, key relationships, what NOT to do yet), 31-60 (start contributing, a quick win), 61-90 (take ownership, establish working style) — ends by naming the ONE thing that most determines whether the 90 days go well.
  • Company & industry accelerator: company overview, 3-4 industry trends, terminology/acronyms to know before day one, smart-vs-out-of-touch early questions, 3-5 sources to follow.
  • Early win identification: 3 candidate early wins (30-60 days, visible, aligned to manager priorities, plays to their strengths) with a success definition and failure mode for each.

Whichever guide fits, the homework hand-off itself is a live-session move: name the 1-2 specific prompts that map to what surfaced in Options/Will, tell them to paste it into Claude/ChatGPT before next session, and check the output at the open of session 2 rather than starting cold.

Beyond the Session: What Else Could Help Each Person

Direct answer to "what else could we do for each person" — concrete, not vague "more support":

  • A one-page takeaway doc after every session — 3-5 bullets: what we worked on, what they're doing next, one resource link. Costs 5 minutes, gives them something to open when motivation dips.
  • A between-session nudge (since there are only 2 sessions, ~1-2 weeks apart typically) — a short check-in message before session 2 asking specifically about the ONE action item from session 1, not a generic "how's it going." Keeps the thread alive without becoming unpaid ongoing coaching.
  • A short resource list matched to their track — if they're in the AI-project track, 2-3 specific tools/prompts to keep practicing with; if job-search track, the relevant Leland guide section as a standing reference they can reopen themselves.
  • Cross-referral within his own network — if a session surfaces something outside coaching scope (e.g., they want warm intros in a specific industry), a quick "I don't have that for you today, but I'll think on it" beats overpromising in the room; follow up only if a real lead exists.
  • Track it. A lightweight session log (coachee, function, track, key action items, session 1 vs 2 status) so Boubacar isn't reconstructing context from memory before session 2 — a dedicated tracker PRD is already in flight separately (task board rows for a Leland coaching-session tracker); this playbook assumes that exists or is coming, doesn't try to rebuild it here.

Book-Derived Best Practices & Sources

From his actual Audible library HIS LIBRARY

Confirmed by reading his real "Audible List" Drive file (2018-02-01, marked "Read"). Coaching-relevant titles and what each contributes here:

  • Good Leaders Ask Great Questions — John C. Maxwell. Direct source for the question-first posture this playbook is built around: the leader's job is to ask, not answer.
  • Triggers — Marshall Goldsmith & Mark Reiter. On behavior change sticking (or not) — relevant to the Will phase: an action item only matters if it survives contact with their actual environment. Goldsmith's core coaching question style ("what's one thing you could do better?") maps directly onto the closing question bank above.
  • Crucial Conversations — Kerry Patterson et al. Useful if a session gets emotionally loaded (layoff grief, anger at the company) — stay in the conversation rather than avoiding or escalating.
  • Thanks for the Feedback — Sheila Heen & Douglas Stone. Relevant if giving them direct feedback on a resume/LinkedIn draft — separate "what's true" from "how it lands," and expect their reaction to it as data, not resistance to override.
  • Transitions: Making Sense of Life's Changes — William Bridges. The classic on transition psychology (Ending → Neutral Zone → New Beginning) — useful frame if a session needs to acknowledge the emotional stage of a layoff before any tactical work can land.
  • Extreme Ownership — Willink & Babin. Relevant to the Will phase's accountability question ("how likely are you to actually do this") — ownership language works well for a population that may feel the layoff was done TO them.
