Griot: what the sequencing check actually found

Griot is orchestrator/griot.py, the deterministic picker that reads the Content Board every weekday morning. Its scoring is small and worth stating exactly, because it is the thing I applied.

  • Board Total Score is the base signal, weight 1.0.
  • +5 for the smallest unused Arc Priority still holding a pending draft.
  • −10 if a candidate's Topic overlaps any post inside a seven-day window.
  • −20 if the same Arc Phase fired inside a three-day window.

This has been fixed since this page was first written. The finding below was correct on the morning of 2026-09-05 and is kept because it is the reason the work happened. What changed is at the bottom of this section.

The finding, as it stood: Griot could not sequence newsletters, and it was not close. Two independent reasons, both checked against the live board rather than assumed.

  1. Griot only considers rows at Status Ready that are not yet scheduled. Every newsletter row on the board sits at Scheduled, Draft, Published or Archived. Not one has ever been eligible.
  2. Every signal it scores on is blank for this lane. All twenty-odd newsletter rows have an empty Arc Phase and an Arc Priority of None, and most have no Topic. With those blank, the −20 penalty and the +5 bonus can never fire, and the −10 fires only by accident. Griot would score every newsletter identically.

So the sequencing above is Griot's logic, applied by hand against titles and themes. That is a claim about method, not a claim that the tool ran. What the check produced:

  • No two consecutive slots share an arc phase, and no phase repeats inside the run.
  • Across the five slots the run covers five distinct phases — show the lens, open conversation, establish the frame, hidden costs, challenge behavior — which is the widest arc coverage any five-issue stretch of this newsletter has had.
  • The only topic that appears twice is the retainer, seven weeks apart and from two different angles. Nothing lands inside the seven-day window.

What shipped instead of a by-hand pass

Griot now has a second lane. A newsletter row is eligible where it never was, and the windows are derived from the weekly cadence rather than copied from the social pool, which is the part that was quietly broken: a seven-day topic window covers exactly one issue at a weekly cadence, and a three-day arc window covers none. The newsletter lane remembers four issues of topic and three of arc phase, and it looks forward as well as back, because sequencing a slot means seeing the slots committed on both sides of it.

Two agents now do this together instead of one agent applying two lenses by hand. Griot answers what can mechanically go in a slot. Kairos answers which of those serves the goal that is live, and it enforces the give-then-ask shape rather than describing it: a second ask cannot land within three weeks of the first, an ask placed before enough gives is penalised, and an angle that promotes an event with no event on the calendar is pushed down. Each decision is fed forward as a committed slot so the next one sequences against the choice just made, which is the part a by-hand pass cannot repeat. If the strategic half is unavailable the mechanical answer still comes back, and it says plainly that only half of it ran.

Run against the live board, not a fixture, and it reached your conclusion on its own. Given the 953 rows as they stand, the rejected room-Q&A angle ranked last of five candidates, carrying two penalties in plain language: it needs material nobody has captured, and it promotes an event with no event near the slot. It also now catches the duplicate class that has hit this run twice, where an approved row turns out to be word for word an issue already sent.

Two honest limits. It is a library with no automatic caller yet, deliberately, because wiring a new autonomous job into the morning heartbeat is a much larger change than the logic itself. And the board data is the second limit: the backfill below is now the thing holding this back, not the code.

One cheap fix worth doing. The four new rows were created with Arc Phase and Topic populated, which is the first time any newsletter row has carried them. If the same is backfilled onto the sent issues, the overlap arithmetic starts working for this lane instead of being decorative. Kairos already owns that backfill sweep as a standing biweekly pass, so this needs no new mechanism — it needs the newsletter rows included in a sweep that has so far skipped them.
