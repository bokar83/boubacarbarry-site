Confound the older cohort has had months to accrue rejections; the newer cohort's oldest application is 15 days old. A recent application with no rejection yet is not evidence the new resume is better — it hasn't had time to be rejected, full stop. The 24% figure above is not usable as-is.

Time-matched comparison (the one that actually controls for age): for each cohort, restricted to applications old enough to have had at least N days, what share was already rejected within N days of applying?

WindowOlder cohort (v1–v5), rejected within windowCurrent cohort (v6), rejected within window

At both matched windows, the current resume's early rejection rate is lower, not higher — the opposite of a "new CV is hurting me" story. Small n the 15-day window only has 11 applications old enough to test, and the 7-day window's 35 span a narrower slice of companies/seasons than the historical baseline's 269 (which spans January–August, a different hiring-market moment). This is a real, honest signal against the "v4 is hurting" hypothesis, but it is not a large enough or clean enough sample to declare the new resume better either — it just clears the bar of "no evidence it's worse."

What more would settle it: another 2–3 weeks of the current cohort aging past the 15/30-day marks (n will grow from 38 toward 60–80+ at the current application pace) would give a real like-for-like comparison. Don't revisit the resume decision before then unless a new, independent signal shows up.
