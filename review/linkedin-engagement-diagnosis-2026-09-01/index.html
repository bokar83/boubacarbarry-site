Ranked diagnosis

1Reciprocal engagement is at zero.Highest leverage You post, then leave. LinkedIn's distribution algorithm rewards accounts that comment on others' posts, especially in the minutes after a post goes up, with distribution on their own posts. Evidence: zero comments received, zero comments given (until today), flat low impressions regardless of topic. This is the single highest-leverage fix -- it is free, it is already a standing rule (the daily comment engine), and it was just reinforced by the Mark Lyman call itself. Section 4 has the concrete next step.

2Format is almost entirely text. 3 videos and 1 carousel out of 325 published pieces. LinkedIn's own algorithm and general platform behavior favor native video and document/carousel formats for reach -- and the tooling to produce video cheaply already exists in-house (HyperFrames), unused for this purpose.

3Content skews informational over personal. The two posts that actually got double-digit likes in the sample are stories with a real day, a real moment, a before/after -- not "here's how to use AI better" tips. This matches the existing "stories, not shared experiences" content rule; the data is one more confirmation, not a new rule.

4No ask of the reader. None of the 5 sampled posts end with a question or an explicit invitation to comment. A post that doesn't ask for a response rarely gets one.

5Measurement gap. The Content Board tracks Likes/Reposts for LinkedIn posts but not impressions -- worth a small fix later, not the priority now.
