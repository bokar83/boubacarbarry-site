The Book Archive: How Many Books Is This?

A structuring pass over the Project Timbuktu corpus · developmental-editor assessment · 2026-07-03 · v1

The decision: one strong book now, a real second book emerging

The archive is one big raw library, not a manuscript. Read end to end, it holds material for one publication-track book today, plus one clearly separable second book that the material supports but has not yet filled out. The other four candidate titles floated in past sessions are not books yet. They are threads, chapters, or aspirations with too little lived material to stand alone.

Why not fold everything into one book? Book 1 and Book 2 serve clearly different readers and carry different promises. Book 1 is a personal memoir-and-playbook for a person who just lost their job and needs a way forward. Book 2 is a builder's field manual for someone constructing and running a system of AI agents. Same author, same moat, but a laid-off HR manager and an indie AI builder do not buy the same book. That is the one condition under which a split beats keeping it together, so the split holds.

Why not more than two? The tiebreaker is fewer books. "AI for HR," "Boring Business + AI," and "1stGen Money" each appear as a candidate, but the archive carries only a handful of anecdotes for each, not a book's worth of material. Forcing them out now would produce thin books. They stay as chapters or future seeds until the corpus feeds them.

Source archive: docs/personal/book-source-material.md (~3,100 lines, append-only) plus the docs/roadmap/timbuktu.md plan and the book-idea memory note. The roadmap itself states the intent plainly: "Multiple books may emerge from the corpus; Boubacar decides which one(s) to write when." This assessment is that read.

Book 1 Write this one

Burned the Boats

Alternate titles: The Bridge That Wouldn't Hold  ·  Conakry to Code
What it is

A memoir that doubles as a playbook. It follows one arc: a boy born unable to walk in Guinea, sent across an ocean at three for surgery, raised by an American family, sent back to Guinea at eleven to a house with no power and no water, who then spends twenty years doing "nice and okay" work while knowing he is living beneath his capacity. In 2026 he cuts off every fallback and bets on himself. The core promise to the reader: the pattern you have been running for twenty years, doing it "for the family" and never quite shipping, is nameable and breakable, and here is one person doing it in the open.

Who it is for
A mid-career knowledge worker, most likely in or near HR or corporate operations, who has just been laid off or can see it coming. They have a family, a mortgage, and a quiet sense they have not lived up to what they could be. Boubacar named this reader himself: "A lot of people will get laid off soon and so they need something, they need that book that will inspire them."

Book 2 Real, but thinner

Mine First

Alternate titles: The Operator and the Agents  ·  Nuggets, Bricks, and Dust
What it is

A field manual for building and running a system of AI agents, told through what actually broke and what the fixes taught. Not a hype book about AI and not a memoir. The promise: here is how one operator built the system into his own business first, the disciplines that kept it from collapsing under its own parallelism, and the way of thinking that turns a stray idea into a system upgrade. Its spine is a single line from the archive: "I know because I built one. Mine first."

Who it is for
A technical solo operator, indie builder, or small-agency owner who is actually assembling AI agents to run real work, and who wants the operating discipline that the tutorials skip. A different reader from Book 1: this one wants method, not inspiration.
