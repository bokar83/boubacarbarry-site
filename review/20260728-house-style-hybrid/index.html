A technical plate drawn on the weave

One direction now, not three. The plate supplies the system and the mark making. The cloth supplies the ground and the thing the plate most needed, which is a single place for your eye to go.

The live deck and the live kit page were not touched. You present Thursday from the deck that exists now, and attendees use the kit that exists now.

The join, in one idea rather than two styles stapled together

A merge fails when it is a shopping list. The two directions had to meet at a single physical fact, and they do: before the cloth goes in the vat, the parts that must stay readable are bound off, and the dye never reaches them.

So every content sheet has one held field. Inside it the warp stripes stop, the vat lines stop, the ground goes flat and quiet, registration ticks mark the corners, and the dimension chain running across the sheet breaks at its boundary and picks up on the far side. Nothing but the message is allowed in there. That is the whole answer to your two second test. The drawing cannot compete with the message because the drawing is not permitted to enter the zone the message lives in, and the annotation stopping at the boundary is what tells your eye where the boundary is.

What each direction contributed, and what was dropped
From the plateThe sheet itself, zone letters, figure numbering, reference numerals on elbowed leader lines, the plan and section drawings of the cloth carrying real arkilla kerka dimensions, PLAIN WEAVE and CANNYUDI as legitimate annotation, and the seven cell title block reading DRAWN: BOUBACAR. That block survives intact.
From the clothThe ground. Six indigo strips at full bleed, each warped at its own stripe pitch because each strip was set up on the loom separately. The vat lines. The dimension annotation reading across the whole field rather than sitting politely beside the drawing. And the one dominant reading zone, which is the contribution that actually fixes the deck.
Two grounds, one flipVat is indigo and is the default, 24 sheets. Reserve is cream, the cloth the dye never reached at all, 9 sheets, and it lands on exactly the slides your deck already runs dark, so the rhythm of the night is unchanged. In an indigo deck the cream sheet is the interruption. That inverts the failure you saw in the kit earlier today: the reserve is rare again, so it means something again.
Dropped, as instructedThe tilted card. Nothing in weaving and nothing in drafting tilts, and it read as styling. One correction on the record: I went back through the shipped code for direction B and there is no rotated card in it. The only rotate() anywhere is the 45 degree cross on a survey mark. So there was nothing to remove, and the rule still stands and is enforced here: no rotation on anything, anywhere, ever.
Direction ADead. Not rendered, not mocked, not folded in.
The discipline, with counts rather than claims

Full apparatus on 11 sheets (heavy border, zone letters, title block): the two covers and the nine reserves. Quiet apparatus on 22 (hairline border, no zone letters, one line strip at the foot). A drawing appears on 7 sheets of 33. Twenty six carry none.

No content ever lives in apparatus type. Everything a person in the room has to read is 31 px or larger on a 1600 px sheet. Dimension figures, zone letters and title block cells sit at 14 px and are genuinely unreadable from row 12. That is allowed only because nothing anybody needs is written there. If one fact ever migrates into the title block the whole scheme fails.

The held field is a hard boundary, enforced in code, not by eye. Every sheet type declares its field rectangle, and every drawing is positioned outside it. That is why the drawing cannot creep back in on a busy build day.

The kit page in the hybrid

A slide is one fixed rectangle read for ninety seconds from twenty feet away. The kit is a two metre scroll with ten builders, around sixty input fields, copy buttons that have to be found under pressure, and a privacy section somebody reads before they will type their prices in. On a phone. Held in one hand. By a person who owns a heating company.

It survives, and the merge is what saved it. Direction C alone had the right structure for a form and no way to keep sixty controls calm. Direction B alone had the right instinct for calm and died at sixty reserves. Put together, the reserve is confined to the one place it is literally true: the only cream on this page is the inside of a control, because the control is the part of the cloth you write on. Labels, hints and examples sit on the flat held field where they belong, so the eye still lands in one place per screen.

The schedule of parts is better form structure than the live page has now. Numeral, label, hint, worked example, control. The numeral is the item number, it is the tab order, and it is what somebody says out loud when they need help at table four. On a phone the row stacks and keeps its numeral, so you always know which of the nine you are on.

Two things had to go, and saying why is the honest part. The sheet border, because a border promises four visible edges and a scroll can never keep that promise. And the per-station title block, because ten of them is exactly the widget failure. There is one title block, at the foot, carrying DRAWN: BOUBACAR.

Where it is weakest. On a wide desktop the held fields cover most of the page, so the cloth reads as a frame around them rather than as a ground. It is fully present on a phone, in the gaps between stations, and behind every drawing in the deck. If that bothers you the fix is narrower fields, and the cost is a shorter line length on a page people read one-handed.

Where it breaks

The join holds across all 33 sheets and across the kit. The two second test passes because the answer is structural rather than a matter of taste: the drawing is not permitted inside the held field, and that is enforced in code, not by eye. Every sheet type declares its field rectangle and every drawing is positioned outside it, so the diagram cannot creep back over the message on a busy build day.

Where it breaks, with the numbers

Two sheets had more copy than the field could hold at full size and were scaled down to fit: sheet 03 at 98 percent and sheet 12 at 92 percent. The auto fit never goes below 90 percent, so nothing silently shrinks into unreadability, but it means the smallest line of actual message anywhere in the deck is 29 px on a 1600 px sheet, on sheet 12. Everything else sits between 30 and 44. If you want a hard 32 px floor, sheet 12 needs a line of copy cut, not a design change.

Worst sheet for the back of the room is still 15, the specification. Ten lines of monospace at 22 px inside a bordered block. Same constraint the live deck has, same mitigation: it is printed on page 1 of the handout and you say so out loud. Its caption is the smallest content type on any sheet at 18 px.

The cloth is quietest exactly where the message is densest. On schedule and work sheets the held field is 1330 px of a 1600 px sheet, so the ground shows as a margin. That is the honest cost of the one dominant reading zone, and I would rather pay it than shrink the message.

What a sheet with nothing to draw looks like

Sheet 04 is the clearest, and 05, 07, 08, 09, 18, 19, 22 and 27 are the same type. Figure number in brass, headline, one 0.7 mm rule, the line that matters at 40 px, the paragraph under it at 34, then the field ends and the cloth resumes. Twenty six of the 33 sheets carry no drawing at all. A system that demands a diagram per slide is not a system, and this one does not.

Where I was tempted to use the pattern as wallpaper

Constantly, and this direction makes the temptation worse rather than better, which is worth knowing before you commit to it. The moment the ground is a woven texture, every empty corner asks for more of it. Three specific refusals: no weave tint inside the held field, ever, because the whole point is that the field is where the dye did not reach. No motif band under headlines, which would have appeared 33 times while meaning something twice; headlines get a plain 0.7 mm rule instead. And the strip stripes are never scaled to fit a slide, they hold one pitch per strip across every sheet, because a stripe that resizes to suit a layout is decoration.

The one I did take, and you should push back if you disagree. Sheet 06 keys the six principles to the six strips of the cloth. Six is the real strip count of an arkilla kerka and the list really is six items, so the drawing carries information. But I chose that content to draw partly because the numbers matched. That is one step from decoration and I would not do it twice in a deck. The five advisors and the three tools are drawn nowhere, because a five strip cloth would be one I invented to fit a slide.

Two things I am not claiming

1. The tilted card. You asked me to drop it. Going back through the shipped code for direction B there is no rotated card in it: the only rotate() anywhere is the 45 degree cross on a survey mark. So there was nothing to remove. The rule is enforced here regardless, and nothing on any sheet or on the kit page is rotated.

2. The kit at full length. The mock shows the hero, one complete builder and the privacy section. It does not show ten builders stacked. My expectation is that the schedule survives repetition because a numbered schedule is meant to be long, but that is an expectation and not a result, and you should not take it from me until it is built.
