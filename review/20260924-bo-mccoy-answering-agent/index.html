Bo McCoy: keep, evolve or replace

Answering-agent decision memo, 30 to 60 day horizon · 2026-09-24

TL;DR

Verdict: EVOLVE. Keep Bo on the stack we own. Do not move him to OpenAI or Grok.

  • Claude has no voice or phone product for developers. It can only be the brain inside Bo, never a replacement for him.
  • OpenAI's realtime voice scored highest (65%) against Bo (59%). The gap is under 10%, so the tiebreaker applies: keep the owned setup. Moving would give up native outbound calling and control of the call data, and support for Boubacar's cloned voice is not confirmed.
  • Grok's voice agent is the cheapest flat rate but has the thinnest enterprise paper (49%).
  • Bo works: 374 tests pass and replies start in 0.9 seconds on a typical turn. It is also barely used: 2 calls in September.
  • Bo is not enterprise-ready and should not be sold that way in the next 60 days.
  • Next action, 2026-10-01: replay Bo's 75 logged caller turns against three models, keep the fastest, and correct the docs that name the wrong model.

Known gaps

  • No live transfer to a human. Bo can take a message but cannot hand a caller over.
  • Not yet screening Boubacar's real calls. His number is not forwarded to Bo. That was his call, pending fixes.
  • One open bug: a call dropped five seconds into the greeting (July 22).
  • No caller allowlist on the screening line.

Where the sources disagree

  • The setup guide and the build spec both say the brain is Claude Sonnet 4.6. The code says Gemini 2.5 Flash, and the server confirms it. Gemini was swapped in because Sonnet left callers in 4.7 seconds of silence. The docs never caught up, so this memo goes by the code.
  • The August 25 research doc listed as a source for this memo is about a book pen name that happens to share the Bo McCoy name. It has nothing to do with the phone agent, so it was set aside.
  • The name is Bo McCoy, his childhood nickname. "Beau" was a dictation slip.

Tiebreaker, shown

OpenAI leads by 6 points, a 9% gap. The rule for this memo: inside 10%, prefer less new lock-in and more reuse of the setup we own. So the tiebreaker was used, and it decides the call.

A check on the weights. If enterprise readiness counts more (25) and phone depth less (20), OpenAI leads 66% to 58%. That is outside the tiebreaker. For a regulated enterprise buyer, OpenAI's realtime voice is the stronger engine. That is why the enterprise verdict below names it as the exception engine and not as a new home.

The move that makes migration unnecessary

The Pipecat version Bo already runs ships working connectors for both OpenAI's and xAI's realtime voice models, confirmed in the installed package. Either can sit inside Bo, behind our own phone line, recordings and client registry. Nobody has tested that path with a live call yet, so it gets a test before anyone promises it.

Pressure test

Five-angle review of the draft verdict. It held, with four changes.

  • Contrarian: the weights pick the winner, so show what happens when they shift (done above). Ten test calls cannot measure a slow-turn speed, and the realtime option is only proven to exist, not to work.
  • First principles: the real issue is workload, not vendor. At 2 calls a month no platform choice matters. Fix what the next real job needs.
  • Expansionist: when we own the call and only rent the brain, we answer the lock-in worry larger buyers raise. Keep that slot open to any vendor.
  • Outsider: a procurement lead hears "one operator, one server, no uptime promise" and stops listening. A plumber only cares that the phone gets answered. Two buyers, two answers. A client product should also not carry Boubacar's personal nickname.
  • Executor: nobody has time to make 30 test calls. Replay the logged caller turns against each model instead. Hours of work, no phone needed.

What changed: the model test became a replay of logged turns. The enterprise exception was downgraded to "test first." The weight check went onto this page. Product naming stays separate from Bo.

Verdict

EVOLVE for both audiences. MIGRATE for neither.
