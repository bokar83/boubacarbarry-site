06What is most likely to blow the timing

7:16, the first heads-down block

Not the Decision Council, and not Q&A. It is slide 10, open Claude and make a Project. Every account that will not sign in, every password nobody remembers, every laptop that never joined the hotspot surfaces at the same moment, in front of you, all at once. Five minutes becomes eleven, and every block after it inherits the slip right through to the module you least want to shorten.
The mitigation, decided now rather than at 7:19: hard stop at 7:21 no matter how many hands are still up. Stragglers finish during the Business Brain block, which is the one block where being seven minutes behind costs nothing, because it is a fill-in-the-blank page they can complete at any speed. Say out loud that you are moving on and that nobody is being left behind. The instinct to wait for the last person is what turns a five-minute block into eleven and it is the single most expensive instinct in the room.

Second, smaller

Slide 17, the real decision, at 7:37. People will want to read theirs out. That is a good problem, but it belongs in the 7:46 block where you have budgeted for it, not at 7:40. Hold them.
