Your posting says something companies your size usually do not say out loud. You want people practices designed to scale alongside the company, built on data and experimentation, before anything breaks. That is the version of this job worth doing, and it is the version I have actually done.

The risk this piece carries, handled head on. This is a company full of engineers, and the fastest way to lose them is to imply you write the code. The draft says plainly that you are not an engineer and did not hand-write it, and puts the claim where it is true and stronger: the architecture, the governance, and the judgment about what a system is allowed to decide. Saying it outright is more credible in that building than hedging, and it protects you in a conversation where someone will ask.

A project of my own self-initiative: the system that runs my business

I started using AI seriously in 2023 and leaned on it heavily in my HR work through 2024 and 2025. When I left GE and started consulting on my own, the problem got concrete fast. One person cannot do research, writing, outreach, delivery and administration at the standard any of them deserve, and hiring is not an option at zero revenue. The usual answer to that is work more hours. I wanted a different answer.

So I designed a multi-agent AI system to run the day to day of the business, and I have operated it every day since.

What it is, plainly. A self-hosted system on my own hardware, running on Ubuntu and Docker. A set of specialized agents handle research synthesis, content development and strategic review. An automation layer connects them, a routing layer sends each task to an appropriate model rather than to the most expensive one, and Postgres sits underneath so nothing that matters lives in a chat window. It is wired into the tools I already work in, so the output lands where the work actually happens.

Two decisions in it I would defend in any review.

First, I wrote the governance before I wrote the capability. Output anomaly scanning and harmful content flagging were in from the first version, not added later after something embarrassing happened. It is the same instinct as safety on a plant site. You do not install the guard after the injury.

Second, I designed in disagreement on purpose. A five-voice review panel pressure-tests any significant decision from five different angles, and I run two frontier models against each other on anything that matters. A system that only ever agrees with me would be an expensive way to be wrong faster.

What I want to be precise about, because you will ask and you should. I am not an engineer and I did not hand-write this system. What I did was the architecture and the judgment. What it should do, what it must never do, where a decision has to come back to a human, and which model at what cost. The building was the cheaper half. Deciding what to trust it with was not.

Why I am offering this one rather than something from my HR career. Nobody asked me to build it, nobody paid for it, and it did not exist until I decided it should. It also runs in production with real client work depending on it, which is a very different thing from having read about these tools. I know where they save an enormous amount of time and I know exactly where they produce confident nonsense, and the second half is the part a people function needs from whoever is holding this seat.
