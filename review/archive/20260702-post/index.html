LinkedInPostNEW
I fixed the same broken layout three times. It failed all three times. The fourth time, I stopped fixing it. I was building a dashboard for my own business. On my phone the layout was broken, everything spilling off the right edge. So I fixed the part that looked broken. It failed. I fixed it again, a little differently. Failed again. Third try, same result. So I stopped and said: this is the last try. If it fails again, do not touch the code. Give me a full breakdown of what is actually causing this (RCA or Root Cause Analysis). The breakdown found it almost immediately. The thing that looked broken was never the problem. The real cause sat one level up, in how the page was built around it. Once we were looking at the right layer, the fix was a single small change. Here is what stayed with me. Those three failed rounds were not spent fixing anything. They were spent fixing the wrong level. The moment I diagnosed instead of patched, it was basically already solved. I have watched business owners do this for years, myself included. Slow month, so you tweak the offer. Still slow, so you tweak it again. But the offer was never the problem. It was one level up, who you put it in front of. The offer is just the part you can see, so the offer is the part you keep touching. When the same fix fails three times, the problem is not your fix. It is where you are looking. Stop fixing. Start diagnosing. That is the discipline I am building into everything I run right now. Root cause first, changes second. Learning it in the open, one wrong layer at a time.

I am building a workshop right now, and the whole session is built around the opposite idea. Owner-operators bring one real decision they are stuck on. You do not leave with a faster email. You leave with a decision that already survived its own worst objections. Because the thing you actually sell, the thing no tool replaces, is judgment. AI is not here to make the call for you. It is here to make sure you looked at the call from angles you would have skipped on your own. I am figuring this out in the open as I build it.
