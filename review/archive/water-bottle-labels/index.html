How it is printed at home

Real bottle dimensions (looked up, not guessed)

The Costco Kirkland 16.9 oz / 500 ml bottle is about 2.6 in diameter, so the full wrap is about 8.1875 in circumference (OnlineLabels OL435 template = 8.1875 x 1.375 in; BottleYourBrand = 8.125 in). The full-wrap width was never the problem. The label canvas is 8.1875 x 1.5 in so it wraps once with a slight overlap. Measure your own bottle with a paper strip before a full run to confirm 8.1875 in, since specs vary slightly between batches.

The centered-zone rule (the fix for "the text felt too wide")

  • Confine all readable content to a centered 4 in zone. Hero, promise, name, and QR all sit in that band, so it reads head-on on the front face without rotating the bottle.
  • The rest of the width, out to both ends, stays blank white. About 2.1 in of white on each side of the 4 in zone.
  • The seam overlap (about 0.25 to 0.375 in) laps white-on-white only, never over the QR or the text. The QR stays flat, clean, and scannable.

Print and trim guide

Print at the full 8.1875 x 1.5 in, keep the content in the centered 4 in zone, wrap so the seam closes on a blank white end.

Exact label size, the centered content zone, the seam overlap, and the measure-your-bottle step.

The sleeper problem: a cold bottle sweats

Straight from the cooler, the bottle is covered in water within minutes. Plain paper wicks it, inkjet ink runs, the paper warps and the label lifts. This has to be solved on purpose.

The fix (cheapest reliable path)

White paper plus a clear packing-tape laminate. Print on ordinary white paper, let it dry, then lay clear shipping tape over the whole face and burnish it flat. The tape seals the ink and gives a waterproof skin; sweat beads on it and wipes off. A few dollars of tape does dozens of bottles.

Ink economy

White is the page. No dark fills, no banners. One display-serif hero line in navy, thin teal line art, one orange accent, and the small QR. Under 10% ink coverage, so it prints cheap on a home printer.
