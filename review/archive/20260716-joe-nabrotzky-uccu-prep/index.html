Discovery Questions

Pick 4 or 5. They get Joe talking about HR and AI at UCCU, and quietly surface where you could help and who he knows.

"You're about a month in. What's the first people problem you actually want to move at UCCU?"
Surfaces his real priorities and where a fractional hand could fit.
"Where does AI actually come up in your world right now — hiring, service, compliance — or is it still mostly noise?"
Reads his AI stance and finds the concrete entry point.
"When your team hears the word AI, is the reaction excitement or fear? How are you handling that?"
People-side of adoption — his home turf, and the exact place you help.
"You spent years advising HR leaders. Who's doing the AI-in-HR thing well, and who's stuck?"
Opens his network. This is the soft path to referrals.
"For a regulated credit union, where's the line you won't let AI cross with people decisions?"
Signals you understand compliance, and sets up keep-the-human.
"If you could hand one piece of the people function to a fractional partner for six months, what would it be?"
Plants the pilot idea without pitching it. Let him say it.

The Two Soft Asks

He is a sharp ex-consultant. Frame both as low-stakes, no-pressure. You are trading value, not selling.

Ask 1 · the referral door "You just came out of consulting, so you know everyone. If you ever hear an HR leader wrestling with this AI question, I'd love an introduction."
Ask 2 · the UCCU feeler "And if it's ever useful to pressure-test where AI could help your own team at UCCU, I'm happy to trade notes. No pitch."

Order matters. The referral ask is easy for him to say yes to and costs him nothing. The UCCU feeler rides in behind it, framed as trading notes, not selling a project. If he only bites on one, the referral is the win — he just left a network full of exactly your buyers.

Landmines

✗

Don't pitch. Thirty minutes, warm reconnect. He will smell a close instantly. Listen more than you talk.

✗

No buzzwords. Kill "revolutionary," "transformative," "cutting-edge," "seamless," "leverage." Concrete outcomes only. Simple converts.

✗

Don't over-explain the AI system. One line of what it does, then stop. He cares about outcome and judgment, not architecture.

✗

Don't lecture him on HR. He has 20-plus years and a Fortune-100 decade. Peer to peer, never teacher to student.

✗

Don't position against HR. He is HR. Frame AI as making the people function stronger with the human kept in the loop, never as replacing it.

✗

Don't assert UCCU facts you're unsure of. Ask about member count, tech stack, AI initiatives. Guessing wrong in front of a detail person costs credibility.

✗

Watch the clock. Leave the last 5 minutes for the asks and a clean next step. Don't let the listen block eat the whole call.
