Three ways to stop looking like everyone else

One idea, three interpretations: Fouta Djallon strip-weave gives the layout system, technical drawing gives the mark-making. Same real slide rendered in all three so you can compare like for like, plus a title slide because that is what a room sees first.

What is banned here, and why. Dark ground with neon accents is the 2024-25 default. Cream or parchment ground with an editorial serif and one warm accent is the current default, and it is what your deck, your bottle label and these review pages use today. None of the three below use either. Your current deck ground is #EFE7D6 with Gloock serif and an amber accent. That is exactly the look being retired.

The bridge, so this is not pattern-as-wallpaper. An arkilla kerka is six woven strips of 22 cm, hand-sewn selvedge to selvedge, and the sixth strip is deliberately woven simpler than the other five. The strips are joined so the motifs register into continuous lateral bands across the seams. That is a grid specification: fixed module, deliberate exception, and a rule that content must line up across a joint. A technical drawing is the same kind of system from the other side: a fixed set of line widths where the weight of a line tells you whether a thing is cut, seen, or only measured. So the cloth sets the layout and the plate sets the marks. The pattern is the structure, never a texture laid on top.

What I actually looked at

Both traditions, real sources, specifics carried into the work rather than a vibe.

West African and Fulani material culture

Bernhard Gardi on arkilla kunta and arkilla kerka (Musee der Kulturen Basel, republished on Adire African Textiles). The hard numbers the layout is built on: a complete kerka is six strips at 22 cm, the sixth woven simpler than the other five; a kunta is five strips at 32 cm, same rule on the last one. Some carry a seventh black and white strip called sigaretti whose only job is to hang the cloth. The weave runs a rhythm of plain weave (cannyudi), weft float pattern (cubbe), then tapestry.

Marie-Therese Wisniowski, Woolen Stripweaves of the Niger Bend. Fulani blankets of four to six panels, each about 15 cm, sewn selvedge to selvedge, and the strips are joined specifically so the motifs line up into lateral bands. Upper and lower borders read as water and fertility; the central motif is the paths taken by the herds. The Fulani name every motif.

Leppi, Fouta Djallon (Guzangs, Guinea: A Textile Civilization in Four Systems, and TRT Afrika). Narrow-strip handweaving assembled into larger cloth, deep indigo, fine light stripes through the blue, structured visual rhythm. Depth comes from indigo leaves fermented, oxidised and layered through repeated dye cycles, so colour is a record of repetition and time, not a swatch. Recognised by OAPI in 2024 as a Protected Geographical Indication anchored to the Fouta Djallon.

Kendeli, Basse-Guinee. Resist-dye held by Susu women: cloth bound, immersed and oxidised in cycles. The rule that direction B is built on comes from here, and it is a fact about the material rather than a style choice: white in an indigo cloth is not printed on, it is protected from the dye. Light shapes are reserves.

Motif vocabulary. Lines, spots, triangles, chevrons and lozenges, read against landscape: hills, grazing land, routes travelled, habitations. Kept to Fulani and Guinean sources rather than blended with unrelated traditions.

Kente construction logic (Smarthistory, Minneapolis Institute of Art) for the general principle only: strips of 7.5 to 11.5 cm woven up to ten metres, cut into equal sections, sewn edge to edge, scale and composition varied by how you cut and order the strips.

Technical drawing conventions

ISO 128-2:2020, basic conventions for lines. Line widths come from a fixed set: 0.18, 0.25, 0.35, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.4, 2.0 mm, with thick to thin held at 2:1. Continuous thick is for visible outlines and edges. Continuous thin is for dimension lines, leader lines, hatching and imaginary intersections. That is the whole hierarchy, and it is why nothing on these slides uses an arbitrary border width.

Architectural section hierarchy. Four steps: heaviest for what the section cuts through, heavy to medium for edges seen but not cut, light for surface and material indication, faint for dimension lines, grid and hatching. Direction A's horizontal band is heavy above and faint below for exactly this reason.

USPTO drawing rules, 37 CFR 1.84 and MPEP 507. Figures numbered consecutively as FIG. n, independent of sheet numbering. Reference numerals at least 0.32 cm, the same element always carrying the same numeral, and no numeral in a drawing without an explanation in the text. Lead lines must not cross, and should be broken or elbowed. Shading uses a uniform hatch angle to show contour, never to decorate. Direction C is built on these rules, including the one that made it interesting: if every numeral must have an explanation, the explanation can be the slide copy.

DIRECTION A

Six strips

The loom is the layout. Six strips, the sixth deliberately quieter, one seam sewn low on purpose, and the headline set so it crosses the seams and registers across them the way a woven motif does. Restrained, mostly line and space. It should feel engineered.

Direction A: the actual system
Grid moduleSix strips at 266.667px, one sixth of the stage, taken directly from the kerka's six strips of 22 cm. Every element starts on a strip edge. Strip six is tinted #D4D9D3 and carries only the time and slide number, which is the kerka rule that the sixth strip is woven simpler.
The seamDrawn, never hidden. Each seam is a 0.35 mm hairline plus a selvedge stitch tick at 22px intervals. Above the type there is a second seam layer, so where the headline crosses a seam the joint continues through the letterform. That is the lateral-band rule: the motif joins across a sewn edge.
The offsetThe top rail is the sigaretti, the narrow black and white strip whose only job is to hang the cloth, so it appears only where the cloth is carried rather than as a border. The sixth strip is sewn 16px low, which is the single deliberate imperfection on the page. It lands on the strip that is already the exception, so one rule carries both ideas instead of two rules competing.
Line weightsISO 128-2 at A3 landscape scale, 1 mm = 3.81px: 0.18 hatching and grid, 0.25 dimension and leader, 0.35 seam, 1.4 the heavy band edge. Thick to thin held at 2:1. The horizontal band is heavy above and faint below, which is the architectural convention for a cut edge versus a seen edge.
TypeArchivo 800 for display, uppercase at -0.03em, and Archivo 400 to 500 for body and lead. Martian Mono 600 for every annotation, eyebrow and dimension. No serif anywhere, which is the single fastest way this stops reading as the parchment default.
Not a default becauseThe ground is cool, not warm. There is no editorial serif and no terracotta. The page is built on visible structural seams that a cream template page never has, and the one accent is used as a measure mark rather than as decoration.
DIRECTION B

Six baths

The ground is dyed cloth, and it records the process that made it: six strips at six different indigo values because no two strips come out of the vat the same. White is not a box drawn on top, it is cloth the dye never reached. The drawing is chalked over the cloth afterwards.

Direction B: the actual system
Grid moduleSame six strips at 266.667px, but here you see them as six separately dyed bands rather than as ruled columns. The values are deliberately not a gradient: #1E3568 / #14264C / #24406F / #0F1D3C / #1A2E5A / #12224A. A gradient reads as a software effect. An irregular sequence reads as six pieces of cloth that went into the vat a different number of times, which is what actually happens.
The reserveThe signature, and it comes straight from resist-dye: white is not printed on, it is protected from the dye. So every light panel is a reserve with a slightly irregular clip-path edge and a soft wicking halo where dye crept under the binding. The headline sits inside the reserve in indigo ink.
SurfaceTwo horizontal vat lines where the cloth hung over the rail, plus a grain overlay at low opacity with a different turbulence frequency per band. Imperfection is the point, but it is one layer, not a texture pile.
Line weightsAnnotation is chalk, so the scale drops to the thin end of ISO 128-2: 0.25 mm dimension lines with tick ends, 0.18 mm extension lines. Nothing heavier, because a chalk mark on cloth is never a cut edge.
TypeFamiljen Grotesk 700 uppercase for display and 400 to 500 for body, chosen for a slightly irregular grotesque with visible personality in the a and g. Fragment Mono for every chalk annotation, vat log and dimension.
Not a default becauseIt is dark, but there is no neon, no glow and no single acid accent. The interest comes from six values of one hue and from the reserve edges. The one warm mark on the slide is a brass lozenge, which is a real Fulani object rather than a highlight colour.
DIRECTION C

The plate

The slide is a drawing sheet. Border, zone letters, title block, figure number, dimension lines. The Fulani strip and lozenge band is the object being drawn, in plan and in section, and because a patent rule says no reference numeral may appear without an explanation, the slide copy becomes the key to the figure. It should feel like a document from a discipline that does not exist yet.

Direction C: the actual system
Grid moduleThe strip is now the object being measured rather than the column system. The plan view draws one strip at 220, dimensioned, with the motif repeat at 37. The title slide draws the whole cloth as 6 × 220 = 1320. Those are the real kerka numbers, which is why the drawing survives being read closely.
The keyThe signature. USPTO rule 37 CFR 1.84 says no reference numeral may appear in a drawing without an explanation in the specification. Turn that around and the slide copy becomes the explanation. Body text is no longer a paragraph under a headline, it is the legend to a figure, and the leader lines physically connect each sentence to the part of the cloth it is about.
Drawing apparatusSheet border at 1.4 mm, inner frame at 0.18 mm, zone letters A to C and 1 to 3 down the edges, a seven cell title block, one revision triangle, section A-A cut with the 2.0 mm heaviest weight because that is what the section passes through. Every one of these is a real convention doing its real job.
Line weightsThe full ISO 128-2 ladder, all eight widths, used correctly: 2.0 section cut, 1.4 sheet border, 0.7 visible outline, 0.35 hidden and secondary, 0.25 dimension and leader, 0.18 hatching and extension. Hatching is a uniform 45 degree angle, which is the rule that says hatching shows contour and never decorates.
TypeAnybody at width 88, weight 750 for figure titles, a variable width face that behaves like drafting lettering. Azeret Mono for every numeral, dimension, note and title block cell. Archivo for the key text, because the legend still has to read like sentences at the back of the room.
Not a default becauseThe ground is a mid-light olive grey with a green cast, which is neither cream nor near black, and it comes from drafting linen rather than from a palette generator. There is no hero headline sitting in the middle of empty space. The page is an instrument.

Recommendation

Take A as the house style, and keep C as the specialist mode. A is the only one of the three that survives contact with a whole website. It carries a headline, a dense paragraph, a table, a pricing block and a form without needing a new idea each time, because its structure is a column system rather than a picture. And its signature, the seam continuing through the headline, works at any size, on any page, in one line of CSS. That is what a house style has to be.

C is the strongest single artefact of the three. If you look at one slide and ask which would make a room go quiet, it is C. But a technical plate is a mode, not a system: every page needs a real drawing to be the specimen or the apparatus turns into costume. Use it where there is genuinely something to diagram, which means the workshop deck, a teardown, an audit report, a process page. Not the whole site.

A and C also share a spine, which is the point: the same strip module, the same ISO line ladder, the same indigo and oxide. A page can move from one to the other without a seam.

The honest part

B is the weakest, and I would not ship it as the house style. Not because it is ugly, it is the most beautiful of the three at first glance, and the reserve idea is the best single idea in the set. Three real problems:

1. It is a dark ground, and although there is no neon in it, it sits one bad decision away from the look you just banned. The moment somebody adds a bright accent for a button state, it becomes the 2024 default wearing a costume.
2. Dense indigo with mottling is punishing for long reading. It works for a title slide and a statement page. A pricing table or a 900 word article on it is a bad experience, and most of your site is reading.
3. The reserve only means something when there is a small amount of it. Once half a page is reserve, it is just a white box on blue, and the whole idea collapses into decoration.

Salvage the good part: take the reserve as a rule inside direction A, for the one moment per deck that needs to stop the room. Keep six baths as a treatment for covers, section breaks and social cards, where it is genuinely strong.

Where this breaks

C is the hardest to carry across a website, by a wide margin. A title block and a sheet border are a promise that there is a drawing on the sheet. On a contact page or a testimonial block there is nothing to draw, so the border becomes a frame and the title block becomes a widget, and the whole thing reads as a theme rather than as a discipline. It also has the highest per-page cost, since every page needs a real specimen drawn for it, and the drawing has to be correct or the effect dies.

A's risk is the opposite one. Six equal columns can flatten into a plain grid if the seam and the offset get dropped for being fussy. The rules that must survive contact with a busy build day: the seam is always drawn, the sixth strip is always quieter than the other five, and there is always exactly one deliberate offset per page.

One thing to watch on all three. These are rendered at a fixed 1600 by 900 slide stage. Turning any of them into a responsive website means deciding what the six strips do on a phone. The honest answer from the cloth is that you do not shrink a strip, you use fewer of them: six on desktop, three on tablet, two on a phone, module width held constant. That rule is in the weave and it happens to be correct responsive design.
