Turning "build your own AI operating system" into something you sell

A pressure-tested read on Jeff Su's "Claude Cowork" content and whether it becomes a workshop, a video, or a bundle, without competing with a bigger creator on his own turf and without pulling focus off July 30.

KEEP → WITH CHANGE Not a clone of Jeff Su's video. A fast-follow upsell inside what you already built.
Not a pass — the on-ramp is real and the proof point (you actually run a business on this, not just a demo of it) is one nobody teaching this niche can match. Not an unconditional yes either — teaching the file structure the way Jeff Su already teaches it, as a standalone new product this week, fails on three fronts: it competes with a creator who already owns the niche, it gives away the exact how for free, and it pulls attention off July 30 at the worst possible moment. The move that survives every angle: fold it in as a fast-follow module inside the workshop you already built, gated until after July 30, using the email inbox-zero build your team is already making as the flagship demo.

The Source Material, In Plain Terms

Jeff Su published a walkthrough of a personal AI setup he calls a "Cowork" workspace: one root instructions file that governs behavior, one memory file that carries context between sessions, a voice file trained on his own writing, and a set of "workstations", separate folders for email, personal finances, travel, each with its own instructions and memory. The pitch: these are just text files, no code, and the system gets smarter every session because it remembers what happened last time. He is building a paid course around it, on a waitlist.

That is genuinely useful and it is genuinely rung one. You already live at the top of that ladder: a real multi-agent operating system that runs outreach, content, finances, and client delivery for an actual business, with governance built in because you have already been burned by the failure modes a beginner setup runs straight into. The opportunity isn't to out-teach Jeff Su on his own video. It's to be the operator standing ten rungs up the same ladder, and to sell the climb.

The Format Decision

Recommended: fold it into the July 30 funnel as a fast-follow upsell ›

Not a new live workshop. Not a standalone paid course to compete with a creator's own course. A lightweight template kit plus a short recorded walkthrough, sold as the next step inside the funnel that already exists for "AI Without Getting Burned." The deck, checkout, ads, and audience are already live and already primed on the exact right topic: using AI without getting burned. This is the natural next offer to the people who just paid to hear that message, not a new audience you have to build from zero.

Why this wins over the alternatives: zero new funnel, zero new ad spend, zero new production pipeline. The only new asset is the kit itself and one recording, and the recording can be the same screen capture your team is already producing for the inbox-zero demo.

Alternative considered: a video he records himself ›

Viable as the delivery mechanism for the walkthrough, but wrong as the whole offer if it stands alone. A free YouTube-style video with no funnel behind it competes directly with Jeff Su's video, on his platform, for his audience, and gives away the how for nothing in return. Use a recording as the deliverable inside the paid kit, not as a public standalone piece.

Alternative considered: a live paid workshop, bundled with July 30 ›

Tempting because the infrastructure for a live workshop already exists, but it is the wrong sequencing. July 30 is days out and fully built already; adding a second live session this close competes for the same prep time and the same attention on the same call. If a live cohort format is ever justified, it comes after the kit has proven demand as an upsell, not before.

Alternative considered: a standalone productized template kit, sold cold ›

The kit itself is the right deliverable. Selling it cold, disconnected from the workshop funnel and the operator story, is the wrong distribution. It becomes a commodity template competing on price against every other "AI setup" template on the internet. Attached to the workshop funnel and the operator positioning, it's not a template, it's the next step from someone who has already earned trust in the room.

I'll show you the file structure, and the governance layer that keeps it from getting away from you, because I've already been burned and built the fixes." Two things make this his and not a copy: the operator claim (he actually runs a business on this today, not a demo of it), and the governance lens (the same don't-get-burned instinct that is already the entire premise of July 30). Every piece of copy for this offer should lead with one of those two, never with "here's how Claude Cowork works," which is Jeff Su's sentence, not his.

Council — 5-angle pressure test

1. Differentiation vs Jeff Su ›

Real, but only if the frame changes. Teaching the identical mechanics, root file plus memory file plus workstations, the way Jeff Su already teaches it, reads as a slower, less-produced copy of a video that already exists and already has a course behind it. The differentiator is not the mechanics, it's the operator claim: you run a live multi-agent system that actually operates a business today, with governance built in from real mistakes. Teach that. Do not teach the file structure as the headline.

2. Commoditization risk ›

Real if the offer is "I'll explain the files." That exact how is now public and free on YouTube. The part that isn't free: the governance layer, the failure modes avoided, the operator story, and a live worked example built for a paying client. Sell the layer Jeff Su's video doesn't have, not the layer it already gave away.

3. Funnel-fit: on-ramp or distraction ›

This is the strongest angle in favor. "Build your first personal AI operating system" is a textbook low-ticket entry point that graduates naturally into a full engagement. It becomes a distraction only if built as a standalone product disconnected from the upsell path. Positioned as the next step inside the existing funnel, it's additive, not competing, for attention.

4. Time and bandwidth against July 30 ›

Real risk if anything new gets built this week. July 30 is fully built already, deck, funnel, ads, checkout. A second live session or a new production project right now would split prep time and attention at exactly the wrong moment. The safe sequencing: nothing new until July 30 ships, then this becomes the fast-follow.

5. Convergence vs divergence with the moats ›

Converges hard on the single strongest moat. "Built a real agentic operating system that runs a business" is the hardest-to-copy proof point owned here, and almost nobody else teaching AI productivity content can say it with receipts. It also converges on the HR governance instinct. It does not touch the African / American bridge moat, and it shouldn't be forced to. Named honestly rather than stretched.

Risks and the Single Biggest Failure Reason

The single biggest reason this fails ›

The positioning slips back into "here's how the Cowork setup works" instead of "here's rung one of the system I run my business on." If the copy or the recording ever reads as re-teaching Jeff Su's video, the whole differentiation collapses and it becomes a cheaper, later, less-produced copy of something already free. Every piece of copy needs the operator claim or the governance claim in the first few lines, never the mechanics alone.

Sequencing risk ›

Anything built or launched before July 30 fully ships pulls prep time and attention from a workshop that's already live with paid ads running. The gate has to hold even if this feels ready sooner.

Dependency risk on the inbox-zero build ›

The flagship demo depends on a build in progress. If it slips past the target window, the plan needs a fallback demo or a delay, not a rushed replacement built just to hit a date.

Scope creep risk ›

The walkthrough recording is the one part of this plan that could quietly grow into its own production project. Hard cap it as a screen capture of the real demo, nothing more, or it becomes a second workshop by another name.
