4. The angle, and why it survives pressure

AI is not your ghostwriter. It is your staff.

Dino's own published thesis leaves his readers with a problem he has not solved. He told them the money moved to implementation, service, access and retention, and that roughly 90 percent of his biggest month came from people who had already bought. All of that is labour. A solo creator cannot do it, which is why almost none of them do. They read the article, agree with it, and go back to chasing new followers.

AI is bad at the job creators point it at, which is sounding like a human, and good at the job they never point it at, which is the unglamorous operational follow-through. So the argument is: keep the robot completely away from your voice, and put it in your back office.

7. Sample two: the short piece, in his history mode

Different mode, showing range. This is the move where he drops a historical figure into the middle of a business argument, the way he uses Caesar and Joe Girard.

THE MAN WHO HIRED BETTER MEN THAN HIMSELF

Andrew Carnegie could not make steel. That was never the point.

Andrew Carnegie did not know how to make steel.

Read that again, because it is one of the strangest facts in American business. The man whose name is on the steel industry could not have run a furnace to save his life. He was not an engineer. He was not a chemist. He was not a metallurgist.

He wrote his own epitaph, and this is what it says:

"Here lies a man who knew how to enlist in his service better men than himself."

Not a man who worked harder than everyone. Not a man who knew more than everyone.

A man who knew how to get the work OUT of other people.

Now think about how the average creator online operates today.

He does everything. He writes the post, edits the video, answers the emails, chases the invoice, updates the spreadsheet, builds the landing page, and somewhere in there tries to have an original thought. Then he wonders why he has been stuck at the same level for two years.

He is not lazy. He is the opposite of lazy. That is the problem.

→ He has made himself the bottleneck in his own business and called it work ethic.

Carnegie understood something the modern solo operator refuses to accept: your output is capped by what you personally touch. Every job you keep is a job that will eventually stop you.

Here is the difference, and it is the only reason I am writing this today.

Carnegie needed capital and a payroll to escape that trap. He needed buildings and managers and thousands of men. For most of history, that was the price of admission, and it is exactly why so few people ever got out of the trap.

That price has collapsed.

You can now hand off the unglamorous half of your business without hiring a single person. Not the writing. Not the thinking. Not your voice. Nobody should ever touch those, and anybody telling you to automate your personality is selling you a fast route to being forgotten.

But the follow-up, the records, the reminders, the hundred small jobs that quietly decide whether a customer stays or leaves? Those were always meant to be somebody else's job.

You just never had a payroll.

Now you do.

Carnegie's genius was knowing which work was his.

That is still the whole game.

Go find out which work is yours.

Then put down everything else.

- Dino Anthony
