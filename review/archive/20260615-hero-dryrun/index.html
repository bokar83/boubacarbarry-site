HERO DRY-RUN · what attendees see when they run it

The Decision Council, on a real decision

This is exactly what an owner gets after pasting their own decision into the installed skill. Worked here on a decision almost every owner-operator faces. The point of the room: they watch this happen on THEIR decision, in 30 seconds.
The decision they paste
Owner's decision
"I'm thinking about raising my prices 20%. I haven't raised them in three years. I'm worried I'll lose my regulars, but I'm working more for less every month."
The council answers (5 voices, 30 seconds)
1. The Contrarian

Raising prices will not fix the real problem if the real problem is that you take every client who calls. You will make more per job and still be exhausted. A 20% raise is a band-aid on a yes-to-everyone habit. And "I haven't raised in three years" is your guilt talking, not a reason your clients will accept or reject it.

2. First Principles

You are assuming a price increase equals lost regulars. Test it: which regulars actually leave over 20%, and are those the ones draining you? The number that matters is not the percent, it is the margin per hour of your time. You are working more for less, so your real cost went up. You are not raising prices, you are correcting a price that quietly fell behind your costs for three years.

3. The Expansionist

Why only 20%, and why the same number for everyone? Raise 20% across the board, and add a premium tier for the clients who already treat you like the only option. Some of your "regulars" would pay double for priority and would feel special being asked. The bold version is not a price change, it is a two-tier offer.

4. The Outsider

A stranger hearing this thinks: three years without a raise while your costs went up is not generous, it is a business slowly bleeding. Every other thing you buy has gone up in three years and you did not quit those vendors. Your clients know that too. The fear of losing regulars is louder in your head than it will be in theirs.

5. The Executor

First move Monday: raise new quotes 20% starting now, and tell your existing regulars the new rate starts in 60 days, in one short message. What kills this: announcing it apologetically, or to everyone at once with no notice. Give notice, state it flat, do not explain twice.

Verdict

Proceed, but change the rollout: 20% on new quotes today, 60-day notice to regulars, and add a premium tier instead of treating every client the same. The price was not too low, it was three years stale.

Premortem (bonus mode) · "assume it failed badly 6 months out"

It failed because you raised the price but kept saying yes to the same draining clients at the new rate, so you are now exhausted AND have lost the two easy regulars who left, with nobody premium added. The failure was not the increase. It was raising the price without raising the standard for who you work with.

Then the Simplicity Check (the second skill, on the same brain)
What they paste
"My client onboarding has 11 steps: intake form, welcome email, contract, deposit invoice, kickoff call, questionnaire, portal setup, Slack invite, calendar share, intro video, and a check-in call."
The Simplicity Check answers

Overcomplicated: the portal, Slack invite, and calendar share are three tools doing one job (stay in touch). The intro video and questionnaire repeat what the kickoff call covers. Simplest version that works: contract + deposit in one step, one kickoff call that replaces the questionnaire and intro video, one channel (not three) to talk. Five steps, not eleven. Basics you are skipping: none of the eleven confirms the client actually knows what happens next, which is the only step that prevents confusion. Cut now: intro video, separate questionnaire, two of the three communication tools.

Dry-run verdict: hero produces 5 genuinely distinct angles (not 5 rewrites of the same point), a decisive verdict, and a premortem that catches the real failure mode. The Simplicity Check gives an owner a concrete cut-list in one pass. Both land on a decision the owner brought. This is the keepable wow. Recorded version of this = the de-risk parachute if live build stalls.
