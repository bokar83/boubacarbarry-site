The fintech-founder post skeleton

Drop fintech material straight into your own structure. Five beats.

  1. Hook - a counterintuitive claim, a customer truth, a real number, or a single image.
  2. The mechanic - one structural fact about how the system actually works. This is the authority beat.
  3. The pivot line - the screenshot sentence. Usually a word-swap or an inversion. Your strongest muscle.
  4. The cost or the proof - a real loss owned, or a real receipt shown.
  5. The close - a mirror, a sharp principle, or a specific question. Never a yes/no or a survey.

Glossary

22 terms a fintech ghostwriter knows cold. Tap to open.

Open the B2B fintech glossary
Interchange - the fee the merchant's bank pays the cardholder's bank on every swipe. Core revenue behind card-issuing fintechs (Brex, Ramp, Mercury).
BaaS (banking-as-a-service) - a licensed bank rents its charter and rails so a non-bank can offer accounts and cards. Fintech is the front end, bank holds the keys.
Sponsor bank - the chartered bank behind a BaaS fintech that holds the deposits and carries the regulatory risk. Its consent order is felt by every fintech on top.
Middleware - the platform between fintech and sponsor bank handling ledgering, compliance, program management. The Synapse failure is the cautionary tale.
Ledger - the internal record of who owns what money and what moved where. The source of truth. A wrong ledger is existential.
Reconciliation - matching your ledger against the bank's records to the penny. Mismatch means money is effectively missing until you find out why.
FBO account (for benefit of) - one pooled bank account holding many customers' funds, tracked per-customer on the fintech's ledger. The ledger, not the bank, says the money is theirs.
Omnibus account - another name for a pooled account. Same structure, same risk. Central to the Synapse/Evolve lockout.
KYC / KYB - know your customer / business. Legally required identity verification before transacting. KYB is harder (ownership, beneficial owners). The friction-vs-fraud tradeoff lives here.
ACH - the batch US network for moving money between accounts (payroll, bill pay). Cheap, slow, settles over days.
Wire transfer - real-time bank-to-bank movement, pricier than ACH, effectively irreversible. The irreversibility is why wire fraud hurts.
Card issuing - creating and managing payment cards, spend controls, and earning interchange. The engine under spend-management fintechs.
Embedded finance - financial products built into a non-financial company's product. The thesis behind "every company will be a fintech company."
Underwriting - assessing how risky it is to lend to a customer and setting terms. Too loose, you eat defaults. Too tight, you reject good customers.
Chargeback - a cardholder disputes a charge and the money is pulled back from the merchant. High rate = direct cost + a red flag to card networks.
PCI DSS - the security rulebook for any company touching card data. Non-compliance = fines + losing the ability to process cards.
Settlement - the final step where money actually moves, vs the earlier authorization. The gap between them is where risk and float live.
Float - money a fintech holds between funds in and funds out, which can earn interest. In a high-rate environment, a real revenue line.
Neobank - a digital-only bank, no branches, usually a fintech front end on a sponsor bank's charter (Mercury). The product is the app, the license is rented.
Interchange-plus - pricing = raw interchange + a transparent markup. The honest way to explain payment pricing vs bundled flat rates.
Sponsor-bank consent order - a regulatory action against a sponsor bank that can freeze every fintech program riding on it. Why founders now ask who their sponsor bank is.
BIN - the first digits of a card number identifying the issuing bank and program. Sponsoring a BIN range is part of standing up a card program.
