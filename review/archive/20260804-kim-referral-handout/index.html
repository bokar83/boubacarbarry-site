Kim and her business partner are one buying unit at the same firm, so this is really one relationship being asked for referrals, not two. The card is built to survive the hardest case: she hands it to someone who has never heard of you, and she does not have to explain you. That is why the credibility beat is the first thing on it.

Nothing on this page asserts anything about a reader's business that we cannot know. The qualifying line is a conditional the reader answers for themselves, not a diagnosis.

Why the Simplicity Check is printed on the card

It is the single change that moved this piece from marginal to shippable. Without it, the reader gets nothing until a session that has no date yet, which is the weakest part of the whole offer. With it, they get a usable thing in thirty seconds, standing in front of the person who handed it to them.

Giving it away costs you nothing. Knowing four questions exist is not the same as running them against a live decision with help in the room, and the card says so out loud: one you take now, two you come and get.

03The one sentence she says

She is not a salesperson and should not have to become one. One sentence, true whether or not the other person bites, and it ends by handing over the card so the card does the rest.

I went to one of his AI workshops in July. Here, this is the next one.

If she genuinely rated it, one optional middle beat. Only if she means it, and it is hers to add or drop:

I went to one of his AI workshops in July. It was worth the ninety minutes. Here, this is the next one.

Why it ends on "Here"

It converts the moment from a recommendation into a handoff. She stops talking, the card is in their hand, and nothing further is required of her. If she had to explain who you are, most people in her position would simply not bring it up.

Why the earlier version was wrong

It read "Ninety minutes, and you walk out with three things you can use." That is brochure rhythm, not speech, and it also put a promise in her mouth about what someone else would get. She can vouch for having gone. She cannot vouch for a stranger's outcome, and a partner at a restructuring firm would never talk that way to a peer.

05The date problem, and how this survives either Thursday

The room is not confirmed. Both candidate Thursdays are live options and neither is booked. Two ways to build around that, and I picked the first.

OptionVerdictWhy
Dateless card, live page carries the dateChosenOne artifact, printed once, never reprinted. Works before the date is announced and after. Works for the session after this one too.
Two-part piece, date on an insertRejectedKim has to assemble it, the insert separates from the card, and it is obsolete the day the session passes. It also makes her job harder, which is the one thing this piece cannot afford.

Why a wrong guess about the date cannot break it

Nothing on the card is date-dependent. The only variable is what the page says, and the page is one edit away at any time. If the room lands on the later Thursday, the card is still correct. If both fall through and it moves to September, the card is still correct. Cards already in circulation keep working.

What the gate changed

  • Moved the who-this-is-for line to the front. A stranger decides whether it is them in about two seconds, and the front was spending all of itself on you.
  • Replaced a flat description of the pain with the active version from your own published post. They closed the tab, which is a choice, rather than something that happened to them.
  • Cut a second "rather than" construction from the email so the same shape does not appear twice across the set.
  • Removed a filler adverb from Kim's sentence.

Refused on purpose

  • A reply-with-a-keyword close, and any ask that requires the reader to produce something with their name on it. Three closing shapes of that family have already been cold-read and all three failed for the same reason.
  • Any manufactured scarcity. No seat counter, no closing-soon line.
  • Any line whose job is to reassure the reader you are not selling.
  • Formal credentials. This is public-facing material and they belong in one-to-one email only.
  • Any geography. Nothing on the card narrows who you serve.
  • July attendance figures, in any form.
