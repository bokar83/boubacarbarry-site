This is the single highest-value finding of the sweep, and I found it by grepping for something else.

The framework is an acronym whose steps run from inventorying the AI tools already in use, through classifying their impact, finding the governance gaps, naming an accountable owner, locking a written acceptable-use policy, and setting a live review cadence. The productised offer is priced at $2,500. The category name and the tagline are both written and both good.

Why this matters more than any new lane on this page. For weeks every document in the system has carried the same blocker: "the fractional offer does not exist, four inconsistent internal price ranges, none dated after 2026-06-18," and that blocker has been named as the thing that caps eleven separate routes at "sounds interesting, send me something." A named, scoped, priced offer with a full delivery kit has existed since April. It is simply not in the Money Map, not in the five-lane plan, and not in anything written since. Nobody lied. The asset fell out of the index and every later pass reasoned from the index.

Why it is a better answer than designing a new offer

  • It has a scope, a price and a term already. That is exactly the three things the offer-decision needs and it is why that decision can take ninety minutes instead of a week.
  • The assessment is the retainer's scope document. Its own final deliverable is a prioritised action plan with named owners and dates. That is a statement of work you got paid to write. This is the standard shape in security: the assessment is sold as a report and its real product is the remediation contract behind it.
  • It sits exactly on the seam only he holds. Governance of AI inside an organisation is a people, policy and accountability problem wearing a technology costume. Fifteen years inside HR at large companies is what makes the people half credible; running a real multi-agent system daily is what makes the technology half credible. Almost nobody advising this market has both.
  • It is one transaction to the dollar goal, against sixty pack sales or thirty workshop seats.

The honest caveats, stated before you act on it

The recommendation, as one option among three, with the reason. 4. The three genuinely new lanes I am most confident in

These came out of the adjacency pass, and each is here for the same reason: it only works because of a collision almost nobody else holds at once. Fifteen years working inside HR at large and Fortune-100 companies, an MBA, and a man who actually builds and runs a multi-agent AI system every day rather than talking about one. None of these three is written down anywhere in the system today. I checked.

N1. Rent shelf space inside an IT provider's existing monthly invoice

The connection. Managed IT providers already bill your exact buyer, the ten-to-fifty-person business with no internal IT, a retainer every single month. They already hold the trust. They already have the billing relationship and the annual review meeting. Their core business is commoditising and they are all being asked for an "AI service line" they cannot staff. You become that line inside their invoice.

Why it is non-obvious. Everyone in this market treats IT providers as competitors or as a vendor category to be evaluated. Nobody treats the invoice they already send as a distribution channel that can be rented. And the HR half is what makes it stick rather than being commodity implementation work: they can do the tooling, they cannot do the policy, the adoption, the training and the complaints, which is precisely your half and precisely the half that generates the recurring conversation.

What it solves. All three of your actual constraints at once. The trust anchor, because theirs transfers. The retainer shape, because their invoice is already monthly. Your hours, because they sell and you deliver. One relationship is many clients rather than one.

The door. Named Utah managed-IT providers approached partner-to-partner, not as a vendor pitch, plus the peer groups and communities those owners sit in. Honest gap: my research pass could not verify a Utah MSP association or peer group, so the door is currently "named firms, one at a time" rather than "one room." That is a real weakness and it makes this slower than it looks.

Speed. Weeks to a first conversation, a month or more to a first referral. Not September money.

N2. Sell the employee AI-use policy as the door, because writing it forces the audit

The connection. Every small business now needs a written rule about what staff may and may not put into an AI tool, and almost none has one. It is the one AI thing an HR person can buy without a procurement cycle. But you cannot write that policy without first listing every tool actually in use, who is using it, and what data goes into it. That inventory is the consulting engagement. The policy is the receipt.

Why it is non-obvious. Policy is treated as compliance paperwork and priced like a template, free to ninety-nine dollars on the HR platforms. Nobody sells it as a diagnostic. The pattern is borrowed straight from security, where a penetration test is sold as a report and bought as a report, and its real product is the remediation contract that follows. Identical structure, and nobody has ported it here.

What it solves. It gives you a sub-thousand-dollar first transaction that requires no trust in you as a strategist, and it structurally produces the findings list that justifies the retainer. It also gives you a concrete sentence to say in any room instead of "I do fractional AI," which is the thing an outside reader already flagged as the gap.

The door. This is not new work. The policy is already a named deliverable inside the $2,500 assessment from section 2, which means N2 is the cheap front end of an asset you already own, not a second product. The channel is the HR rooms: the local chapter's standing speaker form, the fractional-HR membership in N3, and the buyers of the $19 pack.

Speed. This is the fastest of the three. A named price on a small deliverable can be quoted in the first conversation.

N3. Treat the fractional-HR community as a referral network, not a competitor set

The connection. Solo fractional HR practitioners serve exactly your buyer, are being asked about AI by their own clients every week, and have no answer. Each one is a channel with an existing client book. They sell people and process; you sell the AI half; neither of you wants the other's work.

Why it is non-obvious. The instinct is that a room full of fractional HR people is a room full of rivals. It is the opposite, and it is the only lane in this whole sweep where the audience is simultaneously the buyer and the channel. It is the same structure as N1 with a different profession, which is usually the sign that a pattern is real rather than lucky.

The door, and this one is already on your own board and overdue. The membership at fractionalpeoplepeople.com, $240 a year, application at /membership#application. Your Money Map row for it was due 2026-08-08 and is unpaid. Their conference is in Denver in September, 120-plus exact-ICP people, and membership is the entry condition for pitching a speaking slot with enough lead time.

Speed. Membership today. Denver in September. Referrals after that. Not September revenue, and the speaking pitch needs lead time you are already eating into.

Two more adjacencies that did not make the top three, recorded so they are not lost. The second stakeholder nobody serves: in a company of that size there is no CIO, so when "we should use AI" arrives it lands on the HR person or the office manager, who owns the policy, the training, the adoption and the complaints, and owns none of the budget. Every competitor sells to the owner or to IT. You are the only person in the market who has been that person for fifteen years, which changes the opener from a pitch to a rescue and gives you a champion inside the account rather than a prospect. This is a positioning move rather than a lane, so it belongs inside the offer decision in section 2. The bridge: the repository contains zero business-to-business research on African markets, so I am not going to rank a lane on it. It is the least substitutable thing you own and it deserves one honest research pass, not enthusiasm and not silence.
