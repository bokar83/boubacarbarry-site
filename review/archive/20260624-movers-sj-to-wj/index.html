Should You Hire Movers or DIY?

Verdict: Hire movers.
The cost delta vs. physical risk, time, and injury exposure for a 6-person household is not worth it. Estimated delta: $400 to $1,400 extra buys back one full day and zero injury risk.

Why hiring wins for this move

  • 2 real lifters, not 6. Boubacar + spouse are the only capable adults. Kids 10 to 15 can carry light boxes. In-laws at 65+ carry zero heavy items. A DIY "6-person crew" is actually a 2-person crew.
  • Time math. A 2-person DIY pack+move of a 5BR is a 10 to 14 hour day minimum. A professional 3 to 4 person crew finishes in 4 to 6 hours.
  • Injury risk is real. One pulled back or a fall for either in-law turns a move day into an ER visit. Not recoverable.
  • DIY is not free. Truck rental + dollies + moving blankets + straps + gas + return trip = $150 to $400. That is before the 12-hour labor investment.
  • Professional packing is faster and safer. No decision fatigue on fragile items. Properly packed boxes = fewer broken items.

Where DIY has an edge

  • Cost. Full-service local pack + move for a 5BR runs $600 to $1,800. DIY costs $150 to $400 in hard expenses.
  • Control. You decide the order, the fragile handling, and the placement pace. No coordination with a crew schedule.
These advantages do not outweigh a household where 4 of 6 people cannot move furniture safely.
Cost delta breakdown: Pros cost $600 to $1,800. DIY hard cost is $150 to $400. Delta = roughly $400 to $1,400. That delta eliminates a 10+ hour labor day, zero in-law injury exposure, and faster completion on a same-day move. Request drop-only (no unpacking) to stay at the lower end of the pro range.

Watch Out For

Hostage Load

Unethical movers load your belongings, then demand more money before unloading. You are legally entitled to have your goods released upon payment of the non-disputed amount. A binding estimate in writing — before anything goes on the truck — is your main protection.

Non-Binding vs. Binding Estimate

A non-binding estimate can legally go higher once the job is done. Always ask explicitly for a BINDING estimate. If a company refuses to provide one, treat that as a serious red flag.

Clock Start — Yard vs. Door

Some companies start billing when the truck leaves their facility, not when it pulls up at your address. On a 5-mile move this can quietly add 30–60 minutes of billable time. Confirm before booking.

Temp / Day Labor

Movers using day labor are harder to hold accountable for damage. Ask "are your movers W-2 employees?" If they dodge or deflect, proceed with caution.

No-Show Risk

Some smaller outfits overbook on weekends. Call to confirm 48 hours before your move date. Get a confirmation number or email. Don't assume you're locked in without explicit reconfirmation.

Damage Claims

Confirm the company carries cargo insurance before the move. Ask how damage claims work and get it in writing. This is easier to discuss before anything breaks than after.

Double Drive Time

Some states allow billing double the drive time on local moves. Utah law does NOT require this — but some companies try to include it anyway. Ask explicitly whether they charge double drive time.

Cost-Cutting Levers

📦

Self-Pack

Doing your own packing can save $300–800 on a 5BR home. The crew just loads, moves, and unloads.

📅

Weekday Morning Slot

Monday through Thursday morning slots are cheaper than weekends. Movers are also fresher and faster earlier in the day.

🗑

Declutter Before the Truck Shows Up

Fewer items equals fewer hours. Donate, trash, or sell before move day. A 5BR has room to make a real dent here.

🔧

Disassemble Furniture Yourself

Bed frames, desks, shelving units — the crew charges for disassembly time. Handle it the night before and save real hours.

Your Rights — Utah In-State Move

  • Interstate moves are regulated by FMCSA. This is an in-state move — Utah state law applies.
  • You are entitled to a written estimate before the move begins.
  • If a mover holds your goods and demands more money, you can pay the non-disputed amount and demand release of your property.
  • File a complaint with the Utah Division of Consumer Protection: (801) 530-6601 or consumerprotection.utah.gov
