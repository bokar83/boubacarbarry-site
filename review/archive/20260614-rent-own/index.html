Where to put the overage (does it cut taxes?) verify w/ CPA — limits change yearly

  • Utah my529 (kids' schooling) — clearest tax win. Utah gives a state income-tax credit (~4.85% of contributions, up to a per-beneficiary cap). Grows tax-free for education. The one bucket that pays you back on your state return.
  • HSA (if on a high-deductible health plan) — best dollar there is. Triple tax-advantaged: deductible going in, grows tax-free, tax-free out for medical. Fund this before a taxable account if eligible.
  • Traditional IRA — deduction is conditional. It lowers taxable income ONLY if you're under the MFJ phaseout (and that kicks in early if either spouse is covered by a workplace plan — Vania likely is). Once your businesses scale into six figures, the deduction is effectively gone. ~$7K each / ~$14K couple/yr.
  • Roth IRA — no deduction, tax-free growth. Direct contributions phase out at higher MFJ income, so at your target income you'd use a backdoor Roth. No tax cut today, but tax-free forever — strongest exactly when you expect much higher future income, which you do.
  • Taxable brokerage — the catch-all. No upfront break, but full liquidity (this is your runway). Long-term gains taxed at 15% fed + 4.45% UT.

Short answer to "does IRA maxing help with taxes?": a Traditional IRA helps only at lower income; once your businesses scale it stops cutting taxes (use a backdoor Roth then). The bigger Utah-specific tax wins are my529 (state credit) and an HSA if you qualify.

SBLOC & Pledged Assets — the "buy, borrow, die" play read this

Yes — this is exactly how the wealthy use stock without selling it. Worth understanding deeply, because at your asset level it may be your best move.

What it is

  • SBLOC = Securities-Based Line of Credit. A bank (Schwab Pledged Asset Line, Morgan Stanley, Merrill, IBKR margin) lends you ~50-70% of your portfolio's value, using the stock as collateral. You never sell.
  • Why "buy, borrow, die": selling triggers cap-gains tax; borrowing against assets does not — a loan isn't income. You spend the borrowed cash tax-free, the stock keeps compounding, and at death heirs get a stepped-up basis that erases the gain entirely. The rich literally never sell.
  • For your home: fund the down payment (or more) from an SBLOC instead of liquidating. You skip the ~$12-18K cap-gains hit AND keep ~$150K compounding in the market.
  • Pledged-Asset Mortgage (Schwab/Morgan Stanley): pledge securities in place of a cash down payment — finance the home with little/no cash down, no PMI, no stock sale.

The real risks (don't skip)

  • Margin call. If your portfolio drops below the maintenance line, the bank force-sells your shares at the worst possible time — and triggers the exact cap-gains you were dodging. This is the killer risk.
  • Variable rate. SBLOC rates float (SOFR + ~1-3%, varies by balance). If rates rise, your cost rises — unlike a fixed mortgage.
  • Concentration kills lending value. Volatile single names (Tesla, Nvidia, GEV) get low loan-to-value and high call risk. Private shares (SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI) generally can't be pledged at all. Only your diversified/index + large-cap liquid holdings count.
  • It's leverage on leverage if you also carry a mortgage. Compounds both upside and downside.

My honest read for you: use an SBLOC narrowly and conservatively — to cover the down payment and dodge the cap-gains drag, pledging only your diversified/large-cap liquid holdings and keeping the drawn amount well under ~30% of that pledged value so a market dip can't trigger a call. Do not buy a whole house on margin, and don't pledge concentrated growth names you're emotionally attached to. Best-case combo for your goals: modest fixed-rate mortgage (cheap leverage) + small SBLOC for the down payment (no tax hit, stock keeps compounding) + aggressive principal payoff once your $30K/mo income lands. That gets you the leverage, the tax dodge, AND the "kill the interest fast" outcome you want.
