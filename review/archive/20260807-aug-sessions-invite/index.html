The exact SQL, so the numbers are reproducible

Read only. Run against orc-postgres.

WITH rows AS (
  SELECT lower(trim(email)) AS em,
         coalesce(trim(location),'') AS loc,
         coalesce(opt_out,false)     AS oo
  FROM leads
  WHERE email IS NOT NULL AND trim(email) <> ''
),
per_email AS (
  SELECT em,
         bool_or(oo) AS any_optout,
         bool_or( loc ~* '(^|[[:space:],])(ut|utah)([[:space:],]|$)'
               OR loc ~* '^salt lake city$' ) AS is_utah
  FROM rows GROUP BY em
)
SELECT CASE WHEN is_utah THEN 'A_utah_confirmed' ELSE 'B_everyone_else' END AS segment,
       count(*) AS sendable_emails
FROM per_email
WHERE any_optout = false
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

--      segment      | sendable_emails
-- ------------------+-----------------
--  A_utah_confirmed |             315
--  B_everyone_else  |             414

The regex normalises the variants, so Salt Lake City, UT and Salt Lake City, Utah are one place, and the same holds for the Provo, Sandy, Lehi and West Jordan pairs. It also rejects the noise rows that pair a Utah sounding city with an out of state token, such as South Jordan, TX, which lands in B where it belongs.

The judgment call: do not acknowledge the cold list gap

Decision: skip it. No version of "you heard from me a while back about something else" appears in either draft.

The instinct to acknowledge the gap is an honesty instinct, and it is aimed at the wrong problem. The reader's problem is not that a gap exists. It is that they may not place the sender in the half second before they decide to delete.

Naming the gap does not solve recognition. It spends the two most valuable lines in the email on the weakest fact about the relationship, it invites "I do not remember you" and "why am I on this list" as the reader's very next thought, and it sets an apology posture underneath a paid ask.

What the drafts do instead is close the recognition gap directly and move on. I run Catalyst Works is four words, it lands in line one, and the From address on catalystworks.consulting reinforces it. A reader who does not remember can place him instantly, and a reader who does remember loses nothing.

The honesty requirement is still met, because nothing in either draft claims a relationship that is not there. No "as we discussed", no "circling back", no "following up on my last note", no implied prior interest. Each email is self contained: here is who I am, here is what I am running, here is the date and the price.

Recognition, not confession.

The footer is gone as instructed, that is your call and it is applied. But as things stand the send would go out with no disclosed opt-out mechanism. That matters here specifically because this is 729 people on a list migrated from cold outreach, which is the exact population where the opt-out is the enforced part of the standing decision.

Two things are easy to conflate here, so worth separating. The system can process an opt-out: the outreach engine parses inbound STOP, UNSUBSCRIBE and "remove me" replies, writes a suppression row and gates on leads.opt_out. That machinery is real and it works. What is missing is the disclosure, the part that tells a recipient the option exists. An unadvertised keyword parser is a processing capability, not a notice.

Finding 2 · with these exact bodies, the signature would not attach at all

The send path appends the primary signature by default, but it is guarded:

if append_signature:
    sig = _gmail_signature(token)
    if sig and "catalystworks.consulting" not in html_body[-600:]:

The guard exists to avoid duplicating contact details when a body already ends with them, and it matches on the bare domain string. Both drafts carry a registration URL on that domain near the end, so the guard reads them as a signature that is already there and skips the real one. Smallest fixes that respect "no footer in the body"

None of this was implemented. Recommending, not acting.

  • 1. Add a List-Unsubscribe header, plus List-Unsubscribe-Post. Strongest option and invisible in the body, so it satisfies "no footer" exactly. Gmail and Outlook render a native unsubscribe control at the top of the message, and both now effectively expect one on bulk sends. It also helps deliverability on a 729 recipient send.
  • 2. Add one opt-out line to the HTML signature itself. One edit in your Gmail settings, applies to every send, keeps the body clean. This is what your own instruction points at, since you expect the signature to be doing this job.
  • 3. Fix the guard so the signature actually attaches. It should test for the signature marker, the wrapper class or your full name, rather than a bare domain that any legitimate link trips. This one touches the system's own source, which is behind the approval gate, so it is flagged here rather than changed.
Recommendation: 1 and 2 together, then 3. The header plus a signature line covers the disclosure without putting a single character back in the body, which is what you asked for. The guard is the real bug, but the first two are what unblock a send.
