04 Why it filled up, and it was not you 2 defects

You already run a tool that files this mailbox every night at one in the morning. It has been running the whole time. Two faults explain why the inbox still went from 14 messages to 53 in eight days.

1. The sorting labels have been failing silently since mid-July. The nightly tool looks for labels named Mailbox Actions Needed / Follow Up. The labels in your mailbox are actually named InboxZero / Follow Up. The names do not match, so every night it logs a warning to a file nobody reads and then sorts nothing. Last night it found 48 things needing action and labelled none of them. One line of the script fixes it.
2. LinkedIn was never on the noise list. The tool only files senders it already knows are noise, and LinkedIn is not among them. That is the entire pile. 29 of the 42 messages I filed today were LinkedIn, and they arrive daily. Left alone, this comes back within a week.
The judgement call is yours, which is why I did not just change it. Auto-filing LinkedIn mail is not free. The same sender address carries both the newsletters you do not want and the alerts telling you a real person messaged you. Filing all of it means those alerts stop reaching your mail, and you pick them up on LinkedIn instead. That is the trade. My recommendation is to take it, because you check LinkedIn anyway and the notification is a worse copy of a message that already lives there. Another lane is mid-flight on the same script, so I have flagged both fixes rather than editing underneath them.
05 Every change, and how to undo it reversible
