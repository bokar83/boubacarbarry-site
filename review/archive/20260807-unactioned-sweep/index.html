The LinkedIn recommendations, in full

You said you were sure this was planned and never executed. You were right, and finding it changed how I ranked everything else.

Planned twice. Dropped twice. Never started.

26 June. The rolling workshop review, action table, row 5: "Collect 3+ testimonials or LinkedIn recommendations." Owner: you. Due: 29 June. Same page, in the same block: "Even 3 LinkedIn recommendations changes the page conversion rate. Ask directly."

23 July. The data-aware profile audit, independently, four weeks later: a recommendation sprint, ten asks in ten days, waves at weeks 2, 6 and 10, target eight or more current recommendations by day 90. It carried its own tripwire: "if the asks are not out by day 10, stop everything else and send them." Day 10 was around 2 August. It passed with nothing sent.

Why it is worth more than it looks

You have three recommendations and the newest is from 2015. You are selling trust. The audit scored social proof at 1 out of 10, the floor of all twelve dimensions it measures, and said the thing plainly: no copywriting moves that number, only asking does. Every other fix on that page was a paste session. This is the one that takes weeks, which is exactly why it kept losing to things that could be finished the same afternoon.

The actual reason it died, both times

The plan says it has a "pre-named list" and "three named pools." It does not. The pools are categories, not people: practice-era contacts, colleagues from the corporate years, and a refresh from the three existing recommenders. No roster of names exists anywhere in the review archive, the repo, or memory. I looked specifically, and so did two other passes.

So every time you sat down to run a sprint that was described as ready, the first step was still a blank page. The July page even predicted this in its own premortem and called it "rec sprint died of politeness." That diagnosis was wrong. It did not die of politeness. It died of a missing list.

One ruling you should know I made

The plan aims the asks backwards, at the 2015-era network. Nearly half your connections were built between 2013 and 2017 and a fifth share one former employer, which is the exact impression the audit is trying to break. Asking that group for proof risks confirming the thing you are trying to stop being. So I have pointed the sprint forward: August 27 attendees, the Startfest people, Hugo, current clients. It also solves the timing problem, because a forward-sourced ask can be made in the room on the night with phones out, instead of waiting on replies from people who last saw you work a decade ago.

The daily thirty minutes

Your ask, made countable. This is now a live row on the Money Map.

You asked for time, probably daily, of at least thirty minutes spent reconnecting with people and rebuilding your network, done intentionally. "Intentionally" is the load-bearing word, so it needs a definition of done that a machine can check, otherwise it becomes the tenth plan on this page.

Done, for a day

At least three named asks logged. Nothing more.

No new counter was built for this. It reuses the mechanism that already exists: you reply to the daily brief with the name or names you asked, and the day's count goes up.

log: ask Zack Oates, Cameo Doran, Sean Morrison

A day whose count reads three or more is a day that counted. That is the whole system.

One change I made to what you asked for, and why

Written as a pure reconnection ritual, a standing daily warm block breaks a rule you already set: warm contacts are a bonus, never the foundation, because the whole thing has to still work if you were dropped somewhere you know nobody. One of the review voices argued for killing this item outright on exactly that basis.

That objection is right about a warm-only ritual and wrong about this one, so the block counts any ask. A cold approach counts exactly the same as reconnecting with someone from the corporate years. A day spent entirely on strangers still passes. You keep the deliberate daily habit you asked for, and it stops being a machine for rebuilding a dependency you deliberately moved away from.

The number this exists to move

In ninety days there are six logged ask-days: 1, 2, 3, 8 and 9 July, then 4 August. The stated gate is five per day. The most recent entry reads two. This is the clearest number in the whole sweep, and it is the one thing on this page that, if it changes, makes everything else on this page happen on its own.
