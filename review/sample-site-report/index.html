The 30-second read

A new visitor can tell what this business sells. That part works. But there is no reason to act in the first screen, the only way to start is to call, and there is nothing on the page that proves the business is trustworthy. Those three things, in that order, are where it leaks.

What is leaking

01High impact

No single clear action above the fold

The first screen describes the service but gives the visitor nothing obvious to do next. People do not hunt. They leave. There is no primary button, no form, no one clear next step competing for attention.

First fixOne primary action high on the page (book, request a quote, or start here), visually dominant, repeated once lower down.
02High impact

The phone number is the only way in

Calling is a high-friction first step for a stranger who is just comparing options. Many will not pick up the phone for a business they do not know yet. Every visitor who is not ready to call has no path at all.

First fixAdd a low-friction start: a short form or a booking link, so people can begin without a phone call. Keep the number for those who want it.
03High impact

No proof anywhere on the page

No reviews, no photos of real work, no named results, no recognizable trust signal. A first-time visitor has no reason to believe this business over the next tab they have open.

First fixAdd three real reviews and one before-and-after or finished-work shot near the top. Proof beats adjectives every time.
04Medium impact

Slow first paint on mobile

On a real run I load the page on a phone and time it. The hero loads heavy and late, which quietly costs leads before anyone reads a word. Most local buyers arrive on a phone, on a weak connection.

First fixCompress the hero image, defer anything below the fold, and aim for a usable first screen in under two seconds.
05Medium impact

Invisible to AI search

I check whether tools like ChatGPT and other assistants can actually read and recommend the page. This one has no structured data and thin, generic copy, so when someone asks an assistant for a recommendation in this category, this business does not come up.

First fixAdd structured data, a clear FAQ in plain language, and specific location and service phrasing so assistants can read and surface it.

Where I would start

If this were my client, I would not redesign the whole site. I would fix the first screen first: one clear action, a low-friction way to start, and three real proofs near the top. That alone usually moves more than a full rebuild, and it ships in days, not months.
