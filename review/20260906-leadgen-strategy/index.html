What the video actually gave us

One axis that nothing we own touches. Everything we have runs us to prospect: one to one, cold, bounded by how many messages a human can personally send. The video runs us to partner to their trusted audience: one to many, warm by association, bounded by relationships rather than by volume.

A partner is defined narrowly and usefully: someone who already serves our exact buyer, already has their trust, and does not compete with us. Not a referral source to be asked for favours. A distribution channel to be handed something valuable.

The mechanisms worth taking

  • An assessment beats a PDF. It qualifies while it gives. His book-launch figure: roughly 270 of 290 people who started one finished it.
  • Only about 8% of any market is shopping for what you sell. That is the most competitive and most price-driven slice, and by the time you reach it the shortlist is already written. Reach the people who have the symptom but have not gone shopping yet.
  • Write the symptom in their language. The recurring correction all workshop long: we describe the buyer's problem as the absence of our service. A junk hauler does not worry about his AI visibility. He worries the phone is not ringing.
  • Start at the C-list. You serve the same people I do, we do not compete, would you be open to fifteen minutes to see whether there is a way to collaborate.
  • The partner call is a give. Listen for ten minutes. Find what they are trying to achieve. Slot the asset into their goal, or do not bring it out at all. His own start: he did not ask a co-working space for clients, he offered a free session that solved the space's own retention problem. It produced his first six-figure year.
  • Write the agreement in an email the same day. I do this, you do that, in this channel, on this date. Offer to draft their email so their only remaining action is pressing send.
  • Reverse-engineer whether they can pay you. Roughly 10% of revenue is the marketing budget, minus the internal salary it already funds, minus 10 to 20% held back for contingency.

The two things that could kill this

1. We already tried audience holders and got zero

On 2026-08-07, five chambers and BNI chapters were emailed. The result: one out-of-office auto-responder, zero human replies. Those are audience holders. That is this strategy, already tried, already failed.

Why that is not a refutation: those five were asks, variations of here is what I do, would you put me in front of your members. The mechanic being adopted is the opposite shape: I have something ready for your empty slot.

But honesty first. That is a hypothesis, not a result. The chamber test is the only partner-channel data we own, and it says zero. So this plan treats a partner reply rate as unknown and to be measured. The video's quoted 50 to 80% response rate is not inherited, because it is explicitly conditioned on already knowing the person.

2. The parachute test

Your standing rule: warm leads are a bonus, never foundational. Dropped somewhere you know nobody, the system must still work. The video's mechanism is literally someone in your network already has them, which taken at face value would violate that rule.

The resolution is not a compromise: partners are sourced by category, not by network. The question is "who else sells to this exact buyer and does not compete with me", and that is answerable from a standing start, anywhere, by anyone, from public websites. Suppliers, adjacent trades, associations, trade publishers, the software vendor everyone in the vertical uses. Checking that category list against people you happen to know is a second pass that speeds things up. It is never what generates the list.

This is more parachute-compliant than cold email, not less. A stranger in a new city can name the adjacent vendor categories for any local trade in an afternoon.

What starts Tuesday, and the weekly rhythm

Tuesday is deliberately light. The HARTING interview is at 14:00.

  1. Clear the gate tap on the follow-up drafter. Ten seconds. Nothing else is safe to start until a captured lead actually gets chased.
  2. Fix one ICP. One buyer, one city, one vertical, describable as a real person.
  3. Run the message test. Draft, never send, a short question to 15 contacts drawn from the dormant list: I built something that tells you whether you show up when a customer asks an AI for a recommendation in your area, and who it names instead of you. One to ten, how useful is that to you?

The gate: a median rating of 7 or above, go to partners. Below 7, stop and fix the asset before spending a single unit of partner goodwill. That is a real stop, and if it fires it is this plan working, not failing.

Then, weekly

  • Thirty minutes a day, on the calendar, defended like a client meeting. Two partner touches a day, maximum. Volume is the failure mode being escaped, not the goal.
  • Any yes becomes a fifteen-minute call. Three questions, and listen for the first ten minutes. Here is what I already know about your business, what have I missed. What are you trying to achieve right now. What is getting in the way. Do not pitch.
  • Friday, four numbers and only four: conversations held, share commitments, scans completed per share, qualified scans that booked.
  • Stop rule: two weeks with no share commitments, or any single week with four or more touches and zero conversations, and you go back to the message test.
  • Week three, in parallel: run the scan across about 100 businesses in one vertical, roughly five to fifteen dollars, and publish the aggregate. It becomes the thing partners ask to share.

Why this should beat what came before
  1. It fixes the constraint we can actually see. One reply produced one deal. Everything improved today was improved below that constraint.
  2. It is the first motion that instruments itself. A completed scan is a row in the database, a hard, recorded, queryable event. A failure becomes visible within a week instead of invisible for a quarter. The cold channel, by contrast, has one reply flag and no bounce field at all.
  3. Almost nothing has to be built. The two unwired pieces are a ten-second approval and a small routing change.
  4. It unblocks two plans already paid for. The May spec has waited 14 weeks for exactly one thing, a named channel. The June plan has waited 11 weeks for exactly one thing, a pitch that is a give.

The honest caveat. Partner distribution does not fix a weak offer, it amplifies whatever the offer is, faster and more publicly than cold email did, and it burns something that does not grow back. That is exactly why the message test is a hard stop and not a formality.
