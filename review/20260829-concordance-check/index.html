01

What agentsHQ is

Reading only the artifacts, with no prior context: agentsHQ is a solo operator's combined software platform and business-operations system. One person has built a large automation codebase — 15,010 commits, hundreds of scripts, a Postgres database with roughly 100 tables, a fleet of AI “agent” sessions and skills — whose stated purpose is to run several small businesses (consulting, a productized website service, ghostwriting, a digital-product catalog, a content-media portfolio) with AI agents doing most of the labor of building, selling, and even governing itself. verified table list + roadmap files

It also spends a very large share of its own effort maintaining, auditing, and repairing itself rather than running those businesses.

Does this read as a coherent, legible strategy to a first-time observer? Partially. The businesses are legible — five distinct customer-facing ideas, each documented. What is not legible from the artifacts is a single unifying “this is the machine and this is what it's for” — the system's own internal life (merge conflicts, USB-dongle debugging, model-cost overruns) occupies as much or more of the most recent, most detailed record as any customer or dollar does.

How it's set up
  • Codebase + agents. orchestrator/ runs on a VPS container (orc-crewai) driving role-based agents — Router, Planner, Copywriter, Web Builder, Consulting Agent, Hunter Agent — per docs/SYSTEM_MAP.md. verified
  • A Postgres database (orc-postgres) holding ~100 tables spanning leads, email sends, workshop registrations, Upwork tracking, purchase fulfillment, cost accounting, agent coordination, memory, and content review — the CRM/ops layer and the system's self-management layer share one database. verified pg_tables, live query
  • A governance layer (“the Gate”) arbitrates every merge to main and the VPS. It was itself the subject of at least 6 audit-registry entries in the last 3 recorded days (Aug 26–28), including a stale branch that could have reverted a $414/week cost-control fix. verified docs/audits/REGISTRY.md
  • A roadmap system tracks ~15 initiatives under one master roadmap, whose stated deadline — 3 paying clients by 2026-08-04 — is already 3+ weeks past as of today, with no file marking it closed, hit, or missed. inference no closure entry found in lighthouse.md
  • A memory system that is explicitly documented as chronically over capacity — its own maintenance note says the index regenerates to ~30.7KB against a 24.4KB load cap and is “visibly truncating.” verified CLAUDE.md

This is genuinely sophisticated infrastructure. It is also infrastructure whose most recent, most detailed written history is dominated by infrastructure problems, not business results.

Connected — and honestly disparate

Genuinely linked, by design: SW ↔ GW ↔ CW form one upsell ladder — “SW client → upsell GW retainer,” a $2.5K/mo bundle — sharing the same ICP, lead-gen infra, and cold-email engine. verified harvest.md

Linked, but loosely: Workshops and Upwork both feed CW top-of-funnel; HAW is framed as funding “runway” and building an owned list that could someday feed CW — aspirational, not yet evidenced by any actual conversion. inference

Genuinely disparate — and the system says so itself: Studio and Atelier are explicitly “Path B” — agent-run, zero-founder-time bets whose whole logic is that they don't depend on your time or voice, unlike Path A (CW/SW/GW, where “Boubacar-time IS the product”). Dashboards4Sale sits in its own repo specifically so it won't entangle with agentsHQ's codebase. There's no requirement, and no real evidence, that Studio/Atelier/Dashboards4Sale need to relate to CW/SW/GW at all — and the documentation agrees, rather than forcing a unity that isn't there. verified docs/roadmap/README.md

The biggest issue

This is structural, not one bug. Three compounding, independently verified facts:

  • Self-governance now consumes more documented attention than revenue does. The audit registry's most recent 6 entries, spanning 3 days, are entirely about the Gate merge mechanism repeatedly re-alerting on dead branches — including one where a fix for a previous cost overrun ($414/week from mis-routed model usage) nearly got reverted by the very system meant to prevent bad merges. verified
  • The master goal is already blown, with no visible closure. Lighthouse's “done” definition — 3 paying clients across SW+GW+CW by 2026-08-04 — is 25 days overdue as of today, and nothing found shows a reckoning actually recorded. inference — absence noted, not exhaustively searched
  • The one table built to prove a real sale happened contains exactly one row — and it's synthetic. purchase_fulfilments holds one row: livemode: false, a Stripe test session, the operator's own email as buyer. Ghost reports 0 sends / 0 calls / 0 clients at day 56 of 90. The Aug-27 workshop had zero paid registrations against $78+ ad spend. Studio's own status table still shows $0 against a $1,000+/mo gate, unedited since April. verified — all three

Put together: this isn't “one lane is broken.” It's a system that built extraordinarily deep tooling to manage itself while the proof-of-revenue layer, checked directly, shows no confirmed live sale across any of the five avenues in the tables designed to record one.

07

Analysis, verdict, and recommendation

The concordance question, answered directly: the strategies are legible and individually sound — the CW/SW/GW ladder makes sense, Studio/Atelier as separable zero-founder-time bets makes sense, each avenue states a real reason to exist. But the system's evidenced behavior does not currently match a deliberate, revenue-executing strategy. It matches a strategy that was designed well and then substantially consumed by its own tooling.

The clearest single data point: the table that exists specifically to prove a dollar changed hands contains a test row from the operator's own inbox. That is not “revenue is slow” — it's “the proof-of-revenue layer has never fired for real,” four-plus months into a 12-week sprint that was supposed to have closed three weeks ago.

Every avenue justifies its existence on paper — a stated ICP, mechanism, done-definition. Not one, on the evidence pulled, has cleared its own bar yet.

Closest to a 10× outcome with the least additional work: the Signal Works / Catalyst Works cold-email lane. It carries the freshest independently verified activity of any avenue — the leads table has 890 rows, most recent dated 2026-08-22; sw_email_log has 1,106 rows as recent as 2026-08-25. That's real, current, machine-verified motion, unlike Studio (stale since April) or HAW (one synthetic transaction). The infrastructure is already built and running — the gap is conversion, not construction. It's also the oldest lane, and per the tiebreaker rule, fresher verified data beats a better-written plan.

Recommendation: stop building more platform — governance, memory, new roadmaps — until this lane produces one real, livemode: true row in purchase_fulfilments, or until the Lighthouse goal is explicitly re-scoped. The system has ample capacity to sell; the evidence says that capacity has been spent on itself.
