What I want people to walk out with

Most AI sessions are a demonstration. Someone shows a screen, the room nods, and everyone goes home with notes and nothing running. I wanted the opposite. People bring a laptop, and in ninety minutes they build three things in their own free account and leave with those three things working.

The audience I build for is the owner-operator with no technical background. Somebody who runs a real business, keeps hearing that AI matters, and has not found anyone willing to sit down and show them the actual keystrokes.

A word on why I am the one teaching it

I help companies put AI to work without the confusion and the overwhelm. Before this I spent about fifteen years working in HR inside large companies, which is where I spent my time watching how people take up something new, and how they avoid it without ever saying no. That is most of what these sessions are really about. The technology is the easy half.

What I want out of running them

They are how I do this work in public, and they are how I meet the kind of owner I am useful to.

I would rather be straight with you about that than have you wonder. These are teaching sessions, not sales presentations. On July 30 I closed by offering a free one to one working session to anyone who wanted to keep going, and that was the whole of it. If a session run with the SBDC means no offer at all, that is genuinely fine. Tell me and I will cut it.

How people get into the room

This is the honest part, and it is the part I was most glad you asked about.

What I run myself

  • A page and a checkout per session. One page, one date, one button. Reminders go out ahead of the night and a follow-up goes out after.
  • Paid social pointed at that page. Targeted around the venue rather than by job title, because owners do not describe themselves the way an ad platform expects.
  • My own list and my own posts. Email, LinkedIn, X, each with its own tracked link so I can see which one moved someone.
  • Local listings. Chambers, Silicon Slopes, Meetup, the usual places an owner looks when they are searching for something to attend.
  • People I know here. A warm list of local connections that came with me when I moved.

What actually worked best

The single best piece of distribution I have had for these sessions came from you. The SBDC at Salt Lake Community College put the July 30 workshop in your newsletter. Nobody asked you to do that as a favour, and it reached people I had no way of reaching myself.

That is the thing I keep coming back to. Paid ads and my own list can put people in a room. An institution that owners already trust puts the right people in it, and it does it with a credibility I cannot buy. Filling a room from a standing start is the hardest part of this work by a wide margin, and it is the part where a partner changes the shape of the problem rather than just the size of it.

The material I ran on July 30

Thursday July 30, SLCC Miller Campus in Sandy. Doors at 6:30, session 7:00 to 8:30, ninety minutes with a hard stop. Beginner level, laptops open, no technical background assumed.

Everyone set up one free account and one project, and built three tools inside it. Not three accounts, not three subscriptions. One place, three tools, all theirs to keep.

The base

A business brain

Their business on one page. What they sell, who buys it, what they charge, how they talk, what they repeat every week. Written once, then reused every time. It moves to any tool, which is the point.

The one people remember

A decision council

Five advisors argue a real decision from five directions. The contrarian, first principles, the bolder version, a smart stranger hearing it cold, and the person who has to execute it. Then one verdict: proceed, change, or kill. People ran it on a decision they were facing that week.

The simplifier

A simplicity check

Paste in the thing you are overthinking. It comes back with the simplest version that still works, and what to cut.

The shape of the ninety minutes

  • 7:00What tonight is The plan, and what they leave with.
  • 7:05Plain English What the tools actually do, and the one trap that burns owners. The trap is getting locked in.
  • 7:13Open an account, make one project Heads down. Everyone builds from here on.
  • 7:19Build the business brain Their own words, on the night.
  • 7:28Build the decision council The block I will never cut. Everyone types a real decision and presses enter at the same moment.
  • 7:48A live look at a real business On the screen, start to finish, so they see it work at full size.
  • 7:54Build the simplicity check Heads down again.
  • 8:03Save it so it follows them Turning what they built into something that loads in everything they open afterwards.
  • 8:20Where to start on Monday Four minutes of silent writing, then they leave with one thing to do.

What I would tell you about how it went

  • Nothing was cut. The clock held and we finished inside the room window.
  • Questions came throughout rather than being saved for the end, and everybody stayed after to talk.
  • One attendee wrote back unprompted the next morning asking for a longer, day long version.
  • The clearest piece of feedback I got was a criticism, and it was right: I never told the room why they should listen to me before I started teaching. That gets fixed in the next one.

Why this is a low risk fit for a program you already run

You have had AI speakers before. The useful question is not whether I know the subject, it is what you are taking on by putting me in front of your clients.

  • It exists and it has run. This is not a proposal. There is a deck, a run of show timed to the minute, and a room that has already happened on your campus. Nothing would need to be built to try it.
  • It is vendor neutral by design. One of the six things I teach is how to not get locked into anybody's product, mine included. Everything people build is written in plain language and moves to whatever tool they choose later. A public institution cannot be seen endorsing a vendor, and this session does not ask you to.
  • Teaching, not selling. Covered above, and you hold the pen on it. If the answer is no offer, there is no offer.
  • People leave with something running. Three working tools in an account they own, rather than a handout they will not open again.
  • I carry the logistics. Room booking, printing, check in, event insurance, reminders, follow up. I did all of it for July 30. Your team's lift is distribution, not production.
  • It is repeatable. Same structure every time, which means a second session is not a second gamble.
  • It stretches if you need it to. An attendee asked me, unprompted, for a day long version. I have not built one. If a longer format is closer to how your trainings run, I would rather build toward that than fit you into ninety minutes because ninety minutes is what I have.

If I am not the right fit for the program, I would rather hear it plainly.
