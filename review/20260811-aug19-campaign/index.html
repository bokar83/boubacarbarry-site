FIG. 02The positioning
One sentence, and the reason it is the one
The positioning line

Your AI is not underperforming. You never onboarded it, and in sixty minutes on August 19 you will, on your own laptop, for $25.

The campaign does not sell a workshop about AI. It sells a diagnosis the reader has never heard for a frustration they have definitely felt. Almost every owner who has tried one of these tools got a generic answer back and concluded the technology is overhyped. That is a reasonable conclusion from their evidence and it is the wrong one. They skipped day one.

The reframe holds up because the session's actual deliverable is an onboarding document. The live page already describes the business brain as "one file that holds what you sell, who buys it, and what you charge." That is a new-hire brief. Nobody in this market is naming it that way, and naming it that way turns a commodity webinar into the answer to a question the reader is already asking.

Why he is the one who gets to say it

He spent roughly fifteen years inside the function whose entire job is getting a new person productive, he has an MBA, he is a GenX operator rather than a twenty-five year old builder, and he built and runs an AI system for his own business instead of reading about one. Any one of those is common. All four at once is not, and the onboarding lens falls straight out of the collision.

The credential stays out of the public copy. The lens does not.

Standing rule: formal credentials belong in one-to-one emails, never in public posts, and the string about running HR or operations is banned outright because it is not true. So the campaign uses the lens and never states the résumé. Not one public post says HR, Fortune 100, MBA, or a number of years. The insight lands on its own merit, which is stronger copy anyway. The credential is available for the six warm one-to-one notes another lane is sending, where it is allowed and where it helps.
