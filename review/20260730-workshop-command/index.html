The bottles

You are printing black and white at Brantoworks. The files below are PDFs now, not images, because a PDF carries a real 8.5 by 11 page and an image does not. Six labels per sheet, nothing on the page but labels and crop marks. All six codes were decoded straight out of the rendered PDF and all six return catalystworks.consulting/kit. There is exactly one way this goes wrong, and it is in orange right under the artwork.

The full 8.1875 inch wrap. Content sits in a centered zone with 2.48 inches of blank white each side, so the tape seam laps white on white and never crosses the code. Tap it to see it full size.

The one setting that ruins it

Print at Actual size, 100 percent. Turn OFF "Fit to page" and "Shrink oversized pages."

Fit to page silently scales the sheet down a few percent. The label comes out under 8.1875 inches, and then it will not close around the bottle. This is the single most likely way this goes wrong. Say it out loud at the Brantoworks counter: actual size, 100 percent, no scaling. A print shop will default to fit-to-page unless told otherwise.

Have them run one test sheet first. Measure a label edge to edge with a ruler. It must be 8.1875 inches, which is 8 and 3/16. If it is not, scaling is still on. Fix it and reprint. Then cut one out and wrap it around an actual Kirkland bottle to confirm the ends overlap on white.

Where the code points, and why

It points at catalystworks.consulting/kit, the page that holds everything from the night. That was decoded out of the rendered PDF itself, not read off a filename, and all six codes on the sheet returned the same address.

It does not point at the registration page, and that was a choice. Everyone holding a bottle is already in the room and already registered, so a registration link is dead weight to that audience. There is a second code image in the archive that points at registration, and if it had been grabbed by mistake, every bottle in the room would have sent 17 already-registered people to a signup form. It was one wrong file pick away.

No tracking parameters were added on purpose. The printed code is about 0.6 inches square on a curved, sweating surface under conference-room lighting. Doubling the URL length raises the module density at a fixed physical size. Reliability beats attribution on something you cannot revise after printing, and bookings landing Thursday night and Friday are attributable by timing anyway.

The condensation problem

A bottle straight out of a cooler is wet within minutes. Bare inkjet on plain paper turns to mush, the paper lifts, and the code stops scanning. Print, let inkjet dry 2 to 3 minutes, lay clear packing tape across the full face, burnish flat with a card, trim flush, then a double-sided tab on the leading edge and wrap so the seam closes on the blank white end.

Then chill one bottle for 30 minutes, let it sweat, and scan it with your phone. That is the only proof the laminate held.

The recommendation

Label 8 to 10 bottles, not the whole case. Front tables and photos. Unlabeled sealed water covers everyone else. Brantoworks removes the printing time, but not the handling time: cutting, taping and wrapping is still 4 to 6 minutes a bottle once you find a rhythm, so 8 to 10 is 35 to 55 minutes and 16 is 65 to 95. Wednesday already carries the timed dry run, the slide export, the laptop freeze, the account logins, the hotspot test and loading the car. Adding 95 minutes of hand-laminating to that day puts the dry run at risk, and the dry run is worth more than a fully branded cooler. Two sheets covers 12, three covers 18. Hard latest to still have labeled bottles Thursday: Wednesday 8:00 PM, before the car gets loaded. If Wednesday starts slipping, cut the labels entirely without a second thought. They are the cheapest thing on this page to drop.
