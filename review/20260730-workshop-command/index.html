The bottles

The print file is built, rendered and checked. All six codes on the sheet were scanned and all six return the right address. There is exactly one way this goes wrong, and it is below in orange.

The full 8.1875 inch wrap. Content sits in a centered 4 inch zone with about 2.1 inches of blank white each side, so the tape seam laps white on white and never crosses the code. Tap it to see it full size.

The one setting that ruins it

Print at Actual size, 100 percent. Turn OFF "Fit to page" and "Shrink oversized pages."

Fit to page silently scales the sheet down a few percent. The label comes out under 8.1875 inches, and then it will not close around the bottle. This is the single most likely way this goes wrong.

Print one test sheet first. Measure a label edge to edge with a ruler. It must be 8.1875 inches, which is 8 and 3/16. If it is not, scaling is still on. Fix it and reprint. Then cut one out and wrap it around an actual Kirkland bottle to confirm the ends overlap on white.

Reliability beats attribution on something you cannot revise after printing, and bookings landing Thursday night and Friday are attributable by timing anyway.

The condensation problem

A bottle straight out of a cooler is wet within minutes. Bare inkjet on plain paper turns to mush, the paper lifts, and the code stops scanning. Print, let inkjet dry 2 to 3 minutes, lay clear packing tape across the full face, burnish flat with a card, trim flush, then a double-sided tab on the leading edge and wrap so the seam closes on the blank white end.

Then chill one bottle for 30 minutes, let it sweat, and scan it with your phone. That is the only proof the laminate held.

The recommendation

Label 8 to 10 bottles, not the whole case. Front tables and photos. Unlabeled sealed water covers everyone else. Per bottle handling is 4 to 6 minutes once you find a rhythm, so 8 to 10 is 35 to 55 minutes and 16 is 65 to 95. Wednesday already carries the timed dry run, the slide export, the laptop freeze, the account logins, the hotspot test and loading the car. Adding 95 minutes of hand-laminating to that day puts the dry run at risk, and the dry run is worth more than a fully branded cooler. Hard latest to still have labeled bottles Thursday: Wednesday 8:00 PM, before the car gets loaded. If Wednesday starts slipping, cut the labels entirely without a second thought.
