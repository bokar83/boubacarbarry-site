Premortem: how the setup goes wrong
  1. The LLC got formed, but Catalyst Works kept invoicing and taking Stripe payments in your personal name, so the shield never engaged.
  2. SAM rejected the registration because the name didn't match the IRS record character for character ("LLC" vs "L.L.C.").
  3. SAM was submitted days after the new tax ID, before it posted. The IRS match failed.
  4. Money went to a third-party "SAM registration" site or a "Utah SBA(TM)" certificate seller. Both are free or fake.
  5. A third LLC got formed for the government work: split track record, double paperwork.
  6. Federal work went into the water-heater LLC, and a licensing dispute there became a federal problem.
  7. A holding company went on top "for protection" and broke the individual-ownership tests.
  8. Vania was added as a member without the CPA.
  9. An entity got named after a licensed trade.
  10. Business costs went on a personal card: no capital proof, a weaker shield.
  11. The first-meeting minutes and membership certificate were skipped, and NMSDC stalled months later.
  12. The NMSDC application went in before a paid invoice existed.
  13. The LLC moved into the revocable trust without checking the ownership rules.
  14. The S-corp election came too early and cost more than it saved.
  15. Your home address went public as the business address. Decide the business address first.
  16. Industry codes were picked too narrow (invisible to primes) or too broad (claiming work you can't do).
  17. SAM lapsed at the 12-month renewal and blocked an award. Calendar it the day it goes live.
  18. SAM's legal certifications got answered carelessly.
  19. A prime used you without naming you in its proposal, then dropped you.

The 7 tracks, fastest first

A. Federal small-business set-asides, $15K to $350K ONCE SAM IS LIVE (NOV-DEC)

  • Buys in this range are generally reserved for small business. You register; you don't need a certificate.
  • Below $350K there's no cap on subcontracting (13 CFR 125.6(f)(1)), and reselling is allowed. "Win it and sub the parts I can't do" is legal here.
  • Above $350K: no more than 50% can go to subs that aren't small businesses in that field.
  • Work your AI system delivers counts as yours. The test is money paid to outside firms, not your hours.
  • Reality: 6 to 24 bidders on marketing set-asides. Plan on 10 to 20 bids for 2 wins.

B. Subcontracting to big federal primes OPEN NOW

  • Primes on contracts over $900K have to hit small-business subcontracting goals.
  • Your protection: help write the prime's proposal and get named in it, and the prime owes you a good-faith effort (FAR 19.704(a)(12)). A plain quote doesn't count.
  • Method: find a Sources Sought notice on SAM.gov, look up who won that kind of work on USASpending.gov, approach 2 or 3 of those primes about that ONE opportunity, sign a teaming agreement for it. Never mass-message.
  • Pitch: "a small business that makes your subcontracting goal easy and does the AI work faster." Never identity alone.
  • Live triggers: VA Financial Management Leadership Training (due Sep 28; use it for prime research); Air Force "Total Force eLearning" (due Oct 6).

C. Utah state, city and county OPEN WHEN THE LLC EXISTS

  • Utah has no minority preference (a 2024 law cuts against one). You win on the offer and on relationships.
  • Free, same-day registration. The state can hire a consultant for up to $100,000 by direct negotiation. The county buys directly under $15,000.

G. Holding company / "be the face" AFTER A TRACK RECORD

  • Real models: Thompson Hospitality, H.J. Russell, Divisions Maintenance Group. Each one grew out of a core it actually ran.
  • Prosecuted version: Kousisis v. US (fraud even when the work gets done); settlements of $21.3M (Broadway Electric) and $48.5M (TriMark).
  • The line: you find, price, manage and answer for the work. Never lend your name to a bigger firm that does all of that behind you.
  • No holding company while the ownership tests matter.
