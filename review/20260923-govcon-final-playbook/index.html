The 7 tracks, fastest first

A. Federal small-business set-asides, $15K to $350K OPEN NOW

  • Buys in this range are generally reserved for small business. Registration is all you need; no certification.
  • Below $350K there is no cap on subcontracting (13 CFR 125.6(f)(1)), and reselling is allowed. "Win it and sub the parts I can't do" is fully legal here.
  • Above $350K: no more than 50% of the money can go to subs that are not themselves small businesses in that field.
  • Work your own AI system delivers counts as yours. The test is money paid to outside firms, not your hours. A 1099 freelancer counts as a sub.
  • Reality: marketing set-asides draw 6 to 24 bids. Plan on 10 to 20 bids for 2 wins.

B. Subcontracting to big federal primes OPEN NOW

  • Big primes on contracts over $900K must hit small-business subcontracting goals.
  • Your protection: if you help write the prime's proposal and are named in it, the prime has to make a good-faith effort to use you (FAR 19.704(a)(12)). Just sending a quote doesn't count.
  • Method: find a Sources Sought notice in your field on SAM.gov; look up who won that kind of work at that agency on USASpending.gov; approach 2 or 3 of those primes about that ONE opportunity; sign a teaming agreement for it. Never mass-message primes.
  • Pitch: "a small business that makes your subcontracting goal easy and does the AI work faster." Never identity alone. A March 2026 executive order makes race-based supplier picks risky for federal contractors.
  • Live triggers: VA Financial Management Leadership Training (due Sep 28); Air Force "Total Force eLearning" (FA895126Q4012, due Oct 6).

C. Utah state, city and county OPEN NOW

  • Utah has no minority preference, and a 2024 state law (HB 261) cuts against one. You win on the offer and on relationships.
  • Why it's fast: free, same-day registration. The state can hire a consultant for up to $100,000 by direct negotiation. The county buys directly under $15,000.

D. Minority-business certificate (NMSDC) for corporate buyers ~75 BUSINESS DAYS

  • Northwest Mountain MSDC covers Utah. $300 the first year, $250 to renew. You need 51% minority ownership with you as CEO, US citizenship, formation documents, and 2 years of personal tax returns. There's a webinar the first Tuesday of each month (next: Oct 6).
  • It gets you into corporate buyer databases and events. It does not guarantee spend: many corporations are folding "diversity" sourcing into "small and local" sourcing.

E. Catering, supply, broker (the Johnson Gas lane) DEPENDS ON Q4

  • Correction: wildfire crew catering runs on a national Forest Service contract through Dec 2030, with Salt Lake City and Kanab as dispatch points. The door to win it directly is closed until about 2030. The realistic wildfire move is to sub to the companies that already hold that contract here.
  • Open now: National Guard training meals, US Marshals training meals, and Army contract cooks. FY26 awards ran $108K to $247K with 6 to 15 bidders.
  • Food permits and insurance sit with whoever cooks, and the liability flows up to you.
  • Commodity resale (office and IT supplies) is crowded and thin, at 5 to 15% margin. The margin lives in the service wrapped around the goods.

F. 8(a) LATE 2027-2028

  • The race presumption ended 9/10/26. You now have to document discrimination AND material harm to you personally. It also requires 2 years in business with contract history.
  • Trap: 8(a) is STRICTER on subcontracting. The cap applies at every dollar amount, and your subs have to be 8(a) firms too. Loose "win it and sub it out" works best on plain small-business set-asides (A).

G. Holding company / "be the face" AFTER A TRACK RECORD

  • Real models: Thompson Hospitality (Black-owned, about half its revenue through a real joint venture with Compass Group); H.J. Russell (grew from plastering into construction, then airport and stadium joint ventures); Divisions Maintenance Group (a network of 16,000 technicians, no certification needed). Each one grew out of a core it actually ran.
  • Prosecuted version: Kousisis v. US (2025, 9-0) held it's fraud even when the work gets done and the government loses nothing. Broadway Electric paid $21.3M; TriMark paid $48.5M.
  • The line: you find, price, manage and answer for the work. Never lend your name or certification to a bigger firm that does all of that behind you. That offer will come.
  • Available now without government contracts: HOA and property-maintenance brokerage, the same shape as the water-heater play.

New this pass: training for laid-off workers (NAICS 611430) PARKED

  • The state list that pays for laid-off workers' training (WIOA) requires 2 years as a training provider, and the provider has to teach directly with no subcontractors. That makes it a 2028 lever.
  • Side door: Utah rule R152-34-6 exempts a school from registration if it charges no student more than $3,000 in 12 months. A practical-AI course priced under that can run now, paid by the student or an employer, and it starts the 2-year clock. Confirm with the Division of Consumer Protection before marketing it on job outcomes.

Risks + what is not yet verified

Pitfalls on every track

  • Cash flow: the government pays in 30 to 45 days, slower during a shutdown, while trade subs often want to be paid weekly. Ask for progress payments, write "paid when paid" into sub contracts, and start with services.
  • You carry the liability: a sub's failure goes on YOUR record. Stay in trades where you can write a quality checklist, keep a backup sub, and pass indemnity and insurance down to them.
  • Fronting is fraud even when the work gets done.

Not yet verified. Confirm with APEX or an attorney before relying on these

  • The exact wording of the "similarly situated" definition (13 CFR 125.1). The "small subs don't count" lever depends on it.
  • Which subcontracting clause text (FAR 52.219-14) a given bid uses after the FAR rewrite.
  • Bonding for federal construction over about $150K (Miller Act).
  • Utah contractor licensing (DOPL). Painting and most trades need a license even when a sub does the work.
  • Service Contract Act wage rules, which pass down to subs.
  • Whether a sub needs only a UEI (question 1 on the APEX call).
