About · 2,017 / 2,600 characters
If your business is working harder than it's growing, something is slowing it down.

I find that thing. I fix what matters most first, with the right tools and AI only when it fits. Then I do the part most consultants skip: I stay and run the systems with you.

You do not have an AI problem. You have a bottleneck problem. Your team dabbles with a tool here, a chatbot there, and none of it shows up where it counts: your output, your margins, how well you serve your customers. Fix the wrong thing first and a shiny new system just speeds up a process that was never the problem. Find the real constraint and even a small fix pays for itself.

So I do not build AI tools. I build better users of AI.

The work has a simple shape. You run the business. I run the systems with you.

It starts with a short, fixed-scope diagnosis. I go through your operation the way you would if you had the time, bottleneck by bottleneck. Where work piles up. Where the same fire gets fought twice. What a machine should draft, and what should never be handed to one. You get a written plan your people can actually run, in plain English. Then I train your people, we adopt AI safely enough to trust it, and the plan stays current as the business changes. If a fix does not show up where it counts, we change the plan.

I ran all of this on myself before I sold an hour of it to anyone. My own business runs on systems I built, including an AI operation that broke in ways I would not wish on you. I fixed it, it runs in production today, and what it taught me is written down in my Featured section.

This is for you if you run a business with a team, roughly ten to fifty people, you want it to grow without holding every system in your head, and there is no one on staff whose job this is. There is you.

You don't have to book anything. Message me the one thing in your week that keeps getting re-fought, and I'll tell you straight whether it's the real bottleneck or a decoy. If it's worth a longer talk, we'll find a time then.

Post title / opening line
I run my own business on the AI I sell people. Here is what broke.
Post body · paste under the title
Before I sold a single hour of consulting, I put the whole thing to work on myself.

A phone assistant that answers when I cannot. Pipelines that go find the people I should be talking to. The small daily automations that keep my own practice moving while I sleep. All of it running on a real business, mine, not a demo and not a slide.

I did not do this to look impressive. I did it because I have watched too many people buy AI, bolt it on, and quietly hope it works. I came up between two worlds, Guinea and the American family I grew up with, and both taught me the same thing in different languages. You do not get to sell someone a tool you have not lived with yourself.

So I lived with it. And it broke in ways I would not wish on you.

The lead pipeline is the one that still makes me wince. I built it to feed me the right conversations. For a stretch it fed me the wrong ones, confidently, on schedule, every morning. The automation was not the problem. My assumption underneath it was. I had pointed a very capable machine at the wrong target, and it did exactly what I asked, faster than I could catch it.

Fixing that taught me more than any playbook could. The tool was never the hard part. Becoming the kind of operator who could aim it was.

That is why I trust myself with someone else's operation. I found the constraint slowing my own business down. I fixed what mattered first, and I reached for AI only where it actually fit. Then I stayed and ran the systems, because a tool handed over and walked away from is just a bill.

I do not build AI tools. I build better users of AI. You run the business. I run the systems with you.

If you are staring at your own operation wondering where AI actually belongs, message me. I have made the mistakes already.
