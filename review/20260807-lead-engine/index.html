The fastest fix, and the honest caveat

Re-enabling that one crew is a single reversible command. I have not run it, because flipping a crew is a shared-infrastructure change and that is on the list of things I bring to you rather than do.

But re-enabling it alone will not restart posting, and I would rather say that now than have you flip it and see nothing happen. Twenty-eight of the twenty-nine Ready posts carry no quality score, and the publisher blocks anything unscored. So the crew flip restarts the pipe; the posts still need a scoring pass before anything moves.

Which is why the recommendation below routes around it entirely. An agent can score the backlog and hand you one finished post a day. You paste it. That needs no infrastructure change, no crew flip, and no approval from you beyond the paste. It also respects the standing rule that nothing auto-publishes to your personal accounts.

  2. The brand freeze flag from July 30 is still sitting there. The rate that triggered it has since recovered to 1.52 percent, and the sentinel itself now prints flag present but rate now OK, consider removing. Nobody removed it. It currently blocks this brand's sends.

The blocker that outranks the date

Stripping the closing footer did exactly what you asked. What it exposed is that nothing else was carrying the opt-out either.

  • Your signature was read live from the mail settings. It carries your name, title, company, a quote and a link. No opt-out.
  • Both drafts end with a registration link on the same domain. The code that appends the signature skips it when it sees that domain near the end of the body, which it does in both drafts. So the signature would not attach at all.
  • Net result if sent as written: no footer, no signature, no name, no company, no opt-out, to 729 people on a list migrated from cold outreach for a different offer.

The system can process an opt-out. Replies saying stop or unsubscribe are parsed and suppressed. What is missing is the part that tells anyone the option exists. A parser nobody is told about is a capability, not a notice.

Smallest fix that keeps the body clean, which is what you asked for. Add a List-Unsubscribe header. It is invisible in the body, so the no-footer instruction holds exactly, and Gmail and Outlook both render a native unsubscribe control at the top of the message. On a send this size it also helps deliverability rather than hurting it. Add one opt-out line to the signature as the second layer. Fix the signature guard third, since that one is a real bug and touches gated code.

The batch plan

This part changes shape once you look at the arithmetic, so I want to show the arithmetic rather than hand you a schedule.

A safe ramp cannot deliver 729 emails before August 19. Cold sends are Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday only. Between Tuesday the 11th and the session there are four permitted days. Opening at 25 and roughly doubling puts about 175 out by August 13 and about 315 by August 18. There is no version of this that reaches 729 people in time for the online session without a spike that is exactly the risk you named.

So the question stops being how do I send 729 before the 19th and becomes which 315 are worth the window I actually have.

Segment A goes first, and I would cut Segment B from this campaign

The instinct is that Segment B is the perishable half, since those 414 can only attend the online session and their value expires on August 19. That is true and it is still the wrong call.

  • Segment A converts on two offers over twenty days. Every email has two chances and a longer shelf life. Segment B has one chance at a twenty-five dollar item with a hard expiry twelve days out.
  • Segment A is the only half that can fill the room on the 27th, which is the higher-priced one, the capped one, and the one where consulting conversations actually happen. Someone who drives to Sandy on a Thursday evening and pays fifty dollars has qualified themselves by behaviour. An online twenty-five dollar attendee from out of state has not.
  • 278 of Segment B have no location at all. They are in that segment because we could not confirm otherwise. Opening a cold ramp on the least-known slice of the list is the fastest route to the bounce spike you are trying to avoid.
  • By your own rule the session is a tool for finding your buyers, and a signup who is not one is a cost. Four hundred non-Utah strangers, on a list gathered for a different offer, are close to a definition of not your buyer.

Nothing is destroyed by holding Segment B. Those addresses stay sendable for whatever comes next. They just do not get this particular invitation.
