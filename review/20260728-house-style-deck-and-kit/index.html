The whole deck in C, and the kit in all three

Two questions. Does the technical plate survive thirty-three slides, or does it only work on a hero. And does any of the three survive a long scrolling page with ten forms on it, read on a phone.

The live deck and the live kit page were not touched. You present Thursday from the deck that exists now, and attendees use the kit that exists now. Everything on this page is a mock built alongside them.

What changed since this morning

You saw one slide in C. One slide proves an idea can be beautiful. It does not prove it is a system. Thirty-three does. The thing that had to be answered first is the one you would have hit on slide four: what does a technical plate look like on a slide with nothing to draw.

The answer came from the discipline itself. A drawing set is not a stack of drawings. It is a set of sheet types, and most of the sheets in a real set carry no drawing at all: notes sheets, schedules, work instructions, specifications. They are still unmistakably part of the set, because what makes them belong is the sheet, the lettering and the line ladder, not the picture.

Full apparatusSheet border at 1.4 mm, zone letters A to C and 1 to 3, the seven cell title block. Eleven sheets out of thirty-three: the two covers and the nine blueprints. This is the loud version, and it only appears where the room is meant to stop.
Quiet apparatusBorder thins to 0.35 mm, zone letters come off, the title block collapses to one hairline strip along the foot. Twenty-two sheets. This is the default. Same discipline, a fifth of the furniture.
DrawingsNine sheets out of thirty-three carry a drawing. Twenty-four carry none. A drawing is only allowed when there is a real object and a real number. Everywhere else the sheet does the work.
The blueprintYour deck already alternates light and dark. In C the dark slide becomes a cyanotype: white line on indigo. That is not a dark mode bolted on, it is what a blueprint physically is, and it is also what a leppi reserve is, cloth the dye never reached. It is one token flip. Ground and line swap, red oxide becomes Fulani brass because oxide cannot hold on indigo, and no drawing or type changes at all.
Reading distanceHard rule enforced across all 33: no content ever lives in apparatus type. Dimension figures, zone letters and title block cells are set at 14 px on a 1600 px sheet and are genuinely unreadable from row 12. That is allowed, and only allowed, because nothing a person needs is written there. Everything a person must read is 32 px or larger.

Where I was tempted to use the pattern as wallpaper

Three times, and I want them on the record.

1. The empty right half. Notes sheets leave a lot of sheet showing, and the obvious fix is a faint weave tint across it so the slide "feels Fulani." That is decoration wearing the cloth as a texture, and it is the exact failure you named. The empty half stays empty. Sheet is not background, it is stock.

2. A motif band under every headline. A 40 px lozenge strip under each title would tie the deck together instantly and cost nothing. It would also mean the motif appears 33 times while meaning something twice. Cut. Headlines get a plain 0.7 mm rule, which is a real drafting convention and says only what it means: the title ends here.

3. The one I did take, and you should push back on if you disagree. Slide 06 draws the cloth rotated ninety degrees and keys the six principles to the six strips. Six is the real strip count of an arkilla kerka, and the list is really six items, so the drawing carries information. But I chose that content to draw partly because the numbers matched. That is one step from decoration and I would not do it twice in a deck. The five advisors and the three tools are drawn nowhere, because five strips and three strips would be a cloth I invented to fit a slide.

DELIVERABLE 2

The kit is a different problem

A slide is one fixed rectangle you look at for ninety seconds from twenty feet away. The kit is a two metre scroll with ten builders, roughly sixty input fields, copy buttons that have to be found under pressure, and a privacy section somebody reads before they will type their prices in. On a phone. Held in one hand. By a person who owns a heating company.

So the test is not which one is prettiest. It is which one still knows what to do when the content is a label, a hint, an example, an empty box and a button, sixty times over.

Each direction below is a real page, not a picture of one. Desktop is rendered at 1440 px and scaled to fit; the phone frame is a live 375 px viewport. Open either one full size from the link.

KIT IN A

Six strips

The strip is the column module. Six strips on desktop, two on a phone, module width held rather than squeezed. Seams are drawn, never hidden. The sixth strip stays quieter than the other five. One deliberate offset per page.

Survives, and it needed one change to do it. The column module is genuinely useful on a form: label and control in strips one to four, the worked example out in strip five where a margin note belongs, and on a phone the example drops under the label because two strips have no margin left to give. That is the cloth's own rule doing correct responsive design.

What broke and what I changed. Fields were bare underlines, which is beautiful and is not an affordance. At 375 px you cannot see where to tap. I gave the field a body. The second problem is the seam: it is drawn down the whole page, so it ran straight through the middle of an input and read as a rendering fault. The body fixes that too, because now the seam passes behind the writing, which is what a seam does under cloth.

What still costs you. The sixth strip is 17 percent of the page and it is empty on a form. On a slide that silence is composition. On a page where somebody is filling in nine boxes it is just a wide right margin, and I could not find honest work for it.

KIT IN B

Six baths

Indigo ground. The input field is the reserve, the cloth the dye never reached. Chalk annotation over the cloth for hints and labels. The idea is exact: you write in the part that was tied off and protected.

Does not survive, and it is the one I most wanted to save. The mapping is the best idea in the whole set: an input field is a reserve, the cloth that was tied off and kept out of the vat, and you write in the part that was protected. In the hero it is perfect. Look at the word TONIGHT and at THIS LAPTOP in the privacy heading. That is the strongest single moment across all three directions.

Then you add the form and it dies. One reserve is an event. Five, in the screenshot above, is already just cream boxes on blue. The real page has ten builders and about sixty inputs, so the meaning is gone by the second station and what is left is a wall of pale rectangles on a dark ground. The idea is destroyed by the one thing this page does most.

To be accurate about why, this is not a contrast failure. I checked: chalk labels and brass examples both clear the accessibility threshold on the indigo. It fails on density and on volume, not on legibility, and it fails worst exactly where the page works hardest.

Salvage. Keep six baths for covers, section breaks and social cards. And take the reserve into whichever direction wins, as a rule reserved for one moment per page.

KIT IN C

The plate

A scroll has no sheet edge, so the sheet border cannot survive the translation. What does survive is the zone rail: the letters that run down the edge of a drawing sheet become the ruler down the side of a long page, and it tells you where you are. Each builder is a numbered figure with a schedule of parts. The copy button is the issue stamp.

Survives, and it is the only one that came out of the translation better than it went in. I did not expect that. On a slide the plate is an atmosphere. On this page it turns into information architecture, because a form is a schedule of parts and a drawing office has been laying those out for two hundred years.

The schedule row is better than what the live page does now. Numeral, label, hint, worked example on the left, the control on the right, one hairline per row. The numeral is not decoration: it is the item number, it is the tab order, and it is what somebody says out loud when they need help at table four. On a phone the row stacks and keeps the numeral, so you always know which of the nine you are on.

The sheet border had to go, and saying why is the whole answer. A border promises you can see all four edges at once. A scroll can never keep that promise, so the border becomes a picture frame the moment you drag. Two edges are real, the head and the foot, and those are the two I kept. What carries the discipline instead is the zone rail. A drawing sheet letters its edges so two people can say "zone B2" down a phone. Turned onto a scroll that becomes a ruler that answers "where am I", the letter for the zone you are in follows you down and stops at its boundaries, and it costs 26 px on a phone. It is apparatus doing a job rather than apparatus being quoted.

What I had to give up. One title block for the whole page, at the foot, not one per station. Ten title blocks would be exactly the "it becomes a widget" failure. And there is no drawing anywhere on this page, which is correct: there is nothing here to draw.

The honest read

C survives both, and it is the only one that does. That reverses what I told you this morning, and the reason for the reversal is worth more than the answer: this morning I judged C as a picture, and a picture cannot tell you whether something is a system. Thirty-three sheets and a form page can. C held, because a drawing set already had an answer for the sheet with nothing to draw and I did not have to invent one.

Take C for the deck and for the kit. Keep A alive: it is a real second, its column module is the better answer for a marketing page where content is paragraphs rather than parts, and A and C share a spine, so a site can move between them without a seam.

Did the restraint hold across 33 sheets

Yes, and here is the count rather than the claim. Full apparatus on 11 sheets. Quiet apparatus on 22. A drawing on 8; twenty five sheets carry none. The busy problem you named was real and it came from one thing: the original slide put a dense drawing and a block of copy side by side and let them fight. The fix is that the drawing and the full title block now appear only on covers and blueprints, which is where the room is meant to stop, and the content sheets thin their border, drop their zone letters and collapse the title block to a single line at the foot.

The strongest sheets in the set are the blueprints, and the second strongest are the notes sheets, which have no drawing on them at all. That is the result I did not expect and it is the one that should decide this.

Projector legibility, and where it is worst

The rule I enforced is no content ever lives in apparatus type. Everything an attendee has to read is 32 px or larger on a 1600 px sheet, and most body copy is 34 to 42. Dimension figures, zone letters and title block cells sit at 14 px and are genuinely unreadable from row 12. That is allowed only because nothing anybody needs is written there. If a single fact ever migrates into the title block, the whole scheme fails.

Worst sheet: 15, the specification. The Decision Council prompt is ten lines of monospace at 24 px, and from the back of a long room that is a squint. It is the same constraint the live deck has, and the mitigation is the same one: it is printed on page 1 of the handout and you say so out loud. Next worst are 02 and 12, the two column schedules, which run 30 to 32 px. Everything else is comfortable.

What a sheet with nothing to draw looks like

Sheet 04 is the clearest example, and 05, 07, 08, 09, 18, 19, 22 and 27 are the same type. Figure number in oxide, headline, one 0.7 mm rule, the line that matters in indigo at 42 px, the paragraph under it at 36, and then a third of the sheet left as bare stock. No weave, no motif, no tint. Empty stock is not a gap waiting to be filled, it is the material, and that is the single hardest discipline in the whole system to hold on a busy build day.

Where this is still thin

1. Sheet 00's write-in lines for the wifi network and password are not mocked. Real omission, easy to add, but it is missing from what you are looking at.

2. The kit mocks show the hero, one full builder and the privacy section, as asked. They do not show ten builders stacked. My honest expectation is that C's schedule survives repetition better than either of the others because a numbered schedule is meant to be long, but I have not proved that and you should not take it from me until it is built.

3. Neither banned house style crept back in while I extended this. The blueprint is the one place to keep watching: it is a dark ground, and the day somebody adds a bright accent for a button state it becomes the look you banned wearing a costume. The rule that prevents it is that the blueprint is an inversion of the same four colours and never gains a fifth.
