Discovery question bank (fill the gaps live, never assume)

On the "diverse background":

  • "Walk me through the roles and industries you've worked in — what's the thread that connects them, if there is one?"
  • "Which parts of that work actually energized you, and which parts drained you?"
  • "What did you do at Verizon specifically, and how does it relate to what came before it?"
  • "Is 'diverse' about industries, functions, geography, something you studied, or something else? Tell me what you mean by it."

On "the right career path":

  • "When you say the right career path, what does that actually mean right now — a title, a type of work, an industry, stability, something else?"
  • "If you picture yourself a year from now in the right job, what does a normal Tuesday look like?"
  • "Is there a specific role or company you keep circling back to, or is it more a set of conditions you're after?"
  • "What would make a job feel WRONG, even if the title and pay looked good on paper?"

On the job search itself (needed for the framework below):

  • "How many applications have you sent, roughly, over these 6 months?"
  • "Of those, how many turned into a real conversation or interview?"
  • "Where does it usually stall — no response at all, first interview, later rounds?"
  • "How much of this has come through applying cold vs. through someone you know?"
  • "Do you have anything you can point to and say 'this proves I can do the thing' — a project, a writeup, a demo?"

Job-search framework for 6 months post-layoff

Six months with no offer is a stall, and a stall almost always sits in one of three places. The job in this block is to find out, with her real numbers, which one it is — not to assume.

StageSignal it's hereWhat fixes it
TargetingApplying broadly, unclear what role/title she's actually going for, resume tries to cover everythingNarrow to one or two target tracks that fit the diverse background, not all of it at once. A resume that tries to be everything reads as nothing.
Top-of-funnel volumeApplications going out, almost no responses at allTwo levers: (1) most roles fill through referral, not cold application — who in her network is one message away from the companies she wants; (2) materials tailored per-role fast, using AI as the speed tool, not the same resume everywhere.
Interview conversionGetting interviews, not converting to offersUsually a proof-of-skill gap or a story gap — she can talk about what she did, but nothing shows it live. This is exactly where the Ella Track-1 "build a portfolio piece" move earns its keep: a real artifact she can point to beats another bullet point on a resume.

The AI angle, concretely, tied to her stage: not "learn AI" in the abstract — AI as the speed tool for whichever stage is actually broken. Tailoring materials fast for targeting/volume. A demonstrable AI-built artifact for conversion. Research on target companies and warm paths in for volume.

What to leave her with regardless of stage: one target track named out loud, a real number on where her funnel currently breaks, and one thing to build or try before session 2.
