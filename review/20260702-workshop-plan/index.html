2. What attendees walk away with

  • Their own working AI assistant. Not a demo they watched. Set up by their own hands, on their own laptop, connected to their email and the tools they already use.
  • The practical skills loaded into it: scheduling, reminders, the research legwork they can now hand off.
  • That assistant already run on one real decision they walked in with.
  • A one-page plain-English read for Monday morning, plus a 60 second test for judging any new AI tool going forward.

3. The flow (order is set, minutes are approximate)

  1. The two questions every owner is asking. What is worth using right now, and how do you avoid getting burned trying. Plain English, before anyone touches a keyboard.
  2. The build. Everyone sets up their own AI assistant, live, with help in the room. A simple instructions file that teaches it their business (a CLAUDE.md file, which is less scary than it sounds). Then they connect it to email and the tools they already run on. Then the skills that do the daily work: scheduling, reminders, research.
  3. Run it on something real. Everyone points their new assistant at one real decision they brought in the door.
  4. The 60 second test. A simple yes or no they can run on any tool or pitch from now on.
  5. Monday morning. Everyone leaves with one page: what to use, what to skip, the next small step.

4. How I fill the room

  • A free video as the front door. I am putting out a free walkthrough of the real business I run on AI. People watch it free, and the ones who want the hands-on version buy the seat.
