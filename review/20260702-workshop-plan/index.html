3. The flow (90 minutes, order and blocks are set)

Why this shape and not another demo night: the most expensive mistake in any shop is not a badly written email. It is working on the wrong thing, or seeing only option A when B through Z exist. Anyone can teach "AI writes better emails." This session teaches judgment: how to decide the next move and set it up right. That is the part I am best at, and it is the part nobody else is teaching in this valley.

  1. Open (first 10 minutes). The two questions every owner is asking: what is worth using right now, and how do you avoid getting burned or locked in trying. Then a 90 second beat where AI knocks out a routine task, an email or a quote, start to finish. And the pivot: that is table stakes. The edge is deciding what to move on. That is tonight.
  2. Live proof (minutes 10 to 16). A quick tour of my real business running on my own system. About six minutes, not thirty. This is the part nobody can copy from a video.
  3. Build your Business Brain (16 to 30). Everyone writes a one page instructions file for their own business: what they sell, who they serve, how the money moves, what they will and will not do. So we are both exact about what gets built: the Business Brain is a CLAUDE.md file, an instructions file that tells the AI how to work for their business. In the room I explain it in plain language, no tech background needed. Paste based, free tier, no live email or account hookups, so nobody stalls on a password reset.
  4. Pick your decision (30 to 40). A scaffolded shortlist so everyone names one real call they are facing right now: make the hire, drop a service line, raise a price, take the contract.
  5. The main event: the Council (40 to 58). We feed the Business Brain and their decision to the AI and run a five angle pressure test. The centerpiece is option expansion: they walk in seeing option A, and the Council visibly opens it to B, C, D, on down the alphabet, then helps them set up the path they pick. One volunteer on screen so the whole room watches an option space blow open. That moment is the felt win of the night.
  6. Premortem, live (58 to 70). Same decision, one more pass: it is a year out and this failed. Why? The verdict shifts in front of them. Everyone writes their "it caught ___" line on a card. That card goes home in a pocket.
  7. Gap reveal and the 60 second test (70 to 80). I put my fully autonomous system next to their by-hand version: you did this by hand tonight, and mine runs my operation this way on its own. Then the test they keep: does this change a real call you make this week?
  8. Close (80 to 87). Your shop has thirty of these decisions. Free audit, phones out, a three minute booking window. Bring your Business Brain and your Council and premortem cards to the audit.
  9. Buffer (87 to 90). Room settles, and I capture emails from anyone who did not book.
