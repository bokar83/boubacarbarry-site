Boubacar Barry: An Independent Read

Third-party analysis from primary records only · 2026-09-04

TL;DR

A fifteen-year HR practitioner who left a Fortune 100 career, burned the fallback deliberately, and built himself a production AI system to compensate for a mind he says loses focus without one. The most reliable thing in his record is not ambition. It is that he corrects his own story downward more often than anyone else does.

  • What angers him is not error. It is amnesia. Across roughly a thousand recorded corrections, the frustration is almost never about a wrong answer. It is about being asked something he already answered, or handed back work he already delegated.
  • He treats unused authority as a breach. In his words: "I trust you but you are losing my trust by not using that trust." That single line explains most of his management style.
  • He does not kill things. 142 logged decisions, exactly one outright NO-GO. He parks with a return date instead, and polices anyone who proposes deleting his ideas.
  • Watch this: the founding document every session must read still says there is no plan-B job search, while the same repository carries an active job pipeline and a live offer comparison. Nobody has reconciled the two.

1. The record: who he actually is

Born 1977 in Conakry, Guinea, and born unable to walk — a fact he states in his own word, "crippled," and instructs everyone never to soften. Schooling alternated between the United States and Guinea, ending with university in the US. BA in Communications and an MBA in Organizational Behavior and Human Resources, both from Brigham Young University, with Hawes and Eccles scholar recognition. Four working languages, one of which he re-learned at eleven. Lives in Salt Lake City.

The career is HR, continuously, since 2010, in two phases. Consulting delivery first, at The RBL Group, Dave Ulrich's firm, 2010 to 2013. Then ten years inside GE, 2015 to 2025: a leadership programme at GE Healthcare across three sites, a globally-scoped HR business partner role for Power Services Engineering out of Atlanta, and four years in Dubai covering MENA for GE Vernova. That decade spans GE's separation into three public companies, which is an uncommon thing to have carried an HR seat through.

Since 2024 he has built and run a self-hosted multi-agent AI system on his own infrastructure: Ubuntu and Docker, an orchestration layer, model routing, PostgreSQL, integrations across an ordinary small-business software stack, and a five-voice adversarial council that pressure-tests decisions before he acts. Daily AI operator since November 2022. He is explicit in his own files that he is not a developer — he knows HTML, gets by in Java, and calls "programmer" or "engineer" an overclaim.

As of 2026 he is a full-time solo founder with no employer. His founding document states the terms without decoration: no fallback job, roughly eighteen months of runway, boats burned on purpose.

"I have lived beneath what I am capable of for too long. 10+ years at GE and RBL doing 'nice and okay' work superficially while knowing I was not living to my full potential."

That is the motive behind nearly everything else here. The career reads as competent and steadily upward. He does not read it that way.

2. Self-correction: the clearest signal in the file

An investigator hunting for inflation will not find much, because he got there first. The list of things he has forbidden people from saying about him is almost entirely a list of things that would have made him look bigger:

  • Never "ran HR and operations," never "executive." His words: "I was in HR. I helped run stuff in HR and I never actually ran operations specifically."
  • Never "fifteen years at a Fortune 100." Never "led a Kaizen" — approved phrasing is "was part of" or "helped lead."
  • Never "led the HR and org transformation": "I was an active participant and involved in it but did NOT lead it."
  • Never "One Engineering," because "I only worked on the services side of engineering. So reword it so that it states the truth."
  • Never inflate "co-led" to "led." Never claim a certification he does not hold. Never claim current clients he does not have.

Two items go past ordinary carefulness. First, his own resumes described a formal AI enablement programme across MENA through three consecutive versions, and he is the one who flagged it as false: "the AI enablement was for me personally... it was not something that was a process or program." People do not usually audit their own marketing documents in the direction of less.

Second, and sharper, on a programme that was genuinely his idea:

"I did not start it. I was a founding member of it but don't say that... Yes it did stem from me but I don't want to say that out loud and put that on resumes or put it out on my LinkedIn profile."

He also declines to write down a real credential he will say aloud: on interim people-leadership, "stepping in as PL is what I can tell people, i do not need to write it on the CV IMO."

This is calibration, not modesty

The same files show him pushing the other way when the other way is accurate. He objected to "14 countries" being used as his overall scope because it undersold him. He told his own system it had been underselling his prior work as proof: "I'm not starting all these things from scratch." He ordered agents to stop hiding how he worked — on site, walking plant floors, still keeping protective gear in his car — because readers were filling that gap with the wrong assumption and it cost him a real interview. He corrected the record when it wrongly said he had never managed people: "I don't want to make it feel like I've never done that."

Then, when agents over-applied that instruction and his summary started reading as a manufacturing specialist, he corrected the correction: "People see this and they think that I can only do factory and plants. That's actually the area I don't want to work in."

Three moves in three directions on the same axis inside five weeks. The through-line is not humility; it is that he treats his own positioning as a problem with a right answer, and is unusually willing to be the one who says the current answer is wrong. He names the underlying value himself: being a "standard bearer of reality and of truth," and his unmovable line is "never claim that I did something that I did not do."

He is not deferential about the present, though. On his standing to write about AI:

"No one knows anything about AI. Everyone is new to this and so I am just as qualified as other people to do this work and write it. I am actually more qualified than most people because I am in here hands-on."

Modest about the past, forward about the present. That pairing is rarer than either half.

3. How he decides

He keeps a formal decision ledger. At this reading it holds 142 entries, and the distribution is the finding:

VerdictCountWhat it means in his usage
GO56Committed, with a date attached
PENDING51Awaiting a verdict, visible, not lost
PARK34Deferred with a mandatory return date and trigger
NO-GO1Killed outright

One kill in 142 decisions. That is policy, not temperament. "All the ideas I have shared with you need to be in here and you need to give it a go/no-go and why on each one of them versus just stopping things." And after two agents in one day recommended archiving investments he had personally named: "Before you suggest to cancel things or to delete things or remove things, look at it and figure out why I said it and look at the logs."

He guards the obvious flank — an unbounded backlog — explicitly rather than ignoring it: "If there is no goal, if there is no outcome, if there is no specific date that things get done by, we do not do them." Capacity conflicts get sequenced, never culled, and he asks for pushback on the sequencing.

What he gates versus what he delegates

The governing split is his own, and it is cleaner than most delegation frameworks:

"Anything, and I mean anything, that goes out in my name, that's customer-facing... I need to see one way or the other. When it's about the system and the processes we use internally... those things I'm giving you more autonomy to proceed. Just make sure that you log and record what we're doing and why we're doing what we're doing so we can reverse if need be."

Outward-facing and irreversible stops at him. Internal and reversible does not, provided it is logged well enough to undo. He has also built an unusually hard prohibition on agents forging his own approval artefact — an accurate read of the failure mode, since a system that can approve itself has no gate at all.

He punishes one category error consistently: judging a thing by the wrong measure. An agent proposed archiving his Python study because it had produced no revenue. "I need to learn Python to be much more valuable as an AI expert and that is part of growth and development. It is the brain that I need to develop."

Risk tolerance

High, and stated rather than implied. He set a revenue target he himself assessed at 15 to 25 percent probability and wrote down why he chose it anyway: lowering the target lowers urgency, and urgency is the variable he believes was missing for twenty years. He wrote the re-anchor condition in advance and specified that the target may move but the reasoning behind it may not. He is also unimpressed by manufactured caution: "I think that you are making this into a bigger issue than it truly is."

4. How he communicates
  • Terse, and enforced. Every reply must open with a one-to-three-line summary, then short sections. No prose walls. He does not read markdown files — "I do NOT read .md files!!!!!" — so anything he must decide from becomes a web page.
  • Recommendation first, options behind it. A decision handed to him arrives as named options, the context needed to choose, a recommendation stated plainly as one of them, and the reason. A bare question is unfinished work.
  • Disagreement is a requirement. "I don't need a yes man, I don't need a sycophant... I need you to push back and make me stronger so I'm going to push back on you and make you stronger."
  • He celebrates loudly. "I like to celebrate things versus just being very coy about them... Might as well celebrate and be excited." He added the exclamation marks to a message an agent had written too flatly.
  • Clean is a failing grade. A post scoring 93 and 94 on his own quality gates was rejected: "Anybody could write that. It needs to show my unique fingerprints (sarcasme, wittiness, self deprecating, off the wall)." He also has no patience for charm as a substitute: "Don't try to be too cute. You state the facts."
  • He writes his own personal messages. "I'm going to start writing things on my own and having you review them and clean them up versus you writing things for me."
  • Small tell, large signal. He capitalizes and punctuates everything, including texts: "There are things I learned vis-a-vis grammar and language that I just will not break." He also corrected a name's spelling twice in one exchange and it is now spelled that way system-wide.

The content philosophy is the sharpest line in this section, and it is unusual for someone building a personal brand:

"'Hey don't look at me, look how amazing I am' — no. It's more of 'Let's talk about your journey and let me help you attain your full potential'... I'm trying to help them achieve their success, not me achieving my success."

5. What sets him off, and what he values

Roughly a thousand of his memory files are recorded corrections. A theme count across them:

ThemeDistinct files
Stop asking, just act73
Do not hand back work already delegated or resolved56
Check what already exists before asking or building49
Explicit repeat-correction language ("I've told you...")42
Explicit frustration or anger markers36

Those five are one root cause with five faces: the thing in front of him did not read what it already had. That is the single most consistent trigger in the corpus. It is worth being precise here, because it is the most useful thing to know about working with him: he is patient with wrong answers and intolerant of amnesia. The angriest entries on file are not about failure. They are about repetition.

"YOU ARE WRONG!!!!!! ... READ YOUR NOTES, FIGURE OUT HOW TO DO IT. I AM TELLING YOU THAT IT IS THROUGH THE VPS. THIS IS FRUSTRATING AND THAT IS WHY I AM YELLING AT YOU."

"I already told you that I put in hello@... It's been done for a long time! I want you to recognize that you're having trouble. You're not even reading your own logs from this current session and that's not good. It's not acceptable."

"That's on you and we wasted hours on something that was already built."

His own diagnosis of the pattern is better than most outside observers would manage: "You keep on guessing versus going in and looking at actual data and reading things... We are relying on memory and the memory is not good."

The line that explains his management style

The second cluster is about delegation, and it contains the single most revealing sentence in the record. Asked to run a command his agent could have run itself:

"I trust you but you are losing my trust by not using that trust... we lose trust also when we don't use the trust given to us."

Most people treat over-reach as the breach. He treats under-reach as one too, symmetrically. That single belief generates a long tail of consistent instructions: "You see that there's an error, you create it, and you fix it and repair it." "You didn't need me for any of those questions. Next time resolve them yourself and proceed." "I shall not have to say yes to these minor small things that we know have to get done. Just do it." "Come on, you don't even ask that question if you know that we're over the limit."

What he values

  • "I don't know" over a guess. Explicitly licensed in his rules and treated as superior to confident fabrication. An absence claim requires more proof than a presence claim; a success response is not proof anything was saved; name the source or write "unknown."
  • Autonomy earned through logged history. "That's why I wanted to track the history of all the decisions made so that you could learn from the decisions and I can start handing things off to you."
  • Non-obvious connections, which he names as his own edge and has instructed his system to weight higher when the link is real but nobody else would see it.
  • Conversations over machinery. "The reality is that I should be spending most of my day talking to people." He built the automation to buy that, not to avoid it.
  • An ethical floor priced in advance. A standing refusal on banned, illegal, adult, scam and multi-level verticals, "no matter revenue."

Smaller dislikes, all real and all repeatedly violated

No coffee, ever, in any copy. No blue links on dark backgrounds, which he called out for the fourth time as "horrible... I cannot read that." No em-dashes. No specific counts where a general word works, because numbers drift and contradict each other in public. No disclaimers about what a message is not, on the reasoning that naming the thing plants it. No AI slop, which he can identify by sight down to the level of tinted phrases mid-sentence: "It's the 'I use a' and 'I to create this' coloring. It screams."

6. How he treats people

This is the section where the profile gets more favourable, not less, and it is worth reading before drawing conclusions from the frustration above. He is demanding with machines and unusually protective of humans.

Relationship class gates what may ever be asked. He maintains an explicit taxonomy — mentor, accountability partner, peer, prior attendee, cold — in which some people may never be a prospect regardless of how good the pitch is. A mentor gets nothing commercial: "Luke does not need a message at all. Ignore it. It comes from me." A peer who is himself job-hunting is marked never-move, never-sold-to, and his meetings may not be scheduled over even when the calendar entry carries no protective tag — the absence of the tag is not permission.

He cuts anything that flatters or asks. During a night of reconnection notes to former colleagues, he removed his own history from the drafts on the grounds that you do not recite shared experience to the people who lived it, cut an ask entirely because bundling one into a catch-up turns the catch-up into a wrapper, and deleted the line "you are in rooms I'm not" as the sentence carrying the neediness. His stated fear:

"I just don't want them to see this note and think, 'Oh my gosh, he ghosted and disappeared for so long and now he's coming back and is asking for something from me.'"

And, after a dozen rounds: "It should not be this complicated. It's just a re-connection."

He apologises in one clause and moves on. On re-approaching a lapsed contact: "Just say I went quiet due to some personal reasons that are improving now and I am back stateside. Don't dig into it. Don't dwell on it but just at least acknowledge it and we move on." Self-flagellation is banned; so is any phrasing that invites the reader to ask what happened.

He closes his own misses rather than relitigating them. On a question from a senior figure he had left unanswered for months, which kept resurfacing as a task:

"I already messed that up and no need to bring it back up... All it does is put salt in the wounds and make me look worse than I already am."

The system he built still records that miss internally so the pattern stays learnable, and shows him only a count. He also refused to let it auto-close by age, on the correct reasoning that overdue and past-being-useful are different facts and a clock cannot tell them apart. That is a healthier relationship with personal failure than most operators manage.

He corrects labels applied to people, not just actions. Three respected former colleagues had been filed under "banned" alongside genuinely excluded contacts. The operational effect was already correct; he objected to the word anyway, because it disrespected people he admires and could have steered him away from people he wants to talk to.

The recurring register is lighter than his business writing. His own rewrite of a dense progress note to his accountability partner ends: "Take care outside in this heat. Stay hydrated."

7. Values and blind spots, read off the system's design

He has built roughly two thousand memory files. Reading what he built guardrails against is the closest available thing to reading what has hurt him.

The heaviest investments are in never losing anything (archive rather than delete, as a standing rule; "saved" means persisted and proven by reading the record back), never being impersonated (agents are mechanically barred from writing the artefact that constitutes his approval), and never being misrepresented. His response to a failure is never a louder rule — after a rule that already existed was violated, the governing note reads: "A louder rule alone would have been a third copy of one that had already failed." He builds enforcement, not exhortation. He also keeps an append-only corrections log where the wrong entries stay, superseded rather than deleted, and he is willing to turn that scepticism on the log itself: "A corrections log whose own entries need correcting at this rate is not a safety net, it is another surface that rots."

The tension the data actually shows

He asks for autonomy and issues a very high volume of corrections. Both are true, and the volume is measurable:

The corpus grew roughly ninefold. The correction rate did not fall. On 2026-09-04 a session broke three of his rules immediately after he restated them in person, and the resulting record concludes that the failure is not that the rules were unknown but that they were not obeyed at the moment of action.

What he did next is the part worth noting. He did not write more rules. Within the same twenty-four hours he moved the other way and handed his agents more authority, arguing that the ledger already proves he says yes every time. Read alongside "we lose trust also when we don't use the trust given to us," this is not naivety. It is a consistent position: he would rather absorb the cost of a delegated mistake than the cost of a machine that keeps asking. Whether that pays is genuinely undecided by the evidence, and he placed the bet with his eyes open.

Three blind spots the evidence supports

One. The memory system has outgrown its own loader. The always-load index is capped and visibly truncating; a large number of rules he marked as always-load do not fit and reach agents through a secondary mechanism, with more mirrored by hand into a file guaranteed to load. Each patch is individually reasonable. Together they are a system whose answer to too many rules has been more delivery paths rather than fewer rules. The underlying call is flagged as pending his decision, and it has stayed pending.

Two. The governing document is the one document nobody audits. The anchor doc every session is required to read states there is no plan-B job search. The same repository, in the same weeks, carries an active job pipeline, stated relocation and compensation parameters, and a live comparison between two potential employers. Those are probably compatible in his head — funding a venture with a salary is a normal decision — but the reconciliation is written down nowhere, and the document's own review lineage shows no completed pass since it was locked in May against a schedule that began on 2026-06-01. The doc that governs every session is running unreviewed on a premise the rest of the repository has moved past.

Three. Two of his own rules pull against each other and neither has yielded. He is the last reviewer of everything, by explicit standing rule. He also wants his calendar filled with human conversations, and rejects any attempt to shield it: "I'm taking care of the things that only I can do... You're not trying to protect my calendar." Both are defensible. Together they mean the more successfully his machine generates output and meetings, the more of both queue behind the one component that cannot be parallelised. The autonomy push is best read as him attacking exactly this, from the output side only. The review-of-last-resort side has not moved.

One absence, stated rather than padded: there is very little evidence here of him being wrong about a person and having to revise. There is abundant evidence of him revising facts, positioning, priorities and his own claims. Self-criticism of any kind is also thin relative to the volume of corrections he issues — when it appears it is usually either a follow-up failure he owns ("that's my fault for not following up on it") or, more often, cutting down a claim about himself that someone else inflated. Whether his read on people is equally self-correcting cannot be determined from this material.

8. If you were hiring, investing in, or competing against him

Hiring. Hire him. The asset is rare and hard to fake: a senior HR practitioner who has personally built and operated production automation rather than commissioned it, carried an HR seat through a Fortune 100 splitting into three companies, and worked across nearly every continent in four languages. Run the reference call on scope, not on integrity — integrity is already answered by a documented pattern of shrinking his own claims when nobody was making him. What you should actually verify is whether he wants the job, because the record shows a man who has publicly decided that a decade of competent corporate work was beneath him. He will not stay for a title. He will stay for a mandate.

Investing. The person is more investable than the current plan. His own probability estimate on the headline target is 15 to 25 percent and he wrote that number himself, which tells you he is honest and also tells you the plan is a stretch. The model leans on adders he has not built, and one kill in 142 decisions means the portfolio is very wide for a solo operator; he guards ideas from deletion considerably more fiercely than he guards his calendar from them. What would move my assessment is not a better deck but evidence of a single motion reaching real recurring revenue while the rest stay parked. He would likely say the same, and he wrote the re-anchor trigger for exactly that scenario in advance.

Competing. Do not compete on building speed, on cross-domain pattern-matching, or on credibility with buyers who did not grow up with this technology. Those are real edges and he knows it. Compete on focus. His declared weakness, the one he has spent a year engineering around, is attention spread across too many surfaces, and his own rules concede that a scattered system costs him something a rival does not have to pay. Someone who picks one of his six or seven motions and does only that will get there first. That window is closing, though, because closing it is where most of his engineering effort currently goes.

The one-line read. Low risk of being lied to, real risk of being spread thin, and a rate of self-correction fast enough that most of what is on this page he would have found himself within a month.

9. Method, and what this analysis could not see

Everything above is drawn from primary records in his own working repository: the canonical professional profile and its correction history, the founding anchor document he authored, the full decision ledger with its stated reasons, the governance and incident logs, and a wide sample of roughly two thousand memory files, counted by month and by type. Quotations are his own words as recorded at the time, not reconstructions. Counts were taken directly, not estimated.

Deliberately excluded. Family details, faith and political affiliation beyond noting that he keeps them out of public work, and every category his own privacy rules place off limits. Those rules were treated as binding on this analysis rather than as obstacles to route around. The one health-related fact included is one he states publicly himself, in his own chosen word, and instructs others never to soften.

Known limits. This is a read of a man through the corrections he issues to machines. That surface over-represents friction, because a rule only gets written when something goes wrong, and it under-represents everything that goes right silently. There are no third-party references here, no former colleagues interviewed, and no client outcomes independently verified. Financial and revenue figures are his own projections, treated as intentions rather than results. Where the evidence was thin, this page says so instead of filling the gap.
