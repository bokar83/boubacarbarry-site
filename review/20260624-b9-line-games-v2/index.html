Line Games

The only multi-game kit designed for the line itself. One phone, ten seconds, no signal. Rebuilt to a premium quality floor: original mechanics, a real retention and virality engine, and a feel worth paying for.

Authored 2026-06-24 · Grounded in the viability findings · Builds on v1 · Pressure-tested (5-angle Council + 4-filter simplicity)

"One phone, ten seconds, offline — for the whole group."

Heads Up is one party trick everyone already owns. This is the kit you grab before every trip, matched to your group's ages, that turns the wait into the part people film.

0What "$100-app quality" means here

Not a price tag — a quality floor. Every decision answers one question: would a group who has already played Heads Up a dozen times choose this, then tell their friends?

  • Original mechanics, not a reskinned charades clone. A kit of distinct verbs — guess, bluff, vote, draw, react, move, narrate — each tuned for a phone passed around a standing group.
  • Feels designed, not assembled. Motion, sound, and haptics that make a correct answer feel good in the hand. Onboarding under 10 seconds. Offline that never fails.
  • A reason to return AND a reason to share. Streaks, group identity, and a result card born to be filmed.
  • A reason to buy. Themed packs you collect before every trip, priced like a snack, bundled like a season pass.

1Positioning — Own "The Wait"

Heads Up is one mechanic that third parties happen to recommend for lines. It was never built for the line. That gap is the slot. We position at the moment of trip planning ("what do we do in the lines?"), not at the App Store search bar.

Offline-firstNo cell service in a packed park is a real, named friction. "No WiFi required" is customer language.
Ten-second startNo account, no setup, no tutorial wall. A group still shuffling forward is already playing.
Multi-game + multi-themeAge-matched packs serve the whole mixed-age group across every line, not just the first.
Built for one passed phoneTurn handoff, glance-able state, big thumb targets. Everyone else designs for a table.

2The Games

Playable on one phone passed around a standing group of 2-4+, no setup, fully offline, glance-readable, fun in a 2-minute burst or a 40-minute line. Each game is a distinct verb so the kit never feels like the same game in new paint.

Freemium (free tier, ad-supported) — the front door

GuessF1Forehead
The guess-on-your-forehead game everyone knows — one tap to start, never needs signal.
  • Phone to forehead, group shouts clues, tilt down = correct, up = pass. Pass-the-phone.
  • Replay/share: rotating decks; Result Card freezes the funniest miss.
  • The on-ramp that converts a "we already have Heads Up" group, then the rest of the kit surprises them. 60-90s.
VoteF2Bluff or Truth
One player reads a wild statement; everyone secretly votes real or fake before the reveal.
  • Zero physical motion — works in a tight, packed queue where you can't raise a phone to your forehead.
  • Replay/share: genuinely surprising statements; Result Card ranks "most gullible" vs "human lie detector."
  • Killer for trivia-heavy venue packs. 3-4 min, micro-burst friendly.
PerformF3Last One Laughing
The phone dares you to keep a straight face; first to crack loses.
  • Front camera optional but never required — group can judge if camera is off (offline + privacy safe).
  • Replay/share: the freeze-frame of the exact moment someone cracks is the most natively viral asset in the kit.
  • 30-45s per dare. Pure micro-burst.
Co-op recallF4Chain Gang
The whole group versus the app — build the longest chain before the timer beats you.
  • App names a category; each person adds an item out loud before a shrinking timer buzzes.
  • Cooperative — bonds a mixed-age group instead of pitting kids against adults. Nothing in the competitor set does co-op.
  • Replay/share: "your group's best streak vs the app" — beat-your-own-record is the retention loop. 45-90s.
AudioF5Hot Mic
Hum it, beatbox it, make the sound — the group guesses, no words allowed.
  • Audio-OUT only, no mic permission (the phone never listens) — stays offline and permission-light.
  • Replay/share: a grown man humming a ride jingle is a Result Card waiting to happen.
  • Music/concert pack is the obvious upsell hook. 60s rounds.
Social bluffF6Two Truths, One Line
Turns strangers and in-laws in the same line into a game — spot the lie about the phone-holder.
  • The content is the people themselves, so it never runs out. Works for groups who don't know each other well.
  • Replay/share: "the group fell for [name]'s lie 4 times." Hands-free friendly. 2-3 min.

Premium (inside paid theme packs) — the depth that out-classes a one-mechanic app

DrawP1Finger Sketch Relay
Draw it together one stroke at a time and guess what your group accidentally made.
  • Phone secretly shows one player a word; each adds ONE stroke without knowing it; then everyone guesses.
  • The finished disaster drawing IS the Result Card — instantly shareable, never the same twice. No competitor has drawing in the line context. 2-3 min.
Motion arcadeP2Tilt Quest
Steer with the phone, not your thumbs — tilt to dodge, catch, and race while you wait.
  • Accelerometer only, offline. High score passes the phone to the next challenger; group cheers.
  • Replay/share: in-session leaderboard; Result Card shows the run as a looping clip. 45-60s.
DeductionP3Spy in Line
Everyone gets the secret word except one spy — find the faker before they figure out the word.
  • Pure pass-the-phone social deduction, offline. The longest-form game — perfect for a 40-min line, scales down.
  • The depth game that makes the kit feel premium. Heads Up structurally cannot add this. 4-6 min.
UGC + voteP4Caption Battle
Snap the line around you, then everyone fights to write the funniest caption.
  • Camera optional (built-in images if off); photo stays on device, offline.
  • The winning captioned photo is the Result Card — already a perfect post. 2-3 min.
Location triviaP5Where Are We?
The game knows roughly where you are and asks questions that fit your trip — without ever needing signal.
  • Pack ships content bucketed by venue/region; a one-time coarse location check (or manual picker) pre-selects the bucket. All on-device — no live lookups.
  • Answers the "no WiFi" constraint while still feeling smart about where you are. The licensing showpiece. 3-5 min.
Speed vs appP6Beat the Buzzer
Fire back answers faster than the buzzer — the app is the opponent that never gets tired.
  • Has a full hands-free / On the Move variant: prompts read aloud, answered aloud, phone in pocket — the ride-to-ride walking game.
  • Replay/share: "our group hit a 22 streak"; the app's escalating taunts are quotable. 60-90s.

3Freemium + Ad Model

The free tier is a complete, genuinely fun product — never a crippled demo. A group can play an entire 40-minute line and never hit a wall.

Ad placement that doesn't wreck the line

  • Never mid-round. Never on a passed-phone handoff. An ad mid-air between two people kills it.
  • One rewarded interstitial at the natural "Play again?" beat, capped ~1 per 3 rounds.
  • Rewarded, not forced: "watch 15s to unlock a bonus deck this session" — the player chooses and gets value.
  • Offline-safe: ads pre-cache when there's signal; offline (the common case) the app skips and plays on. The game never blocks on a network call.
Honest read: offline play means low ad impressions by design. Ads are a floor, not the engine. The real money is packs + the trip-contextual buy loop + the season pass. eCPM (rewarded ~$8-15, banner ~$0.5-2) is a planning assumption to validate week 1 — not a research figure.

Free → paid path

Great line moment → film a Result Card → end-of-session card surfaces a trip-matched pack → rewarded-unlock gives a taste of a premium game → the real conversion is the pre-trip buy the night before.

5Retention + Virality Engine

Retention

  • Trip Streaks (not daily streaks — wrong for an episodic product): "3 trips in a row you made the wait fun."
  • Crews: a group names itself ("The Back-of-the-Line Bandits"); the name rides every Result Card + a persistent local crew record. Same crew next trip = a reason to reopen. Offline, no accounts.
  • Beat-your-own-record loops on Chain Gang, Tilt Quest, Beat the Buzzer.
  • One opt-in pre-trip reminder ("park day tomorrow — grab the coaster pack?"). One, not a stream.

Virality — the Result Card is the growth org

Every game ends in a 9:16 Result Card auto-generated from the round — the funniest miss, the disaster drawing, the cracked-up face, the crew's streak. One-tap share with crew name + subtle watermark. The concert-line mechanic: Concert Queue cards tag to the show ("Beat the Buzzer streak in line for [show]") — exactly what a 16-30 crowd posts unprompted. No login to share.

The flywheel: free games → great line moment → Result Card filmed and posted → new group downloads free → has their own moment → buys a pack before their next trip → posts again. Packs fund the polish; polish drives the share; the share drives installs.

6Distribution

1 · Concert-queue TikTok (near-zero CAC)

The 16-30 concert crowd is native to short-form video and posts Result Cards organically. Film 3-5 clips of real groups in actual show/festival lines; hook in the first 2 seconds. One clip at 50K views = validation; 500K+ = launch accelerant.

2 · Pre-trip SEO + content (compounding)

Trip-planning families search "games to play in Disney lines," "apps that work without WiFi at theme parks" weeks ahead, in a high-spend mindset. 3-5 articles on those queries; guest-post outreach to happymomhacks.com, parkmagic.com, touringplans.com, getawaytoday.com (they already rank for these and recommend Heads Up — pitch a better multi-game alternative). Near-zero cost, compounds.

3 · Venue licensing (moat — traction-gated)

Six Flags / Cedar Fair (merged — Cedar Point, Knott's, Kings Island, Carowinds) and Universal (Orlando, Hollywood, Epic Universe) have no official line-game app; Disney's is locked to its own IP. Wait time is a top guest complaint. Pitch: "an app guests already use + a co-branded pack that makes the wait a guest-sat win for you." Ask ladder: a social/newsletter mention for a free co-branded pack → revenue-share licensing. Don't approach before ~1,000 installs + a session signal. Also: Live Nation / AEG for festival queues.

4 · Pre-trip retail adjacency (opportunistic)

A QR on family-travel packing lists and newsletters — "add this to your park bag" alongside sunscreen and ponchos. Compounds the SEO play.

7Premium Quality Bar

What makes it feel $100-caliber, not a bargain clone.

  • Onboarding under 10s — open → three big game buttons → pick a pack → Start. No account, name, permission wall, or tutorial screen. Measured: time-to-first-round under 10s on a mid-range phone.
  • Motion design — weighted card flips, a timer that pulses as it shrinks, a spring-pop on correct, a Result Card that assembles like it was produced. 60fps. The single biggest visible gap vs utilitarian competitors.
  • Sound — a tight original kit (satisfying "correct," comedic "miss," escalating buzzer), distinct per game. Output-only, never needs a mic permission.
  • Haptics — crisp tap on correct, buzz on time-out, double-tap on a streak milestone. A premium tell most party apps ignore.
  • Offline reliability — the entire core runs in airplane mode, every time. No network call in any play path; ads/shares degrade to "skip."
  • Accessibility — large-type, high-contrast, colorblind-safe state colors (never color-only), VoiceOver/TalkBack labels, generous thumb targets. Also a market expander.
  • Sunlight-readable — big glance-able state legible at arm's length outdoors. The line is outside; design for it.

8Lean MVP + Cut List

The premium bar is the destination. v1 ships a focused, polished slice (still single-HTML-friendly for the smoke build), then earns the rest.

MVP must-build (v1)

  • Three free games, three distinct verbs: F1 Forehead (guess), F2 Bluff or Truth (vote), F4 Chain Gang (co-op) — proves "kit, not clone" with the least build.
  • Two free starter packs (~50 cards/game each).
  • Ten-second start, no account.
  • On-screen timer + turn indicator, glance-able, sunlight-readable.
  • One Result Card (F4's "best streak") with one-tap share — the virality seed must be in v1.
  • One paid IAP pack: Rollercoaster Chaos $1.99, unlocking P5 Where Are We? — so v1 proves a premium game sells, not just a content deck.
  • Offline-first end to end.

Cut list (not in v1)

  • Online multiplayer / sync · accounts / login / cloud leaderboards (crews are local-only).
  • Remaining premium games (P1-P4, P6) — ship in packs post-validation.
  • Camera games (F3, P4) — adds a permission step + QA surface; add once core is proven.
  • Subscription billing — IAP singles first; add the $24.99 pass once 3+ packs exist to bundle.
  • Full original-sound + haptics polish — v1 ships a basic kit; the full premium pass is the fast-follow funded by early validation.
Smoke-test-first gate: run the smoke test (paper/mockup prototype) BEFORE writing the real app. Build only after it passes.

9Risks (Council) + how the plan answers them

1 · "Still just Heads Up with extra steps."Seven distinct verbs. The MVP itself ships three different verbs on day one. Spy in Line and Finger Sketch are mechanics Heads Up structurally cannot add.
2 · The whole thing rests on people installing a NEW app vs opening the Heads Up they own.This is the explicit kill-risk (§11). Tested in the smoke phase BEFORE build via direct "would you switch?" questions, not assumed away. Decision rule pre-committed.
3 · Offline kills the ad model — where's the money?The plan does not pretend ads carry it. Ads are an explicit floor; packs + the trip-buy loop + the season pass are the engine, matching the research.
4 · Premium polish + 12 games is a lot of build (scope risk).The cut list is aggressive on purpose. v1 = 3 free games + 1 premium game + 1 Result Card, single-HTML-friendly. The 12-game kit is a roadmap, not a v1 commitment.
5 · A viral TikTok and a venue deal are "hope," not a plan.SEO (priority 2) is the compounding, controllable channel that doesn't depend on virality. Venue licensing is traction-gated so it never becomes a survival dependency.
Karpathy (simplicity / anti-bloat): v1 is deliberately small — no accounts, no backend, no network in play; offline-first deletes the entire backend; the cut list removes accounts, sync, subscription, camera, and most premium games from v1. The one over-build risk (location) is held to a one-time coarse check + manual picker. Council ↔ Karpathy converged: premium is the quality floor for each shipped thing; the cut list keeps v1 small. No padding survived.
