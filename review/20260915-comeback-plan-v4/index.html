TL;DR - what V4.0 changes

The training plan does not change. Not one session, not one rule, not one date. V3.1 works and the final rule of V3.1 still stands: do the plan, do not keep redesigning the plan.

V4.0 adds one thing: how you eat. A bowl method, borrowed from the Filling Diet, with one structural modification so it fuels a running block instead of fighting it.

  • Every meal is a bowl with three fixed parts: unlimited protein, unlimited volume food, exactly one measured fat.
  • Training days add a fourth part: the fuel scoop, one measured starchy carb serving. The standard Filling Diet leaves this out on purpose because it is built for fat loss. You are training to run, so it goes back in, measured rather than unlimited.
  • Rest day (Monday) is the standard three-part bowl. No fuel scoop.
  • No calorie counting. The volume does the work.
  • The scorecard does not change. Completion stays at 10 points. One line is added to the Monday review, and it is not worth points.
The honest caveat, up front

The bowl method controls calories through fullness rather than arithmetic. That is its whole advantage and also its whole limit. It will keep you fed and stop the drift, but it will not tell you your numbers. If at Week 13 you want to actually optimize, you track protein and calories for two weeks and find out. The bowl is a practical simplification, not a substitute for knowing.

Change one thing at a time

You are picking this up mid-block. Add the food. Do not also change the training in the same week. Standing rule 7 still governs calisthenics progression, and it still only opens at the Week 5 and Week 9 reviews. Nutrition is not a training variable, so adding it now breaks nothing, but stacking two changes in one week would make it impossible to read what did what.

2. The bowl - three fixed parts

Take a large bowl. Fill it in this order. No weighing, no app, no counting.

Part 1. Protein, unlimited

Lean and high volume. Eat until satisfied, there is no cap.

"Unlimited" means no portion rule, not no limit. Appetite is the limit. In practice you will stop well before it becomes a problem, because these foods are hard to overeat.

Why this works without counting

Protein and fibrous volume are the two most filling things you can put in a bowl, and both are expensive to overeat. The fat is capped because it is the only cheap-to-overeat part. That structure produces a sane calorie intake on its own, which is why the method survives a busy week when a tracking app does not.

3. The fuel scoop - run days vs rest day
The modification, stated plainly

The Filling Diet as published is a fat-loss protocol. Protein plus vegetables plus one fat is, by construction, a very low carbohydrate day. That is fine if the goal is only to lose weight. It is the wrong fuel for a block whose entire purpose is to get you running again and keep you there through November 9. Pasted in unmodified it would quietly raise your perceived effort, flatten the last runs of the week, and push you toward the RPE ceiling for a reason that has nothing to do with fitness. So carbohydrate goes back in, on training days, in a measured amount.

One fuel scoop equals one of these

OptionServing
Rice1 cup cooked
Potato or sweet potato1 medium
Pasta1 cup cooked
Oats1/2 cup dry, cooked
Bread or tortilla2 slices, or 1 large tortilla
Beans or lentils1 cup cooked (also counts toward protein)
Banana plus berries1 large banana

How many, and when

  • Run day (Tue, Fri, Sun): 2 scoops. One in the meal before the run, one in the meal after.
  • Calisthenics day (Wed, Sat): 2 scoops. Same split. Strength work runs on the same fuel, and these sessions sit between run days, so under-fueling here shows up in the next run rather than in the session itself.
  • Bike day (Thu): 1 scoop, in the meal after. Thirty-five minutes at RPE 2 to 4 is genuinely easy and does not need a pre-load.
  • Rest day (Mon): 0 scoops. The standard three-part bowl. This is the one day the diet runs in its original fat-loss form, and one day a week of that is enough to keep the whole block moving in the right direction without ever compromising a session.

Measured, not unlimited. The scoop is portioned exactly like the fat serving is, and for the same reason. Unlimited carbohydrate would undo the mechanism that makes the bowl work in the first place.

Do not over-correct

N2R runs in this block are short run/walk intervals at RPE 3 to 4. This is not marathon fueling and you do not need gels, drinks, or a carb-loading day. Two scoops on a training day is the whole adjustment. If you find yourself building a nutrition program on top of a training program, you have started redesigning the plan again.

4. Timing, hydration, and the morning-run rule
Safety, not preference

Do not run the early session completely fasted and under-hydrated. During Weeks 1 to 3 the outdoor window is before 9:00 a.m. or after 7:00 p.m., so the morning run lands on an empty overnight stomach by default. Given the historical near-fainting pattern, this matters more for you than it would for most people: post-exertional orthostatic events are associated with low fluid volume and abrupt stopping, and arriving at a run under-fueled and under-fluid stacks a second risk on top of the one the plan already has a protocol for. Before an early run, have something small and easy, half a banana, a slice of toast, a few spoonfuls of oats, plus the fluid the plan already calls for. That is the rule. A full meal is not required and is usually a bad idea.

Hydration, unchanged from V3.1

  • Start reasonably hydrated. If you have not had much fluid, roughly 12 to 16 oz during the hour before is a practical default.
  • Do not force fluids beyond thirst and normal needs.

Around the session

  • Before: the pre-run meal carries one fuel scoop. Keep it modest and give it a couple of hours if you can. If the run is early, the small carb above is the version that fits.
  • After: a full bowl, protein first, plus the second fuel scoop. This is the meal that actually matters for recovering the next session.
  • The rest of the day: bowls as normal, one fat each.

5. Add-on techniques (and where not to use them)

Three small levers. Each one is real, and each one has a day it does not belong on.

TechniqueWhat it doesWhere to use it
Carbonated drink with the meal Increases the sensation of fullness during the meal Any day. Harmless and it works.
Bone broth or soup pre-load Cuts total intake at the following meal by roughly 20% Monday only. This is a fat-loss tool. Used before a pre-run or post-run meal it removes exactly the fuel you added the scoop to supply.
Smaller utensils Slows eating, so satiety signals land before the bowl is gone Any day. Costs nothing.
The pattern to notice

Every one of these makes you eat less. On a rest day that is the point. On a training day it works against the session you are about to do. Same tool, opposite effect, depending on the day. That is the single idea to carry out of this section.

6. Honest limits of this method
  • It is not tracking. The bowl controls intake through fullness. It genuinely works, and it will not tell you your protein or your calories. If you want precision you have to measure, at least for a couple of weeks, at least once.
  • "Unlimited" is satiety-limited, not literally unlimited. The word is a simplification that stops you portioning chicken. It is not permission to eat past full.
  • It does not have a protein floor built in. The method caps what is easy to overeat and never states a minimum. If the calisthenics adaptation stalls through Weeks 5 and 9 while everything else looks fine, under-eating protein is the first thing to check, and checking it means measuring for a week.
  • The fuel scoop is an estimate, not a calculation. Two scoops on a training day is a reasonable starting point for run/walk intervals at RPE 3 to 4. If late-week runs feel progressively heavier while sleep and stress are steady, add a third scoop on run days before you touch anything in the training plan.
  • Weight is not the scoreboard in this block. V3.1 already bans manual transcription of weight, and V4.0 does not reopen it. The outcome on November 9 is healthy, still running, ready for Block 2.

Standing rule 9 (new)

Never use food to compensate for a training week. A missed session is not a reason to cut the fuel scoop, skip a bowl, or add the broth pre-load to a training day. Missed work is not debt, and that rule now covers the plate as well as the calendar.

8. Training plan, unchanged from V3.1

Everything below is V3.1 as sent on August 17, reproduced so this page is the one place you need. Nothing here has been edited.

Every run and cardio session

  • 10-minute progressive walking warm-up.
  • Target RPE 3 to 4. Hard ceiling RPE 5.
  • If N2R and the ceiling conflict, the ceiling wins. Insert walk breaks and repeat that N2R week.
  • Use the talk test. A full sentence should usually be possible.
  • Ignore pace. Do not turn watch data into a target.
  • Finish with 5 minutes of easy walking. Never stop abruptly and stand still.
  • Weeks 1 to 3: outdoor cardio before 9:00 a.m. or after 7:00 p.m.
  • Routes: one main flat or gently rolling route, one backup. Stop route-shopping.

The eight standing rules

  1. Calistree A/B replace N2R strength sessions. Never do both strength systems.
  2. No bonus running, extra cycling, circuits, HIIT, sprinting, or max tests.
  3. Extra easy walking is allowed, but do not turn it into a power-walk, ruck, long hike, or hidden training session.
  4. Never catch up. Missed work is not debt.
  5. Repeating an N2R week is correct when RPE, breathing, or tissue tolerance says so.
  6. Nov 9 closes Block 1 regardless of which N2R week you reach. Do not compress, double up, skip ahead, or create a catch-up week. Block 2 resumes from the next incomplete N2R week.
  7. Progress calisthenics only at the Week 5 and Week 9 reviews. Zero changes is valid.
  8. No Strava until Week 13.

9. Safety, breathing, and the stop signal
The stop signal

Wooziness or tunnel vision means the session is over. Get safe. If possible, lie flat and elevate your legs. Sitting is the fallback if lying down is not safe or practical. Do not try to finish the workout. A new fainting or near-fainting episode reopens the medical gate and pauses progression.

Zero-day safety override

Minimum viable work is for life friction. It does not override illness, injury, or warning symptoms. When exercise is inappropriate, recovery is the task and still counts as adherence.

  • Fever, systemic illness, or meaningful chest or lower-respiratory symptoms.
  • Focal bone, tendon, or joint pain that makes the activity unsafe.
  • Tunnel vision, near-fainting, fainting, chest pain or pressure, or palpitations with dizziness.
  • Severe or unusual breathlessness that does not settle appropriately with slowing or stopping.

Emerging lower-leg injury

Focal worsening pain, point tenderness on bone, or pain that changes your gait is not a push-through signal. Stop running, keep the time slot with easy non-impact activity only if comfortable, and get evaluated if symptoms persist. Return conservatively, usually one N2R week back rather than jumping forward.

Breathing and asthma

  • Warm up gradually before every run and bike session.
  • Relax jaw and shoulders. Keep breathing continuous.
  • Exhale on the effort during calisthenics. Do not hold your breath.
  • Do not force nasal-only breathing.
  • If breathing becomes disorganized, slow to a walk and let it settle.
  • Repeated cough, wheeze, or chest tightness should move the asthma review forward.
  • Book the non-urgent asthma review if it is still open. Do not restart an old inhaler prescription on your own.

Gate reopens immediately if

  • Any new fainting or near-fainting episode.
  • Loss of consciousness with little or no warning, or while still actively moving.
  • Chest pain or pressure with exertion.
  • Palpitations with dizziness.
  • Breathlessness grossly disproportionate to effort, or not settling after stopping.
Medical note

This is a training and nutrition framework, not a diagnosis or a substitute for individualized medical care. New or recurrent fainting or near-fainting, chest pain, concerning palpitations, or unusual severe breathlessness override the schedule and the plate both. If you have a condition that makes an unlimited-protein or low-carbohydrate pattern inappropriate, that overrides this page.

Final rule, carried forward from V3.1

Do the plan. Do not keep redesigning the plan. That now includes the food.
