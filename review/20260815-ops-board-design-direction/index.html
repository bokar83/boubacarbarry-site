What I think the ops board should become, and why

You asked for a rendering, not another summary. Everything below is drawn here in the page, in the board's own colours and type, so you can look at it and say yes, no, or not like that. Nothing here has been built yet unless it is explicitly marked as already live.

2026-08-15 · every mockup names the evidence behind it · palette stays dark, per your instruction

TL;DR — the whole direction in six lines
  • The board stops being a wall of tiles and becomes a ranked list of what needs you, with everything else demoted. That is the single biggest change, and it is the one I would spend the weekend on.
  • Every number carries its own seven-day shape. A count alone tells you nothing. A count with a sparkline and a signed delta answers your second question without you asking it.
  • Every count becomes clickable and opens the rows behind it. No number on the board is a dead end.
  • The noise goes in a drawer. Heartbeats, cron, errors are top-level cards today. They should be one collapsed line that turns amber only when it matters.
  • Three new things worth building: a replay scrubber for the day, a one-tap daily digest you can copy, and a chat pane with the four questions you actually ask pre-loaded as buttons.
  • Palette unchanged. Dark navy, serif headings, terracotta accent, exactly the tokens already in the stylesheet. Fixing what is in there and how it is presented comes first, per your own priority order.

What changes versus today

The strip already exists and already has sparklines. Three specific upgrades:

  • The sparkline moves inside the tile, under the number, edge to edge. Today it is an overlay fetched separately. Inside the tile it reads as one object: the number is the now, the line is the week, the delta is the verdict. Your eye takes all three in one movement.
  • The fourth tile is drawn dashed and grey on purpose. Plan usage comes from a file written on this laptop, not from the database. A dashed line and a muted colour say “estimate” visually before you read the label. Solid line equals live query, dashed equals estimate, and that rule holds everywhere on the board.
  • The number is a click target. Tapping 14 opens the fourteen tasks. That rule is section 04's, and it starts here.
Evidence The sparkline-inside-the-tile move is straight from observability reference A, which puts the trend inside the KPI card rather than in a separate chart. The three-question framing and the median-not-mean choice come from the spec's own section 04, which is itself built on your words: “how much work is being done, how effective we are that day compared to the previous days, how you are doing on usage.”

Why this and not a grid of counters

  • It is written in sentences, not labels. “Gate: 3 held” makes you do the translation. “A branch that touches the system's own source has been waiting on your tap for 2 days” does it for you. That is the whole point of the pattern.
  • It is ranked, and the ranking rule is printed at the bottom. One ordered list, not four competing badges. The rule being visible is what makes the order trustworthy instead of arbitrary.
  • Rank 1 is defined as the thing only you can do. A branch held for your approval outranks a lead reply because an agent can draft the reply and no agent may ever tap that approval. Everything an agent could have done should not be on this list at all.
  • Every row ends in a button, and the buttons are coloured. Approve-shaped actions are green, the accent is used for the primary move, and nothing here is red. Red stays reserved for a state, never for something you are supposed to press.
  • Rank 4 shows a hand-entered item with no automated source, and says so. Better an honest manual row than a blind spot.
Evidence The plain-English ranked priority panel is the strongest single idea from observability reference A, where an assistant panel turns raw signals into a short ordered list of what to fix. The coloured, prominent action buttons are reference B. The tap-to-act-on-the-item-itself rule and the green-approve / neutral-revert / never-red colour rule are yours, from the 2026-05-18 review-dashboard instruction. The “only you can do it” ranking rule is your standing use-him-only-for-what-only-he-can-do principle applied to a sort order.

Why

  • The toggle exists because the board currently shows one blended list. Four states with counts, one selected, is how you actually think about it: what needs me, what is teed up, what went out, what I killed.
  • “Needs approval” is the default tab, always. The board opens on the only tab that can require something from you.
  • The score sits on the row. You should not have to open a post to find out it passed the quality bar.
  • Send back is neutral grey, not red. Your rule, and it is the right one. A rejection is a routing decision, not an alarm.
  • This is the one place I would break the read-only rule, and only here, because approving a post is the exact action you would otherwise leave the board to perform. Everything else stays a GET.
Evidence Tap-to-act with approve / revert / edit on the item itself, decisions persisting, is your 2026-05-18 rule verbatim. The approval-inbox framing is one of the relayed video patterns and it lines up with your rule rather than competing with it. The read-only exception is flagged deliberately because the spec's section 04 says no writes ride along with this pass, so this is a proposal to amend that, not a quiet override.

Why

  • Grey is a real state, not an omission. An agent idle for nine days is information. Hiding it makes the roster look healthier than the system is, which is the exact failure that killed the last board's credibility.
  • Amber means blocked on you, and it is the only bridge between this tile and the “needs you” panel above. Anything amber here should already be a row up there.
  • The inline reply is small and worth a lot. A blocked agent today waits for you to notice a message somewhere else. One text field on the board turns a stall into twenty seconds.
  • Last-run times, not uptime. A heartbeat proves a process is alive, it does not prove work happened. The spec already makes that distinction and the roster should honour it.

Replay the day — new, and the most interesting of the three
Replay scrubber · not built

at 12:40 · 3 agents running · $0.21 spent · 8 tasks closed
llm_calls, lane_tasks, gate_log, agent_messages, all already timestamped · no new instrumentation needed

Why it matters for you specifically

  • You are not at the desk when the work happens. A live board answers “what is true now”. It cannot answer “what happened between 9 and noon while I was on a call”, which is the question you actually have when you sit down.
  • Dragging the playhead makes the whole board show that moment. Same tiles, same layout, different timestamp. Nothing new to learn.
  • It costs almost nothing to build. Every table this reads is already timestamped. This is a filter over data you already have, not new plumbing.
  • It is also the single best thing to show someone. Watching a day of an autonomous system replay in ten seconds is a demo. That is a side benefit, not the reason.
Evidence, and its limit This is a relayed video pattern with no durable record in the repo, so I cannot cite what the original actually showed. The case above is mine, argued from your workflow. If the reasoning does not hold for you, kill it, and nothing else in this study depends on it.

NOT WIRED — a tile whose source was never connected renders exactly like this, never as a zero
When anything in here goes wrong, the summary line turns amber and names the thing. The drawer does not open itself. It just stops being quiet.

Why

  • Six cards of infrastructure health currently sit at the same visual weight as the things that need your decision. That is backwards. When they are all green they are worth one line.
  • Collapsed does not mean hidden. The summary line always states the state, so “all clear” is a claim the board is making, not an absence of information.
  • Run now on a cron is the one internals action worth exposing. A missed window that you can retry from the board beats one that requires a terminal.
  • The not-wired box stays visible in the drawer. A source that was never connected must never render as a zero. That contract is already in the spec and this is where it gets tested hardest, because a drawer is exactly where a lie hides best.
Evidence The demotion is the “if it does not help me decide at 8am before coffee, cut it” filter, relayed from the videos, applied as demote rather than delete. The three-state failure contract and the pulse-is-not-proof-of-work caveat are from the spec's own section 05. The run-now and delete controls on a cron monitor are a relayed video pattern; I kept run-now and dropped delete, because deleting a schedule from a dashboard is the kind of irreversible action that does not belong on a glance surface.
