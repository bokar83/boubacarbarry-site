01The logic

A newcomer fills a room with leverage, not a list

With no warm list, the worst move is to grind cold DMs to strangers. The best move is to borrow rooms other people already built. Every channel below is ranked by one thing: how many of the right owners it puts in front of me per hour of my time.

There is a warm list of 63 local connections that came with me, and it helps. But this engine is designed to fill the room if that list were zero. The order matters. Free talks and co-hosts go first because they hand me an audience I did not have to assemble. The cold channels go last because they cost the most effort per seat.

02The playbook, ranked

Channels in leverage order

Work top to bottom. Do not skip to the bottom because it feels productive. The top three fill most of the room.

01

Free talks, then funnel to the paid roomHighest leverage

Offer to speak free for 30 to 45 minutes to a group that already meets: a chamber lunch, an SBDC class, a coworking community, a SCORE workshop. They own the audience. I bring value, then the close is one line: "I run the long version on July 30, here is the seat." A single talk to 40 owners can fill a quarter of the room. This is the engine. Everything else supports it.

02

Co-host with a vendor who owns an SMB listHigh leverage

A bookkeeper, a local web agency, a business insurance broker, a payroll rep, an IT shop. They sell to the exact owners I want and they email a list every month. I bring the content and the room, they bring the list, we split the seats. I rent the list I do not have, and pay in value, not cash.

03

Get listed everywhere a local owner looksHigh leverage

Silicon Slopes events and newsletter. Chamber member calendars. Meetup. Alignable. Nextdoor with the new-resident angle. Eventbrite. Each listing is passive distribution that runs while I sleep. None of them cost money and a few of them carry real traffic. Be findable in the five places a Salt Lake owner already checks.

04

Local podcasts and radioMedium leverage

Utah has active small-business podcasts looking for guests every week. A 30-minute episode gives me a credible third-party intro to thousands of local owners and a clip I reuse everywhere. Slow to book, strong when it lands. Pitch now, air whenever they can fit me.

05

Referral asks to director and academic nodesMedium leverage

The warm list is heavy with university directors and HR leaders. Most are not the buyer, but each one knows ten owners. The ask is not "come to my workshop." It is "who is one owner in your circle who should be in this room?" One forward from a trusted node beats fifty cold messages.

06

In-person ground game with the QR cardSteady leverage

I am new here, so I go where owners gather: chamber mixers, coworking events, Silicon Slopes meetups. A simple card with a QR to the seat link turns every handshake into a possible registration. Slow per-seat, but it builds the local face that makes every other channel work.

07

The 63 warm connectionsDirect seats + referrals

Not a cold channel, so it is not the engine, but it is the fastest direct seats available. A focused DM and email pass to the local A-tier, each with one concrete ask and a referral nudge, lands the first handful of registrations and warms up the referral nodes that feed channel 05.

05Today to July 30

The 10-day run-up

A realistic order of operations. Channels that take time to land (talks, co-hosts, podcasts) get pitched first so they have room to say yes. The warm list and the listings carry the early seats while the bigger rooms get booked.

1Set up

Confirm the basics, build the assets

Confirm July 30 room, time, and that the $47 seat link works and the cap is open. Print QR cards. Write the seat-link short URL on everything. Nothing goes out until the seat link is verified live.

2Pitch big

Send every free-talk and co-host pitch

Template A to the SBDC, SCORE, ChamberWest, South Valley, Murray, and Salt Lake chambers. Template B to four complementary vendors. These need lead time, so they go first, all in one morning.

3Get listed

Submit every listing

Silicon Slopes events + newsletter, Eventbrite, Meetup, Alignable, Nextdoor (template E), and ask each chamber for a member-newsletter mention. Passive distribution starts running now.

4Warm A-tier

First warm-list pass, top of the A-tier

Template C1 and C2 to the top 20 local A-tier connections, one message each, one ask each. Brandon Orton, Khalique Zaman, the Sandy and SLCC names first. This lands the first direct seats.

5Podcasts

Pitch the podcasts, work the inbox

Email UtahPreneur, Silicon Slopes show, Utah Business pod. Reply to every chamber and vendor response with the ACA move: acknowledge, compliment, ask the next question. Book the talks that said yes.

6Referrals

Referral asks to the director nodes

Template D to the university and HR director cluster on the warm list. Offer to write the forward. One name from each beats fifty cold sends.

7Ground game

Show up where owners are

Attend one chamber mixer or coworking event in person with QR cards. Deliver any free talk that got booked for this week. Every handshake is a possible seat.

8Second wave

Warm-list pass two, plus B-tier local

Second pass to A-tier who have not replied (new angle, never a repeat) and a first pass to confirmed-local B-tier owners. Nudge the co-hosts to send their email. Add new value, never "bumping this."

9Close gaps

Count seats, fill the gap deliberately

Tally registrations against the 15 to 25 target. If short, run the highest-leverage open channel harder: another talk, a co-host reminder, a focused referral round. Decide from the number, not the vibe.

10Day-of

Last call, confirm, deliver

A short "doors are filling, here is your seat" to anyone who clicked but did not buy. Confirm the room and AV. Send registered owners the time and parking. Then walk in and close from the front of the room, which is where I convert.

06Does it add up

The seat math

A realistic build to 15 to 25 from cold. No single channel carries it. The room fills because four of them stack.

6 to 9
Two free talks to groups of 35 to 50, modest conversion
4 to 7
One co-host emailing their SMB list once or twice
5 to 8
Warm list, two passes, A-tier direct plus referrals
3 to 5
Listings, podcasts, ground game tail

That stacks to 18 to 29 before any no-shows. Plan the channels to overshoot the 15 to 25 target, because some talks will not get booked in time and some seats will not show. The fix when the count is short is always the same: run the highest-leverage open channel harder.
