The Roads Plan

Distribution and traffic go-to-market across every URL asset · Aug 19, 2026

TL;DR

The infrastructure is real and the roads to it do not exist. Six lead-magnet pages are live on humanatwork.ai with working email capture. The humanatwork.ai homepage links to none of them. None of the six are in the sitemap. Two carry an explicit noindex. Three are linked from no other page anywhere on the site. A stranger has no path to any of them. That is a distribution problem, not a capture problem.

The uncomfortable number that should shape everything else. The newsletter has 36 active subscribers as of Aug 12, verified against the platform. Personal X sits at 769 followers with about 21 impressions per post, last measured May 31 and stale. The automated publish rail to personal LinkedIn and X has published nothing since Jul 31. So this is not a case of good roads pointed at the wrong place. There are almost no roads and almost no traffic on them.

The recommendation: build one Front Door, then accept that only one road has real reach today. One page holding all six magnets behind a single email field. Then be honest about ranking: LinkedIn, and specifically his own presence on it, is the only channel with real reach right now. X is a rounding error at 21 impressions. The newsletter at 36 people is a destination, not yet a channel. Search is a slow compounding road worth starting and not worth waiting on. The plumbing still has to be built, but it will not produce volume this month and saying otherwise would be a lie.

What is genuinely automatable, stated narrowly. The one-time structural fixes: the Front Door, the cross-site footer, the sitemap and indexability repair, the road tagging. Plus one real rail: once he approves a post, the leg from a queued row to a published post works without him. The step that used to feed that queue automatically is broken and is not worth repairing, because his approval has to sit at exactly that point anyway.

Cost to him: about 35 minutes in week one, about 25 minutes a week after. This is deliberately not a new content operation. The north star is fractional engagements, not an audience, and the twenty selling hours are not available to fund a media business.

The publish rail, honestly

An automated path from an approved content row to a published post on LinkedIn and X exists and is wired. It has published nothing to a personal account since Jul 31. The reason is specific: the step that promotes a finished post into the queue the publisher drains is a crew that has been disabled since around Jun 19, and it cannot be re-enabled through the documented route because the gate that would clear it requires usage records the crew never writes.

Why that turns out not to matter much. The publisher itself is healthy. It simply finds an empty queue. And a standing hard rule says every post must be shown to him and approved before it is scheduled at all, which means a human decision belongs at exactly the point the broken crew used to automate. The broken piece is a step this plan does not want automated anyway. Setting the row to queued after his approval reaches the working leg directly. Do not spend a day fixing the crew.

The mechanism: one Front Door, and one honest road

The teardown that started this thread is worth copying for its shape, never its niche or its volume. The shape is one high-volume channel feeding one owned surface feeding a cheap qualification purchase feeding the real offer. One path, no branching.

The current setup is the inverse: six capture surfaces, five domains, no top-of-funnel road, and a tripwire nobody can find. But the correction the numbers force is sharper than "add roads". There is currently no channel with volume to point anywhere. So the plan has two halves that must not be confused.

Half one: the destination. Build it once, and it is permanent.

One page holding all six magnets behind a single email field, reusing the capture pattern already proven live on the investigation pack. Every road terminates there. It is the one URL for his bio, every site footer, every post, every profile field.

Why one and not six. Six URLs cannot be promoted; one can. The six stop being six competing offers and become the contents of one. It also removes the which-one-do-I-link decision that is part of why none of them ever got linked.

Why humanatwork.ai. The magnets already live there, the capture endpoint is already proven there against a row read back out of the store, and the personal site's newsletter form currently posts to a contact webhook rather than a real subscribe. Do not put the main entrance on the side of the building that does not work yet.

One naming trap. humanatwork.ai/ai-agents-start/ already exists and is an event registration page. Whatever slug the Front Door takes, it must not be confusable with that one. Flagged rather than decided, because the name is copy in his name.

The roads, one by one

Road 1. LinkedIn, his own presence. The spine, and it is already half running.

Three to five voice-gated comments a day is already a standing daily rule, and it is already being executed and logged, with 19 actions recorded on Aug 18. That is the only distribution motion in the whole system with real reach behind it. Two adjustments turn it into a road rather than only reputation work:

  • Never a link inside a comment. It suppresses reach and reads as promotion, which is the opposite of the ally posture the rule is built on. The road is his profile: the website field and the featured section point at the Front Door, so every profile visit a good comment earns converts with no extra work per comment.
  • His own posting has to restart. Nothing has gone out to a personal account since Jul 31, and his own read is that the commenting helps but he still has not been active enough. Comments earn profile visits; posts are what make the profile worth following. Three a week, Monday, Wednesday, Friday, is the documented target and it is the right size.

Neither half of this can be automated on the writing side and neither should be. An automated comment on a well-known practitioner's post is a reputational liability, not a road.

One live risk to resolve first: the publishing tool's LinkedIn connection was flagged as expiring and that has not been confirmed resolved. If it has lapsed, the automated leg does not fire regardless of approvals. Check before relying on it.

Road 2. Make the six pages findable at all. One-time config, nothing to approve.

Remove the two noindex tags, add all six magnets to the sitemap, and link them from the site's own navigation as well as from the Front Door. Today this is the difference between invisible to every search engine and every AI answer engine, and merely reachable.

Honest limit: this makes them reachable, not ranked. They are thin pages in a crowded topic and search is slow. The real value is that internal links plus a sitemap are what let the Front Door itself be indexed, and the Front Door is the page worth ranking.

Road 3. The shared cross-site footer. One hour once, then permanent.

Five owned domains with two public cross-links between them in total. Add one shared footer line on every page of every brand site pointing at the Front Door. Ships through the deploy webhook each repo already has, so it goes live on push and then never needs touching.

Scope: the brand sites and the personal site. Never the faceless channels or their email backbone, for the reason in Road 6.

Road 4. The publish rail, retargeted rather than repaired.

The rail's working leg takes an approved, scored, queued row and publishes it on schedule. Use that leg. Do not rebuild the broken promotion step above it, because his approval belongs at that exact point anyway.

The change is a standing rule, not a new system: every post carries one closing line pointing at the Front Door, rotating which magnet it names. The board is already under a standing rule to be reviewed weekly and refilled every two weeks with two to three weeks of approved content, so this rides an existing cadence rather than adding one.

Split clearly: publishing is automatic once he has approved. Writing and approving are not, and must not be.

Road 5. The newsletter, as an archive more than a broadcast.

At 36 subscribers this is not yet a distribution channel and should not be described as one. Its send path is manual and will stay manual, because the platform's send API is not available on the current plan. Two moves that are still worth it: each issue carries the Front Door link and names one specific magnet, and the issue archive is published as indexable pages. The archive is the only compounding organic asset in this entire plan, because every issue becomes a page findable later by someone who was never on the list.

One reliability note, since it undercuts the channel: the Aug 10 issue was drafted and never sent, confirmed against the platform rather than the repo. Whether the Aug 17 issue sent is unverified. A weekly channel that misses weeks does not compound. Fix the reliability before investing in the reach.

Road 6. The faceless channels. Deliberately NOT a road.

The faceless family is anonymous by construction, with a separate neutral email backbone that exists specifically so those channels do not trace back to his identity. Pointing any of them at a capture surface carrying his name breaks that firewall permanently and cannot be undone once a viewer makes the connection.

They remain an adjacent revenue line. They are not top-of-funnel for his list. The strongest one is genuinely working on its own terms, near-daily with real if small view counts, and that is a reason to leave it alone rather than a reason to redirect it.

Road 7. Directories and communities. The smallest road here.

Legitimate placements only: his own company pages, relevant newsletter directories, and the professional communities he already belongs to, where the rule is participate first and link only where the community explicitly permits it. Two hours once, not two hours a week. Named rather than sold.

What genuinely runs without him, and what never will

This section exists because the ask was explicitly for things that run without him. Some of it does. Some of it cannot, and claiming otherwise would produce content that reads as a bot writing in his name, which is a rule violation and a reputational cost, not a shortcut.

Runs today, no human per instance

  • Publishing an already-approved, already-scored, queued post on schedule. This leg works. Subject to confirming the LinkedIn connection has not lapsed.
  • The per-magnet welcome that fires when someone captures. Already wired.
  • Site deploys. Every brand repo deploys on push, so a footer change ships itself.
  • The video path to the faceless channels, which is outside this plan by design.

One-time build, then runs forever with nobody touching it

  • The Front Door page.
  • The shared cross-site footer line.
  • The sitemap, noindex and internal-linking repair.
  • The road tagging that makes any of this measurable.

Needs him, and should not be automated

  • LinkedIn comments. Judgment about what a specific post left out.
  • His own posts. The thing that makes the profile worth following is that it is him.
  • Post approval. Standing hard rule: shown inline, approved, then scheduled. An auto-published post he never read is a defect, not efficiency.
  • The newsletter send. Manual is the only available path, not a choice.
  • Anything at all that speaks in his name. Voice battery every time, at or above the bar.

Explicitly NOT worth automating, and this is a recommendation not an omission

The broken crew that used to promote finished posts into the publish queue. Repairing it would restore an automation whose only job is to make a decision a standing rule says must be his. Leave it broken and set the queue state after his approval instead.

A sketch of the closing line the standing rule would add, written only to show the shape. It has not been through the voice or social-quality gates and must not ship until it has.

What is deliberately not in the cadence

A new content operation. The teardown's 464 videos are a full-time media business built over two years and copying that volume would consume exactly the selling hours the dual-track plan just allocated. Also not in the cadence: any meaningful investment in X. At about 21 impressions per post it does not earn a slot, so posts get mirrored there at no extra cost and nothing more is spent on it.

Measurement, and the blind spot that has to be named

Site analytics have been locked for non-payment since Aug 17. Every query returns a subscription error, so all eight sites are currently blind. Separately, the workshop funnel pages never carried a tracking token at all, so restoring the subscription alone would not have measured that funnel either.

So today there is no way to tell which road works. A plan that cannot be measured cannot be corrected, which makes this a section rather than a footnote.

Two options, and the recommendation

  • Option A, restore the subscription. Returns page-level traffic across all sites. Costs money, and does not fix the pages that were never instrumented.
  • Option B, count captures by road tag. Cruder, free, works today, because every capture endpoint already persists a row and the tagging helper already exists. It measures the only number that matters, which is people on the list, and attributes them to a road.

Recommendation: do Option B this week regardless, and decide Option A separately on its own merits. Option B is what makes the plan falsifiable, it costs nothing, and it does not wait on a billing decision. Option A is a nice-to-have on top, not a prerequisite.

The number to watch, and the honest baseline

New capture rows per week, split by road. Not impressions, not followers, not page views. The baseline is 36 subscribers. That is small enough that the first real movement will be unmistakable, which is the one advantage of starting here. If the number has not moved in three weeks, the roads are wrong and the plan gets rebuilt rather than repeated louder.
