SPINEThe six principles›
1

An LLM predicts from what you give it. How you direct it (context plus clear instruction) is the real skill.

2

Give it your context once, then reuse it (your brain file / memory).

3

Turn what you repeat into a saved recipe you own: a "skill."

4

Agentic means it DOES multi-step work, not just answers. You delegate, not just ask.

5

Pressure-test and simplify your thinking. The Council and the Simplicity Check are meta-skills that ride any tool.

6

Own it, move it. Never get locked in.

Proof line: "I went from ChatGPT to Gemini to Claude, and other tools since, and never lost a step, because I kept the principles, not the product."
BUILDThe live build: 3 steps›
Golden rule on stage: every paste below also sits in PARACHUTE.txt in a second window. Never type from memory. If anything stalls, read the pre-recorded reveal off the screenshot and keep moving. The Council cannot "fail"; it just argues. Say the one-liner BEFORE you paste, so the room knows what to watch.
Step 1 Install + run The Decision Council›
▸ Paste: install prompt (into the Project's custom instructions)
You are my Decision Council. When I give you a decision, respond as five
separate advisors, each in one short paragraph:

1. THE CONTRARIAN: argue hard against the decision.
2. FIRST PRINCIPLES: list the assumptions I am making and which ones are shaky.
3. THE EXPANSIONIST: describe a bolder, bigger version of this.
4. THE OUTSIDER: react with zero context, like a smart stranger hearing it cold.
5. THE EXECUTOR: give the first concrete step and the single thing most likely to kill it.

Then finish with VERDICT: proceed, change, or kill, in one sentence, and why.
Be direct. No flattery. Do not agree with me by default.

My decision is: [paste your decision here]
▸ Then paste a decision (ask the room first; use this sample if nobody bites)
Should I hire a part-time VA or build an agent to handle my inbox?

Coach the input out loud: "Give it a REAL decision with stakes, not 'should I use AI.' The sharper your decision, the sharper the council." Take a volunteer's only if it's specific.

◆ Say it out loud
Watch: five real advisors are about to pressure-test a decision YOU could be making this week, and end with a verdict, in about ten seconds.
● What the room sees / expected output

Five clearly separate, different voices in ~30s: one arguing against, one stripping to assumptions, one going bigger, one reacting cold, one naming the Monday move, then one blunt VERDICT: line. They visibly DISAGREE (the Contrarian actually pushes back). Short, scannable, under a page.

⚠ Fallback
Stall/error: switch to PARACHUTE.txt, read the perfect run off the screenshot: "here's the exact run I did an hour ago." Too agreeable: add "The advisors must disagree with each other, and the verdict must name the single biggest risk." and re-run. Login hiccup: open the frozen Project from pre-flight. Never debug auth on stage.
Step 2 Add the premortem›
▸ Paste as a new message
Now add one more advisor: THE PREMORTEM. Assume this decision failed badly
six months from now. Walk it back and tell me exactly why it failed and the
earliest signal I would have seen. Then give me your updated VERDICT.

Tighter version (also in PARACHUTE.txt):

Assume this failed badly six months from now. Walk it back and tell me why.
◆ Say it out loud
One line, 'assume this already failed', and the same tool now catches the thing that would've cost you, before it costs you.
● What the room sees / expected output

The same council reruns; a new THE PREMORTEM block imagines the failure already happened, names a concrete cause + the earliest warning signal, and the VERDICT shifts or hardens. The contrast is the point: it caught what round one missed.

⚠ Fallback
The premortem IS the universal save. If Step 1 ran flat, this almost always produces real tension. If the whole thing errors, read the pre-recorded premortem screenshot. The message ("one sentence upgraded my tool") survives even from a screenshot.
Step 3 Use the Council to build the Simplicity Check›
▸ Move A: ask the Council (paste into the Decision Council project)
My decision is: should I build a second skill called the Simplicity Check —
a tool that takes anything I am building or overthinking and finds the
simplest version that still works, plus what to cut? Give me the full council
and a verdict.
◆ Say it out loud
I'm going to let the tool I just built decide whether the NEXT tool is worth building. The system is about to improve itself, live.

Room sees: the tool you built ten minutes ago evaluates its own next upgrade. Five advisors plus a verdict (almost certainly proceed). If it says "change," even better; follow its advice while building.

⚠ Fallback
Move A errors: read the frozen Council-verdict screenshot: "it told me to build it, so let's build it." Momentum preserved.
▸ Move B: build it (new Project "Simplicity Check", paste into custom instructions)
You are my Simplicity Check. When I give you a plan, a process, or something
I am building, answer in four short parts:

1. WHERE IT IS OVERCOMPLICATED: name the parts adding weight, not value.
2. THE SIMPLEST VERSION THAT WORKS: describe the stripped-down core.
3. AM I SKIPPING THE BASICS: call out any boring fundamental I am missing.
4. CUT LIST: the specific things I can remove right now.

Bias hard toward simple. Favor the plain version over the clever one.
Be direct, no flattery.

Here it is: [paste what you are building here]
▸ Run it on this (pre-baked sample)
Here it is: my plan to get more clients is — start a newsletter, a podcast,
a YouTube channel, post daily on three platforms, run paid ads, and build a
referral program, all starting this month.
◆ Say it out loud
The council said build it, so we just built it, live, and now it's going to tell me what to throw away.
● What the room sees / expected output

A brand-new tool created in under a minute, then it rips the overstuffed plan down to what matters and hands back a literal cut list. Four labeled parts (Where overcomplicated / Simplest version / Skipping basics / Cut list); the CUT LIST is concrete (e.g. "cut the podcast and the ads this month"); bias toward simple is obvious.

⚠ Fallback
Move B errors during the build: open the Simplicity Check Project frozen from pre-flight: "here's the one I built earlier, same prompt." Output thin: add "Be specific to my situation, not generic" and re-run. Either way the takeaway ("you now own two tools you built tonight") is intact.
The floor: even if everything else fails, Step 1 + Step 2 alone (Council + premortem) is the minimum viable wow and must never be cut. One owned tool plus one "whoa, on MY decision" beats a feature parade. Nothing is built cold on stage. You're re-performing a known-good run, not gambling.
