1. The bet, and what it rejects

The thesis, in one paragraph

We sell retained fractional AI leadership at $5,000 a month to owner-operators of Utah businesses with ten to fifty employees, and three to five of those retainers is the whole target. The binding constraint is not reach and it is not copy. It is that no stranger has a reason to believe him, and we have the receipt. So the strategy is not to send more or be seen more. It is to borrow trust that already exists, three ways at once. First and largest, rent his AI capability to firms that already hold the client relationships he does not have, so their credibility does the vouching and he stops needing to be found. Second, serve a programme rather than sell to a room, because an institution that puts him in front of its own people has lent him its name. Third, be findable as the answer rather than as an ad, which today means being the source an AI assistant cites when someone asks who does fractional AI work in Utah, a surface that is winnable right now because almost nobody is contesting it. Under all three sits the only proof he actually owns: he runs several businesses on an AI system he built and operates every day, which is demonstrable in ninety seconds and does not require a client he does not have. Everything else in the portfolio exists to feed a retainer.

What this bet rejects

A strategy that rejects nothing is an inventory. This one rejects four things that currently have advocates inside our own documents.

  • It rejects volume. The volume ramp written into the master roadmap's Lane C, roughly 1,000 emails a month plus 400 connects plus 200 DMs, was ratified five weeks after our own pressure test concluded "cold email is a calibration instrument, not a 90-day client machine" and five weeks after 767 sends returned zero. More of a motion that converts at zero converts at zero.
  • It rejects being found first. Discovery work is real and it is section 7, but it compounds over months and he needs money in weeks. A strategy that leads with SEO is a strategy that has decided to wait.
  • It rejects the portfolio as a growth engine. Five brands and eight faceless channels are not five paths to a retainer. They are one path plus a great deal of surface area. Section 12 says which ones stop.
  • It rejects earning trust from scratch. A case study takes a client, and a client takes trust, and that is the loop we have been stuck in since June. Borrowing trust breaks the loop instead of grinding at it.

The organising logic

"All that we do is to get ourselves to that retainer stage, whether it's the SW work, whether it's the faceless channels, all that is leading towards this." Boubacar, 2026-08-11.

That is the test this document applies to every lane in it. A lane must be able to name the chain out loud: this act, this human, this conversation, this retainer. If the chain cannot be named, the lane does not get resourced. This is the value-add test from the running rules, applied at the level of the whole business rather than the level of a single task.

5. Borrowed trust: the primary lane

"How come you don't reach out to RBL and talk to them about working with them on the side as one of their consultants, a contractor. Add myself as a contractor to multiple different firms that may want to expand into the AI world but don't want to hire on a full-time employee." Boubacar, 2026-08-11.

The principle, stated so future work can extend it

Rent capability to whoever already holds the trust. They have relationships and no AI answer. He has the answer and no client list. Their credibility becomes the trust anchor that 767 cold sends could not buy. It also inverts his hardest constraint, because he stops needing to be found.

A category qualifies as a lane when all four are true. Use this to generate new ones rather than treating the five below as the list:

  1. They have recurring advisory contact with our buyer, so the relationship already exists and is periodic rather than transactional.
  2. Their clients are asking them AI questions they cannot answer, so the demand arrives at their door and stalls there.
  3. They cannot justify a full-time AI hire, because the demand is real but not yet a headcount.
  4. They sell time or advice rather than product, so adding a capability is margin, not a channel conflict.

This inverts the usual direction of an ask, and the direction matters. He is not asking to be added to a list of vendors. He is offering to be the answer they currently have to decline.

Question two: SEO, the slow half

Search optimisation is a months-long compounding asset and it should be resourced as one, not as a September play. Four concrete jobs, in order of cost to benefit:

  1. Rebuild the sitemap with every live page and real last-modified dates. Twenty minutes.
  2. Complete the Google Business Profile and get reviews. Today it exists and is used purely as a place to post workshop events. There is no completeness audit, no category work, and zero reviews. For a local service business this is the highest-yield local search asset there is, and the existing ninety-day plan already specs fifteen reviews by day ninety with no execution record.
  3. Build the two pages that do not exist. There is no fractional page and no about page. The queries he named, fractional AI leader and fractional HR leader in Utah, have no page to rank. You cannot rank for a thing you have not written.
  4. Build the citation base. Consistent name, address and phone across the directories the ninety-day plan already lists. Target in that plan: forty to sixty consistent citations by day ninety. Current count: effectively zero.

Question three: GEO and AEO, the winnable half, and its own section for a reason

Generative and answer engine optimisation is not search optimisation with a different name. Search asks "which page ranks". Answer engines ask "which entity does a model believe is the answer, and which sources will it cite". The mechanics are different, the competition is thin, and this is the surface where a business with no client list can win, because models cite structure and consistency rather than social proof.

Six jobs, and they are not the SEO jobs:

  1. Create the Wikidata entity. Our own ninety-day plan calls this "the single highest-leverage AEO action most businesses skip". It is skipped.
  2. Get into the third-party sources assistants actually read. Crunchbase, Clutch, the LinkedIn company page that already exists, association directories. None of these exist today except the LinkedIn page. A model recommending a consultant is reading other people's pages, not ours.
  3. Put an llms.txt on every domain. One of three has it. It is a small file that tells a model what the business is, who it serves and what it sells, and it is the cheapest thing on this list.
  4. Fix the entity graph. Complete schema.org markup with a correct sameAs array linking every profile to every other one. Today the plan's own template carries placeholder identifiers and a LinkedIn company URL that does not match the real one.
  5. Write answer-shaped content. Pages that literally answer "who provides fractional AI leadership for small businesses in Utah" in a form a model can lift in one paragraph. Question-and-answer markup is already used on the homepage and works; it is simply not pointed at the queries that matter.
  6. Measure it weekly and actually look. The scanner exists at scripts/ai_visibility_weekly_scan.py with a Sunday schedule writing to Postgres. The only recorded mention rate is the baseline: zero out of thirty, from 2026-06-28. The target in the existing plan is thirty percent by day ninety.

The cobbler's children defect, and it is the most quotable thing in this document. geolisted.co, which is the AI visibility product, has no robots.txt, no sitemap.xml, no llms.txt, no canonical tag on the homepage, and a LocalBusiness schema node whose url points at a page on a different domain entirely. We sell AI visibility from a site that is invisible to AI. The methodology to fix it is written, adversarially reviewed five times, and already proven on the consulting site, which went from 75 to 100 on our own scanner in a single pass on 2026-07-08. It was never applied here. Filed as a SYS item, and it is the first fix in this whole section because a prospect who checks will find it.

The second cobbler's defect. That 75 to 100 improvement is real, measured and logged, and the log's own words are "the weekly cadence is owned, not aspirational". It has one entry. Nothing since 2026-07-08. A measurement discipline that lapsed after one reading is not a discipline, and the before-and-after is the closest thing to a case study in the portfolio.

One thing that is already right, and should be said

The consulting site's robots.txt explicitly welcomes the AI crawlers by name, with allow rules for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended and the rest, and carries the comment "AI assistants and search crawlers are explicitly welcome." Fourteen pages carry structured data. That is genuinely good work and it is the foundation the six jobs above build on.

9. The principles and running rules

These exist scattered across memory files and this is the first time they are in one place. Written as rules someone can follow, each with the reason it exists, because a rule without its reason gets negotiated away the first time it is inconvenient.

How the day is chosen

1. We are not in the business of busy-ness. Class A actions first, always.

The rule. Classify every candidate action before ranking it. Class A puts him in front of a human: a call, a meeting, an email that makes an ask, a room, a reply to a live thread. Class B builds the thing that brings humans to him. Class C is everything else: tweaks, refactors, hygiene, tidying. Always work the highest available Class A first. Class B is only justified when it unblocks a specific named Class A, and the name has to be stated out loud; Class B with no named Class A downstream is Class C wearing a better hat. A day with zero Class A actions is a failed day, and it gets said out loud, unprompted, before the day ends.

Why. 2026-08-07 is the proof and not a hypothetical. A great deal shipped and zero asks were made. Five chamber emails, two list emails to 729 people and a live expiring conversation all sat undelivered while infrastructure got built around them. Return-per-minute ranking alone causes this: a cheap tidy task scores beautifully and a hard conversation scores badly, so the day fills with plumbing and reads as productive.

2. The value-add test: rank by nearness to a paid conversation.

The rule. Rank every candidate action by its proximity to a paid conversation, never by its size, its tidiness, its deadline, or how complete it will feel when finished. Output volume is not the unit. The chain must be nameable out loud: this act, this human, this conversation, this contract. If the chain cannot be named, the item is not value-add. Five minutes can outrank five hours.

Why. His words, 2026-08-11: "I can write a thousand posts but if those thousand posts lead to zero dollars, it's not value add. If I can spend five minutes with somebody and get a meeting that leads to a contract, that is value add. That's the level I need you to be at." That last sentence is a bar on the agent, not on him. This is the parent principle that Class A, the daily ask and the retainer north star all descend from.

3. Use him only for what only he can do.

The rule. Split every plan into two explicit lists. Only he can do: say the words in a live conversation, make the judgment call, give the go-ahead to send, sign the price, decide the goal, show up in the room, be the person in the relationship. Short by construction. The system does: everything else, each item carrying a named owner, because an item with no owner is not assigned, it is just written down, and it lands back on him by default. At equal yield, a route the system can run unattended ranks above a route that needs his hours. Effort is measured in his minutes, never machine minutes. A plan that hands him work the system could run is a failed plan. Not suboptimal, failed.

Why. He spent roughly two hours defining what the Chief of Staff is for, and during those same two hours the emails it owed him had not gone out. His time went to work the system owes him, on the day he named the rule. Carve-out: this is never permission to send, publish, post or contact anyone on his behalf. The irreversible outward act stays his.

4. One finished ask to one named human, every day.

The rule. One file per day naming one human and carrying one finished message ready to send. Never a list, never a shortlist, never a ranked table of candidates. Subject and body, in a code box, voice-gated at ninety or above with the real score written in. One ask per message; never bundle a second one in, however cheap it looks, because two asks makes the small one read as the setup for the big one. Tomorrow's candidate is named at the bottom of today's file, with a one-line reason. That last part is the load-bearing part. He sends; no agent sends anything in that folder.

Why. An archive sweep counted nine fully-built lists of named people against five sends traceable from any of them in ninety days. The lists were not badly made, they were never finished. A list is an invitation to decide, and deciding is the expensive part, so the list loses to whatever else is on fire. The recommendation sprint died twice, both times by starting from a blank page. It is not courage that is missing, it is a finished thing to send.

How work is recorded

5. Every action is dated and carries both branches.

The rule. When you create an action anywhere, write the Money Map row in the same turn. Not later, not at review. Four fields, all required: the action naming a specific person, a date, the expected outcome, and both branches, what happens if they respond and what happens if they have not responded by a named date. A row with an action and no date fails. A row with only the happy path fails.

Why. Branches decay silently. The response case is easy to remember because something arrives to prompt it. The no-response case has nothing to prompt it, so if the date and the next move are not written at the moment the action is created, they are never written at all and the thread quietly dies. The acceptance test is his: if everything else is lost, can someone open the Money Map alone and run the business from it.

6. Every error found gets logged, including the ones fixed on the spot.

The rule. File a tracked item for every error, defect or problem, in the same session it is found. If you fix it immediately, still file it, recording the defect, the evidence, the fix and the merge reference. The fix does not delete the record. Telling him in chat is not filing it. A note in a handoff is not filing it. Scope is everything, not just web pages: copy, scripts, schedules, data, infrastructure, a wrong number in a document, a claim that turned out to be false.

Why. A fixed bug with no record is indistinguishable from a bug that never existed, and that is how the same defect ships three times. One clipped label is cosmetic; eight of them across four sites is a missing responsive check in the build process, and you can only see that pattern if both were written down.

How people are treated

7. Relationship type gates what may be asked, and some people are never a prospect.

The rule. Classify the relationship in writing next to the person's name before a single word is drafted. The class decides what may ever be asked.

ClassWhat may be asked

Why. A draft offered a paid seat to his own mentor. The draft's own notes acknowledged the person runs a competing agency and does not need it, then asked anyway for a read or a referral. That reasoning is the tell: an agent noticing the relationship makes the ask inappropriate and then hunting for a different ask rather than stopping. Selling to a mentor inverts a relationship that took years and cannot be re-earned by apologising. This gate runs upstream of the copy battery.

8. Never lead the witness.

The rule. Two halves, and they are the same failure pointed in two directions. Toward the prospect: when someone names a problem in passing, restate only their own words and ask for the shape of it. Never write back a diagnosis. Hedged phrasing does not rescue it; cut the claim, do not soften it. Toward him: for every sentence in copy going out in his name, ask whether he actually said or did this, or whether an agent decided it for him. If the second, cut it. The chain is: agent idea, then his decision, then his action, and only the third one may be written as fact.

Why. An attendee mentioned "an ongoing invoicing problem" and a draft turned it into a confident three-clause diagnosis of what that problem was, every specific invented. His correction: "That actually makes us lose credibility before we get in the room with him." Separately, a check-in draft said he was "circling that Wednesday Rotary lunch to ask in person", which he had never agreed to. His words: "Who says i am going to the Rotary lunch?" Both would have gone to a real person.

Naming note: this rule is coined here. The two source files are the never-diagnose rule and the never-state-a-recommendation-as-his-intent rule, and neither uses this phrase. Recorded so nobody hunts for a file that does not exist.

How the whole thing is designed

9. The parachute test: it must work with zero warm network.

The rule. Found every plan, lead engine and campaign on a repeatable cold-discovery engine that needs no warm network. Imagine him dropped into a city where he knows nobody and maybe does not speak the language: the system must still generate clients. Warm contacts may only top up leftover slots after the cold pool and may never be the base of a plan or outrank a cold prospect. Reproducibility is the product: "anyone, anywhere can succeed with this" is the thing being proven.

Why. His words, and he has near-zero warm network by his own statement, so a plan that depends on one is a plan that depends on something that is not there. Note the tension this document creates and resolves: the borrowed-trust lane leads with a former employer, which looks like a warm-network play. It is not. RBL is one named starting point inside a category that is cold-discoverable by anyone; the other four lanes have no relationship at all and are found by search. If RBL never replies, the lane is unharmed. That is the test passing.

10. Nothing untrue, ever.

The rule. Every claim in a draft in his name must trace to a lived, verifiable fact you can point to. If you cannot name the source, cut the claim. Internal work is "my own work", never "client work". No implied testimonial, metric or quote from another person without attribution and a date. Distinguish carefully: "operators who paid me" is false at zero clients; "operators who tested this" may be true.

Why. A published draft claimed prompts had been tested "on real client work". They had not; they were internal. Both quality gates passed the fabrication because they trusted the input premise. This is now enforced in production code with a test that fails if the constraint is removed. A strategy built on traction that does not exist is worthless, which is why section 1 leads with the ledger.

How the Chief of Staff is required to behave

11. It reasons and generates. It is not a ranker of rows somebody already wrote.

The rule. Every pass reads the world, asks the operator questions per revenue engine, generates candidate actions rather than waiting for a row to exist, pressure-tests, ranks by value against effort, names the first move on every single action, and closes out what is resolved. An engine with no row is a finding, not an absence. "First move: not stated" is the system handing the thinking back to him. Never patch the ranking when the defect is that the system cannot think.

Why. A run listed four items as blocked on him and all four were already resolved, ranked first an item whose only remaining move belonged to the other party, and omitted every engine he actually cares about because the ranker can only surface rows that already exist. A ranker over a register nobody closes out fills with zombies and destroys trust in the whole surface.

12. It owns follow-up, proactively.

The rule. Anything sent that gates other work gets a real reply watch armed in the same turn as the send. Not a note, not a task row, not a promise to check tomorrow, because a promise to remember dies with the session. Set the silence nudge as well as the hit alert, because no reply is itself information and it is the case that actually needs chasing. Report the loop state unprompted, so "did they reply?" is answered before he asks. He is the last reviewer, never the monitor.

13. It traces consequences of decisions, unprompted.

The rule. When a decision lands, a subscription cancelled, a flag flipped, a date moved, a page taken down, trace what depended on it outside the domain the action happened in, time the consequence against what he is doing right now, and surface it unprompted with a recommendation attached, never a bare warning.

Why. A cancelled billing line turned out to be the site analytics, expiring in precisely the week he was pushing registrations, and also a live evidence source for the Chief of Staff itself. A trace at decision time costs a minute; the same finding two weeks later costs a campaign's worth of data that cannot be backfilled.

14. The Money Map is the plan. Tweak it, never rebuild it.

The rule. Keep a dated, sequenced week and month in the Money Map. Open each day from today's slice of that standing plan and report adjustments to it. Never compose a fresh order of operations. Consolidate into the Money Map; do not accumulate beside it. A new plan document is not a deliverable; if a lane finds itself writing another standalone plan, that is the signal to fold it in instead.

Why. His words: "right now it feels like every single day we come up with a brand new plan." A ranking recomputed from scratch on every run is a brand-new plan every morning, by construction, no matter how good the ranking is. Better inputs do not fix that; only a persisted dated plan does.

This document is subject to that rule and says so. It is a strategy, which is the layer above a plan and is meant to persist, but its dated actions belong in the Money Map and nowhere else. That is what section 13 is for. If this page ever starts carrying this week's sequencing, it has become the thing it was written to stop.

The diagnosis

These are not nine failures of will. They are one structural failure repeated: each of them ends at a decision rather than at a finished artefact. A list of names is an invitation to decide who, and deciding is the expensive part, so it loses every morning to whatever is on fire. A plan to pitch podcasts requires choosing a show, writing the pitch and pressing send, three decisions stacked. A speaker kit with three abstracts still needs someone to pick one and fill a form.

The anchor document already names this exact pattern and gives it a test: does what I am about to recommend break the twenty-year pattern, or repeat it? Its listed red flags are more research, more frameworks, defer the cash move, add another lane, polish instead of shipping. A strategy document is four of those five unless it changes how things get sent.

What this strategy does about it

  1. Nothing in here is done until it is a dated Money Map row with both branches. The plan and the execution surface are the same object. That is rule 5 and it is the whole answer.
  2. The daily ask is the delivery mechanism, not a nice habit. One named human, one finished message, ready to send, tomorrow's name written at the bottom of today's file. It exists specifically because lists do not convert into sends.
  3. Every lane in this document carries a kill condition with a date. A lane that cannot fail cannot be evaluated, and a lane that is never evaluated is never stopped, which is how nine of them accumulate.
  4. The system does everything except the conversation. Every unexecuted route above stalled at a step the system could have taken. Lists, drafts, applications, forms and research are the system's job; only the send and the conversation are his.

If, thirty days from now, the routes in section 8 have the same production history they have today, the correct conclusion is not that the strategy was wrong. It is that the execution mechanism failed again, and that is a different and more urgent problem than anything on this page.
