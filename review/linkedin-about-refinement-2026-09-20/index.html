The research behind the WHO/WHAT/FOR-WHOM candidate that was proposed for Section 1 on 2026-09-24 and rejected by Boubacar on 2026-09-25 in favor of his existing live headline: a blind A/B test against the old live headline, benchmark research on how top HR/consulting-figure LinkedIn profiles position themselves, and the iteration path that got here. Blind A/B test

A blind judge (no knowledge of which version was "his" or which was proposed) was shown two versions and asked to pick the stronger one, with reasoning.

VersionWhat it wasWhat it implied to the judge
A The old live headline + About, pre-refinement A generalist AI/HR consultant with a strong operating philosophy, light on concrete proof, still building their book of business.
B The proposed version (this page's refined headline + About) Someone with a real, verifiable corporate pedigree — more senior and more concretely positioned.

Verdict: B

Reasoning, verbatim from the judge:

  • B gives verifiable specifics (10 years, Fortune 100, Dubai, Catalyst Works) a reader can anchor credibility to. A's only concrete claim was buried and untethered to a client or context.
  • A's numbered 3-step framework read as a sales-page technique.
  • A's closing line pushed for engagement over credibility.
  • B's headline was one sharp claim versus A's three stacked ideas.
  • B stopped after stating its case rather than converting in the last line, which read as more confident.

LinkedIn's own official best-practice guidance

  • Headline field allows 220 characters, but only about 60–70 render on mobile and in search previews. Front-load who you are and what you do.
  • LinkedIn's own line: "the profile headline is your own personal ad that should be treated like a mission statement."
  • Sources: LinkedIn Talent Blog — eye-catching headlines and LinkedIn Talent Blog — profile summaries we love.

Synthesized pattern

  • 4 of 5 benchmark figures lead with a credibility noun or title before anything else — not an outcome clause first.
  • Keyword density is explicit: the field, the domain, the discipline, stated plainly.
  • Named assets beat abstract adjectives — Effron names his actual books instead of describing himself as "results-driven" or similar.

Honest gap assessment

The earlier draft headline had no role or credibility noun and no searchable keyword beyond "AI" and "HR." It also failed the mobile-crop test — it got cut off mid-clause at roughly 70 characters, the point where most readers stop seeing it.

Final headline candidate — WHO / WHAT / FOR WHOM

AI + HR Consultant | I help leaders fix what's actually stuck
  • WHO: AI + HR Consultant — the role/credibility noun every benchmark figure above leads with.
  • WHAT: fix what's actually stuck — the real diagnostic value, stated plainly, not as a metaphor.
  • FOR WHOM: leaders.
  • Fits entirely inside the roughly 70-character mobile crop as one complete sentence.
