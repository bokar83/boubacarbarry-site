Go-to-market: where the customers actually come from

Visibility, lead pool and conversion, read against live numbers · 2026-09-05

TL;DR

You do not have a visibility problem. You have a conversations problem, and every number I read today says the leak is at the bottom of the funnel, not the top.

Across every channel you own, measured live today: 68,346 paid impressions, 52 LinkedIn posts, 80 X posts, 63 YouTube videos, 331 cold emails and 16 newsletters have produced one paid workshop seat, zero newsletter clicks, and zero positive cold email replies. Not few. Zero.

Adding traffic to that does nothing. So the plan is three moves, in this order:

  1. Convert the one motion already running. You comment on other people’s LinkedIn posts every day. You have sent 8 connection requests ever. Every comment gets a connection request the same day. This is a behaviour, not a build, and it starts Monday at zero cost.
  2. Put your workshop in other people’s rooms. Utah SHRM, chambers, associations, accelerators. They already have your buyers on a list and a calendar to fill. This has been sitting undone in a memory file since 2026-05-06.
  3. Fix legibility before volume. 46 people get your newsletter, six open it, and none of them has ever clicked a link. The links are there. I checked. That is an offer problem and it gates everything else.

I am explicitly not proposing: a comment generator, a cold email restart, a new lead magnet, more Reddit work, or any new capture surface. Reasons in section 7.

The diagnosis: audience is the wrong variable

The instinct when every number is small is to ask how we get more eyeballs. I want to argue against that, because the evidence points somewhere else.

The leak is at the bottom, not the top

Six people open a newsletter. None clicks. If I add 460 subscribers I get 60 opens and still zero clicks. 195 people landed on the workshop page from paid traffic in August and none of them bought. 331 people got a cold email and none replied. Every conversion joint in this business currently reads zero. Pouring attention into the top of a funnel whose every joint reads zero is how you spend $835 to sell one seat, which is exactly what already happened.

The link check that decided this

Council flagged one thing as the cheapest question that could change the whole plan: do the newsletters actually contain a clickable link? If not, zero clicks is a trivial bug and growing the list is fine. So I pulled the last six issues from the Beehiiv API and counted.

The links are there. Between 2 and 16 per issue, including real offer links: humanatwork.ai/k/start-ai-24h, catalystworks.consulting/ai-checklist, catalystworks.consulting/w/nl. So zero clicks is not a bug. Six people read the whole thing, saw a link to something buyable, and did not touch it. Sixteen times.

You are not running a media business

A media business needs audience. A consulting practice needs a small number of specific named people who have a problem you can name back to them. To close three engagements you do not need five thousand readers. You need somewhere around thirty to fifty real conversations with the right people.

Look at what is actually built: 6,336 analytics rows, a scanner reading 6,500 Reddit posts, an 891-row lead table, a weekly idea miner. That is media-business infrastructure pointed at a consulting problem. It produces impressive counts and no revenue, and that is not a coincidence, it is the predictable output.

The binding constraint is conversations. Not names, not impressions, not subscribers. Every channel in the current setup is a numbers channel. Not one of them is a conversation channel.

Why the offer is invisible

Read your last six newsletter subject lines cold, as someone who does not know you: “Revenue is up. That is the problem.” “I put my name on the door.” “What did you build that nobody saw.” They are good essays. None of them tells a reader what they can buy, from whom, for how much, or what changes in their business if they do.

Your positioning is not the problem. The locked pitch is strong and it is right: you help companies put AI to work without the confusion and the overwhelm, and you have already crossed the bridge everyone else is still talking about. The problem is that the pitch lives in a memory file and never appears in the thing people actually read. Zero clicks is what a reader does when there is nothing legible to click toward.

Move 1: convert the motion already running

The single highest-return action available to you, and it is not a build.

You are already commenting on other people’s LinkedIn posts. 33 in four days, at least ten a day under the rule set on 2026-09-02, logged on boubacarbarry.com/review/linkedin-comments/. This is the only motion in your entire system that puts you in front of people who have never heard of you, and it costs nothing because someone else built the audience.

You have sent 8 connection requests. Ever. So every bit of attention that motion earns evaporates the moment the person scrolls past. Someone reads a sharp comment from you, maybe clicks your profile, and there is no next step.

The change

Every comment gets a connection request to the post author the same day. Not a pitch. A connection, with a one-line note referencing the actual thing you commented on. Ten comments a day becomes ten connection requests a day, which sits inside LinkedIn’s safe limits and compounds weekly.

That is it. No software. It attaches to work already happening and costs no new hours.

Measurement, and the honest limit

There is no database table for comment activity. The old one was archived on 2026-08-18 and the ledger is now the Archive section of an HTML page. Two watchdogs check freshness and repeats; neither records outcomes. So today we cannot tell you whether comments produce anything.

The cheap version, which I would do rather than build a table: count connection requests sent, accepted, and replies started, weekly, by hand, on the page that already exists. Four numbers. If after three weeks acceptances are climbing and conversations are not, comment quality is the problem. If neither moves, the motion is not working and should stop rather than be automated.

I am deliberately not proposing a comment generator. The staleness checker’s own source explains why better than I can: generation needs a logged-in LinkedIn read plus the voice battery on every single item, which a cron cannot do without shipping unvetted copy in your name. Ten battery runs a day is a real cost nobody has priced. The hand-built version already hits the required rate.

Move 2: put the workshop in other people’s rooms

The highest ceiling in this document, and it has been sitting undone since May.

Every channel in the current setup is you, publishing to strangers, from a standing start, with no borrowed credibility. That is the hardest possible way to acquire a client and you are running five versions of it at once.

You have a workshop. Two of them, with pages live, Stripe live, and zero registrations. The framing has been “how do we sell seats.” That framing hides the real asset: a workshop is something other organisations want and will provide the audience for.

Who has your buyers on a list already

  • Utah SHRM chapters, which have a calendar to fill and members who are exactly your buyer.
  • Chambers of commerce, which run standing education series. There is already a chamber_standing_series_2026_08_07 source in the lead table with 3 rows, so a thread exists.
  • Industry and trade associations with monthly member sessions.
  • Coworking spaces and small-business accelerators.
  • Payroll, benefits, insurance and bank partners who run client-education webinars and are constantly short of speakers.

Every one of them has a program chair whose actual problem is filling a calendar, no budget objection because you are free, and a list of members who already trust them. You run the identical session you have already built. They fill the room. You keep the relationship and the follow-up.

Compare the economics honestly. The kairos paid plan says a registration costs at least $40 and both seats are near break-even at best. A partner room costs a handful of emails and delivers a pre-warmed audience. This converts a seat-selling problem into a booking problem.

This was already found and never done

memory/project_traffic_strategy.md, dated 2026-05-06, Council-reviewed and approved at the time, lists as its top two next actions: apply to the Work Elevated HR Conference CFP, and contact Utah SHRM for a free webinar slot. Four months later neither has happened. That is the finding I would most want you to sit with. The highest-leverage move in your go-to-market has been written down, approved, and untouched since spring.

The approach, outlined not drafted

Per the standing rule that a lead source ships with its approach, here is the shape. The actual sends run through the voice battery first and none of this goes out until you say so.

  1. Target: the program chair or education chair by name, not an info@ address. Ten named humans, not a list.
  2. Angle: their members are being told daily that they have to use AI and they are overwhelmed. You give their members a breath and a clear read on whether they even need it, where it fits, and how. That is your locked pitch pointed at an audience owner instead of a buyer.
  3. The ask: one session for their members, free, at a date that suits their calendar. Never a pitch for consulting in the first message.
  4. Proof: you have run this room before. There are real photographs of the July 30 session at SLCC, which is credibility that costs nothing to include.
  5. Sequence: initial ask, one follow-up at seven days referencing their next open calendar slot, then stop. Three touches maximum.
  6. Success measure: rooms booked, not replies. One booked room beats twenty polite noes.

Move 3: legibility before volume

This is the move that gates the other two, because there is no point growing an audience that does not act.

Put a buyable thing in front of the ~90 people who already raised a hand

The genuine hand-raisers, once the 703 wrong-audience rows are stripped, number under about 90, and they overlap with the 46 active newsletter subscribers. These are the warmest people you have and almost none of them has had a real conversation with you. That is the conversation gap, in one number.

The next newsletter should not be an essay with a link buried in it. It should say what you do, who it is for, what it costs, and what to click. Once. The essays can resume after.

The welcome scheduler works. The forms feeding it do not tag anyone, so it has nothing to act on, and its day-since-subscribe cadence means nobody currently on the list can ever be reached by it anyway. Two separate fixes:

  • For the 46 already there: a one-time re-introduction send. This is a send, so it is yours to authorise. Section 8.

Validate the capture surfaces

39% of the list is invalid addresses. That is a form with no validation, and it is quietly poisoning your deliverability, which then makes every future send land worse. Cheap to fix, and it makes everything downstream cheaper.

Council: what it changed and where it split

Five voices, run before this was written, converging at about 92%. Two things they changed and one they disagreed on, kept here rather than smoothed over.

What Council changed

My first thesis was comments first: make the comment motion generated and measured. Council killed the sequence on two grounds and it was right on both.

  • The Contrarian: the plan promoted the one channel with no outcome data at all over channels whose data is bad but real. Absence of measurement was being read as promise. And 33 comments in four days already meets the rate, so automating it is a build placed ahead of a free behaviour.
  • The Contrarian again, and the Outsider independently: six opens and zero clicks is not a size problem. Growing a container that leaks everything is the error. The thesis put the leak at the top; the data puts it at the bottom.
  • First Principles: the variable being optimised was wrong. Not audience, conversations. It is the only reframe that explains zero clicks, zero replies and zero registrations with one diagnosis instead of five.
  • The Expansionist supplied move 2, which no other voice saw, and found that it had already been approved and left undone since May.
  • The Executor pointed out that the only genuinely seven-day step in the original plan was the connection request, which had been buried at number two and described as a project when it is a behaviour.

The blind spot all five shared

Nobody had checked whether the newsletters contain a clickable link. Zero clicks is either a devastating offer problem or a trivial bug, and the difference decides whether a third of this plan is worth anything. I ran that check before publishing. The links are there. It is the offer.

The divergence, not averaged away

The Expansionist wants partner rooms, which take weeks and have the highest ceiling. The Executor wants connection requests on Monday, which take hours and have a low ceiling. These genuinely compete for your attention, and doing both badly resolves nothing. The allocation that lets both survive: the connection request bolts onto work already happening and costs no new hours, so the new hours all go to partner outreach. That is why move 1 is free and move 2 is where the effort goes.
