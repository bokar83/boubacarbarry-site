Three tools you keep

Take-home handouts for "AI at Work Without Getting Burned" · Thu 6/25. Each one is portable: it works in Claude, and it moves to whatever tool you use next. That is the whole point. You own these, not the app.
Handout 1 · the hero
The Decision Council

Paste any real decision you are sitting on. Five advisors argue it from five different angles, then force a verdict. You make decisions every week and almost never pressure-test them. This does it in thirty seconds.

The five voices
  • The Contrarian:argues the other side. "Here is why this is wrong."
  • First Principles:strips it to what is actually true. "What are we really assuming?"
  • The Expansionist:thinks bigger. "What is the version of this ten times larger?"
  • The Outsider:no context, fresh eyes. "A normal person would say this makes no sense because..."
  • The Executor:cares only about shipping. "What is the first move Monday, and what kills it?"
Paste this into Claude to install it
You are my Decision Council. When I give you a decision, respond as five
separate advisors, each in one short paragraph:

1. THE CONTRARIAN: argue hard against the decision.
2. FIRST PRINCIPLES: list the assumptions I am making and which ones are shaky.
3. THE EXPANSIONIST: describe a bolder, bigger version of this.
4. THE OUTSIDER: react with zero context, like a smart stranger hearing it cold.
5. THE EXECUTOR: give the first concrete step and the single thing most likely to kill it.

Then finish with VERDICT: proceed, change, or kill, in one sentence, and why.
Be direct. No flattery. Do not agree with me by default.

My decision is: [paste your decision here]
Bonus mode: the premortem

Add one line: "Assume this failed badly six months from now. Walk it back and tell me why." You catch the failure before it costs you.

Install it for keeps (3 steps)
  1. In Claude, start a new Project and name it "Decision Council."
  2. Paste the instructions above into the Project's custom instructions.
  3. From now on, open that Project, drop a decision, get your council. It remembers the setup.
Built on real thinking you can look up: Gary Klein's premortem (imagine the failure first), Charlie Munger's inversion and mental models (argue the opposite, use many lenses), and red-team practice from decision science. I run a version of this on my own calls every week.
Handout 2 · the cleanup
The Simplicity Check

Paste anything you are building, planning, or overthinking. It finds the simplest version that still works and tells you what to cut. Owners drown in complexity they added themselves. This pulls you back out.

The four questions it asks
  • Is this too complicated? Name the parts that add weight but not value.
  • What is the simplest version that still works? Strip to the core.
  • Am I doing the basics? The boring fundamentals usually matter more than the clever part.
  • What can I remove? Done is when there is nothing left to take away, not nothing left to add.
Paste this into Claude to install it
You are my Simplicity Check. When I give you a plan, a process, or something
I am building, answer in four short parts:

1. WHERE IT IS OVERCOMPLICATED: name the parts adding weight, not value.
2. THE SIMPLEST VERSION THAT WORKS: describe the stripped-down core.
3. AM I SKIPPING THE BASICS: call out any boring fundamental I am missing.
4. CUT LIST: the specific things I can remove right now.

Bias hard toward simple. Favor the plain version over the clever one.
Be direct, no flattery.

Here it is: [paste what you are building here]
Install it for keeps (3 steps)
  1. Start a new Claude Project named "Simplicity Check."
  2. Paste the instructions above into its custom instructions.
  3. Any time something feels heavy, open it and paste the thing in. Sixty seconds.
Same roots in real thinking: Andrej Karpathy's push for simple, correct, readable over clever. Antoine de Saint-Exupery said it best: "perfection is reached not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away." Plus Occam's razor. I run this against my own work so I do one thing excellently instead of five things badly.
Handout 3 · the foundation
The Business Brain

Fill this in once. Paste it into Claude before you ask for anything, and every answer comes back about your business, in your voice. This is the "give it your context once, reuse it forever" principle, on one page.

Fill in the blanks
My business is __________.
I sell __________ to __________ (who they are).
The problem I solve for them is __________.
My prices / packages are __________.
My tone of voice is __________ (e.g. warm and plain, sharp and direct).
Things I say a lot / never say: __________.
The tasks I repeat every week are __________.
Right now my biggest goal is __________.
Use it for keeps (3 steps)
  1. Fill in every blank in your own words. Keep it to one page.
  2. Start a Claude Project named after your business and paste this into its custom instructions.
  3. Work inside that Project. It already knows your business, so you stop re-explaining yourself every time.
This is the base the other two tools sit on. Context once, reused forever. The same brain file moves to any tool you switch to later, which is exactly why you are never locked in.
