T1 — day 0

Subject (test 3): from the other side of your submittals  /  the client-side reason a search stalls  /  a note from the hiring-manager side
Hi [First Name], I spent over a decade in HR, including years inside GE, a Fortune 100 company. For most of it I was the person on the other end of a recruiter's submittal. The client side. The one who was slow to say yes. So I've watched a lot of good candidates stall for reasons that had nothing to do with the candidate. An intake that was never really aligned. A hiring manager sitting on a decision. An offer that gets reopened at the last minute. The search looks stuck on your end, but the drag is usually on mine. I work with a handful of recruiting firms on exactly that. Reading the client side the way I used to live it, so fewer of your searches stall where you can't see. I stay hands-on the whole way, no handoff and disappear. I'm not going to pitch you in a cold email. If any of that sounds familiar, reply and I'll share the one client-side pattern I saw stall the most searches. No call needed. Boubacar

T2 — day 3 (delivers the promised insight)

Subject: the pattern I mentioned
Hi [First Name], Following my last note. Here is the client-side pattern I saw stall the most searches, whether or not it's yours. The req gets opened before the hiring manager and their boss actually agree on what "yes" looks like. Your candidate then runs into a moving target. Feedback contradicts itself, the timeline slips, and a strong person walks. It reads like a sourcing problem. It's an alignment problem, upstream, on the client's side. When I was inside, the searches that closed fast were the ones where someone forced that alignment before the first interview. That's the kind of thing I help your firm get in front of, from the outside, in the client's language. If that's worth ten minutes some day this week or next, reply and say so. No deck, no pitch. Boubacar

Free, and yours to use whether or not we ever work together. I only do this with a few firms at a time because I stay hands-on. [Firm] is one I'd genuinely like to be one of them. That's the whole difference in how I work. I stay in it with you until the fix holds, then I get out of your way. If you ever want that kind of outside read on a stuck search, my inbox is open.
