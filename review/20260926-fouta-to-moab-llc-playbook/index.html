Utah rule, confirmed: the principal-office field only has to be a real street address where records can be said to be kept — a PO box is barred, but a virtual-mailbox address is not. The stricter rule is on the registered agent field, which by statute must be a location where the agent is actually present to accept legal service during business hours — that's why a scan-only mailbox vendor can serve as principal office but should not be your registered agent.

Why the bundle-with-your-RA recommendation over a dedicated vendor: you're already paying for Northwest's address as your registered agent; using it for the principal office too costs nothing extra at your mail volume (a brand-new solo consultancy), avoids a second login/vendor relationship to maintain, and the $20/mo scan upgrade is there if volume grows later. 0. Statute Verification Results (background)

Every Utah Code citation in this package was checked against the live statutory text at le.utah.gov. The load-bearing citation is Utah Code § 48-3a-304(2): failure to observe formalities is NOT, by itself, grounds for personal liability. Caveat: Utah's common-law alter-ego test (unity of interest + injustice) remains available regardless, and Section 304 never shields a member from the member's own professional negligence — that exposure is addressed only by the MSA (Appendix A) and E&O insurance.

Utah Code Title 48, Chapter 3a is being renumbered to Title 16, Chapter 20 effective 2026-10-01 (same act, substantive text carried forward). None of this package's citations (§§ 48-3a-102, -304, -401, -405, -408, -701, -703, -711) is the one section that showed "Repealed" rather than "Renumbered," so this is closed as a non-issue for this package, not an open action item.

1. Operating Agreement

OPERATING AGREEMENT OF FOUTA TO MOAB LLC

A Utah Single-Member, Member-Managed Limited Liability Company

This Operating Agreement (“Agreement”) of Fouta to Moab LLC (the “Company”) is made and entered into effective as of the Effective Date set forth in Section 12.4, by Boubacar Barry, an individual (the “Member”), being the sole member of the Company.

Article 1 — Formation, Purpose, and Principal Office

1.1 Formation. The Company was formed as a limited liability company under the Utah Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act, Utah Code Title 48, Chapter 3a, as amended from time to time (the “Act”), by the filing of a Certificate of Organization with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code. This Agreement constitutes the “operating agreement” of the Company within the meaning of Utah Code § 48-3a-102(16), and governs the relations among the Member, the Company, and (if applicable in the future) any additional members, managers, and the Company's business and affairs, to the fullest extent permitted by the Act. Where this Agreement is silent, the default provisions of the Act govern.

1.2 Name. The name of the Company is “Fouta to Moab LLC.” The Company may conduct business under one or more assumed or “doing business as” names, provided any such name is properly registered with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code and any other jurisdiction in which the Company conducts business under that name.

1.3 Purpose. The purpose of the Company is to engage in any and all lawful business activity for which limited liability companies may be organized under the Act, including without limitation:

(a) providing consulting services, including human-resources and artificial-intelligence-training consulting, AI-enabled marketing and business-operations consulting, and related advisory, coaching, and professional services to businesses and individuals;

(b) pursuing, bidding on, and performing government contracting and subcontracting work, including small-business set-aside contracts with federal, state, and local government agencies and their prime contractors; and

(c) engaging in any other lawful act or activity for which a limited liability company may be organized under the laws of the State of Utah, whether or not related to the foregoing, as the Member may from time to time determine.

This purpose clause is intentionally broad so that the Company may expand or shift the scope of its consulting and contracting activities without the need to amend this Agreement solely to reflect an ordinary change in the line of business pursued, provided the activity remains lawful.

1.4 Principal Office; Registered Agent. The Company's principal office is located at:

[PRINCIPAL OFFICE ADDRESS -- street address, city, Utah, ZIP]

The Company's registered agent and registered office in the State of Utah, as filed with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code, is:

[REGISTERED AGENT NAME], located at [REGISTERED AGENT ADDRESS, Utah]

(Select one and delete the other before execution: (i) the Member acting as the Company's own registered agent at the address above, or (ii) a commercial registered agent service. The Member may change the registered agent or registered office at any time by filing the appropriate change of registered agent/office with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code; no amendment of this Agreement is required to do so.)

Note before filing (public-record exposure)

In Utah, both the registered agent's street address and the principal office address are public on the Division's business-entity search. Do not use a home address here. A commercial registered agent service is the standard fix, priced roughly $99-$200/year depending on provider. For the principal office field, a paid mailing address keeps a real, checkable address on the public record without exposing a residence. Choose and confirm the address before the Certificate of Organization is filed.

1.5 Term. The Company shall have a perpetual existence unless and until dissolved in accordance with Article 8 of this Agreement or the Act.

Article 2 — Member; Capital Contribution; Capital Account

2.1 Sole Member. Boubacar Barry is the sole member of the Company (the “Member”), holding one hundred percent (100%) of the membership interests in the Company. No other person or entity holds, or has any right to acquire, any membership interest in the Company as of the Effective Date.

2.2 Initial Capital Contribution. The Member's initial capital contribution to the Company (the “Initial Capital Contribution”) shall consist of the cash and/or property described in the Member's written resolution confirming the Initial Capital Contribution (Resolution 6 of the Organizational Resolutions accompanying this Agreement), contributed promptly after the Company's bank account is opened. The amount and date of the Initial Capital Contribution, once made, shall be recorded in Resolution 6 and in the Company's books and records, with the deposit receipt or bank confirmation attached to that resolution as its supporting record.

2.3 Capital Account. The Company shall maintain a single capital account for the Member, credited with (i) the Member's Initial Capital Contribution and any additional capital contributions made by the Member from time to time, and (ii) the Member's allocated share of Company profits, and debited with (i) distributions made to the Member, and (ii) the Member's allocated share of Company losses. Because the Company has only one member and is treated as a disregarded entity for federal income tax purposes as provided in Article 4, the capital account is maintained for the Company's internal record-keeping and creditor-protection purposes and does not itself create or divide any interest among multiple members.

2.4 No Obligation to Make Additional Contributions. The Member is not required to make any additional capital contribution to the Company. The Member may, but is not obligated to, make additional contributions from time to time, which shall be credited to the Member's capital account.

2.5 No Interest on Capital; No Withdrawal Right. No interest shall accrue on any capital contribution, and the Member has no right to withdraw or demand the return of any capital contribution except as provided in this Agreement or upon dissolution and winding up under Article 8.

Article 3 — Allocations of Profits and Losses; Distributions

3.1 Allocation of Profits and Losses. All items of income, gain, loss, deduction, and credit of the Company shall be allocated one hundred percent (100%) to the Member for so long as the Member remains the sole member of the Company.

3.2 Distributions. The Member may cause the Company to make distributions of cash or other property to the Member at such times and in such amounts as the Member, in the Member's sole discretion, determines, subject to Section 3.3.

3.3 Limitation on Distributions. The Company shall not make a distribution to the Member if, after giving effect to the distribution, the Company would not be able to pay its debts as they become due in the ordinary course of business, or the Company's total assets would be less than the sum of its total liabilities, consistent with Utah Code § 48-3a-405. The Member shall exercise reasonable business judgment in determining whether a proposed distribution satisfies this Section.

3.4 Tax Distributions. To the extent the Company has sufficient cash and is not otherwise restricted under Section 3.3, the Member may cause the Company to distribute amounts sufficient to allow the Member to pay income taxes attributable to the Company's income, consistent with the Company's disregarded-entity tax treatment described in Article 4. This “distribution” is not a tax-favored category for a disregarded entity — all net Schedule C profit carries self-employment tax (15.3% up to the Social Security wage base, 2.9% above it, plus an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% above the applicable threshold) regardless of whether any of it is actually distributed out of the Company's account.

Article 4 — Tax Matters

4.1 Default Tax Classification. As a limited liability company with a single member, the Company is, and is intended to be, disregarded as an entity separate from the Member for federal income tax purposes under Treasury Regulation § 301.7701-3, and the Member shall report the Company's income, gains, losses, deductions, and credits directly on the Member's individual federal income tax return (currently, Schedule C of IRS Form 1040), and on the corresponding Utah state income tax filings. No election has been made, and none is currently intended, to treat the Company as an association taxable as a corporation or as an S corporation.

4.2 Authority to Elect Different Tax Treatment. Notwithstanding Section 4.1, the Member retains full authority, exercisable at any time by a written resolution of the Member (in the form used for the Organizational Resolutions in this package or a substantially similar form), to cause the Company to elect to be classified as an association taxable as a corporation under IRS Form 8832, and/or to elect S corporation status under IRS Form 2553, effective as of a date the Member selects, without any amendment to this Agreement being required, provided that the Member shall cause this Agreement to be reviewed and, if necessary, amended to remain consistent with the elected tax treatment (including, if S corporation status is elected, any requirements applicable to reasonable compensation, payroll, and distribution mechanics under that election). Form 2553's deadline, if an S election is ever made, is 2 months and 15 days into the tax year the election is to take effect (late-election relief exists but is paperwork-heavy). Once an S election is in effect, “reasonable compensation” through payroll becomes mandatory, and distributions with no salary are the single most common S-corp audit trigger.

4.3 Employer Identification Number; Tax Filings. The Company shall obtain and maintain its own federal Employer Identification Number (EIN) separate from the Member's Social Security Number for use in connection with its bank accounts, contracts, and filings, notwithstanding its disregarded-entity tax treatment. Every client engagement receives a Form W-9 in the Company's legal name using this EIN, never the Member's Social Security Number.

Article 5 — Management

5.1 Member-Managed. The Company is, and shall be, member-managed within the meaning of the Act. The Company shall NOT have “managers” as that term is used in the Act; all management authority is vested in the Member as set forth in this Article 5. This Agreement does not create, and shall not be construed to create, a manager-managed structure.

5.2 Authority of the Member. The Member has full, complete, and exclusive authority, power, and discretion to manage and control the business, affairs, and properties of the Company, to make all decisions regarding the business of the Company, and to perform any and all acts customary or incidental to managing the Company's business, including without limitation the authority to:

(a) open, maintain, and close bank, brokerage, and merchant-processing accounts in the Company's name;

(b) enter into, execute, deliver, amend, and terminate contracts, consulting agreements, government contracts and subcontracts, leases, and other agreements on behalf of the Company;

(c) hire, engage, compensate, and terminate employees, independent contractors, and professional service providers (including attorneys, accountants, and consultants) on behalf of the Company;

(d) borrow money and grant security interests in Company assets, and incur other obligations, on behalf of the Company;

(e) acquire, hold, manage, encumber, and dispose of Company assets, whether tangible or intangible, including intellectual property;

(f) bring and defend actions and proceedings, at law or in equity, in the name of and on behalf of the Company;

(g) execute and file all tax returns, elections, and other governmental filings on behalf of the Company, including the tax elections described in Section 4.2; and

(h) delegate any of the foregoing authority to officers, employees, or agents of the Company, provided that the Member remains ultimately responsible for the management of the Company.

5.3 No Requirement for Meetings. Because the Company has only one member, formal member meetings are not required to approve Company action. All actions requiring “member approval” under the Act or this Agreement may instead be evidenced by a written resolution signed by the Member, consistent with the record-keeping practices of Article 6.

5.4 Officers. Any officer so appointed acts as an agent of the Company and derives all authority from, and remains subject to the ultimate control of, the Member. No officer is appointed as of the Effective Date.

Article 6 — Records; Formalities; Separateness

6.1 Purpose of This Article. The Company is, and shall be operated as, a legal entity separate and distinct from the Member. This Article describes the practices the Company adopts to reflect that separateness in its ordinary operations. The practices in this Article are internal operating guidelines; they are not conditions of, and shall not be construed to limit, the Member's limited liability under Section 10.1 and the Act, and no third party is a beneficiary of this Article.

6.2 Separate Bank Accounts; No Commingling. The Company shall maintain one or more bank accounts in the Company's own legal name and its own EIN, separate and distinct from any bank account held by the Member in the Member's individual capacity. The Member shall NOT:

(a) deposit Company funds into the Member's personal bank account, or personal funds into the Company's bank account, except as a properly documented capital contribution (credited to the Member's capital account under Section 2.3) or a properly documented distribution (recorded under Section 3.2);

(b) pay personal expenses directly from Company funds, or Company expenses directly from personal funds, without such payment being characterized, contemporaneously and in the Company's books, as either a distribution to the Member, a capital contribution by the Member, or a bona fide loan on commercially reasonable terms evidenced in writing; or

(c) use Company funds or Company credit to satisfy the Member's personal, non-Company obligations.

All Company revenue shall be deposited into a Company bank account before any portion of it is used for any purpose, including a distribution to the Member. Section 6.8 below separately governs reimbursement of Member-paid business expenses.

6.3 Separate Books and Records. The Company shall maintain its own books of account, separate from the Member's personal financial records, reflecting the Company's income, expenses, assets, liabilities, and the capital account activity described in Section 2.3. The Company shall maintain, at its principal office or another location designated by the Member, at minimum: (a) this Agreement and any amendments; (b) the Certificate of Organization and any amendments filed with the State of Utah; (c) the Organizational Resolutions and any subsequent written resolutions of the Member; (d) the Membership Certificate; (e) federal and state tax returns and supporting records for at least the period required by applicable law; and (f) records of all capital contributions and distributions.

6.4 Minutes for Major Decisions. The Member intends to document, by a signed and dated written resolution maintained with the Company's records under Section 6.3, each of the following categories of decision (a non-exhaustive list of what constitutes a “major decision” for this purpose): (a) any capital contribution or distribution outside the ordinary course; (b) the opening or closing of any Company bank or brokerage account; (c) any borrowing, guaranty, or grant of a security interest by the Company; (d) any tax election described in Section 4.2; (e) the purchase, sale, or encumbrance of any material Company asset; (f) the admission of any additional member or any transfer of the membership interest described in Article 8; and (g) any amendment of this Agreement. A written resolution in the form of the Organizational Resolutions in this package, adapted to the subject matter, satisfies this practice. Contemporaneous documentation is the objective — a resolution should be signed at or near the time of the decision, not reconstructed later. The absence of a written resolution for any decision does not invalidate that decision, and a resolution may ratify a decision made earlier, provided the resolution states the date of the original decision.

6.5 Company Identification in Dealings With Third Parties. In all contracts, invoices, proposals, correspondence, and government-contracting submissions, the Member shall identify the counterparty as contracting with “Fouta to Moab LLC,” not with the Member individually, and shall sign in a representative capacity (e.g., “Boubacar Barry, Member, Fouta to Moab LLC”) rather than in an individual capacity, except where a form or statute requires an individual signature (e.g., a personal guaranty knowingly and separately given). Every invoice, proposal, and payment link names Fouta to Moab LLC as payee and shows only the Company's own bank/payment details; every client receives a Form W-9 in the Company's name with its own EIN.

6.6 Capital and Insurance. The Member intends that the Company maintain capital and insurance appropriate to its business, and will review the Company's capital position and insurance coverage at least annually and record the review in the annual compliance certification under Section 6.7.

6.7 Annual Compliance Certification. Within 30 days after the end of each calendar year, the Member shall sign a short dated certification recording: (a) that the Company's bank account statements were reconciled to its books for each month of the year; (b) any Company revenue received other than into a Company account, and how it was corrected; (c) the resolutions adopted during the year; (d) the insurance policies in force and their limits; and (e) confirmation that the Company's annual renewal was filed with the Utah Division of Corporations. The certification is a record of practice, not a condition of the Member's limited liability.

6.8 Reimbursement of Member-Paid Expenses. The Company shall reimburse the Member for ordinary and necessary business expenses the Member pays personally, including the business-use portion of a home office, phone, internet, computer equipment, and vehicle, only upon the Member's submission of a written or electronic expense report within 60 days of the expense, stating the amount, date, business purpose, and (for mixed-use items) the business-use percentage and its basis. Reimbursements shall be paid from a Company account and recorded as Company expenses, not distributions. (For so long as the Company remains a disregarded entity reporting on Schedule C, this reimbursement has no separate tax effect of its own; it becomes a true tax-recognized accountable plan only once an S-corporation election is in effect.)

Article 8 — Succession on Death or Incapacity; Transfer Restrictions; Dissolution

8.1 No Automatic Dissolution on Death or Incapacity. The death, incapacity, or disability of the Member shall NOT cause the dissolution of the Company. The Company shall continue in existence, and this Agreement shall remain in full force and effect, notwithstanding the Member's death or incapacity, consistent with Utah Code § 48-3a-701. See also Section 8.8 below, which addresses the separate 90-day no-member dissolution trigger that this Section 8.1 alone does not cover.

8.2 Succession of the Membership Interest on Death. Upon the Member's death, the Member's entire membership interest in the Company (100% of all economic and governance rights) shall pass to the Member's estate, and thereafter to the beneficiary or beneficiaries designated to receive it under the Member's last will and testament, revocable trust, or other estate-planning instrument then in effect (the “Successor Member”). The personal representative of the Member's estate, or the trustee of the Member's revocable trust, as applicable, shall have the authority, pending final distribution of the membership interest to the Successor Member, to exercise all of the Member's management and economic rights under this Agreement as if that fiduciary were the Member, including the authority to operate the Company's bank accounts, sign on the Company's behalf, and take any action described in Article 5, solely for the purpose of preserving the Company's business, assets, and government-contracting relationships until the membership interest is fully transferred to the Successor Member. Upon distribution of the membership interest, the Successor Member (or, if more than one person, the Successor Members) shall become a member (or members) of the Company with all rights and obligations of the Member under this Agreement, effective upon that distribution; each Successor Member shall sign a joinder to this Agreement acknowledging its terms, but failure to sign a joinder does not delay or defeat that admission. If the membership interest passes to more than one person, the personal representative shall, before distribution, notify the recipients in writing that the Company will be treated as a partnership for federal income tax purposes from the date it has more than one member, unless a different election is made.

8.3 Succession on Incapacity. If the Member becomes incapacitated (as determined by a licensed physician's written certification, or by the terms of the Member's durable power of attorney or revocable trust, whichever the Member's estate-planning documents specify), the person named as the Member's agent under a durable power of attorney, or as successor trustee under the Member's revocable trust, whichever applies, shall have the authority to exercise the Member's management rights under Article 5 on the Member's behalf for so long as the incapacity continues, and shall return full management authority to the Member upon the Member regaining capacity, as determined in the same manner. If no durable power of attorney or revocable trust is in effect, the Special Representative designated under Section 8.7 shall exercise the Member's management rights under Article 5 during the incapacity, limited to preserving the Company's business and assets. To the fullest extent permitted by the Act, neither the Member's incapacity, nor the appointment of a guardian or conservator for the Member, nor the Member becoming a debtor in bankruptcy, dissociates the Member from the Company. (Signing an actual durable financial power of attorney naming the same person as the Special Representative is the real solution here; this Section is the fallback if that has not yet been done.)

8.4 Transfer Restrictions. Because the Company has only one member, no transfer of any part of the membership interest may be made during the Member's lifetime except: (a) to a revocable trust of which the Member is the grantor and initial trustee, for estate-planning purposes, without changing the beneficial ownership of the interest; (b) as otherwise expressly authorized by a written resolution of the Member under Section 6.4(f); or (c) pursuant to Sections 8.2 and 8.8 upon the Member's death or the events described in Section 8.8. Any Membership Certificate issued by the Company shall bear the restrictive legend set forth on the Membership Certificate below.

8.5 Dissolution — Events. The Company shall be dissolved, and its affairs wound up, only upon the occurrence of any of the following: (a) the written consent of the Member (or, following succession under Section 8.2, the Successor Member) to dissolve the Company; (b) the entry of a decree of judicial dissolution under Utah Code § 48-3a-701; (c) the occurrence of any other event requiring dissolution under the Act that cannot be avoided by the continuation provisions of this Article; provided that neither the death nor incapacity of the Member is such an event, as stated in Section 8.1.

8.6 Winding Up. Upon dissolution, the Member (or, if applicable, the Successor Member or a person designated by written resolution) shall act as the person winding up the Company's activities and affairs under Utah Code § 48-3a-703, and shall: (a) deliver to the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code, for filing, a Statement of Dissolution stating the Company's name and that the Company is dissolved; (b) collect the Company's assets; (c) discharge or make reasonable provision for the Company's known liabilities, including any contingent or unliquidated claims, applying the Company's assets first to its creditors (including any Member who is a creditor) and then to any surplus in the order of priority required by Utah Code § 48-3a-711; (d) give notice to known claimants, and publish notice of dissolution to the extent permitted by the Act, so as to bar claims not presented within the applicable statutory periods; and (e) once winding up is complete, deliver to the Division, for filing, a Statement of Termination stating the Company's name and that the Company is terminated. The Company's existence continues after dissolution solely for the purpose of winding up, until the Statement of Termination is filed and effective.

Upon the Member's death, certified incapacity, or the Member becoming a debtor in bankruptcy, the Special Representative shall immediately and automatically, without any waiting period and without court appointment, have authority to exercise the Member's management rights under Article 5 solely to preserve the Company's business, assets, accounts, and contracts, to wind down or continue engagements in progress, and to pay the Company's obligations, until a personal representative, trustee, agent under a durable power of attorney, or conservator is in place and acting, at which point the Special Representative shall account to that fiduciary and cease acting. It is the Member's intent that the Company continue notwithstanding the Member's death, certified incapacity, or the Member becoming a debtor in bankruptcy. Upon any such event, the Member's personal representative (or, until one has been appointed, the Special Representative under Section 8.7, acting on behalf of the Member's estate) is automatically admitted as a member of the Company, effective immediately upon that event, without any further act, consent, or waiting period, and the Company shall continue without dissolution. This Section is a backstop; nothing in it relieves the Member of the responsibility to maintain an actual will naming a personal representative and a durable financial power of attorney, which remain the primary protection against any gap this Section is designed to cover.

Item 0 — before the first client engagement, not merely “at formation”: bind professional liability (E&O) insurance covering consulting, HR advisory, AI-enabled services, and sold digital products, with the retroactive date set to the LLC's first engagement. Add general liability and cyber/data coverage at the same application if the E&O carrier bundles them. Sign in this order:

  1. Operating Agreement — sign and date first; this governs everything else.
  2. Resolutions 1-5, 7, and 8 — sign second, before going to the bank (Resolution 5 authorizes opening the account; a bank typically wants Resolutions 1, 3, 4, and 5 in hand to open it, so these must exist first, not after).
  3. Resolution 6 — sign separately and later, once the actual capital-contribution dollar amount and transfer date are known, filled in, and matched to Operating Agreement Section 2.2. Attach the deposit receipt.
  4. Membership Certificate — sign fourth, once 1-3 are complete; keep it with the Company's permanent records (it does not need to be given to anyone else since there is only one member).

Registered agent and principal office address — decide before filing, not after: never use the Member's home address for either field (both are public on the Utah Division of Corporations business-entity search once filed). Recommendation: Northwest Registered Agent ($125/yr flat, no renewal bump) as registered agent, and use its own address for the principal office too — free limited mail forwarding (5 pieces/yr) is already included with the RA plan, with a $20/mo upgrade to full scan-and-store if volume grows, so no second vendor is needed at this stage. Runner-up if a distinct, dedicated mailbox is wanted from day one: iPostal1 ($14.99/mo, largest real-street-address network of the vendors compared). Full vendor comparison (Harbor Compliance, Registered Agents Inc as registered-agent alternatives; iPostal1, Anytime Mailbox, PostScan Mail, Alliance Virtual Offices as dedicated mailbox alternatives, with cost/network-size/notes for each) lives in the interactive checklist's “Registered agent & principal office address” decision at the top of this page, and in memory/reference_fouta_to_moab_llc_formation_documents_v3_2026_09_26.md. Utah rule, confirmed: the principal-office field only needs a real street address (no PO box) where records can be said to be kept; the stricter physical-presence-during-business-hours rule applies to the registered-agent field only, which is why a scan-only mailbox vendor works for principal office but not as registered agent.

Business bank account — recommendation: Bluevine (Standard plan) — $0 monthly fee, $0 minimum balance, free standard ACH (only same-day ACH at $10 and outbound wire at $15 carry a fee), cashback debit (up to 4%) + checking APY (up to ~1.3-3%) from day one with no revenue gate, and a longer single-bank-partner track record (Coastal Community Bank since 2013). Same-day pivot: Mercury ($0 fees, cleanest ACH/wire mechanics of the five compared) — but its one standout perk, the IO card, needs a ~$25k balance to qualify, unrealistic on day one, and it is a fintech on a partner bank that has changed before (2024), a real consideration for a government vendor needing a stable routing number over a multi-year contract. Full 5-way comparison (Mercury, Bluevine, Novo, Relay, Chase Business Complete on fee/minimum-balance/ACH/wire/limitation) lives in the interactive checklist's “Business bank account” decision at the top of this page, and in the reference doc named above. Federal/SAM.gov payments run on standard ACH via Treasury's EFT rail, which every option compared supports — the real risk across fintech options is routing-number stability over time, not ACH compatibility.

Before signing the first client engagement: every engagement is signed under the Master Services Agreement + Statement of Work (Appendix A), and every sold digital product is sold subject to the Product Terms of Sale (Appendix B). Neither the Operating Agreement nor the Membership Certificate can protect against a client claim — only a signed client contract and insurance can.

Client-payment discipline: every invoice, proposal, and payment link names Fouta to Moab LLC as payee and shows only the Company's own bank/payment details. Every client receives a Form W-9 in the Company's name with its own EIN. If a client ever pays the Member personally in error, move the full amount to the Company account within 5 business days and record it as Company revenue received in error, never as a capital contribution.

Capital-contribution consistency: the dollar figure in Operating Agreement Section 2.2 (by reference) and the dollar figure in Resolution 6 must be the same number, taken from the actual bank transfer, with the deposit receipt attached to Resolution 6. Even a modest amount is fine; what matters is that it happened and is recorded consistently in both places.

Compliance/reconciliation habit: keep a running one-line monthly bank-reconciliation log (date reconciled, any exceptions found) so the annual certification is a five-minute summary of twelve entries, not a from-scratch review.

Estate-planning fallback: sign a durable financial power of attorney (Utah statutory form) naming the same person designated as Special Representative in Section 8.7. This is the real fix for the succession gap; Sections 8.7/8.8 are the automatic fallback if it is not yet in place, not a substitute for it. A will or revocable trust naming a Successor Member is the corresponding real fix on the ownership side.

Utah-specific recurring items:

  • Annual renewal with the Utah Division of Corporations — calendar a reminder one month before the anniversary of formation; a lapsed renewal leads to delinquency and eventually an administrative dissolution proceeding.
  • Form W-9 in the Company's legal name with its own EIN, given to every client.
  • Local city/county business license where the Company's activity or location requires one.
  • Quarterly estimated tax payments covering both income tax and self-employment tax on Schedule C profit.
  • S-corp election deadline, if an S election is ever considered: Form 2553 must be filed within 2 months and 15 days of the start of the tax year the election is to take effect.
  • Sales tax on sold products (not pure consulting services): a sold digital product, template kit, or packaged good may be taxable under current Utah Tax Commission guidance even though consulting services generally are not — check current guidance before the first product sale.
  • Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI/Corporate Transparency Act) filing status: the most recent check found BOI reporting is not required per FinCEN's final rule. Reconfirm this against current FinCEN guidance at the time of actually filing, and record the result in the first annual compliance certification.
  • E&O “tail” coverage at winding-up, not only at formation: when the Company is dissolved, evaluate whether to purchase E&O tail coverage extending the claims-reporting window beyond the policy's cancellation date. Decide this at the time of winding up, not as an afterthought.

The three habits that keep the liability shield real, not paper:

  • Separate account, always. Every dollar of Company revenue lands in the Company's own bank account first — never a personal account — and every Company expense pays out of that same account.
  • No commingling, ever. Personal and Company money move between each other only as a documented contribution, a documented distribution, or a documented reimbursement under Operating Agreement § 6.8 — never as a quiet transfer with no paper trail.
  • Minutes for major decisions. Every time something on the Article 6.4 list happens, write and sign a short dated resolution the same week it happens — not months later from memory. Missing one does not, by itself, break the shield, but the habit is still what a court and an insurer actually look for.

Outstanding attorney-needed items
  1. Enforceability of the MSA's limitation-of-liability and arbitration clauses against a consumer (an individual coaching or product buyer) rather than a business. A judgment call on current Utah consumer-protection and unconscionability law.
  2. Whether a non-court-appointed Special Representative (Operating Agreement § 8.7) can validly give the continuation consent within the 90-day window (§ 8.8) on behalf of an estate that has no personal representative yet. Utah Code § 48-3a-701(3)'s cure requires consent from the “transferees” holding a majority of distribution rights — practically, the estate acting through its personal representative — and a Special Representative appointed only by contract is neither a transferee nor formally that representative's legal stand-in. The drafting here is the strongest self-executing version achievable by contract alone; whether a Utah court would honor it over an heir's objection, absent the actual will and durable power of attorney being in place, is a statutory-interpretation question this pass did not and could not resolve.
  3. Government contracts. Federal and state contracts are governed by their own terms (FAR clauses and agency terms), which usually override a contractor's own limitation of liability and warranty terms. The MSA does not apply to them; the specific solicitation's liability, insurance, and indemnity terms need review before the first government bid.

Separately, and not legal: an actual estate plan (a will naming a guardian and a personal representative, plus a durable financial power of attorney) is the real fix for the succession gap Sections 8.3, 8.7, and 8.8 above only fall back to. Those sections are the safety net if the estate plan is not yet done; they are not a substitute for doing it.
