What research-02 already found (not duplicating it)

Posted to the agent bus 2026-09-22, subject "Anti-AI-slop design findings + WOW-lock gap." The finding, stated plainly: every anti-slop mechanism we own fires AFTER generation and only says what NOT to do — our design-audit ban list, the no-blue-on-dark checker, the em-dash ban. A ban list produces the absence of slop, never the presence of taste. The fix that research found (and that two independent absorb sessions converged on independently) is a positive, specific, per-build style commitment written before generation, not another rule added to the pile.

This page applies that finding to exactly one case — instead of banning "teal-on-navy," it commits, up front, to a specific alternative palette that is grounded in something real and personal rather than picked by aesthetic instinct alone. That is the whole method below.

Where this is grounded

Your own photos (LinkedIn, headshots used across the site) show a kente-pattern stole worn over a formal suit — a specific, personal visual signature tied to your Guinean/American background, and it currently shows up nowhere in these graphics' color or pattern choices. Two things pulled from real, sourced references, not invented:

  • Traditional kente color associations (sourced: The African Gourmet, Cultureville, Sankofa Edition — cross-checked, not a single source): gold reads as royalty, wealth, high status, and spiritual purity; black reads as maturity and ancestral depth; green reads as growth and renewal; red is the heaviest color historically, carrying associations with sacrifice and conflict, so it is used here sparingly and desaturated rather than as a bright accent.
  • The structural fact of how kente is made: narrow strips, woven separately, then sewn together into the final cloth. That structural idea — parallel strips joined at a seam — is what the border treatment below borrows, as a plain geometric motif, never as a reproduction of an actual named cloth pattern.

What I deliberately did not do: name or use a specific Adinkra symbol. Adinkra symbols carry real, specific meanings tied to specific communities, and I could not verify a specific one well enough to use it correctly and respectfully. If you want a named symbol in future work, that's a real answer to go get, not something to guess at.

Three directions

All three keep the same content and diagram logic already approved in the v2 review — only the visual treatment changes.

Built below

1. Charcoal & Gold

#161311 warm charcoal (not navy) #C9A227 gold (royalty / wealth) #7A2437 desaturated burgundy #3F6B4F muted forest green #F0E9DD warm off-white text

A warm near-black ground instead of the tech-navy every dark AI page uses, one gold accent doing most of the work, burgundy and forest green as quiet seconds. A thin strip-and-seam border along each diagram's edge stands in for the neon glow — it reads as premium and personal instead of "AI SaaS landing page." Editorial, not glowing.

Not built

2. Ink & Ochre (more restrained)

#100E0C near-black ink #B8863B ochre #E7DFCF bone text

Two colors total, almost no accent color at all — the most "printed report, not a tech demo" of the three. No strip-border, no secondary hue. If Charcoal & Gold still reads as "trying," this is the fallback: it can't look like a template because it barely looks decorated.

Not built

3. Deep Emerald & Bronze

#122019 deep forest ground #B87333 bronze #EFE7D8 cream text

Leans on green (growth/renewal) as the ground color rather than an accent, bronze doing what gold does in option 1. Closer in spirit to a heritage/legacy brand than a personal-tech one — worth testing if "AI and HR" content wants to feel less like software and more like an institution.

Why Charcoal & Gold got built first: gold is the strongest, most unambiguous kente association (royalty/wealth is the dominant reading across every source checked), it reads as premium in general design language independent of the kente reference (see the "luxury color palette" research below), and warm charcoal is the biggest single departure from the navy-plus-neon template without losing "dark and serious."
