The spine: one hour, two goals

Prior planning treated the consultancy and the job search as two portfolios competing for the same hours. That framing is what made the plan expensive. Fractional and interim HR leadership collapses the competition: the same conversation, with the same person, on the same day, is a paid engagement, bridge income, and an audition.

The one move

Sell fractional and interim HR leadership to companies that also hire director-level HR seats. Not two lists. One list, worked once, that pays out either way. A company with a vacant HR leadership seat and no interim cover has a problem today and a hiring process that will take four months. Offering to cover the gap is the most natural sales conversation available to him, and it is the only one where the founder title helps instead of hurting.

Why this is the spine and not just another channelEvery other activity is judged by whether it feeds a fractional or interim conversation. Content that does not, gets cut. Applications that do not, get capped. Search firms get worked because the same firms staff interim seats and permanent ones, so one relationship serves both tracks at no extra hour.

The collapse condition

Stated as a rule, not a guessThe bridge track pauses when contracted fractional and interim revenue covers the monthly income floor, sustained two consecutive months, on contracts with ninety or more days of remaining term. All three conditions, not any one of them. Coverage alone is not enough: a single ninety-day engagement ending in November leaves him running the same search in December from a colder start. The income floor is his number to set and it is the first open question below. Until he sets it the collapse condition cannot fire, which is exactly why setting it is action one.

The inverse holds too. If the bridge lands first, the consultancy does not shut down, it goes to maintenance: the short list stays warm, the content cadence continues, no new client load is taken. That is a true answer to the interview question about what happens to his clients, and it stays true whether or not anyone asks it.

Pricing the spine

Priced on outcome, never on hours. The engagements this plan targets have a dollar outcome the buyer can name: a vacant seat stalling a reorganization, a hiring process running long, a reporting workflow eating leadership days. The method already in use floors the fee at ten percent of the twelve-month outcome value and ceilings it at one percent, with a premium on must-have outcomes because the cost of not getting it is what makes them must-have.

Never quote a rate before the outcome is namedIf the buyer cannot state the dollar value of the problem, the next move is a paid diagnostic that produces that number, not a rate card. A rate quoted against an unnamed outcome is an hours conversation wearing a value conversation's clothes, and it always lands low.

Narrative pack

One public story, true on its face, that survives an offer-stage cross-examination. The ranking of the two goals appears nowhere in this section and nowhere below it. Not because it is shameful, but because it is not the reader's business, and saying it out loud would make a true story sound like a hedge.

The positioning line

One line a stranger can repeat to a third person

Boubacar runs HR operations and the systems underneath them, and he builds the AI that takes the manual work out.

Test applied: can someone who met him once say this to a colleague without checking notes. Sixteen words, two concrete nouns, no adjectives to forget.

His own first-person version, for a bio line or the top of a conversation:

I spent fifteen years in HR, ten of them inside GE while it split itself into three public companies. Organizational design, restructuring, and the operating model underneath it. Now I do that work independently, and I build the AI systems that take the manual load out of it.

Every clause traces to the approved resume. No operations claim, no executive claim, no AI enablement program claim, no rounded headcount, no country count attached to the whole tenure.

The three questions, answered true

1. Are you still running the consultancy?

Yes. Catalyst Works is mine and I am not going to pretend otherwise in an interview.

What I would want you to know is what it actually is. Since I left GE I have been doing this work independently and building the systems I would have wanted inside a company. I have deliberately not taken on a wide client load, because the version of this that is spread across six small engagements teaches you nothing and leaves you available to nobody. So there is no competing book of business here. If we get to an offer, I will tell you exactly what my commitments are before you have to ask.

True as written. Does not claim clients he does not have, and does not volunteer a zero either. The middle sentence turns the real situation into the answer to the question underneath the question, which is whether his attention is already spoken for.

2. What happens to your clients if you take this?

Anything I am in gets finished or handed off cleanly. That is not a courtesy, it is the only reason anyone hires an independent twice.

Practically, I scope in weeks rather than years, so there is no long tail to unwind. Rather than reassure you, I would give you the actual list and the actual end dates.

Answers the real fear, which is divided attention, not ethics. Offers a list instead of a promise, which is the only form of this answer a skeptical hiring manager accepts. Stays true whether the list has three items or none.

3. Why leave self-employment?

I am not leaving the work. I am choosing depth over breadth.

Working independently gave me something I could not have gotten any other way. I had to build the whole thing myself, so I now know where automation earns its keep, where it creates risk nobody is watching for, and where a person still has to make the call. What it cannot give me is one organization for long enough to see a transformation land end to end. I did that at GE and it is the work I am best at.

Reframes the question from what is wrong with self-employment to what is right about the role. The middle clause is verbatim from his approved resume summary, so it is already voice-checked and already true.

Outreach assets: two sequences, never blended

Two different products sold to two different buyers. The failure mode is one email trying to be both. A consultancy email that hints at availability reads as a candidate pretending; an employer email that pitches a service reads as a vendor. The same person can receive both, weeks apart, in either order, as long as neither one is hedged.

Sequence A: consultancy track

The offer, as a formula

I help mid-market people functions that have lost an HR leader keep the transformation moving while the seat is open, in ninety days, without hiring a firm or waiting out a four-month search. The mechanism is that I have done the operating-model work from the inside, so I cover the seat and rebuild the process at the same time instead of holding it steady.

ICP: one persona, one pain, one trigger

  • Persona. The executive one level above a vacant HR leadership seat at a US company of roughly two hundred to two thousand employees. Usually a chief operating officer, a chief financial officer, or a general manager. Not the HR team, they cannot fund it.
  • Pain, in their words. The reorganization stalled when she left and I am the one running one-on-ones now.
  • Trigger. An HR leadership departure, or a posted HR leadership role open more than thirty days. Public and checkable, which is what makes this list buildable cold by anyone with no warm network.

Sequence B: employer track

What was wrong with the draft this replacesThe supplied concept promised the reader a few concrete observations about their organization and then delivered none. That is the worst thing a cold message to an executive can do, because the promise is the only reason they kept reading and the miss is the moment they stop. The rule now: either the observation is in the email, specific enough that it could not have been written to anyone else, or the promise is cut and the email is shorter. There is no third option.

What will NOT be done

Stated explicitly, because a plan that does not name its exclusions has not made any choices.

  • No mass applications and no one-click apply. Applications only where the company is already on the target list, or a human message went out the same day. Portal response rates for that style of application are reported at one to four percent across multiple 2026 analyses, and that figure is not seniority-segmented, so it is likely worse at director level rather than better.
  • No new website, landing page, lead magnet, or funnel. Nothing here requires a build. Everything is an email, a message, or a conversation. A build is the most comfortable way to avoid selling.
  • No neutralizing or hiding Catalyst Works. Not on the resume, not on LinkedIn, not in conversation. Hiding it makes the honest answers in the narrative pack unusable.
  • No blending the two sequences. Not one hybrid email, not once, not even for a contact who might be either.
  • No open-to-work banner and no availability post.
  • No paid ads for either track. Neither offer has converted once. Paid spend on an unconverted offer just accelerates a broken funnel.
  • No relocation commitment, no geography narrowing, and no comp anchor until a real conversation with a real employer is underway. Answering those in the abstract only shrinks the list.
  • No workshops, content products, or audience building treated as the objective. They are means. The north star is a fractional engagement.
  • No new tools and no new subscriptions. Every channel above runs on email, LinkedIn, and the send engine already wired.
  • No new tracker or dashboard. The Money Map is the board. If a number matters it goes there.

The unresolved council split from the brief, closed

The prior council split on whether the founder title disqualifies him as an employee candidate or is his strongest asset. The spine partially resolved it: he keeps the consultancy. The remainder was the public narrative for an employer who will correctly perceive an active practice. That closes as follows.

The founder title is a liability only when it arrives attached to an application, and an asset whenever it arrives attached to an observation. An application makes the reader ask whether he is available. An observation makes the reader ask whether he is right. That is why the no-posting variant of the employer email is the better one, why the application is always sent the day after the human message rather than before it, and why the resume line names the work rather than the entity. Nothing is hidden and nothing is neutralized. The title is simply never the first thing in the room.

Open questions

Four things this plan could not resolve from the repository and did not guess. Two are decisions only he can make, and the first one blocks the plan's ending condition.

1. The monthly income floor the bridge role has to cover

Not on file anywhere. Every money target in the repository is a business goal rather than a survival number: the twelve-week target of three thousand dollars collected, the client-side pricing ladders, the tool subscriptions. Nothing states what he personally needs per month.

Why it blocks: tripwire 9, the collapse condition, compares contracted revenue to that number. Without it the bridge track has no stopping rule, which means it runs forever by default and the two tracks never collapse into one. It also sets the floor under any interim day rate, because a rate that does not clear the floor at a realistic utilization is not a bridge, it is a hobby.

What is needed: one number, net, monthly. Ten minutes. He does not have to share it with anyone or put it in a file that syncs.

2. Remote-only, Utah-compatible, or open to relocation

Nothing concrete on file. The identity and profile records place him in Salt Lake City and confirm he has lived and worked in Dubai, Guinea, and five US states. The only remote-related datum found is descriptive of a target role rather than of a preference: a cover letter on file is addressed to a role listed as Remote US. That says he will apply to a remote role. It does not say he requires one, refuses onsite, or would move.

Why it matters more than any other input: the eighteen-company table is entirely Utah. That is only correct if he is Utah-anchored. If he is remote-open, the same rubric run nationally produces a list several times longer with materially better matches. If he is relocation-open, it opens the two coastal HR-technology clusters where nearly every firm on the search-firm list actually places. This single answer changes the target list more than the offer, the copy, or the cadence.

3. Does the plan compete with the direct offer lane launched the same day

A separate lane began its own outreach on 2026-08-19 with a ramp to three plus one contacts a day by the week of 09-08, and its own dated kill condition of 2026-10-17. This plan prescribes eight to ten target-company approaches a week plus two search-firm approaches plus five warm touches. Those are the same hours and possibly the same people.

This is not resolved here because resolving it unilaterally would be the exact mistake of one lane assigning work another lane already owns. The question for him: are these one motion with two lists, or two motions competing for one calendar. The recommendation, if he wants one, is that they are one motion, this plan supplies the targeting and the tripwires, and the other lane supplies the daily count, because two ask counters is how both get gamed.

Workday Rising runs October 12 to 15 and the HR Technology Conference runs October 20 to 22, both in Las Vegas, five days apart. One trip, two rooms, and one of them is the densest concentration of the channel this plan rates most likely to produce contract work. No budget is assumed and no decision is made here.
