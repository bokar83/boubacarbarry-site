About

First line is 52 characters, inside the forty-to-eighty band.

Scheduling. Chasing an interview panel for feedback. Writing the summary a manager waits a week for. Keeping the same three forms in sync. Still done by hand, in most places, in 2026.

I am not going to pretend I know why yours are still manual. Half the time it is the vendor charging extra for the module. Sometimes it is a data rule, a works council, or an integration nobody wants to own. Those constraints decide what can actually be automated, and they are the first thing I ask about.

When AI does go into hiring, it usually lands on screening. I would put it somewhere else. Screening is the one step where a wrong call is invisible, because a machine that rejects the right person does not send anyone a note about it.

So here is the work. I take one HR process, not the whole function, and go through it with you step by step. I tell you which steps a machine should never touch and which ones it should already be running. Then I build the second group inside what you already own. I have been on the inside of a Workday rollout, so I know the difference between what a system can do and what it will let you do.

I can do that because of the order I learned it in. Long before any of this existed I worked on hiring processes inside a large company, at a size where nobody could fix anything by asking people to try harder. On one of them, a cycle that took about sixty days came down to under thirty, and none of that was AI. It was the same question I ask now: which steps were doing real work, and which ones were there out of habit.

Ten years in global HR leadership for a Fortune 100 company, most recently four years in Dubai. So the HR half of this is not theory for me.

The machine half is not theory either. I build and run the AI systems my own company runs on. Not a demo. The thing I use every day. It broke on me in ways I would not wish on anyone, and I fixed it. I taught myself enough code to understand the machine, not to become an engineer, so I can bend it to what a team needs.

Most people in this conversation sit on one side of a line. They decide, or they build. I do both, and that is the only reason I can tell you to take AI off a step instead of selling you more of it.

Message me the one step in your hiring or HR work where AI was supposed to help and did not. I will tell you straight whether the tool is wrong or the step is.

2. Why this wording

How the no-trophy constraint is honoured

"I don't want to call out the end results as something miraculous that I did and hang my whole expertise upon that alone... that's not it."

Four mechanical things, each checkable rather than asserted:

  1. The result is not in the banner, not in the headline, and not in the first four paragraphs of the About. The capability claim occupies every one of those positions. The number appears once, in paragraph five, as a subordinate clause.
  2. The paragraph it sits in is about the question, not the achievement. It closes "It was the same question I ask now: which steps were doing real work, and which ones were there out of habit." The copy frames the result as evidence of a repeatable pattern, in the copy itself, not only in this document.
  3. and none of that was AI deliberately shrinks the result's apparent relevance to the offer. That clause costs the copy shine and buys what round 5 called load-bearing: the fact proves he fixes hiring processes, not that he likes AI.
  4. The verb is worked on, never led, ran or rebuilt. Required by the Claim Downgrade Gate and the canonical profile. It is also what stops the sentence reading as a trophy: a person who worked on a class of problem has a practice, a person who led one famous fix has an anecdote.

How the recognition test is met

"...see that first line of my About section, and be able to stop and say, 'Oh yeah, I know that pain. I know what he's talking about and I'm interested to see how he solves it,' or 'Yep I know that pain but it doesn't interest me.'"
  • The pain is four concrete nouns from the reader's own week. Scheduling, chasing an interview panel, the manager's summary, three forms out of sync. The third blind read's words for that list were "That is my actual week." No amount of internal review produced that. A stranger did.
  • Nothing is asserted about the reader's department. Four drafts failed here in four different ways and a stranger convicted each one. The copy now states where AI lands in this market, gives his own position on it (I would put it somewhere else), and explicitly refuses to guess why the reader's steps are manual.
  • It filters on purpose. A reader whose scheduling is already automated thinks "not us" and moves on. That is one of the two outcomes he asked for. The fourth read returned exactly that outcome, which is why it is reported here as a pass rather than dressed up as a win.
  • No parsing is required. No category label to decode, no compound value proposition to reconstruct before the reader knows whether this is about them.

The order change the third blind read forced, and why it does not re-open the decision

"I get to 'know that pain' only in paragraph three, when he finally says scheduling, chasing panel feedback, and manager summaries. That is my actual week... the thing I would actually pay for is buried below the fold."

So the hand-done work moved to the front of every surface and the screening argument moved to paragraph two. The door is unchanged. D-20260911-486748 settled that the pain he solves is AI-caused hiring failure, and that is still the whole argument of the About and still the second half of the headline's first clause. What changed is which half a stranger meets first, which is a copy question rather than a positioning one, and it is exactly the question this document was asked to settle.

It also strengthens the round-5 requirement rather than weakening it. The judgment claim is now in the crop as a juxtaposition rather than a claim taken on trust.

Gates that fired and changed the copy:

  • Claim Downgrade Gate. A draft read "I was fixing hiring processes before AI existed." Downgraded to "I worked on hiring processes."
  • Career/Credential Framing by Context. A draft cut the Fortune-100 line entirely. An About is a formal-background context, so the locked construction is mandatory there and was restored, demoted.
  • Relatable-beats-impressive. The demotion move, not a rewrite. The credential is real, it stays, it is not the opening bid.
  • Three-check pre-send formula, item 2. The constraints paragraph is diagnostic humility applied to profile copy, which is where three separate drafts had been failing.

Gates that ran clean: Fabricated Story (every first-person claim traces to the canonical profile or the elevator-pitch file), Manufactured Vulnerability ("It broke on me in ways I would not wish on anyone" is situational, not a character claim, and survives the everyone-swap test), AI-Assist Disclosure (AI runs his operations, nothing implies AI touches his words), Brand Spine and Connection Bridge ("They decide, or they build. I do both" is the bridge and nobody else in the niche can write it).

Hard constraints: zero em-dashes, first person throughout, sentence case clean, zero commercial-intent disavowals ("Not a demo" is a substance claim about the system, not a disclaimer about intent), no new unverified numbers. About sixty days / under thirty is the documented approved phrasing, and "at a size where nobody could fix anything by asking people to try harder" conveys scale deliberately without asserting a headcount.

4. Council, premortem mode

Question: it is March 2027. This exact wording produced no real inbound. Why?

The Contrarian, the chain from the moment it became unfixable

It died in week one and not for the reason anyone measured. A profile is not a post. A post is read by people who chose to read it; a profile is read by people checking who just commented under theirs. The draft opened "AI made your HR work harder" on every surface, so the reader met an accusation about their own operation before they had his name. They read it as a cold pitch and did not click. Profile views stayed flat, which everyone then read as the pain thesis was wrong, and the door was abandoned in month three. The unfixable moment was a measurement error built on a one-word copy error: your.

Addressed, and independently confirmed three times. Blind reads 1, 2 and 3 each convicted a different presumption sentence, unprompted. The copy now asserts nothing about the reader's department.

The First Principles Thinker, the assumption nobody wrote down

That a profile produces inbound. It does not. A profile converts attention that something else generated. Six rounds optimised the conversion surface while the generation surface stayed empty, so in March 2027 the copy was excellent and almost nobody had seen it.

Addressed by scope, not by copy. See section 7.

The Outsider, what was obvious from outside

It read as a man who fixes other people's AI mistakes. That is a repairman, and repairmen get an hour at an hourly rate, not a fractional engagement. A promise built entirely on which steps to leave alone is a promise of subtraction, and nobody has a budget line for taking something away.

Addressed. The About now leads with the steps a machine should be running and is not, and the work paragraph says he builds them, inside what the buyer already owns, on a stated scope of one process rather than the whole function. Something arrives, not only leaves.

The Expansionist, what was never tried that would have saved it

The 2026 algorithmic-hiring exposure. Round 5 called it possibly the strongest fact in the file (Mobley v. Workday certified nationwide with the just-a-software-provider defence rejected, the NYC Local Law 144 audit duty sitting on the employer) and then kept it out of the copy entirely. One clause naming that the machine's decisions now have an owner moves a reader from annoyed to exposed, and exposure is what makes a stranger message a stranger.

Deliberately not applied, and this is a real divergence rather than a smoothing. Round 5's boundary was exposure is the urgency, never the deliverable, because he holds no certification to sign an audit. A profile line is permanent and invites the certification question with no context to absorb it; a post is disposable and carries its own. Exposure belongs in posts, where the round-4 decision already puts the narrow aim.

The Executor, the earliest visible warning and the week

Week of 2026-09-15. The artifact produced was copy again, and the round-4 and round-5 outcome had zero rows on it four days before its own deadline, visible before any market feedback existed.

Addressed only by naming it. See section 7.

5. Council, five-angle pass on the finished copy

The Contrarian

The duplication is fixed: the banner states scope and method, the headline states the opinion and the standing behind it, the About opens on the reader's own week. What remains genuinely weak is the one-person question the fourth read named: "one person, no team, no successor. I have to ask who maintains this in eighteen months." The open-ended half of that promise is deleted. The solo half is a true fact about the business and cannot be written away. Flagged, not smoothed.

The First Principles Thinker

The copy has one job: make a stranger conclude that this person decides rather than sells. Every sentence serves that except the Fortune-100 paragraph, which exists to satisfy a standing construction rule and does no work for that job. It earns its place as proof and nothing more, which is exactly why it deletes cleanly.

The Expansionist

The buried upside is the closing line. "Message me the one step in your hiring or HR work where AI was supposed to help and did not" is a diagnostic question that produces, free, the exact raw material five rounds of research could not buy: employer-side verbatim about AI-caused hiring failure, in the buyer's own words, from the buyer. Reddit was unreachable on five consecutive routes across rounds 3, 4 and 5. This CTA is the replacement research channel and nobody has said so out loud until now. Every answer it produces should be captured, not just replied to.

The Outsider

Read cold, the shape lands fast now: someone who knows which parts of an HR process should not have a machine on them, and who builds the parts that should. "A machine" reads slightly archaic for software, which cuts both ways since it is also concrete and unmistakable. Paragraph five is the longest thing on the page and sits exactly where a skimmer stops. If anything gets cut later, cut inside paragraph five, never the first four.

The Executor

None of this is live. The headline and the About are two paste operations and can be live in one sitting today. The banner needs one comp inside the measured geometry, and the feat/linkedin-banner-v5-0820 branch has been sitting unmerged since August carrying a superseded line. That branch gets merged or explicitly superseded this week rather than re-dated a third time.

Chairman synthesis

Convergence, all five voices, approximately 93 percent: the copy now matches the decision, which is the one thing round 5 said it did not; the pain and the judgment claim both land inside the phone crop and both contain concrete nouns; the result is correctly demoted; and the binding constraint remains the priced offer and the named list, which is now the sixth consecutive pass to say so.

Divergence, named and not averaged: the Expansionist wants the algorithmic-hiring exposure in the identity lines, the Contrarian holds that a permanent line invoking legal exposure invites a certification question he cannot answer from a profile. Resolved by surface rather than by compromise: exposure lives in posts, never in the banner, headline or About. If a buyer conversation surfaces the exposure angle unprompted twice in the same month, revisit.

Verdict: CHANGE, small, changes already applied. Ship the copy as written.

6. The blind stranger reads

Run through a subagent that loads none of this repository's instructions, memory, or prior rounds, so it reads the words the way a stranger does. Each prompt contained only the banner, headline and About opening as plain text, with no indication of who wrote them or why, and instructed the reader to be blunt rather than polite. Four reads, each on the copy as it stood at that moment.

The three-way test: "I know that pain, tell me more" / "I know that pain but it does not interest me" / "I do not recognise this." The second answer is a pass, not a failure. The copy is built to filter.

Read 1 — verdict (c), on an abstract claim

Tested: "AI made your HR work harder" on banner and headline, "I know which steps a machine should never touch."

"the whole thing is written in riddle-voice, so I scroll."
"'I know which steps a machine should never touch.' It is the centerpiece and it is empty. Which steps? Name one. Resume screening? Rejection emails? Reference checks? Give me one concrete example and I would believe you instantly. Withholding it reads as coy, not authoritative."
"'I worked on hiring processes before AI existed' — that is trying to say 'veteran' but it lands as 'before 2022,' which is not a credential, it is a birthday."
"'You put AI into your HR work and the work got harder' — you are telling me, a stranger, what happened inside my department. Confident-stranger-diagnoses-my-problem is the oldest cold-pitch move on this platform."

Changed as a result: the claim got a concrete noun (screening), the birthday phrasing went, and the possessive came out of every surface.

Read 2 — verdict (c), on structure

Tested: the screening claim on all three surfaces.

"Three surfaces, one idea, zero stacking. You had three chances to answer three different questions — what's the insight, what do you do, who are you — and you spent all three on the insight."
"The crop cuts mid-word at the only part carrying the surprise. What survives is a generic industry observation I've read ten times this month."
"'Nobody automated those, because they are boring and nobody complains about them in a leadership meeting.' You're telling me, a person you have never met, why my department made the choices it made, and the answer you supply is that we're lazy and vain."
"'and I build the AI systems I run my business on.' That's about your business, not mine."

Changed as a result: three surfaces got three jobs, the crop was re-cut so the claim completes at character 54, and the motive-attribution sentence was removed.

Read 3 — verdict (a), barely, and it moved the copy again

"He audits an HR process end to end, tells you which steps to automate and which to leave alone, then builds the automations himself into your existing systems. That is clear. Clearer than 95 percent of profiles in this category."

The part the copy cannot fix, stated plainly rather than padded
