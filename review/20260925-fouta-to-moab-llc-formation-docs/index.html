1. Operating Agreement

OPERATING AGREEMENT OF FOUTA TO MOAB LLC

A Utah Single-Member, Member-Managed Limited Liability Company

This Operating Agreement (“Agreement”) of Fouta to Moab LLC (the “Company”) is made and entered into effective as of the Effective Date set forth in Section 12.4, by Boubacar Barry, an individual (the “Member”), being the sole member of the Company.

Article 1 — Formation, Purpose, and Principal Office

1.1 Formation. The Company was formed as a limited liability company under the Utah Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act, Utah Code Title 48, Chapter 3a, as amended from time to time (the “Act”), by the filing of a Certificate of Organization with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code. This Agreement constitutes the “operating agreement” of the Company within the meaning of Utah Code § 48-3a-102(19), and governs the relations among the Member, the Company, and (if applicable in the future) any additional members, managers, and the Company's business and affairs, to the fullest extent permitted by the Act. Where this Agreement is silent, the default provisions of the Act govern.

1.2 Name. The name of the Company is “Fouta to Moab LLC.” The Company may conduct business under one or more assumed or “doing business as” names, provided any such name is properly registered with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code and any other jurisdiction in which the Company conducts business under that name.

1.3 Purpose. The purpose of the Company is to engage in any and all lawful business activity for which limited liability companies may be organized under the Act, including without limitation:

(a) providing consulting services, including human-resources and artificial-intelligence-training consulting, AI-enabled marketing and business-operations consulting, and related advisory, coaching, and professional services to businesses and individuals;

(b) pursuing, bidding on, and performing government contracting and subcontracting work, including small-business set-aside contracts with federal, state, and local government agencies and their prime contractors; and

(c) engaging in any other lawful act or activity for which a limited liability company may be organized under the laws of the State of Utah, whether or not related to the foregoing, as the Member may from time to time determine.

This purpose clause is intentionally broad so that the Company may expand or shift the scope of its consulting and contracting activities without the need to amend this Agreement solely to reflect an ordinary change in the line of business pursued, provided the activity remains lawful.

The Member may change the registered agent or registered office at any time by filing the appropriate change of registered agent/office with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code; no amendment of this Agreement is required to do so.)

1.5 Term. The Company shall have a perpetual existence unless and until dissolved in accordance with Article 8 of this Agreement or the Act.

Article 2 — Member; Capital Contribution; Capital Account

2.1 Sole Member. Boubacar Barry is the sole member of the Company (the “Member”), holding one hundred percent (100%) of the membership interests in the Company. No other person or entity holds, or has any right to acquire, any membership interest in the Company as of the Effective Date.

2.2 Initial Capital Contribution. The Member has contributed, or shall contribute concurrently with the execution of this Agreement, the following as the Member's initial capital contribution to the Company (the “Initial Capital Contribution”):

Cash in the amount of $[INITIAL CONTRIBUTION AMOUNT], and/or the following described property: [DESCRIPTION OF ANY CONTRIBUTED PROPERTY, OR "NONE"].

The Initial Capital Contribution shall be deposited into, or transferred to, a bank account held in the Company's own name in accordance with Section 5.2, and the transfer shall be documented in the Company's books and records and in the Organizational Resolutions referenced in this package.

2.3 Capital Account. The Company shall maintain a single capital account for the Member, credited with (i) the Member's Initial Capital Contribution and any additional capital contributions made by the Member from time to time, and (ii) the Member's allocated share of Company profits, and debited with (i) distributions made to the Member, and (ii) the Member's allocated share of Company losses. Because the Company has only one member and is treated as a disregarded entity for federal income tax purposes as provided in Article 4, the capital account is maintained for the Company's internal record-keeping and creditor-protection purposes and does not itself create or divide any interest among multiple members.

2.4 No Obligation to Make Additional Contributions. The Member is not required to make any additional capital contribution to the Company. The Member may, but is not obligated to, make additional contributions from time to time, which shall be credited to the Member's capital account.

2.5 No Interest on Capital; No Withdrawal Right. No interest shall accrue on any capital contribution, and the Member has no right to withdraw or demand the return of any capital contribution except as provided in this Agreement or upon dissolution and winding up under Article 8.

Article 3 — Allocations of Profits and Losses; Distributions

3.1 Allocation of Profits and Losses. All items of income, gain, loss, deduction, and credit of the Company shall be allocated one hundred percent (100%) to the Member for so long as the Member remains the sole member of the Company.

3.2 Distributions. The Member may cause the Company to make distributions of cash or other property to the Member at such times and in such amounts as the Member, in the Member's sole discretion, determines, subject to Section 3.3.

3.3 Limitation on Distributions. The Company shall not make a distribution to the Member if, after giving effect to the distribution, the Company would not be able to pay its debts as they become due in the ordinary course of business, or the Company's total assets would be less than the sum of its total liabilities, consistent with Utah Code § 48-3a-405. The Member shall exercise reasonable business judgment in determining whether a proposed distribution satisfies this Section.

3.4 Tax Distributions. To the extent the Company has sufficient cash and is not otherwise restricted under Section 3.3, the Member may cause the Company to distribute amounts sufficient to allow the Member to pay income taxes attributable to the Company's income, consistent with the Company's disregarded-entity tax treatment described in Article 4.

Article 4 — Tax Matters

4.1 Default Tax Classification. As a limited liability company with a single member, the Company is, and is intended to be, disregarded as an entity separate from the Member for federal income tax purposes under Treasury Regulation § 301.7701-3, and the Member shall report the Company's income, gains, losses, deductions, and credits directly on the Member's individual federal income tax return (currently, Schedule C of IRS Form 1040), and on the corresponding Utah state income tax filings. No election has been made, and none is currently intended, to treat the Company as an association taxable as a corporation or as an S corporation.

4.2 Authority to Elect Different Tax Treatment. Notwithstanding Section 4.1, the Member retains full authority, exercisable at any time by a written resolution of the Member (in the form used for the Organizational Resolutions in this package or a substantially similar form), to cause the Company to elect to be classified as an association taxable as a corporation under IRS Form 8832, and/or to elect S corporation status under IRS Form 2553, effective as of a date the Member selects, without any amendment to this Agreement being required, provided that the Member shall cause this Agreement to be reviewed and, if necessary, amended to remain consistent with the elected tax treatment (including, if S corporation status is elected, any requirements applicable to reasonable compensation, payroll, and distribution mechanics under that election).

4.3 Employer Identification Number; Tax Filings. The Company shall obtain and maintain its own federal Employer Identification Number (EIN) separate from the Member's Social Security Number for use in connection with its bank accounts, contracts, and filings, notwithstanding its disregarded-entity tax treatment.

Article 5 — Management

5.1 Member-Managed. The Company is, and shall be, member-managed within the meaning of the Act. The Company shall NOT have “managers” as that term is used in the Act; all management authority is vested in the Member as set forth in this Article 5. This Agreement does not create, and shall not be construed to create, a manager-managed structure.

5.2 Authority of the Member. The Member has full, complete, and exclusive authority, power, and discretion to manage and control the business, affairs, and properties of the Company, to make all decisions regarding the business of the Company, and to perform any and all acts customary or incidental to managing the Company's business, including without limitation the authority to:

(a) open, maintain, and close bank, brokerage, and merchant-processing accounts in the Company's name;

(b) enter into, execute, deliver, amend, and terminate contracts, consulting agreements, government contracts and subcontracts, leases, and other agreements on behalf of the Company;

(c) hire, engage, compensate, and terminate employees, independent contractors, and professional service providers (including attorneys, accountants, and consultants) on behalf of the Company;

(d) borrow money and grant security interests in Company assets, and incur other obligations, on behalf of the Company;

(e) acquire, hold, manage, encumber, and dispose of Company assets, whether tangible or intangible, including intellectual property;

(f) bring and defend actions and proceedings, at law or in equity, in the name of and on behalf of the Company;

(g) execute and file all tax returns, elections, and other governmental filings on behalf of the Company, including the tax elections described in Section 4.2; and

(h) delegate any of the foregoing authority to officers, employees, or agents of the Company, provided that the Member remains ultimately responsible for the management of the Company.

5.3 No Requirement for Meetings. Because the Company has only one member, formal member meetings are not required to approve Company action. All actions requiring “member approval” under the Act or this Agreement may instead be evidenced by a written resolution signed by the Member, consistent with the record-keeping requirements of Article 6.

5.4 Officers. The Member may, but is not required to, appoint officers of the Company (e.g., President, Treasurer, Secretary) to carry out day-to-day operations, and may prescribe their titles, authority, and compensation by written resolution. Any officer so appointed acts as an agent of the Company and derives all authority from, and remains subject to the ultimate control of, the Member.

Article 6 — Records; Formalities; Separateness

6.1 Purpose of This Article. The limited liability shield that protects the Member's personal assets from the Company's liabilities depends on the Company being operated, and being demonstrably provable to have been operated, as an entity separate and distinct from the Member. This Article states the specific, mandatory practices the Member shall follow to maintain that separateness. Failure to follow these practices materially increases the risk that a court could disregard the Company's separate existence and hold the Member personally liable for Company obligations (“piercing the veil”).

6.2 Separate Bank Accounts; No Commingling. The Company shall maintain one or more bank accounts in the Company's own legal name and its own EIN, separate and distinct from any bank account held by the Member in the Member's individual capacity. The Member shall NOT:

(a) deposit Company funds into the Member's personal bank account, or personal funds into the Company's bank account, except as a properly documented capital contribution (credited to the Member's capital account under Section 2.3) or a properly documented distribution (recorded under Section 3.2);

(b) pay personal expenses directly from Company funds, or Company expenses directly from personal funds, without such payment being characterized, contemporaneously and in the Company's books, as either a distribution to the Member, a capital contribution by the Member, or a bona fide loan on commercially reasonable terms evidenced in writing; or

(c) use Company funds or Company credit to satisfy the Member's personal, non-Company obligations.

All Company revenue shall be deposited into a Company bank account before any portion of it is used for any purpose, including a distribution to the Member.

6.3 Separate Books and Records. The Company shall maintain its own books of account, separate from the Member's personal financial records, reflecting the Company's income, expenses, assets, liabilities, and the capital account activity described in Section 2.3. The Company shall maintain, at its principal office or another location designated by the Member, at minimum: (a) this Agreement and any amendments; (b) the Certificate of Organization and any amendments filed with the State of Utah; (c) the Organizational Resolutions and any subsequent written resolutions of the Member; (d) the Membership Certificate; (e) federal and state tax returns and supporting records for at least the period required by applicable law; and (f) records of all capital contributions and distributions.

6.4 Minutes for Major Decisions. The Member shall document, by a signed and dated written resolution maintained with the Company's records under Section 6.3, each of the following categories of decision (a non-exhaustive list of what constitutes a “major decision” for this purpose): (a) any capital contribution or distribution outside the ordinary course; (b) the opening or closing of any Company bank or brokerage account; (c) any borrowing, guaranty, or grant of a security interest by the Company; (d) any tax election described in Section 4.2; (e) the purchase, sale, or encumbrance of any material Company asset; (f) the admission of any additional member or any transfer of the membership interest described in Article 7; (g) any amendment of this Agreement; and (h) the appointment or removal of any officer. A written resolution in the form of the Organizational Resolutions in this package, adapted to the subject matter, satisfies this requirement. Contemporaneous documentation is the objective — a resolution should be signed at or near the time of the decision, not reconstructed later.

6.5 Company Identification in Dealings With Third Parties. In all contracts, invoices, proposals, correspondence, and government-contracting submissions, the Member shall identify the counterparty as contracting with “Fouta to Moab LLC,” not with the Member individually, and shall sign in a representative capacity (e.g., “Boubacar Barry, Member, Fouta to Moab LLC”) rather than in an individual capacity, except where a form or statute requires an individual signature (e.g., a personal guaranty knowingly and separately given).

6.6 Adequate Capitalization. The Member shall use reasonable business judgment to ensure the Company maintains capital and/or insurance reasonably adequate for the scale and risk of the business it conducts from time to time, recognizing that chronic undercapitalization is a factor courts consider when deciding whether to disregard the Company's separate existence.

Article 7 — Indemnification

7.1 Indemnification of the Member. To the fullest extent permitted by the Act (including Utah Code § 48-3a-408 and any successor provision), the Company shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the Member from and against any and all claims, demands, losses, damages, liabilities, judgments, settlements, and reasonable expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees) incurred by the Member by reason of the fact that the Member is or was a member and/or manager (in the member-managed sense used in this Agreement) of the Company, or is or was acting on behalf of the Company, provided that the Member acted in good faith and in a manner the Member reasonably believed to be in, or not opposed to, the best interests of the Company, and, with respect to any criminal proceeding, had no reasonable cause to believe the Member's conduct was unlawful.

7.2 Advancement of Expenses. The Company shall advance to the Member, in advance of the final disposition of any proceeding described in Section 7.1, the reasonable expenses incurred by the Member in defending the proceeding, upon receipt of an undertaking by the Member to repay such amount if it is ultimately determined that the Member is not entitled to be indemnified under this Article.

7.3 Non-Exclusivity; Insurance. The indemnification rights provided in this Article are in addition to, and not exclusive of, any other rights to which the Member may be entitled under the Act, any insurance policy, agreement, or otherwise. The Company may, but is not required to, purchase and maintain insurance on behalf of the Member against liability asserted against the Member in that capacity, whether or not the Company would have the power to indemnify the Member against such liability under this Article.

7.4 Limitation. Nothing in this Article shall require the Company to indemnify the Member for the Member's fraud, willful misconduct, or knowing violation of law, to the extent indemnification for such conduct is prohibited by the Act.

Article 8 — Succession on Death or Incapacity; Transfer Restrictions; Dissolution

8.1 No Automatic Dissolution on Death or Incapacity. The death, incapacity, or disability of the Member shall NOT cause the dissolution of the Company. The Company shall continue in existence, and this Agreement shall remain in full force and effect, notwithstanding the Member's death or incapacity, consistent with Utah Code § 48-3a-701 (which does not list a single member's death as an event automatically requiring dissolution) and the Member's express intent stated in this Section.

8.2 Succession of the Membership Interest on Death. Upon the Member's death, the Member's entire membership interest in the Company (100% of all economic and governance rights) shall pass to the Member's estate, and thereafter to the beneficiary or beneficiaries designated to receive it under the Member's last will and testament, revocable trust, or other estate-planning instrument then in effect (the “Successor Member”). The personal representative of the Member's estate, or the trustee of the Member's revocable trust, as applicable, shall have the authority, pending final distribution of the membership interest to the Successor Member, to exercise all of the Member's management and economic rights under this Agreement as if that fiduciary were the Member, including the authority to operate the Company's bank accounts, sign on the Company's behalf, and take any action described in Article 5, solely for the purpose of preserving the Company's business, assets, and government-contracting relationships until the membership interest is fully transferred to the Successor Member. Upon the membership interest being distributed to the Successor Member in accordance with the Member's estate plan, the Successor Member shall automatically become the sole member of the Company with all rights and obligations of the Member under this Agreement, without the need for any further act, consent, or amendment, other than the Successor Member's execution of a joinder to this Agreement acknowledging its terms.

8.3 Succession on Incapacity. If the Member becomes incapacitated (as determined by a licensed physician's written certification, or by the terms of the Member's durable power of attorney or revocable trust, whichever the Member's estate-planning documents specify), the person named as the Member's agent under a durable power of attorney, or as successor trustee under the Member's revocable trust, whichever applies, shall have the authority to exercise the Member's management rights under Article 5 on the Member's behalf for so long as the incapacity continues, and shall return full management authority to the Member upon the Member regaining capacity, as determined in the same manner.

8.4 Transfer Restrictions. Because the Company has only one member, no transfer of any part of the membership interest may be made during the Member's lifetime except: (a) to a revocable trust of which the Member is the grantor and initial trustee, for estate-planning purposes, without changing the beneficial ownership of the interest; (b) as otherwise expressly authorized by a written resolution of the Member under Section 6.4(f); or (c) pursuant to Sections 8.2 and 8.3 upon the Member's death or incapacity. Any Membership Certificate issued by the Company shall bear the restrictive legend set forth in Article 3 of the Membership Certificate below.

8.5 Dissolution — Events. The Company shall be dissolved, and its affairs wound up, only upon the occurrence of any of the following: (a) the written consent of the Member (or, following succession under Section 8.2, the Successor Member) to dissolve the Company; (b) the entry of a decree of judicial dissolution under Utah Code § 48-3a-701; (c) the occurrence of any other event requiring dissolution under the Act that cannot be avoided by the continuation provisions of this Article; provided that neither the death nor incapacity of the Member is such an event, as stated in Section 8.1.

8.6 Winding Up. Upon dissolution, the Member (or, if applicable, the Successor Member or a person designated by written resolution) shall act as the “person winding up the Company's activities” under Utah Code § 48-3a-702, and shall: (a) file a notice of intent to dissolve and, following winding up, articles of dissolution with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code; (b) collect the Company's assets; (c) discharge or make reasonable provision for the Company's known liabilities, including any contingent or unliquidated claims, in the order of priority required by Utah Code § 48-3a-708; and (d) distribute any remaining assets to the Member (or Successor Member) in accordance with the Member's capital account and this Agreement. The Company's existence continues after dissolution solely for the purpose of winding up, until articles of dissolution are filed and effective.

Article 9 — Amendment

9.1 Amendment by the Member. This Agreement may be amended, restated, or repealed, in whole or in part, at any time by a written instrument signed by the Member (or the Successor Member, once succession has occurred under Article 8), without the need for any other person's consent, provided that no amendment may retroactively eliminate a right to indemnification that has already accrued under Article 7 for conduct occurring before the amendment.

9.2 Effect of Adding Additional Members. If the Member ever admits one or more additional members to the Company, this Agreement shall be amended and restated in its entirety to address, at a minimum, capital contributions, profit and loss allocations, voting and management rights, transfer restrictions, and buy-sell/succession provisions applicable to a multi-member LLC, in consultation with a Utah-licensed business attorney and a CPA, before any additional member is admitted.

Article 10 — Liability of the Member

10.1 Limited Liability. Except as otherwise expressly provided in the Act, the debts, obligations, and other liabilities of the Company, whether arising in contract, tort, or otherwise, are solely the debts, obligations, and liabilities of the Company, and the Member is not personally liable for any such debt, obligation, or liability of the Company solely by reason of being a member, consistent with Utah Code § 48-3a-304, provided the Member complies with the formalities and separateness practices required by Article 6.

10.2 Standard of Conduct. In performing the Member's management duties under Article 5, the Member shall discharge those duties consistent with the contractual obligation of good faith and fair dealing implied in this Agreement and required by the Act. Because the Company is single-member, the Member owes no fiduciary duty of loyalty or care to any other member (there being none), but remains subject to the good-faith and fair-dealing standard, and to any duties owed to third parties (such as creditors upon insolvency) under applicable law.

Article 11 — Miscellaneous

11.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement constitutes the entire operating agreement of the Company and supersedes any prior oral or written agreement, understanding, or arrangement relating to the subject matter of this Agreement.

11.2 Governing Law. This Agreement, and all questions concerning its construction, validity, interpretation, and enforcement, shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Utah, including the Act, without regard to conflict-of-laws principles.

11.3 Severability. If any provision of this Agreement is held to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and effect, and the invalid provision shall be reformed to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable while preserving its original intent.

11.4 Binding Effect. This Agreement is binding upon and inures to the benefit of the Member and the Member's estate, heirs, personal representatives, successors (including any Successor Member under Article 8), and permitted assigns.

11.5 Counterparts; Electronic Signature. This Agreement may be executed in one or more counterparts, including by electronic signature, each of which shall be deemed an original.

Fouta to Moab LLC — Formation Checklist

Sign in this order:

  1. Operating Agreement — sign and date first; this governs everything else.
  2. Organizational Resolutions / Initial Minutes — sign second, after the bank account is open and the actual capital-contribution dollar amount and transfer date are known and filled in.
  3. Membership Certificate — sign third, once 1 and 2 are complete; keep it with the Company's permanent records (it does not need to be given to anyone else since there is only one member).

The three habits that keep the liability shield real, not paper:

  • Separate account, always. Every dollar of Company revenue lands in the Company's own bank account first — never a personal account — and every Company expense pays out of that same account.
  • No commingling, ever. Personal and Company money move between each other only as a documented contribution or a documented distribution — never as a quiet transfer with no paper trail.
  • Minutes for major decisions. Every time something on the Article 6.4 list happens (contribution/distribution outside the ordinary course, opening/closing an account, borrowing, a tax election, buying/selling a major asset, an amendment), write and sign a short dated resolution the same week it happens — not months later from memory.
