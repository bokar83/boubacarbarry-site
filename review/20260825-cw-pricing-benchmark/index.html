Findings

1. Bowen AI Strategy Group — real, checkable, published pricing

Tyler Bowen, solo operator, Canonsburg/Pittsburgh PA. Published rates:

  • Fixed-fee builds: $1,500-3,000 setup + $250/month care plan
  • Custom engagements: start at $3,000/month

Source: bowenaistrategygroup.com/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-consultant-cost-2026.html

Caveat: partial comparability — this is more website/automation-build pricing than AI-adoption advisory. But it is a real solo operator's real floor, which is the most load-bearing single data point in this file.

2. Go Fractional's claimed "2026 State of Fractional Work" report — UNVERIFIED

Secondhand figures (search-engine paraphrase only, their pages returned HTTP 403, no visibility into methodology, no confirmed sample or survey design):

  • Fractional CEO: ~$207/hr (~16 hrs/wk typical)
  • Fractional CMO: $200-350/hr ($8k-15k/mo typical retainer)
  • Fractional CFO: $175-500/hr (median ~$300, $3k-10k/mo retainer)

Status: directionally useful only, not confirmed. Do not cite as a hard number to a prospect.

3. Blog-consensus range — explicitly not primary data

Converges across roughly 15 independent "AI Consulting Cost" listicle sites, none of which disclose a survey or name respondents — this is an SEO-content citation loop, not a market survey:

  • ~$100-350/hr independent AI consultant
  • $2,000-8,000/month "essential" advisory retainers
  • $5,000-15,000/month for fractional CAIO/CTO-level engagements
  • $10,000-15,000 for a 4-6 week SMB implementation project

Caveat: skews technical/implementation, not HR/people-ops change-management — the niche Boubacar is actually positioned in.

4. HR/people-ops-adjacent AI pricing — thin, unpriced

No real pricing data found for "AI adoption for HR/people teams" as its own priced category. This confirms it is a narrow, emerging, not-yet-priced wedge — which is a genuine positioning opportunity, not a research gap to apologize for (see Action 4 below).

5. Retainer structure norms — the most actionable finding

Fractional retainers typically buy 5-20 hours/month, most commonly cited as 8-15 hrs/month ("a few hours a week"). At that structure, a $5,000/month retainer implies:

  • Roughly $250-1,000/effective-hour at the low end of hours, or
  • ~$310-625/hr scoped at 8-16 hrs/month

This is the number that turns a flat monthly fee into a defensible, explainable rate.

The verdict already reached

Boubacar's stated $12,000/month floor is HIGH for a first engagement with no client roster yet. It becomes realistic once he has 2-3 clients as proof, not as an entry price.

His $5,000/month x 3-5 retainers framing is VALIDATED by the real data — it sits inside the real observed range (Bowen's real floor is $3k/month, the blog-consensus range centers $2k-8k/month for "essential" retainers). The one gap: none of the source data prices an unbounded retainer, so hours-included needs to be explicitly scoped in how the offer is stated, not left open-ended.

Concrete actions (2026-08-25)

1. Opening retainer price for client #1: $4,500/month, scoped at 10 hours/month.

Not $5,000 flat and not a range. Reasoning: $4,500 sits comfortably above Bowen's real $3,000/month floor (the only fully verified solo-operator number in this file) while staying inside the blog-consensus "essential retainer" band ($2k-8k/month), and $4,500/10hrs = $450/effective-hour, which lands mid-pack in the $310-625/hr band implied by typical 8-16hr/month fractional structures. Pricing just under the round $5,000 number also gives room to negotiate up to $5,000 without it reading as a discount — it reads as "you got the standard rate." First client's job is proof, not maximizing revenue per hour.

2. Re-introduce the $12,000/month framing after client #3 is signed and delivering, not on a calendar date.

Concretely: once 3 paying retainer clients exist simultaneously (matching the $5,000 x 3-5 framing already validated), the next NEW client conversation is where $12,000/month first gets quoted — priced as a higher-touch tier (more hours, faster response SLA, or a named outcome commitment), not a blanket price hike on existing clients. Existing clients renew at their signed rate unless they ask to expand scope. Trigger = client count (3 concurrent), not months elapsed, because proof is what justifies the jump, and proof is countable while time on the calendar is not.

3. State hours-included explicitly in every pricing conversation, going forward, using a fixed script line rather than leaving it implicit

"This is a $4,500-a-month retainer, and it includes up to 10 hours of my direct time each month — strategy, implementation work, whatever the month calls for. If a month needs more than that, we talk about it before any extra hours happen, never after."

This does two things the current framing doesn't: it prevents scope creep from turning a flat fee into an unpaid open-ended commitment (the exact gap the research flagged — none of the comparable data prices an unbounded retainer), and it gives the prospect a concrete unit ($450/hr-equivalent) to mentally compare against the $100-350/hr solo-consultant range they may have already seen, which reads as a premium without ever having to say the word "premium."

4. One concrete next action on the unpriced HR/people-ops AI-adoption gap

Ask the next 2 real discovery calls (not a survey, not a cold guess) one specific question and log the answer verbatim:

"If you were already paying someone monthly to help your HR/people team actually use AI day to day, what would you expect that to cost?"

This is cheap (costs nothing but a question already being asked in calls that are happening anyway), it produces primary data in a category where none currently exists (closing the exact gap Finding 4 identified), and it lets Boubacar set the anchor number in the prospect's head before quoting — turning him from a price-taker guessing against SaaS/technical-consultant comps into the price-setter for a category with no established public number yet. Two real answers beats another round of blog-scraping.
