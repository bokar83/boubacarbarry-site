Your opener: four rules, and that is the whole thing

  1. True, and about them. Their life right now, or a shared era.
  2. Do not call back the favor they did you. This is the one that is easy to break. Mention the thing they did and they will write the recommendation about that thing, which drags the script back in through the side door.
  3. No bridge into the ask. Do not write "which is why I'm reaching out." The body turns on its own with "Asking you for something."
  4. Never a rank claim. "We worked together at GE" is fine. Anything that says you led or ran something is not.

When someone says yes and then stalls

This is the part the earlier plans never covered, and it is where most of the twelve will actually sit.

  • The request does not expire and does not nag. It sits in their LinkedIn messages. Nobody is being pressured by your silence, so you are not rescuing anything by following up early.
  • One follow-up, about ten days after their yes, on the same thread. One line, warm, no new ask, no deadline. Something like: "No pressure on timing, just did not want it to get buried." Then stop.
  • No second follow-up. Ever. A third message turns a favor into an obligation, and it costs you more relationship than the recommendation is worth.
  • If they say they are stuck, send ideas, never a draft. Three bullets on what they actually saw you do. If you send them sentences, you get your own sentences back, and it reads exactly like what it is.
  • After the follow-up, close it quietly. It does not go back on a list and it does not get counted as a failure. You asked, they meant to, life happened. Ask someone else.

The tripwire, kept from the original plan because it was the right call. If the first three asks are not out by end of day Thursday 2026-09-17, that is the signal that something else is eating the slot, and the sprint outranks it. Send them before anything else on Friday.

The character limit, checked rather than guessed

The August plan said plainly that it could not verify LinkedIn's limit for this field and refused to quote a number. I checked it properly.

LinkedIn publishes no character limit for the recommendation request note. Its own help page on requesting a recommendation describes the message box and states no limit. Two independent limit compendia list every other field and neither carries this one.

FieldPublished limitIs it this field?

So your 364-character message ships as written. Trimming approved copy to fit a limit that does not exist would have been the exact thing this page is supposed to stop.
