The Niche Operating System

Absorb → promptfix → lateral-leverage → council + premortem → simulation · 2026-09-06

TL;DR

The niche problem is not a picking problem. It is a finishing problem, and it is measurable.

The system holds 23 niche labels. Eight were worked for exactly one day. Not one has ever been run to a verdict, because the outcome data was never keyed on niche. So every niche has been permanently PENDING since May.

A join nobody had ever run — email_events.to_addr to leads.email — produced the first per-niche numbers in the system's history in about thirty seconds, from data that was already sitting there. Roofing: 148 prospects, 323 sends, 1 reply. Auto repair, the vertical a rigorous 21-market sweep validated eight days ago: 5 prospects, 6 sends.

So the strategy is not a better way to choose. It is a mechanism that makes a choice finishable: a cheap supply test before any contact, a commitment window that cannot lapse quietly, and a verdict that is computed rather than remembered.

Tuesday is not a picking day. The pick was made on 8/29 and never executed. Tuesday is five pre-flight items, and one decision that only you can make.

What the video says

Chris Do, live Q&A, roughly 2,800 words of transcript. Ten usable ideas, paraphrased.

Why it hurts. Serving everyone serves no one. His claim is stronger than the usual one: specialization and success are not merely correlated, they are causal. He asks the room to identify as generalist or specialist, and the specialists are the ones doing well.

The real failure mode, and this is the part that matters here. He asks a man struggling to niche how long he tried the last one. A few months. He points out the man pursued his wife for three years. Then he draws Seth Godin's Dip: early effort produces easy results, then results collapse while effort rises, and that trough is where nearly everyone quits and starts over. Each restart resets the clock. He likes difficult things because everyone else quits in the trough.

The false dilemma. The money business and the soul business do not have to be the same business. One funds the other.

The picking method. Forced binary cuts where "both" is not an allowed answer. Men or women. Early, middle or late career. Then age, the shared trait, the industry shape, a revenue band as a specific number, a region, their morning complaint, and how they currently get inquiries. Two questions eliminated most of the market. His framing: picking is easy, deciding is what people avoid.

The ICP shortcut. Think of the best client you ever had — paid well, paid on time, easy, decided fast, and there are many of them. Look at your own history.

On prompting. Use the term ICP directly. Require clarifying questions before any recommendation. Require a market-size check first. Then pressure-test the answer yourself, because sloppy input produces sloppy output and that is the operator's fault, not the model's.

On finding the offer. Ask for the buyer's problems in the buyer's own words, never marketing language. Rank them by pain and separately by willingness to pay. His closing point: you will probably find you sell none of the top-ranked ones. That gap is the opportunity.

"How long did you try it for?"

The video's central question, answered honestly against the send dates.

Fifteen niches have ever had an email sent. Eight of them have a first-send date identical to their last-send date — one day, then never again. The longest sustained attempt in system history is roofing at 21 days. The last outbound send to any trade vertical was 2026-07-10, nearly two months ago.

The video describes people who quit after a few months. The measured behaviour here is quitting after an afternoon.

The verdict that could never fire

The gate needs 30 sends to read at all and 100 to kill. Only one labelled niche has ever crossed 100 prospects. So it returns PENDING for essentially everything, forever. Confirmed independently in the ledger: after the 09-05 opt-out migration, the gate over all 16 live rows returned kill 2, iterate 2, pending 12, SCALE 0.

Nothing can be scaled. Nothing can be killed. So the only available move is to start something else — which is exactly what has happened, 23 times.

This was diagnosed 15 weeks ago

From the SW niche council of 2026-05-22, the Critic voice, verbatim:

And your own framing that triggered it: "Pattern observed: tool fails → swap tool. Never: niche fails → reconsider niche."

That council was right and changed nothing, because it produced a verdict and this document produces a mechanism. A verdict has to be remembered. A mechanism does not.

The validated answer that was never executed

The 2026-08-29 national sweep tested 21 markets in two rounds. Auto repair returned 39.8% KEEP (231 of 580) across 11 cities, against 12.5% for every other vertical, driven by website adoption of 43-72% versus near-100% elsewhere. The standing recommendation written that day: make it the primary sourcing vertical.

In the eight days since: six emails. The 580 scanned records were never saved as leads — the five auto-repair rows in the database predate the sweep by six weeks.

That is the disease in one line. The picking machinery works. Nothing downstream of it runs.

Two standing rules this had to reconcile

Both were nearly violated. Naming them is what keeps this from being generic advice.

Pain, not industry (2026-07-16)

I don't come in with a hammer looking for a nail… pain is industry agnostic.

Resolution on file: narrow on WHAT, broad on WHO, and do not re-litigate industry breadth. The video's method narrows on WHO. Taken naively, it contradicts a standing hard rule.

The reconciliation, and it is the load-bearing idea here: the forced-binary pick is not a positioning decision. Positioning is locked and stays locked. The pick chooses a sourcing cohort — which list gets harvested next. The boring-business mandate is explicitly a sourcing rule, filed as "same axis, opposite end of the pipe" from the pain rule. Pain defines the offer; the cohort defines the list.

A competitor count is a supply signal (2026-09-06)

Before any agent calls a market saturated it must answer, in its output: did I look at sellers, or at buyers?

Answered explicitly. The KEEP rate is a supply-side signal about website adoption. It may veto a cohort for saturation. It may never confirm demand. "39.8% of auto shops lack a website" and "an auto shop will reply to Boubacar" are different claims, and treating the first as evidence for the second is exactly the substitution the crucible's evidence grader exists to reject.

The strategy — six layers

Net new code across all six: one SQL view and one argument change. Everything else is existing assets pointed at a new job.

L0 — Separate the three layers

Say out loud which layer a question belongs to before answering it. A positioning question gets refused, not answered. An offer question routes to you. Only a sourcing question enters the machine.

L1 — Pick the cohort in twenty minutes, capped

Reuse the filter written earlier today in the one-system playbook. One question, not six:

Does this business lose money when the phone does not get answered?

Then the economics: is a $500-$1,000 repeatable customer plausible here. Then the rewritten ICP prompt to turn the cohort into a profile. Output is one sentence with no "or" in it. If it has an "or", it failed.

L2 — Supply test before any contact

The geo scanner across three metros, piped into the category-whitespace profiler. Roughly six API calls of a 900/month cap that is currently near-idle. Under an hour.

  • KEEP ≥ 25% → opening, proceed.
  • KEEP ≤ 12.5% → saturated, stop. This vetoes.
  • Under 30 rows → too thin to act on. The tool says so itself.

Stated limit: supply signal only. It can stop a cohort. It cannot start one.

L3 — The commitment window (the actual mechanism)

Once committed, a cohort gets 100 unique prospects or 21 days, whichever comes LATER.

"Later," not "sooner." That word is the whole anti-quit clause.

Enforced with a coordination claim on niche:<name> with a 21-day TTL — a mutex built for agents, repurposed as a commitment device. While it is held, no second cohort may be harvested. Two additions the council forced:

  • The claim cannot lapse silently. A TTL that simply expires is indistinguishable from a commitment honoured. Release requires a verdict string attached. An expiry without one is itself the alarm.
  • Days 4 through 20 need defined content. A lock with nothing behind it is three weeks of watching a number that has not moved yet — which is precisely the trough the video describes.

Why 100: the kill threshold is already 100. Why 21 days: it is the longest real attempt in system history. The floor is set at the historical best, not at a fantasy.

L4 — The verdict is computed, not remembered

At the window's end the gate returns SCALE / KILL / ITERATE from the niche-keyed view, on a date. No judgement call, no feeling, no meeting.

L5 — A bad result kills the angle, not the market

The market only dies after two angles have died in it — which is an existing constant, not a new rule.

Live consequence: per the 09-05 ledger entry, the SW cohort at 294 sends already reads KILL on two angles. By the system's own constant, the SW audience review is not a proposal. It is due.

L6 — One cohort in flight

Everything else is a PARK with a return date and a trigger. The queue already exists and already has four rows opened today, including the speed-to-lead retainer whose blocker on file is literally "no vertical or metro is chosen" — which L1 and L2 exist to unblock.

Simulation against real data

Scenario A — auto repair, the validated candidate

L1 passes: an auto shop with an unanswered phone loses a job. L2 passes on record at 39.8% KEEP across 11 metros, well above the gate. L3 stalls — the database holds five auto-repair leads and the 580 scanned records were never persisted. L4 cannot fire at 6 sends against a 30-send floor.

The strategy stops exactly where the Executor predicted, which is the useful result: the simulation caught the failure before the calendar did.

Scenario B — roofing, the incumbent

L2 would have rejected it outright: the 8/29 sweep returned 0% KEEP for roofing in every market tested. It has nonetheless been tier-1 for months and absorbed 323 sends. L4 on real data returns KILL. L5 holds it at angle level.

Verdict: roofing should never have entered, and the cheap test that would have stopped it costs six API calls.

Scenario C — the 449-prospect cohort at 7.6%

The council called this the most valuable unexamined fact in the document. I ran it.

It is a measurement artifact, not an opportunity. The largest block is 79 prospects and 346 sends under a reply poller — meaning these are ongoing conversations rather than cold sends, so replies are counted where replies were guaranteed. The rest is 67 unattributed sends from April-May, before pipeline tagging existed.

Chasing it would have been a mistake, and the query that shows it took thirty seconds. This is also the strongest available argument for the instrument check: the funnel is not currently trustworthy enough to carry a verdict.

The ICP prompt, after promptfix

Two-phase gate, licensed uncertainty preserved. Paste at the start of every cycle.

You are helping me define one ideal client profile (ICP) for a specific niche I am
considering. I am Boubacar, 48, based in Salt Lake City. I sell AI automation and
advisory services to small owner-operated service businesses in the US.

Run this in two phases. Do not merge them.

PHASE 1 - QUESTIONS ONLY.
Ask me between 4 and 7 questions whose answers would most change your
recommendation. Ask nothing you can answer yourself. Then STOP and wait.
Do not propose an ICP, a niche, or a pain list in Phase 1. Output in Phase 1 is
the questions and nothing else.

PHASE 2 - after I answer, produce exactly these four blocks and nothing else.

1. ICP - one paragraph, maximum 60 words. Name the business type, the owner's
role, the revenue band as a single range, the employee count, and the geography.
No "or", no alternatives. One profile.

2. MARKET SIZE - how many US businesses match block 1. Give the number, the
named source, and the year of the data. If you cannot find a source you can
name, write "UNVERIFIED" and state exactly what you would need to look up. Do
not estimate from general knowledge and present it as a figure. An honest
UNVERIFIED is a correct answer here; a plausible invented number is a failure.

3. PAIN LIST - 6 to 8 problems, each one sentence in the buyer's own spoken
words, first person, the way he would say it to another owner at a counter.
"I hate that...", "I can never...", "It drives me crazy when...". Never
marketing language: no "streamline", "optimize", "leverage", "pain point",
"solution", "efficiency". If a line could appear on a software vendor's
homepage, rewrite it.
Then rank the list in a table with these columns and no others:
   Problem (his words) | Pain 1-10 | Will pay 1-10 | Why the two scores differ
Pain and willingness-to-pay are scored independently. A problem he complains
about constantly but has never paid to fix scores high on pain and low on pay,
and saying so is the point of splitting the axes.

4. THE GAP - from the ranked table, list every problem in the top 5 by
willingness-to-pay that my current services do NOT solve. State plainly whether
the highest-paying problem is one I currently sell against. If I already cover
all five, say so rather than inventing a gap to fill this block.

Hard limits: Phase 2 under 500 words. No preamble, no summary, no closing offer
to help further. Blocks 1-4, in order, then stop.

What changed: "feel free to ask questions" became a hard two-phase gate with an explicit STOP, because a soft invitation gets skipped. The market-size check gained a named-source requirement and a licensed UNVERIFIED escape so it cannot invent a figure. The pain list gained a banned-vocabulary list and a concrete register test. The single ranking split into two independent axes with a required explanation of divergence. And the gap block was added as a required output with an anti-fabrication clause.

Why this should hold when twenty-three did not

Not because the rule is stricter. Because for the first time the loop closes without anyone remembering to close it.

Every prior attempt ended the same way: a decision was made, nothing measured it, and the absence of a result was indistinguishable from a bad result. The May council was right and changed nothing, because it produced a verdict rather than a mechanism.

Three things are different, and each is verifiable rather than aspirational:

  • The verdict is computed. The gate returns SCALE / KILL / ITERATE from data, on a date. No one has to feel like deciding.
  • Quitting requires an act. Releasing the claim demands a verdict string. Silence becomes a detectable state instead of the default one.
  • The cheap test runs before the expensive one. Six API calls would have kept roofing out and saved 323 sends.

And the honest caveat the council would not let pass: none of that survives if the funnel instrumentation is wrong. Open rates above 100% mean the measuring stick is bent. The instrument check is not housekeeping — it is load-bearing, and everything else here is worth less until it is done.
