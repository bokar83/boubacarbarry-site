The five things that went wrong, in order of how much they cost

1. The ads never pointed at a registration page

Both ads sent traffic to /ai-checklist/, the free lead-magnet page. Not /workshop/, not a seat, not a checkout. Someone who saw the ad, wanted in, and clicked, landed on a checklist download. There was no path from the ad to a paid seat at any point in the month. This is the whole ballgame and it is a one-field fix.

2. The A/B test was not a test

"Free" and "Direct" ran byte-identical creative to the same URL, both tagged utm_content=track_free. The only real difference was budget. So the $100 that looks like it bought a lesson about direct-response asks bought nothing of the kind. There is no read here to act on.

3. The targeting that worked in June was thrown away

The June campaign carried a real owner-operator stack: the Small business owners behavior, plus the Owner, Owner/Manager/CEO, Owner-operator and Owner and CEO work positions. July shipped with none of it. Age 18 to 65, all genders, Sandy plus 25 miles, and nothing else. Broad targeting is a legitimate strategy when the pixel has conversion history to learn from. This pixel did not.

4. Zero custom audiences exist on the account

I checked. The count is 0. No website-visitor audience, no engagement audience, no customer list, no lookalike. Meanwhile 34,693 impressions and 619 clicks went through in July and evaporated, the pixel is alive and fired as recently as yesterday, and there are 790 leads sitting in the database. The cheapest audience available has never been built.

5. A broken tracking parameter

Both ads carried utm_medium=   paid with literal spaces in the value. It arrives as %20%20%20paid and will not group with anything else. Minor next to the rest, but it means the July attribution data is dirtier than it looks.

Correction to the number on record. The figure carried as kpi-workshop-spend-actual-meta-jul is 438.15 for 2026-07-01 to 2026-07-28. The account reports $455.69 for that exact window and $461.33 for all of July. I do not know the origin of the $438.15 and did not chase it. Use $461.33 as July's real cost.

Image direction

Lead recommendation: a screen recording still of the tool being built. Your own screen, mid-build, the file taking shape. It is the only image that proves the promise instead of decorating it, it needs nobody's permission, and it is the cheapest fix available for the weakest part of the offer, which is proof.

  • Frame 1, primary. Your screen at the moment the tool answers a real question about a real business. Slight angle, real desk, not a clean product shot.
  • Frame 2. A laptop open at a kitchen or shop table at mid-morning, natural light. It reads as the eleven-in-the-morning promise without a word of copy.
  • Frame 3, house style. Technical-drawing treatment on cream, navy line work, matching the registration page so the ad and the page feel like one object.

Do not use stock robots, glowing brains, or blue circuitry. Every competitor uses them and they signal the exact noise the copy promises to cut through.

Permission check needed before any July 30 room photo ships on this. Photos with Bill Lines or Kim Floor in frame are cleared for free-session material only. Anything promoting a paid session needs them blurred, cropped out, or a fresh permission. This is why the recommendation above avoids people entirely.
