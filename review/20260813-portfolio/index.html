01

What I do, in one breath

▸

The work is this. Sit down with a business, find what is actually costing them time, and build what fixes it. Sometimes that is AI. Often it is plain automation. Then sit next to the person who has to run it after I leave.

When a room is what is needed instead, I teach. Same principle. People work on their own laptops on their own business, and I walk the room.

You run the business. I run the systems with you.

Who is teaching

I build AI tools, and my own business runs on the ones I build. Everything I put in front of a room is already doing work in my week. That is why I can tell people what to skip.

Before this I spent about fifteen years working in HR inside large companies, watching how people take up something new and how they avoid it without ever saying no. The technology is the easy half.

02

What it takes on your side

▸

Written for the program on the other side of this page. A small business development center, a university, a chamber, or a company bringing this to its own people would hear the same thing from me.

  • I carry the logistics. Room, printing, check in, insurance, reminders, follow up. I did all of it in July. Your lift would be distribution, not production.
  • It exists and it has run. Not a proposal. A deck, a run of show timed to the minute, and a room that already happened.
  • It is vendor neutral by design. A public institution cannot be seen endorsing a product. This does not ask you to.
  • Teaching, not selling. If a session run with you means no offer at all, tell me and I will cut it.
  • It repeats, and it stretches. Same structure every time, so a second session is not a second gamble. Somebody asked for a longer version after July 30 and it does not exist yet. Say the word and I will build toward it.

The hard part of this work

Filling a room from a standing start is the hardest part of this by a wide margin. The best distribution I ever had came from an institution putting one of these in its newsletter. Nobody asked.

Ads put people in a room. An institution people already trust puts the right people in it.

The shape I would recommend

Quarterly. One ninety minute session per quarter, in your room, for the owners already in your pipeline. Often enough to build a habit, rare enough that each one fills. Two alternates I would run just as happily: once per cohort, slotted inside something you already run, or monthly and online, shorter, one trade at a time.

On money. The prices on those session pages are what I charge when I fill the room myself. With a partner it is a different question, and it usually takes one of four shapes: a flat speaker fee, a share of seat revenue, seats sponsored by the program, or already funded inside a grant. Tell me which one your program can route.

05

Why I teach it this way

▸

Most AI sessions are a demonstration. The room nods, everybody goes home with notes and nothing running. I wanted the opposite.

I am not teaching people how to use AI. I am showing them the framework for approaching a problem.

The tool you learned tonight changes next quarter. The way you approach the problem does not. Three things hold that together.

  • Not everybody needs to be a power user. AI is a bit like Excel. Everybody has it, not everybody uses it, almost nobody needs to master it. Pretending otherwise is why so much AI training fails.
  • It is a tool, not a savior and not a devil. Most people talking about this sell either utopia or doomsday. Both are self serving.
  • Everyone leaves with one thing to do. There is an old story about turkeys who went to training on how to fly and soar, and then walked home. I do not want turkeys.

It is not a skills gap. It is a permission gap. Most people just have not seen somebody like them do it.
