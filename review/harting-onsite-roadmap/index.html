2. First 90 days — the causal chain

Not a checklist. Each phase exists because the phase before it produced something the next phase needs, and the chain ends where HARTING said it should: a running AI task force with a leadership-endorsed roadmap, reporting through Daniel to the CHRO.

Days 1–30: Get the charter and the map, not the mandate

Align with Daniel and the CHRO on the task force's charter, authority and how success will be measured — before proposing a single initiative.
Produces: a scoped mandate he's actually accountable to, instead of guessing what "own the roadmap" means in practice.
Run a structured listening tour across HR functions and business units, globally, to find where AI can remove real friction — the same discipline behind the 6-to-8-hour-to-10-minute reporting dashboard he built at GE Vernova, applied here at enterprise scale instead of for himself.
Produces: a prioritized inventory of pain points and any shadow-AI usage already happening, which becomes the raw material for the first-draft roadmap.
Checkpoint, day 30: review the inventory with Daniel. Does the data support the assumptions the charter was built on, or does the charter need to flex first? This is the first planned pivot point, not a status update.

Days 31–60: Build the coalition before the mandate, prove it small before it's big

Draft the first-cut AI HR roadmap from the inventory, sequenced by impact and feasibility, and recruit the task force's founding members from the people already doing good AI-adjacent work inside HR — not appointed from the top down.
Produces: a coalition with real buy-in, which is what let the GE Vernova hiring Kaizen move a 60-day cycle under 30 — the fix worked because the people who did the work owned it, not because it was mandated.
Pilot one or two of the fastest, highest-confidence wins from the roadmap with that founding group.
Produces: an early, visible proof point — the credibility asset the harder structural changes in days 61–90 will need to survive scrutiny.
Checkpoint, day 60: did the pilot(s) actually move a number, or did they just ship? If a pilot stalls, this is the pivot point to re-sequence the roadmap around what's proven, not what was planned.

Days 61–90: Formalize, present, land the outcome HARTING named

Formalize the task force's charter, membership and reporting cadence using what days 1–60 actually proved works, not the original guess.
Produces: a task force structure that survives contact with the org, because it's built from evidence instead of an org chart drawn on day one.
Present the validated roadmap plus early pilot results to Daniel and the CHRO, with the next-90-day sequence attached.
Lands on: the outcome HARTING named — an operating AI HR transformation function, a leadership-endorsed roadmap, and a running global task force reporting through Daniel to the CHRO.
Checkpoint, day 90: the review with Daniel/CHRO IS the pivot point — whatever they push back on becomes the first item in the next 90-day sequence, not a footnote.

3. Three questions for HARTING's global AI lead, on-site
  1. Where does HR's AI task force sit relative to IT's existing AI/data governance? Is HR building its own capability deliberately separate from IT, or is this meant to be a close partnership — and where's the line on tooling, data access, and who approves what. Partially answered already (see section 1): Daniel has said HR should own this, not IT, on data-sensitivity grounds — worth confirming this holds at the CHRO/IT-leadership level too, not just as Daniel's personal read.
  2. What's already been tried inside HR — pilots, vendor tools, shadow AI usage — that either worked or stalled? Knowing this before day 30 means the listening tour maps new ground instead of rediscovering what's already been learned.
  3. What does success look like at the 12-month mark, from your own vantage point — a shipped tool, a functioning task force, or a cultural shift in how HR uses AI? That's the actual north star the 90-day plan above should be pointed at, and it's worth hearing in the AI lead's own words rather than assuming it matches Daniel's framing exactly.
