The Faceless Problem: Strategy and Roadmap

Why nothing launched, what to run instead, and the Tuesday-start sequence · 2026-09-06

TL;DR

You do not have a faceless problem, a tooling problem, or a credibility problem. You have a send problem. Two podcast pitches that scored 92/100 in your own voice have been sitting in the repo, finished, for 77 days. Nobody sent them. That single fact explains the last four months better than any strategy document, including the three written before this one.

The recommendation, in one line: stop the anonymous channels, keep the factory, point it at your own face, and put a non-negotiable daily send floor underneath everything so this plan cannot die the way the last three did.

Three things that are uncomfortable and load-bearing:

  1. The anonymous channels have to stop. 191 posts across three channels, roughly 1,159 total views, dark since early July, $0. Not because the content was bad, but because no offer ever sat behind any of them, and the daily hand-warmup they need is 45-60 minutes a day that nobody ever budgeted.
  2. The seed videos sell a business model that would score the wrong objective. Your own standing rule says booked fractional AI work is the north star and products are means. The honest base rates: median digital-product income is about $72 a month, and 44% of Gumroad products have earned exactly nothing.
  3. Going faceless throws away the only thing the 20-year-olds cannot copy. They go faceless because anonymity is their only option. You would be volunteering to hide ten years in global HR leadership at a Fortune 100, an MBA, and a working AI system you built and run. The Gen X angle is not "faceless, but better." It is: the factory is faceless, the operator is not.

Both live refinements are folded in. "How do we build the roads that lead to me?" moved the spine of this plan from content mechanics to go-to-market. "Sell the problem before the solution" is now the structural rule for every piece of copy here. The stuck-agency-builder audience is in, but narrowed hard, because "people building agencies" as written is the most saturated audience online.

The one question only you can answer: what is the daily send floor you will actually hold? 1, 3, or 5. A plan built on 5 that you hold at 0 is worth less than a plan built on 1 that you hold. Three plans have died because this number was never named.

1. What the research actually says

The two seed videos

Both are Richard Yu. Neither is a faceless-channel tutorial. Both are pitches for a digital-product business and both end at a free masterclass funnel, which shapes everything they say.

Video 1 sorts the "one-person AI business" into three models and disqualifies two. The agency is a job with extra bosses, because clients fire agencies in 3-6 months (he says he does this himself as a buyer). Software is real but the wrong first move: his own AI software company took a year, a team of engineers, and over a million dollars. That leaves digital products, built in four parts: the product (copy proven demand, never invent), the store, the content, and the ladder, where a $27-47 entry product exists to convert a stranger into a buyer and a several-thousand-dollar tier sits above it.

Video 2 is 35 product ideas in six buckets. The genuinely portable mechanics, stripped of the pitch:

  • Price by the size of the buyer's problem, not the size of your deliverable. A budget tracker protects $40 and sells for $10; a rental-property analyzer protects a $200,000 decision and sells for $99. Same build effort.
  • The narrow version of a saturated market is wide open. You cannot win "weight loss." You can own "weight loss for women in menopause."
  • Sell the tool, not the lesson. Buyers want the finished thing, not to become experts.
  • Collect the money before you build. One page, a buy button, three places the buyer already is.
  • Find the cold-start wall and sell the ladder over it.
  • Everything small is an on-ramp to one high-ticket offer, delivered done-with-you so it does not become a prison.

The skepticism these videos have earned

Every number in both is an unverifiable claim from someone selling a masterclass on the strategy those numbers are meant to prove. The $80,000-a-month friend, the $31,000-in-40-days student, the $32 million lifetime, the calligraphy community at $11,000 a month: treat all of it as marketing, not data. He is also open that he no longer runs a one-person business (75 staff across three companies), which quietly undercuts the model he is selling.

What the broader research says about your actual angle

  • Podcast guesting is the highest-ROI credibility channel available, because it borrows trust instead of building it. A host's introduction transfers authority instantly, and guesting beats hosting for anyone without an audience.
  • Gen X is an underserved audience with money, and Gen X creators are under-supplied. Audiences over 45 are markedly less receptive to obviously AI-generated content. This cuts directly against running anonymous AI-persona channels and directly for being visible.
  • Displacement is real but not where the headlines say. Workers aged 22-30 are being displaced at roughly three times the rate of workers 40-55, while 59% of US workers need reskilling by 2030. Your instinct is directionally right, but the better-funded buyer is not the displaced individual. It is the HR leader who has to absorb the disruption and has a budget line.

3. The strategy

Strategy is what the business is and why it should work. The roadmap is the sequence. You asked for both, separately.

3.1 The core move: the factory is faceless, the operator is not

The 20-year-olds go faceless because anonymity is their only option. They have no credential to put a face on, so the format hides a weakness. Copying the format copies the weakness and discards your only unfair advantage. You would enter a 3%-success-rate, 12-18-month lottery against people with more time and less to lose, having first hidden ten years in global HR leadership for a Fortune 100 company, most recently four years based in Dubai, an MBA, and a working multi-agent AI system you built and run your own business on.

So invert it. Keep the faceless machinery. Retire the faceless identity.

  • Faceless is a manufacturing method. The studio modules, content multiplier and render stack stay, eventually repointed at producing volume around your material. Same factory, different product on the line, no new build.
  • The face is the product. Nobody in the AI-content space can say what you can say.
  • The eight anonymous handles become syndication endpoints, later, once there is something worth syndicating. Not the business, not September's work.

3.2 The positioning is already decided. Do not reopen it.

Your words, on file since 2026-08-28: "I need to be an expert, the expert, when it comes to the merging of AI and HR." Settled, and this document does not reopen it. It needs one honest correction, which came from the adversarial pass, from the voice arguing as a cold outsider who is literally the buyer:

"Go-to person for the merging of AI and HR" means nothing to me, and I am the buyer. I do not wake up wanting AI and HR merged. I wake up because roles sit unfilled for 70 days, because my managers write the same review four times, because onboarding eats a week per hire.

Both are true at different layers. "AI and HR" is the category you are claiming. It is correct positioning and it is what makes hosts and newsletters want you. It is not an offer, and a buyer cannot buy it. Underneath it you need named operational pains with a budget line already attached: time-to-fill at 60-70 days, manager time burned on repeated performance narratives, onboarding eating a week per hire, policy nobody can find or trust, and an AI initiative that was announced, funded, and has produced nothing measurable. That last one is the sharpest, and it is the one you can prove.

3.3 The Gen X differentiation, stated precisely

Not "I am older, therefore trustworthy." That is a claim anyone can make and nobody can check. Three things nobody in this niche has together:

  1. You have sat on the buyer's side of the table. When an HR leader describes their constraint, you have lived inside it, with that budget, in that political weather. A 24-year-old AI creator is guessing.
  2. You built the thing and run your business on it. Not a course about AI. A working multi-agent system, plus a real analytics and automation dashboard delivered inside a Fortune 100 organization.
  3. You will publish the losses. This is the whitespace, and it was the single largest missed opportunity in the entire analysis.

3.7 Sell the problem before the solution

A format rule applied to every artifact in the roadmap, not a slogan:

  1. Name the problem in the reader's own words, more precisely than they would say it. Specific enough that they feel caught.
  2. Show the cost of it continuing. Concretely, ideally with a number, never with hype.
  3. Only then the mechanism, and only enough to prove the problem is solvable.
  4. The ask is small and singular.

Applied: a relief pitch leads with the audience-holder's problem (an empty slot on Thursday), never with your angle. A direct-buyer message leads with a named operational pain, never with "AI and HR." The ledger leads with $10,108 and $0, never with a lesson.

4. The roadmap

4.0 The spine: the send floor

Everything above is subordinate to one mechanism, because the adversarial pass converged hard on a single finding: the bottleneck is the act of sending, and every previous plan increased the amount of preparation standing between you and that act.

THE SEND FLOOR: a fixed number of outward asks you personally make every working day, that no other work is allowed to displace, counted daily on a surface that cannot auto-close itself.

Three rules make it work, each because a specific past failure is on record:

  1. It is counted daily, by you, on a durable surface. Not a Money Map prose row, those auto-closed. If the count is not written, the day is a zero. A visible zero is the whole point.
  2. Nothing may delay it, including improving it. The relief-pitch reframe in this document is better than the pitches sitting in the repo. It is also, mechanically, a fifth excuse not to send the ones you have. Day one sends the existing 92/100 drafts unedited. The improvement applies from pitch three onward.
  3. It has a kill-date. If the count is zero on Wednesday 2026-09-09, this plan is dead and it gets said out loud, not quietly rolled forward.

The rest of this roadmap assumes 3.
