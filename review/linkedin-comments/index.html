1. Amy Heitmeyer Neubelt — real data vs. predictions on AI in L&D

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7494539081838735360/

This is the right instinct. I run my own business on AI, every day, and the biggest gap I see isn't a lack of predictions, it's that most people are still guessing instead of tracking what got used a second time. Adoption isn't the moment someone tries a tool, it's the moment they open it again tomorrow without being told to. If your survey captures that repeat-use signal specifically, you'll have something most of the prediction pieces are missing. Curious what you're seeing so far, is the gap bigger at the tool level or the habit level?

2. Gael De Talhouet — the discipline of killing AI pilots

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7483527066445946880/

The line about killing pilots being the real leadership move is the one people skip past too fast. I see this from the other side, running things solo on my own AI system, the portfolio is smaller but the discipline matters just as much, maybe more, because there is no team to hide a bad pilot inside. The businesses I talk to that are stuck are not stuck on the technology, they are stuck because nobody wants to be the one who says stop. Naming that out loud, the way you just did, is worth more than another framework. That is the kind of honesty that helps the rest of us building this alone.

3. Heidi Carson — the 2026 L&D priorities cascade

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7493989480668798976/

The cascade you laid out is right, and the piece most companies skip is the third step, honestly naming the actual workforce capability gap before they go shopping for a tool. I see it constantly, leadership buys the AI solution first, then tries to reverse-engineer which capability it was supposed to fix. When I sit down with a business that feels overwhelmed by AI, the first real work is always upstream of the tool, figuring out if they even have the gap they think they have. Your framework puts that step in writing, which is more useful than most people posting about 2026 priorities are doing. Who else is teaching this cascade in a way people can apply on a Monday morning?

4. Kathi Enderes — Patricia Frost on reskilling and the human last mile

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7383879290054037504/

Patricia Frost's line about the last mile being human terrain is the part that gets lost every time this conversation turns into a technology debate. I've watched companies chase the reskilling story because it sounds better in a board deck, then hire around the people they said they would invest in once the timeline got tight. What she is describing, keeping the manager-to-team connection intact while the tools underneath change, is the harder and slower version of the same promise, and it is the one that holds up. It also matches what I see from the outside, the businesses that survive an AI transition are the ones that treated their people as the constraint to solve for, not the cost to cut. Who else is holding the line on reskilling when the pressure to just replace people shows up?
