2. Megan Bickle — past the AI pilots, humans in the loop

Megan Bickle, first-degree. Talent Management Strategist. Posted two hours ago from the iVentiv conference: teams have moved past AI pilots into real working HR products, and the honest half of the conversation was how to keep humans in the loop and employees engaged through the uncertainty.

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7496262744090767361/

Humans in the loop is where most of these conversations stop, and it is the part that needs the most work. In the room it sounds like a value. In the build it is a question with a name attached. Which human, at which step, with the authority to say no and the time to look. I have watched HR tools ship with a review step nobody was staffed to do, so it got clicked through in seconds. That is not a human in the loop. That is a human holding the pen. Worth asking the teams who got past pilots whether they named the person or only the step.

3. Marcus Lowe — agents you stop checking on

Marcus Lowe, co-founder of Anything, launching Skydive three hours ago. Agents with names and their own computers, owning whole jobs across support, engineering, recruiting and ops. His closing line: the agents he is most excited about are the ones he hardly checks on anymore.

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7496249865543254016/

I run a setup like this, and the line I would push back on is the last one. An agent I stopped checking is not my best agent. It is my least verified one. Quiet does not mean correct, it means nothing has surfaced, and those are different claims. What changed things for me was not giving agents more autonomy. It was making each one produce something I could check without rerunning its work. A file, a row, a number. If the only evidence a job got done is the agent saying it did, you have not removed the work. You have moved it to whoever finds out later.

1. Josh Bersin — The Messy World of AI-Powered Recruiting

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7495226827037188096/

The part that stings is that we have seen this movie. Keyword ATS taught a generation of candidates to stuff resumes, and we called it gaming the system. Now the model does the reading and the same stuffing works better than before. I spent years inside HR watching us buy a tool to fix the last tool's side effect. What changed is speed, not judgment. Nobody on either side of that screen has been given a reason to trust it, and trust is the only thing that was ever holding the process together.

2. Josh Bersin — Despite Massive AI Investments, HR Jobs Are Booming

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7493666146739494912/

Every automation I have built created a new decision that a person now has to own. The work does not disappear, it changes shape, and the new shape needs someone senior enough to say what good looks like. That is an HR job. My worry is not headcount. It is that we are hiring people into roles nobody has defined yet, and then measuring them against the old ones.

Corrected 2026-08-20 afternoon. The version published this morning said "I once led a Kaizen event that simplified a hiring process." That breaks your own standing rule, in your words: "We don't say I led a Kaizen. I was part of the Kaizen. I was not the main leader of it." The line below is the corrected one. Olga is naming the real failure. Saving hours is not a result. It is a step you take before the result. I was part of a Kaizen on a hiring process, and the lesson was that the hours we freed went straight to whatever was loudest, because nobody had decided in advance where they should go. Cutting the role first removes the person who would have made that call. That is not efficiency. That is deciding by accident.

4. Hung Lee — This Week in Recruiting, Issue 281

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7495092452903575552/

Half baked is generous, this one is done. The tell is who absorbs the cost. When AI writes for the market, the market filters it. When AI writes for a colleague, the colleague becomes the filter, unpaid and unasked. In my own setup I stopped letting one agent hand raw output to another for exactly that reason. Something has to sit between them and decide what is worth passing on. In a company that something is a person, and right now nobody is naming them or paying them for it.

5. Allie K. Miller — Voice workflow while walking

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7495895153958785026/

The part people will skip is that voice only works if the thing on the other end already knows your context. I talk to my own setup while I am walking too, and it is useful because it has a file that holds who I am, what I sell, and who buys it. Without that, voice just makes it faster to be vague. The walk is not the upgrade. The context you loaded before the walk is.
