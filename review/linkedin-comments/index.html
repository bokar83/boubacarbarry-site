This tracks with what I've seen from the reading side too. The real risk with AI-polished writing is not that it sounds robotic, it's that it sounds like everyone else's AI-polished writing. Sameness is the tell, not tone.

2. Ethan Mollick — agentic orchestration

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7497488356260618240/

The orchestration question is the one that gets skipped. Clients ask which model or which tool first, when the harder and more useful question is who owns the outcome when an agent's tool call gets it wrong. Adoption stalls right there, not at the technology.

3. Laszlo Bock — Seedglass launch

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7495493032792719360/

Congrats on the launch. From what I've seen, sourcing speed was rarely the real bottleneck in hiring, it was how fast the hiring manager could actually decide once good candidates showed up. If Seedglass also compresses that decision step, not just the sourcing step, that is where the 105x really compounds.

4. David Green — Workday going private

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7496897746621231104/

Going private removes one constraint, the quarterly pressure, but it does not automatically pick the right one to work on next. The real test is whether that freed-up space gets spent rebuilding for a world where agents sit inside HR workflows, or just gets spent on cost cuts that look good on a slide.

5. Meghan M. Biro — workforce planning as GPS

https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7496614279446458368/

The GPS framing is the right one. The annual spreadsheet did not survive this long because it worked, it survived because it was the only thing leadership knew how to budget and hold people accountable against. Real-time workforce planning needs a different accountability model underneath it, not just better data on top of the old one.
