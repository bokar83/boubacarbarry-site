Premortem (council skill, premortem mode)

Three recipient personas roleplayed cold against both versions, then a full 5-voice Council premortem on the whole approach, 6 months out, decision already dead.

Persona A — chamber CEO pitched constantly (Draper, Davis, South Jordan, ChamberWest style)

V1: Opens because the opener names her org specifically, not a mail-merge tell. Reads past line 1. Doesn't smell like a disguised pitch, "I don't know yet" language disarms that read. But the body is generic enough that she's seen 4 or 5 versions of "I do AI work for business owners" this quarter already — nothing distinguishes this from the pack except the concrete dates. Likely reaction: files it, might reply eventually, probably needs a second touch to actually book.

V2: Same opener, but the body is scannable in about 8 seconds — she reads 200 emails a day, this fits between two others. "Got 15 minutes" lowers the bar to reply. Slight risk: brevity can read as templated to someone sharp at spotting mail-merge, but the specific dates and the personalized opener carry it. Her verdict: "Short enough that I answered it between two other things. Any longer and it goes in the pile I never get back to."

Persona B — volunteer-run service club president (Kiwanis SLC, Murray Rotary, SLC Lions style)

V1: More receptive by default — these are people always hunting for a program, so "worth exploring for your members" solves a real, standing problem. The fuller sentences read warmer, fits the more relational culture of a volunteer club. Likely reply: "This actually sounds like a good program for us, let's talk."

V2: The offer itself still lands (exactly the kind of thing they're short on), but the clipped tone reads a little corporate for a Thursday-lunch crowd used to warmer exchanges. Still likely to reply because the content is genuinely relevant, but with a slightly higher chance of feeling impersonal.

Persona C — closed CEO peer-group chair (Vistage / Stephanie Geisler)

Both versions: Format mismatch, not a copy problem. Vistage groups are peer-led and closed to outside guest speakers by design — already flagged in the original research. Neither version addresses that structural barrier. Her likely reaction: "Nice note, wrong ask for my format. I chair a closed CEO group, I don't book outside speakers the way a chamber does." Probably no reply, or a polite "that's not how this works" rather than a booked meeting. This is an org-fit issue independent of which version ships.

Full Council premortem — 6 months out, decision already dead

THE CONTRARIAN: It died because both versions leaned on the same weak lever: a vague "worth exploring" ask with no concrete next artifact and no forced follow-up. Curious recipients had no low-friction way to say yes beyond replying cold. Replies trickled to almost nothing over 2–3 weeks, both versions blurred into "another AI guy" in a saturated inbox. No meetings booked by month 2, and the planned phone-call follow-ups never got scheduled because nothing forced the second touch.

THE FIRST PRINCIPLES THINKER: The wrong assumption, never written down: that a well-crafted first email converts a gatekeeper to a meeting on its own. It never was going to. These are people who filter by relationship and reputation, not hook quality. The real lever was a second credibility signal, a referral or a first "yes" to point to, and the plan never built one before firing all 14.

THE OUTSIDER: A stranger reading this cold sees an unsolicited email from someone with no visible track record with THIS specific org. Every one of the 14 gets pitched by consultants weekly. Neither version answers the first question a skeptical gatekeeper asks: who else has done this and gotten a result? No case study, no name of a prior org that ran the session. That gap was visible from the outside on day one.

THE EXPANSIONIST: Never tried: leading with a name-recognizable proof point once one existed, "I just ran this for [org], thought of you next" for send 5 through 14. Never tried: attaching one concrete artifact (a short clip from the July session, a one-page "what a session looks like") so a curious-but-busy recipient could self-qualify without needing a call first. Either move would have raised perceived credibility far more than any wording change.

THE EXECUTOR: The warning sign was visible in week 1: zero replies from the first 3–4 sends within 5 business days. That silence should have triggered an immediate follow-up call the same week. Instead the calls got deprioritized against other work, and by week 3 the emails were cold and forgotten in 14 inboxes with no call ever landing to revive them.

CHAIRMAN SYNTHESIS. Convergence: all five voices agree the real failure mode in both versions was never the phrasing, it was the missing next-touch mechanism and the missing growing proof point. Both versions clear the "not a pitch" bar; neither one alone was ever going to close 14 gatekeepers on copy quality.

Divergence: the Contrarian and Executor place the failure downstream (no follow-through discipline); the First Principles voice and Outsider place it upstream (no credibility lever in the ask itself). Both are true and point to different fixes.

Recommendation: CHANGE, not kill. Send Version 2 first, but sequence it, easiest 2–3 targets first (Kiwanis SLC, Murray Rotary read as best-fit per the persona roleplay), bank the first yes as a real proof line for the rest, and build the phone-call follow-up into the calendar before the first send, not after silence.

Open question only you can answer: send all 14 in one batch, or a first wave of 2–3 to bank a real proof point before touching the harder ones (Vistage, and Rotary SLC which still has no verified contact)?
