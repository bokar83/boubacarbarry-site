What broke, and what to do about it

Two ideas explain everything below. Get these and you do not need the deep-dive sections unless you want the receipts.

1. The "break the circle" idea

Cold strangers will not pay to see an unknown, first-time host with no proof. But the only place proof comes from is a room that already happened. No room means no proof, and no proof means no room. The circle stays empty and ads cannot fix it, because ads scale what already works -- they cannot create demand from zero.

You break the circle by filling July without cold ads:

  1. Borrow a trusted audience. Get the SBDC, the Chamber, or a partner to put the event in front of their list. Their trust transfers to you in one email.
  2. Send direct personal invites. Real one-to-one messages to warm contacts and local connections, not a broadcast.
  3. Run the event and capture the proof. Full-room photos, testimonials, a clean recording, real attendance numbers.
  4. Now turn on paid ads. They finally have proof to point at, so they convert. This is where ads belong -- last, not first.
Decision needed Monday -- July pricing
July is a PROOF event. Price it for attendance, not revenue.
Recommended
$47

Low enough that people say yes, still signals real value, and keeps the Stripe funnel and buyer-intent data live. Comp a block of seats for a partner's list to guarantee bodies in the room.

Alternative
Free

Maximum bodies, lowest friction -- use this only if a partner commits to filling the room. The trade-off: weaker buyer signal and a harder shift to paid for event three.

Decision: $47 vs free. Default to $47 with comped partner seats. Go free only if a partner guarantees the fill. Either way, the goal of July is proof in the room, not money in the account. Revenue comes from event three, once the proof exists.

▸03 Why We Did Not Get People to Sign Up

This section goes deepest. The second Council pass (run on a stronger model) hardened the diagnosis and re-ranked the causes. The "no signups" vs "signups that don't show" distinction is addressed below.

Diagnostic clarity first There were zero signups -- not "signups who did not show." The breakdown was entirely at the conversion step, not the commitment or logistics step. There was no attendance problem. There was a purchase problem.
What the second pass changed vs the first The first pass ranked "personal asks never sent" as the heaviest cause. The second pass found that was a symptom, not the root. The deeper truth: there was no warm local list to ask and no borrowed list activated, so cold paid traffic was the only channel -- and cold paid traffic cannot sell a first-ever paid event from an unknown host. You were trying to sell proof you had not made yet. Three other re-rankings followed: borrowed-list now outranks personal asks (it supplies the audience AND the trust), the ad problem is reframed from "too few creatives" to "wrong objective bought junk clicks," and the 13-day runway drops from a top cause to an amplifier that merely exposed the missing foundation.

Root Cause Hierarchy (Hardened, Second Pass)

1
The proof was never manufactured -- so there was nothing to sell trust with

A first-ever event has a chicken-and-egg problem: you cannot sell paid seats to strangers without proof (a full room, photos, testimonials, a recording), and you cannot get that proof without first running a full event. This was never broken. With no warm local list and no borrowed list activated, cold paid ads were the only channel left -- and cold ads asking strangers to buy a first-ever $47 event from an unknown host is asking them to pay for the very proof the event was meant to produce. This single root absorbs what the first pass split into "no personal asks" and "no social proof." They are the same problem: nothing warm and nothing proven behind the ad.

Heaviest
2
Borrowed-list / partnership channel never executed

AP's first piece of advice was "borrow the list from local orgs." It is the single highest-leverage move for a founder with no local audience, because it supplies both the people AND the trust transfer in one email from a name they already trust. SBDC and the chamber were treated as passive co-marketers, never as list partners. This now ranks above personal asks: personal asks only work if you already hold a warm local list, and that list did not exist at the scale needed.

Heavy
3
Personal / warm asks never sent

No direct human message went to IC Slack members, Utah-local connections, or warm contacts. This is free, same-day, and the highest-converting channel you fully control. It stays high -- but it is capped by the size of the warm local list, which is the gap named in cause 1. A national or online network does not fill a Sandy room. Still, zero asks sent while $200 went to anonymous ads is the order of operations reversed.

Heavy
4
Wrong ad objective and hardest-possible format stacked the difficulty

Meta optimized for Traffic, not Purchases, with no pixel -- so it bought the cheapest, lowest-intent clicks it could find. That means the "350 views" stat is partly vanity: a chunk were never buyers, which is a large part of why the page converted at 0.0% rather than a weak-but-nonzero 0.5%. The objective error did more damage than the "3 creatives instead of 9" gap. On top of it, the format itself was the hardest fillable combination: in-person + paid + cold + first-time + 13-day runway. A free or co-hosted virtual session would have removed the geographic cap and the venue risk while still producing the recording and testimonials.

Significant
5
Cold seat page had no proof -- and possibly hard funnel friction

Zero testimonials, zero past-event photos, no counter, no countdown, from an unknown organizer. A cold audience evaluating a $47 purchase needs all of those. But 0.0% (not 0.5%) is worth a second look on its own: a broken or high-friction checkout, a mobile bug, or price-hidden-until-checkout would also produce exactly 0%. Audit the actual checkout flow before assuming the only fix is trust copy.

Contributing
6
13-day runway -- now seen as the trigger, not the root

The first pass ranked this a top-three cause. The second pass demotes it: 13 days fills a room if a warm or borrowed list exists, and 13 weeks would not have filled it without one. The short runway did not cause the failure -- it removed the slack that would have let the missing foundation get built in time. It exposed the real gap rather than being it.

Amplifier
Council Pressure Test -- Against the "Ads Are the Gate" Hypothesis

His hypothesis: "The ads are the gate. Better ads, properly A/B tested, would have filled the room."

Council verdict: Partially correct. Structurally incomplete.

Better ads plus A/B testing plus a longer runway equals meaningful improvement. True. But "ads fix everything" externalizes the problem to an improvable tool while leaving the harder problems untouched. AP's ads work because AP has social proof and a community. Better Boubacar ads pointed at the same zero-social-proof page still underperform. The personal-asks channel was skipped entirely -- not an ad problem. The borrowed-list channel was never executed -- also not an ad problem.

Corrected hypothesis for July: Ads fill the room IF the page has social proof from the recording, the borrowed-list channel runs first, personal asks go out simultaneously, and the ad system has a pixel, retargeting, and 9 tested variants. Ads are the amplifier. The other three are the foundation.

Second-pass escalation: there is a layer beneath even that. For the next event specifically, ads may not belong in the foundation OR the amplifier yet -- because there is still almost no proof to amplify. The harder, more honest move is to treat the next event as a proof factory: fill the room by borrowed list, personal asks, and comped anchor guests; charge little or nothing; capture the full-room photos, testimonials, and a clean recording. Run heavy paid acquisition on the event after that, when there is finally proof worth scaling. Spending on ads to sell seats you cannot yet prove is the same mistake at a higher budget.

▸04 How to Fix It

Concrete and sequenced. Next week first, then July. The order matters as much as the individual steps.

Fix order matters Decide first, then sequence. Step 0 (Monday): decide if the next event is a paid push or a proof event filled by borrowed trust and direct asks. Reversing this order again produces the same result.
