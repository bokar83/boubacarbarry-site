▸★ Read this first
Plain English

What happened, in six steps

No jargon, no internal shorthand. If you read nothing else on this page, read this.

  1. We made the seat free. June charged for the seat and sold none. July gave the seat away and eighteen people claimed one.
  2. The list filled fast and late. Every one of the eighteen signed up inside an eleven-day window, and eight of them in the final six days. Nobody booked a month ahead.
  3. Two people told us in advance they could not come. That left sixteen expected in the room.
  4. Four walked in. Eduardo, Kimberly, Bill and Luke. Nobody showed up who had not registered. The other twelve simply did not arrive.
  5. The night itself worked. Nothing was cut, nothing broke, everyone stayed after to talk, and three of the four turned into meetings this week.
  6. The follow-up landed and moved nobody. Sixteen emails went out the next day. One person replied. Nobody booked anything.

The one sentence that matters

Free fixed the problem June had and created the problem July has. A seat that costs nothing is a seat that costs nothing to skip.

What we still cannot tell you

Why twelve people did not come. The door sheet names them. It does not explain them, and nothing was asked of them afterwards, so there is no exit survey to fall back on. Boubacar's read is that they forgot despite the reminders, or that it never rose high enough on their list. That is a read, not a finding. Worth knowing before you accept it: those twelve have already received one email since, and not one of them wrote back.

▸01 What We Did Well

01 What we did well

Free removed the constraint June could not get past

June ran paid and produced zero registrations against 350 landing page views and more than $200 of ad spend. July ran free and produced eighteen registrations. That is not a marginal improvement, it is the difference between a funnel that moves and one that does not. Fifteen of the eighteen came through the free-seat page itself, two from the newsletter list and one was added by hand.

The room worked

Boubacar's account: the room felt energized, questions came throughout rather than being saved for the end, and all four attendees stayed after to talk to him. Four people who stay after is a different signal to four people who leave at 8:30.

One of them, Luke, came while on vacation with a four-month-old at home. He leads the group the invitation went out through, he shot photos and video of the room, and he posted them publicly on the Thursday and the Friday. Those are the only visual record of the night we have, and an attendee made them rather than us.

Delivery held, and the deck stands

  • Nothing was cut live. A few slides were moved past quickly. Boubacar's position is that the deck stands as it is and he would not tweak it.
  • The clock held. The night started five to ten minutes late because people had not arrived on time, still covered everything, and finished inside the contracted 8:30 PM room window. That was pre-registered as the second most likely thing to hurt the week. It did not.
  • The equipment held. Wifi, projector and the offline copy of the deck all worked. The offline copy was built precisely so a wifi failure could not end the night, and it was not needed.

The reminder sequence fired

Three of the eight defined email touches are confirmed sent from the mailer's own state file: two days out to 17 recipients, the night before to 16, and the day-of to 16. The day-of send was pre-registered as the single most likely thing to break, one gate-check away from silently not sending. It sent.

Worth naming rather than rounding away: the 17 at two days out against 16 on the later two sends is not a send failure. One address present at T-2 is absent from both later sends, and a different address absent at T-2 appears in both. That is a roster change mid-week, and it is visible only because the counts were checked rather than assumed.

Every follow-up went out, and we can prove it

Within a day of the event all sixteen follow-ups were sent and independently confirmed twice, against the send log and against a live re-fetch of every individual message from the sent folder on 2026-08-03.

The recap decision

The group recap email was written, passed all three quality gates on 2026-07-29, and was deliberately never sent. The archived note in the mailer reads:

That is the most defensible call of the week. A broadcast to four people reads as a broadcast. The personal notes replaced it, and they produced the only reply and the only piece of external proof this run generated. Quality was never the reason the recap was held. Size was.

The follow-up is already converting, in the room at least

Three of the four attendees turn into meetings this week. Eduardo came on behalf of his boss and produced a meeting with the decision maker. Kimberly and Bill are meeting Boubacar again as a pair. Bill replied to his thank-you note the next morning, unprompted:

Bill, the morning after, unprompted "We hope you'll develop some day-long workshops we can attend, as well."

In the same reply he wrote that he is applying what he learned to a live invoicing problem in his business. That is the closest thing this run produced to a documented result, and nobody has yet asked him for permission to publish it. See section 07.

The build shipped and was verified

The print pack, the bottle labels, the room signage, the handout and the check-in roster all shipped with published copies and recorded file hashes. The deck shipped as a live page, a presenter view and a PDF cut from 15.9MB down to 704KB, with screenshot evidence captured rather than assumed. The two private files, the check-in roster and the host briefs, were correctly kept off every public surface.

▸02 What We Did Not Do Well

02 What we did not do well

The Big One June was run with no attendance instrument at all. July had one, and it worked: a printed check-in sheet at the door, one row per registered email, ticked by hand as people arrived. That sheet is where the 4 comes from and it is why section 03 can be trusted. The half that is still missing is the system half. There is no attendance column in the registration table, so the count lives on a photograph of a sheet of paper. Everything else we know about these people lives in the database.

We never told the room why to listen

Luke gave this verbally after the session. He said the night was really good and that he liked what Boubacar did with it. His one fix: establish credibility at the very beginning. Some proof of background and expertise, so the room understands why it should be listening before the teaching starts.

This is the first substantive attendee feedback captured anywhere in either run, and it lands on the opening. The deck goes from the title slide straight into what the night is. Nothing in it answers the question a room asks silently in the first two minutes, which is why this person and not somebody else.

It matters more for the virtual sessions than for the room. Somebody who drove to a building, parked, walked into Room 105 and picked up a name tag has already decided to give the night a chance. The room itself does most of that work. A virtual attendee gets none of it. No room, no host introduction, no cost already sunk, and one click to leave. Credibility has to land in the first ninety seconds or they are gone.

We predicted the wrong failure

The week before the event we wrote down the five things most likely to hurt us: the day-of email silently failing, the night running past 8:30, the recap not firing without a manual flag, the print files not being where he would look for them, and nobody having seen the material cold. Four of the five were real and were handled. None of the five was low attendance.

That is the finding, not the risk list. We spent the week de-risking delivery, which June had already proven, and spent none of it de-risking the one thing we had never measured.

Sixteen follow-up emails produced one reply and no bookings

This is the hardest number on the page, and it is the one that was almost missed. Delivery is not in question: every message was confirmed twice. Twelve no-shows received a personal follow-up the day after the event and not one of them has written back. Four attendees received a personal note and one replied. Across the entire follow-up there are zero bookings on the calendar.

The honest reading is that we cannot separate the two candidate causes yet. It may be a copy and offer problem. It is definitely partly a plumbing problem, because the link every one of those emails pointed at cannot be used by someone holding a normal job. Until the evening band exists, a zero here is not evidence about demand, it is evidence about our own funnel. Fix the link first, then a second zero would actually mean something.

The registration page has no way to say no

One registrant came back to the page at 15:52 Mountain on the day of the event, roughly three hours before doors, and re-claimed the seat. The record shows an update to an existing row, not a second signup. Boubacar's read is that this person was trying to cancel and the page gave them nothing to cancel with. The database cannot confirm intent, so that read stays a read. What is not in doubt is the outcome: they did not attend, and a person who tried to tell us became indistinguishable from a person who forgot. A missing cancel path inflates the no-show count with the people who were trying to be polite.

Kim asked twice and we had nothing to give her

She asked, on the night, twice, for marketing material she could hand to other people. That is an attendee volunteering to distribute for us, in the room, unprompted, and the ask is still unfilled. It is the cheapest item on the August list and it has the shortest path to a new seat.

We cannot draw the funnel

June has both landing page views and ad spend on the record. July has neither, because nobody opened the ads account for this review. Without those two numbers the month-over-month comparison has holes in exactly the cells that would tell us whether free improved conversion or simply improved traffic.

A superseded email is still sitting in the mailer

The mailer still carries an older version of the no-show body that was replaced before the real send went out. It is disarmed and it has sent nothing. If anyone ever arms it, the same twelve people receive a second, superseded email. Leave it disarmed, and delete it once run 3 is scoped.

We produced no record of our own night

The only photographs and video of the July room were taken by an attendee, on vacation, on his own phone, and published on his own accounts. That worked out. It should not have been the plan.

▸03 Why 12 of 16 Did Not Show

03 Why 12 of 16 did not show

Read This Before the Rest of the Section We do not know. The check-in sheet names exactly who did not walk through the door, so the twelve are not in question. Why they stayed away is, and nothing was ever asked of them. There was no exit survey and no question put to the no-shows afterwards. What follows is a ranked set of hypotheses with the evidence for and against each, and the test that would settle it. Nothing below is a finding.

June's version of this section asked why nobody signed up. July's asks the opposite question, and that inversion is the most useful thing on this page. The two runs fail at opposite ends of the same funnel. We have now proven we can get commitment and we have proven we can deliver. We have never proven we can convert a commitment into a body in a chair.

Boubacar's read, stated as his

He does not know why. His read is that they forgot despite the reminders, or that it was not a high enough priority. Nobody messaged him on the day to bail, which is consistent with both readings.

One piece of evidence arrived after the event

All twelve were emailed the following day and every send is confirmed. Not one has replied. That does not tell us why they missed the session, but it does narrow things. These are not people who were blocked by a delivery failure and it does not look like a group that was merely inconvenienced on the night. Twelve out of twelve staying silent when written to personally reads more like a list that was never deeply committed than like a room that got unlucky.

Hypotheses, ranked

RankHypothesisWhat supports itWhat would test it

2 They forgot, or it slid down the list. Boubacar's own read. Three reminder emails went out and all three are confirmed sent, so the failure is not a missing reminder. It would be an ignored one. Ask them directly with a question they can answer in six seconds.

What we are deliberately not concluding

Hypothesis 1 is the one everyone will reach for, and it is the one with the least direct evidence behind it. It is structurally clean and it fits the June contrast, which makes it seductive rather than proven. Twelve people is a large enough group that more than one of these is almost certainly true at once.

The Instrument That Would Answer This A one-question email asking the twelve what got in the way. It is written and it has passed the voice gate at 93 out of 100. It has not been sent and it will not be until Boubacar says so. Until it goes out, this entire section stays a set of hypotheses, and run 3 gets planned against a guess. Worth going in clear-eyed: these twelve have already ignored one personal email, so a second one has to earn a reply the first did not, and a low response rate is itself an answer.

These two data points argue in opposite directions and this page will not pretend otherwise. Charging again carries a real risk of repeating June's zero. It is recorded here as Boubacar's decision, taken with that risk stated rather than buried.

The tension underneath, named properly

Reading these as "paid failed, free worked" is the comfortable version and it is not quite right. June did not fail because the price was wrong. It failed because nobody buys a room from a stranger sight unseen. July did not succeed because it was free. Free lowered the bar far enough for curiosity to clear it, and curiosity is not the same thing as demand.

Stated as plainly as it deserves: nobody has yet paid for this workshop without first experiencing it. That sentence is the actual risk in the August decision, and it is sharper than "paid produced zero once." It also points straight at where the money most likely is. Four people have now experienced it. They are the only people alive with a real basis to pay.

What is different this time, and it is not nothing

June sold to strangers from a standing start. August does not. These are the specific reasons the same price may not produce the same result, stated at their real size.

  • There is a list now, which is not the same as an audience. Eighteen people clicked a button for a free seat. Four of them have actually seen the product. Fourteen have not, and twelve of those fourteen have since ignored a personal email. In June there was nobody at all, so this is real progress and it is still eighteen people.
  • There is the beginning of social proof. One attendee posted photos and video publicly on the Thursday and Friday. One other wrote, unprompted, that he wants a longer version. That is two people, not a wave, and neither has yet given permission to use their words on a sales page. June had a recording of an empty room.
  • There are four warm people who can refer. A referred paid seat is a different sale to a cold paid seat.
  • The ask window is two weeks, not four. Boubacar's reasoning, and the registration dates support it: almost everyone who signed up for July did so in the last couple of weeks, and people who sign up far ahead forget about it.

What has to be true for this to work

Stated as conditions rather than predictions, so they can be checked afterwards rather than argued about now.

  • The July list is actually worked. Twelve no-shows plus two who declined is a warm list of fourteen, and it is the primary audience for the paid run rather than a leftover.
  • That list is approached knowing it has already ignored one email. Sending the same kind of message again and expecting a different result is the failure mode here.
  • The four attendees are asked for referrals before the campaign opens, not during.
  • Bill's written result, or another attendee's, is published with permission before the paid page goes live. Paid needs the proof that free did not require.
  • The booking band is widened first. A paid campaign pointing at a link working people cannot use repeats July's zero bookings at a higher price.
  • The two-week window is treated as a constraint on preparation, not only on advertising. A shorter window with the same preparation is simply a smaller campaign.
Recommended gate before the August page goes live

Every previous run shipped with the same gaps open, which is how they stayed open. This is the smallest set that stops August repeating July, offered as a recommendation to sign off or override rather than as a decision already taken.

  • Attendance is instrumented, so run 3 produces a measured number instead of a remembered one.
  • The evening booking band is open, so the paid campaign does not point at the same closed door.
  • At least one attendee result is in writing with permission to publish it.
  • The four who attended have been asked for referrals, before the campaign rather than during.

Two numbers only Boubacar can set, and the page deliberately leaves them blank: the ticket price, and the floor he will not discount below. Both belong on the record before the page is live, not in week two.

How we will know, either way

Whatever August does, it is only worth running if the result is readable afterwards. Written down now so it cannot be reinterpreted later.

  • Paid registrations above zero settles the question June left open. Even a handful proves people will buy without experiencing it first.
  • Paid registrations at zero is not a repeat of June, it is a stronger result than June, because this time there was a list, proof and referrals behind it. That would say the constraint is the offer itself rather than the audience.
  • Show rate against July's 25 percent is the whole point of charging. If a paid room shows up at a materially higher rate, the free-seat hypothesis in section 03 is confirmed.
The Failure Mode to Watch A paid run that fills slowly will create pressure to drop the price at the last minute. June already established that honoring public pricing matters for credibility. Decide the floor before the page goes live, not in week two.

What broke, and what to do about it

Two ideas explain everything below. Get these and you do not need the deep-dive sections unless you want the receipts.

1. The "break the circle" idea

Cold strangers will not pay to see an unknown, first-time host with no proof. But the only place proof comes from is a room that already happened. No room means no proof, and no proof means no room. The circle stays empty and ads cannot fix it, because ads scale what already works -- they cannot create demand from zero.

You break the circle by filling July without cold ads:

  1. Borrow a trusted audience. Get the SBDC, the Chamber, or a partner to put the event in front of their list. Their trust transfers to you in one email.
  2. Send direct personal invites. Real one-to-one messages to warm contacts and local connections, not a broadcast.
  3. Run the event and capture the proof. Full-room photos, testimonials, a clean recording, real attendance numbers.
  4. Now turn on paid ads. They finally have proof to point at, so they convert. This is where ads belong -- last, not first.
Decision needed Monday -- July pricing
July is a PROOF event. Price it for attendance, not revenue.
Recommended
$47

Alternative
Free

Maximum bodies, lowest friction -- use this only if a partner commits to filling the room. The trade-off: weaker buyer signal and a harder shift to paid for event three.

Decision: $47 vs free. Default to $47 with comped partner seats. Go free only if a partner guarantees the fill. Either way, the goal of July is proof in the room, not money in the account. Revenue comes from event three, once the proof exists.

Diagnostic clarity first There were zero signups -- not "signups who did not show." The breakdown was entirely at the conversion step, not the commitment or logistics step. There was no attendance problem. There was a purchase problem.

Root Cause Hierarchy (Hardened, Second Pass)

1
The proof was never manufactured -- so there was nothing to sell trust with

A first-ever event has a chicken-and-egg problem: you cannot sell paid seats to strangers without proof (a full room, photos, testimonials, a recording), and you cannot get that proof without first running a full event. This was never broken. With no warm local list and no borrowed list activated, cold paid ads were the only channel left -- and cold ads asking strangers to buy a first-ever $47 event from an unknown host is asking them to pay for the very proof the event was meant to produce. This single root absorbs what the first pass split into "no personal asks" and "no social proof." They are the same problem: nothing warm and nothing proven behind the ad.

Heaviest
2
Borrowed-list / partnership channel never executed

AP's first piece of advice was "borrow the list from local orgs." It is the single highest-leverage move for a founder with no local audience, because it supplies both the people AND the trust transfer in one email from a name they already trust. SBDC and the chamber were treated as passive co-marketers, never as list partners. This now ranks above personal asks: personal asks only work if you already hold a warm local list, and that list did not exist at the scale needed.

No direct human message went to IC Slack members, Utah-local connections, or warm contacts. This is free, same-day, and the highest-converting channel you fully control. Heavy
4

Meta optimized for Traffic, not Purchases, with no pixel -- so it bought the cheapest, lowest-intent clicks it could find. Zero testimonials, zero past-event photos, no counter, no countdown, from an unknown organizer. A cold audience evaluating a $47 purchase needs all of those. Audit the actual checkout flow before assuming the only fix is trust copy.

Contributing

The first pass ranked this a top-three cause. The second pass demotes it: 13 days fills a room if a warm or borrowed list exists, and 13 weeks would not have filled it without one. The short runway did not cause the failure -- it removed the slack that would have let the missing foundation get built in time. It exposed the real gap rather than being it.
