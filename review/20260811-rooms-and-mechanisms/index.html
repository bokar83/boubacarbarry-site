1. The Rotary story, read properly

The naive reading is "go to networking events." That reading throws away everything that made the story work.

Eleven of twelve is not a conversion rate. No consultant converts eleven of twelve people they meet at a lunch. It is what happens when the mechanism does the selecting for you, and then hands you the room already warmed.

Two independent sources, same mechanism, and this makes the play more credible rather than less. Your ghostwriting programme's own material teaches sourcing from companies that just closed funding, on the reasoning that companies flush with cash are required by their investors to spend it. That is the same discovery arrived at from a completely different industry: money that has just landed, with an obligation attached, creates a buyer who has to act. When a pattern shows up twice from unrelated directions, it is usually structural rather than lucky.

The attachment test, and how to use it

Score any candidate mechanism out of five. A room you merely attend scores one, and weakly. A program you serve scores four or five. Use it to rank, but never on its own, because it measures quality and says nothing about time. Every item below carries a separate clock score, since the best mechanism on the page has the worst timing and would otherwise rank first.

The direction of attachment matters as much as the target

She did not apply to be a supplier. She served the program. That is why the endorsement transferred. Being on an approved vendor list makes you a cost the participants are spending money on. Being part of the delivery makes you someone the host has already vouched for. The practical version: mentor, instructor, office-hours provider, judge, or speaker, in that order of preference. Never "please add me to your list of consultants."

Said plainly, because it should not be dressed up: this is one story, from one person, relayed to you second-hand, with no visibility into what she charged, how long the engagements ran, or whether they were $500 or $50,000. It is an anecdote with an unusually good shape, and a good shape is exactly what makes an anecdote persuasive beyond its evidence. It is worth reproducing. It is not proof.
