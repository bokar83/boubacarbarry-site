The angle

What you actually teach

How one person runs a company's worth of work with an AI operating system. Not a lecture on prompts. You take one real thing you built and walk through the whole move: idea, to directing a set of AI agents to research and build it, to putting it live, to testing it, to fixing what broke on the first try.

The case study is the AI phone assistant you built this session. It answers a call, holds a real conversation, and switches languages mid-call. And you did it in an afternoon with receipts: the build, the live test, the carrier issue you diagnosed and cleared. That is a stronger proof than any course ad you have read, because it happened and you can show it.

Why you, and no one else in the niche You lived the full analog to digital to AI run, and you built a real working system you run your own business on. Most people teaching this either grew up with the tech or have never shipped anything with it. You did both the hard transition and the real build. That is the bridge nobody else is standing on.

The audience

Who it is for

Business owners who did not grow up with this technology. National, not local. The Gen X operator and the older owner who runs a real business, is not anti-AI, and is tired of being taught by a 25-year-old who has never made payroll. They trust a peer who speaks their language and has done the work. This is an underserved, higher-trust, often higher-budget audience most AI marketers skip past.

Second lane, same session: the HR buyer. HR is your seat, roughly fifteen years of it and about ten at a Fortune 100. The same teaching session, framed for HR people who want to up-skill in AI, is the natural on-ramp to the HR product direction you are chasing. One recording, two audiences.

The funnel fit

Free teaching → where it leads

This is not a standalone piece of content. It plugs into the map as the authority-and-traffic lane that feeds the paid lanes over time. The quick demos already on the map are the short clips that point here; this session is the long-form asset they point to. One content plan, not two.

1Short demos (already on the map, weekend record block). 30 to 90 second clips on YouTube and social. Top of the funnel, the scroll-stoppers.
2The teaching session (this plan). The 20 to 35 minute walkthrough the clips send people to. Gated by an email opt-in. Mid-funnel, the lead magnet.
3The email list + starter files. They give an email, they get the session plus the real starter files you use. Now you can nurture.
4Two exit ramps. The HR product direction for the HR crowd, and the fractional-operator consulting or a future paid course for the owners. The session warms both.
