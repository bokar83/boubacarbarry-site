One diagnostic habit, three domains, one author who has lived and written about all three.

Memoir question, answered directly

Per the brief: is "Building Is A Debt I Am Paying" the same book as "the book of my life," or a different one?

They are not the same book yet, and forcing them together now would hurt both. "Building Is A Debt I Am Paying" as currently scoped is Act One only — birth through age eleven, entirely sourced from a single 2014 interview transcript. It does not currently contain a single dated adult-life chapter: no RBL Group years, no corporate-exit day, no marriage or fatherhood chapter, no founding decision, no AI-build era. The full "book of my life" people have been asking for 35-40 years would need all of that, plus the childhood act, plus — arguably — material that currently lives in concepts 1, 4, 5, and 6 as their own standalone stories.

Recommendation (Council's Expansionist and Executor voices, independently converged on this): ship the shorter, focused books first. Once 2-3 of them exist as finished, real, published work, "the book of my life" becomes an achievable omnibus — a longer volume that pulls the origin chapters from Building Is A Debt, the founder-arc chapters from The Empty Room and I Built My Own Employees, and adds the adult-life connective tissue (RBL years, marriage, faith, the founding decision) that none of the current source material covers yet. That is a real, buildable path to the memoir, built out of work that's already shippable, rather than one from-scratch project competing with everything else for writing hours. The alternative — writing one single life-spanning book now — is the honest option too, but it means starting from the thinnest material on this entire list (a single interview transcript) rather than the richest.

This is a judgment call for Boubacar to make, not one this analysis makes for him. Both paths are real; the ladder path is simply more finishable given what exists in the corpus today.

Addendum, 2026-08-25: concept 8 — a Gen X + AI sarcastic book series (NEW)

Boubacar's brief, verbatim: "And do we do a book series on AI for Gen X? Kind of have the tone of a Gen Xer who's very sarcastic. We don't care about anything. We don't care what people think about us and just kind of how we approach and how we could and should approach AI. It might be a series of different books but just an idea. I'm going to take that area and own it what do you think of that and let's go ahead and say that as well."

This is a distinct concept, not a rename of concept 2. Concept 2, "It's Kind Of Like Excel," already covers the Gen X / mid-career-worker AI-adoption angle, but in his usual measured, consulting-adjacent voice. This new idea shares the same demographic but is a different product: a deliberately irreverent, sarcastic, "we don't care what people think" register, conceived as a series with its own name, not a single book.

Assessment (asked for directly: "what do you think of that")

Good niche to own, worth doing — with one real caution on execution, not on the idea.

  • Fits his own documented positioning exactly. His stated moat, on file since 2026-07-01, is "GenX = the forgotten one" — a Gen X operator who lived the full analog-to-digital-to-AI transition, trusted by a generation most AI content ignores. A sarcastic, unapologetic Gen X voice on AI is the moat turned all the way up, not a stretch from it.
  • Does it cannibalize concept 2? Not if the two stay visibly different products. Concept 2 is "here's how to actually get value from AI at work," handed to a boss or a peer. This series is "here's how Gen X should actually feel about all this, and we're not sugarcoating it" — identity and attitude first, advice second. They need distinct names, covers, and tone-signaling so nobody mistakes one for the other.
  • Real market appetite, reasoned not assumed. Gen X is genuinely underserved in AI content because almost nobody markets to it directly, and almost nobody does it with irreverence — the tone options right now are hype aimed at younger readers or cautious hand-holding aimed at older/less technical readers. A series format fits a recurring-reader-identity voice better than a one-off book.
  • The one real caution: sarcasm is harder to sustain across a whole series than a measured advisory voice. It needs enough real, specific, lived material to stay funny and true rather than becoming a comedic veneer over generic AI advice — that material does not exist yet (see gap below).

Proposed series name + book titles (sarcastic register, not the measured CW-consulting voice)

Series name (recommended): Old Enough to Remember Dial-Up, Smart Enough to Use AI — a concrete generational detail instead of an abstract claim. Backup: The Gen X Field Guide to Not Giving a Damn About AI.

Book title candidates: We Survived Windows 95. We Can Survive ChatGPT. (recommended lead), AI Won't Fix Your Personality, But It'll Save You Twenty Minutes, Nobody Asked Gen X, So Here We Are Anyway. ("Twenty Minutes" is illustrative title phrasing, not a claimed or verified figure.)

A voice-instrument note, flagged rather than forced

The standard boub_voice_mastery gate was run against these titles. All clear its universal checks (no em-dashes, no hype, no fabricated numbers or vulnerability). But that gate is calibrated for his usual measured, understated register — a deliberately sarcastic voice is a different instrument, since sarcasm leans on exaggeration and attitude on purpose. The titles above were judged on whether they sound like Boubacar in a more irreverent register, not forced through a rubric built for a different tone. Whoever drafts real chapters next should treat the existing gate as necessary but not sufficient for this register.

The real gap — this is an idea and a voice instruction right now, not yet material

There is currently very little source material written IN this specific sarcastic, "we don't care" register. His documented voice is direct, blunt, and opinionated — not the same thing as sarcastic. No sarcastic-sounding line has been invented or attributed to him to paper over that gap, and none should be. This concept exists today as an idea and a voice instruction; it needs Boubacar to actually write or dictate content in this register before a real chapter can be drafted.

Verdict: own the niche — his positioning supports it directly and the market gap is real. Keep it as concept 8, distinct from concept 2, and treat the sarcastic-voice source-material gap as the actual blocker to drafting, not the idea itself.
