The Anonymizer Program: One Page, All Four Passes

Synthesis of four independent strategy passes run the same evening · research lane · 2026-09-16

TL;DR

Three agents who could not read each other's work all landed on the same reframe: the product is the receipt, not the anonymizer. A fourth pass then narrowed it. Today's receipt is a self-generated integrity digest, so it is a conversation opener and engagement evidence, not yet a procurement wedge. Anchoring it to an independent timestamp is the cheap fix that would change that.

Do not pivot to enterprise. Services-attached stays primary. The enterprise track runs in parallel on four cheap artifacts and nothing more.

Nine decisions are waiting on you in section 3, each with options and a recommendation already made. Nine Monday actions in section 4, in order, merged from all four documents. Three collision risks were flagged and all three resolve cleanly, plus one nobody flagged: two of the four documents are written against a build state that is out of date.

1. The headline finding

Three agents worked this program in parallel and none of them could read the others' output. The enterprise pass, the embedded portfolio pass, and the blind standalone-company pass each arrived, independently and by a different route, at the same inversion: the thing worth selling is not the tool that anonymizes the file, it is the record the tool emits proving what happened to that file. The blind pass put it as "stop selling privacy, sell proof." The embedded pass put it as "the receipt is the asset and the tool is its first consumer." The enterprise pass counted the approval functions inside a large organization and found four of five are served by the receipt and only one by the anonymization. Three routes, one place. That convergence is the strongest signal any of these four documents produced, and it should reset what the program is about.

And then the fourth pass narrowed it, which matters as much as the convergence itself. The enterprise pass ran a Council specifically on this claim and cut it down. The receipt today is a SHA-256 integrity digest generated by the party being audited, on a machine that party controls. It is not a cryptographic signature and the code correctly never calls it one. An auditor's first question about any control is who produces the evidence and whether they can alter it, and the honest answer here is unflattering. So the receipt is a conversation opener with a privacy function, and evidence inside a services engagement. Those are real jobs worth real money. "The thing procurement buys" is not one of them, and presenting it that way would be found in the first serious meeting.

The fix arrived from the one document that could not see the others. The blind pass's own cross-review spotted the same weakness and proposed the answer in one line: anchor each receipt to an independent trusted timestamp, an RFC-3161 timestamp authority or a public transparency log. It costs almost nothing, it requires becoming nobody's auditor, and it converts a log file you wrote yourself into evidence that provably existed at a moment you did not control. Read the two documents together and you get the full arc, which neither one has alone: the receipt is the product, today's receipt is too weak to carry that claim, and the thing that would make it strong is a small build nobody has scheduled.

One more piece of the same arc, from the enterprise pass and independently strong: a receipt exported to a location the organization controls is no longer evidence the vendor holds about himself. It is evidence the organization holds, somewhere he cannot reach. That is arguably worth more than a signature, it costs a modest build, and it does not touch the offline promise.

What this means in one sentence: the receipt earns the headline position the three agents gave it, but only after two small builds that nobody has put a date on, and until then every claim made for it holds at the lower setting the enterprise pass established.

2. The sequencing recommendation

This is the enterprise pass's own conclusion, stated in its terms rather than re-argued here.

Do not pivot to enterprise. Near-term revenue stays exactly where the existing go-to-market plan put it, on the services-attached path to solo and small HR consultants. The enterprise track starts now as a cultivation motion bounded by four named artifacts, and it is not permitted an hour that the primary track needs.

Why, in the pass's own reasoning: it went back through all six arguments the original plan used to rule out an enterprise-direct motion, and checked each one against today. All six still hold. Nobody in the enterprise privacy tier sells to a solo consultant, and no Fortune 100 buys a security tool from one, so the disqualifier cuts both ways. There is still no distribution. The proof score is still low because the Re-identification Trial still has not run. Enterprise cycles are still six to eighteen months against a revenue-this-month goal. Six for six, nothing expired.

The finding that is genuinely new, and it runs against enterprise rather than for it. The market window is closing on that side, not opening. Microsoft's data-loss prevention for its own AI assistant reached general availability around mid-2026 and moves detection and blocking natively into the stack most large organizations already own. Anthropic shipped enterprise safeguards in early September 2026 that move customer data into infrastructure the customer controls. The asymmetry is the whole point: a large organization is precisely the buyer that can negotiate a zero-retention agreement, deploy the native controls, and make a vendor put data in its own infrastructure. The enterprises this would be sold to are the ones least likely to still need it by the time the sale closes. A solo consultant has none of those options and never will, which is exactly the buyer already chosen.

What the enterprise track is permitted to produce before the Trial runs. The pass replaced an unenforceable "four hours a week" cap with a budget of named artifacts on named dates, on the grounds that a time cap has no owner and no way to notice when it is breached. The enterprise pass calls this the one question only you can answer, and says every other answer in that document moves when it changes.

Recommendation: engagements. Five Council voices converged on it independently. It uses the asset that is actually scarce, which is that you can walk into a Fortune 100 HR function and be read as a peer, it needs nothing that does not already exist, and it is the only one of the two that can produce a dollar inside the period you are measuring. Choosing "product" is not wrong, but it is a different company than the one you are running this quarter and it should be chosen deliberately rather than drifted into.

2. Is this a company you want to run, or an asset you want to sell?

The blind standalone pass's closing question. It is not the same question as item 1 and the two answers point at different first moves.

  • The format play. Publish the receipt format openly, anchor it to an independent timestamp, monetize the tooling and the verification around it. A five-year company-building move that is worth very little in year one.
  • The service play. Do the anonymization by hand as a done-for-you engagement for the first ten clients, hand each one the receipt, and find out whether anyone cares about the receipt before building a company on it. Cash in thirty days, nothing durable built.
Recommendation: the service play first, and hold the format reframe without acting on it. This is where the embedded pass's Council landed on the same tension: the receipt-as-primitive reframe is cheap to hold and expensive to act on, so record it so the next builder builds it first, and do not start a format program. The service play also happens to be the cheapest test of the format play's own hinge assumption. If the standalone company ever does become the answer, the naming work is already done and is a separate call: ASSAY took a direct hit in its own Council for being misread as "Essay," and HANDOFF is the honest alternative if zero-context clarity outranks metaphor.

3. The shape of the free offer, and how many free offers there are

This is the most-collided item on the page. Three documents proposed a free hands-on offer to three different buyers, and one Council voice attacked the pattern itself.

  • (a) As originally approved: they send you a file, you anonymize it by hand.
  • (b) A representative extract rather than the full production file.
  • (c) A screen share where the file never leaves their building.
  • (d) All three, offered in that order of preference.
Recommendation: (d), leading with (c). The privacy counsel voice in Council put the objection better than any internal reasoning did: "you are asking me to send a real employee file to an outside individual to prove that sending files to outside individuals is risky. That is the thing I exist to stop." The screen-share version removes the strongest objection at zero cost to the demonstration, and the demonstration is the whole product. This changes what you approved, which is why it is flagged rather than substituted.
Two additions from the reconciliation, both yours to rule on in the same breath. First, there must be one free hands-on program with one capacity cap of five, not three parallel ones. The enterprise pass's free assessment to an internal AI governance group and this offer are the same founder hours spent on different buyers, and the enterprise pass already caps its own version at exactly one test run. Second, an outside voice in the enterprise Council read the whole plan cold and said this: "everything you are offering is free. A free tool, a free assessment, a free talk, a free briefing. Individually each is smart. Read together from outside, it says nobody has ever paid for any of this." The blind pass's Executor independently recommended the opposite instrument, a paid done-for-you engagement at five hundred to fifteen hundred dollars a time for the first ten clients. Both cannot be the default. Suggested split: the screen-share demonstration stays free and capped at five, and the hands-on done-for-you work is priced.

7. The certification mark

Scratched by the creative pass on prior art plus a direct conflict with the product's own honesty rule. Agents propose; a no-go on your own idea is yours.

The load-bearing objection is that a mark is worth exactly what its issuer's reputation is worth, and the same idea was already run through Council on 2026-07-26 and returned a vanity-credential verdict. Two additional objections are specific to this version: a mark whose first and only holder is the issuer's own tool is a logo rather than a trust artifact, and a verification seal is a status claim by construction, which the product is permanently banned from making by its own spec. The blind pass killed the identical idea independently and kept only the honest survivor, a public page that verifies a receipt the customer produced rather than a seal you grant.

Recommendation: convert the ambition rather than kill it. The achievable form of the same ambition is the published method: pre-registered pass-fail bars and a reproducible methodology anyone can run against any tool. A method others run becomes a standard from the bottom up; a mark others must accept has to be granted from the top down. If you want the mark itself kept alive, it should be PARKED with a return date rather than left as a rejected item, because a park is first-class and a silent kill is not.
