LinkedIn Positioning: Sniper, Not Shotgun

The message on your page and the message you decided on are two different messages · 2026-09-11

TL;DR

The niche is not an open question. You answered it yourself on 2026-08-28: “the go-to person for the merging of AI and HR.” The live profile still carries July copy written against a different strategy. So this is a propagation failure, not a strategy choice.

Recommendation: sniper, aimed at AI for HR and recruiting, with recruiting and time-to-fill as the beachhead. One profile. No second brand and no second website until there is a paying recruiting client.

Do this first, before any copy: turn the public Open-to-Work badge off. A blind reader saw it today, which means it is set to all members, not recruiters only. Under five minutes.

The thing you told Jordan you were missing, you already have. You said the blocker is being able to say honestly “I’ve done this.” You can. You were part of a project that took a hiring cycle from roughly two months to under one. It has been invisible because generic operations positioning gave it nowhere to sit.

One question only you can answer, and the whole plan hangs on it: is the HR and AI role a job you would actually take, or a client you are pitching?

What was found

Two separate prior plans exist. They are about different surfaces, and conflating them is what has made the state of this hard to read.

The July plan was the COPY plan, and it DID ship

The headline a blind cold-reader saw on your profile today is the July plan’s proposed headline, verbatim, character for character. The About preview it quoted is the July plan’s About. The paste session happened. A correction already settled this independently on 2026-09-03 after a live browser read, and the gap it recorded is the same one the cold read found eight days later: the headline and About never say HR.

What did not ship from July was the behavioural half. Its Priority 0 never happened. Open-to-Work is still on, publicly. The recommendation sprint, the storefront fix and the Featured re-order have no evidence of shipping.

The August plan was the BANNER plan, and it did NOT ship

This is the August work you remember. Its own finding was that the headline should not change and that the open item is the banner. It proposed a banner line, scored it, and then five banner directions were designed, pushed to a branch, tagged work-in-progress, and never merged. Verified today: still not merged. They were never put in front of you as a mockup and never uploaded. The tracking row was re-dated twice and then archived for being too long. The live banner has never changed.

So your memory is right and the record is right, and they were describing different halves. The copy shipped in July. The banner stalled in August and is still sitting on an unmerged branch.

Three things from the August work that change what gets written now

  1. You already rejected the line that is currently live in your headline. The record has “You run the business, I run the systems” marked rejected, in your words, as pretentious. It is still the closing clause of the live headline. The rewrite below drops it. That is applying a decision you already made, not a style preference.
  2. The banner geometry is a measured hard constraint. Canvas 1584x396, upload at 3168x792, and your avatar is a 152px circle that covers the left half of the banner on a 390px phone. Banner copy has to live in the right portion or it sits behind your own face on mobile.
  3. Your standing rule for it: “It must be scroll stopping and do my selling for me... Everything on the banner must EARN it’s place.”

What neither plan could have known

July positioned you as a general bottleneck consultant, with AI deliberately demoted and HR deliberately absent. August explicitly left the headline alone. Both were considered calls at the time. Then on 2026-08-28 you set a different niche in your own words. Nothing propagated it to the live profile. That gap, not the wording, is the thing to fix, and it recurs in November unless this plan closes it.

The decision: sniper or shotgun

Where you are today

Four target groups, named by you on the call: general AI, recruiters and recruiting organisations, fintech people for ghostwriting, and HR gurus.

The cold read is the evidence on what that costs. A reader with no biographical priming, looking straight at your live profile, concluded: “an AI-adjacent operations consultant / fractional ops person,” “reads corporate-coach, not personal,” and “feels like two different accounts: consultant-for-hire vs. content-creator building a personal brand.”

Notice what is missing from that entire verdict. HR is not in it. Recruiting is not in it. Your single scarcest asset, a career inside HR paired with systems you built and run yourself, was invisible to a reader looking straight at the page.

The three options

Option A. Stay broad. Keep all four groups, keep the current copy.
Gets you: nothing is foreclosed, and the ghostwriting revenue line keeps its aim.
Costs you: the cold read is what this option already produces. Jordan’s read of it is the accurate one, and it is about capacity, not marketing: “I’m building a stadium. I’m building a railroad... Each one of them has three bricks... but I don’t have any building, and I got no tenants, and I got no rent.”

Option B. Sniper on AI for HR and recruiting. Recruiting and time-to-fill as the beachhead. One profile, one message. RECOMMENDED.
Every surface says AI for HR and recruiting. The content, comments and DMs aim specifically at recruiting firms and the time it takes them to fill a role. The other three groups are not killed, they are re-classed:

  • Fintech ghostwriting stays a live revenue line. It is paid work and does not need the profile pointed at it. Stop aiming, keep earning.
  • HR gurus become a distribution channel, not a target segment. Commenting on the former boss who tags you back is reach, not a market.
  • General AI becomes the topic you have opinions on, not the identity you sell. Which is what your own niche statement already says.

Option C. Jordan’s literal proposal. A separate brand, site and social presence for AI-for-recruiting now, with the personal site as the umbrella.
Gets you: the sharpest possible targeting, and a page you can send a recruiting executive to that is about nothing else.
Costs you: a sixth building with three bricks in it. His own metaphor argues against his own tactic here, and he was reasoning without knowing how many surfaces already exist. A second brand needs its own content, its own proof and its own maintenance, and there is no recruiting client yet to put on it.

Recommendation: Option B

  1. It is not a new decision, it is the one you already made. Choosing a niche is the hard, frightening part, and you did it on 2026-08-28. Treating this as open re-opens a settled question and invites a fifth option.
  2. You already own the proof you told Jordan you were missing. You were part of a project that took a hiring cycle from roughly two months to under one. That is the exact claim a recruiting firm buys, it is true, and it is documented. Narrowing to hiring is what makes your strongest existing proof point usable.
  3. Recruiting is the sharpest pain inside the niche, not a different niche. Jordan’s instinct was right and his framing was one level off. AI-for-recruiting is not a fifth thing competing with AI-and-HR. It is the doorway into it.
  4. It protects the north star. Fractional AI engagements inside HR are the goal. A recruiting-only brand would have to be rebuilt the moment the first HR-leader conversation happens.

The split the Council would not average away

Two voices disagreed on the width, and the disagreement is real. Argued cold, with no insider context: “AI for HR and recruiting is still two things, and HR is the bigger word. I would hear HR guy, and I have already met eleven of those.” Argued from the market: the buyer with budget in 2026 is the HR leader told to do something about AI with nobody on staff who can, and recruiting alone leaves that buyer unaddressed.

The call: profile broad by one notch, content narrow. The profile says AI for HR and recruiting. Every post, comment and DM for the next six months aims at recruiting firms and time-to-fill. That is what a sniper is. One scope, one target, standing on ground wide enough to move.

Six months, and what ends it

Commit through March, then re-evaluate. It is not forever, and saying so out loud is what makes it possible to start. What would reverse it: a paying fractional AI-and-HR engagement arriving from outside recruiting, or a full six months of aimed recruiting effort producing no conversation with anyone who can sign.

Post cadence and the Trade Test

Cadence

Drop to one post a day, with a written queue and a batch-writing session rather than daily drafting. The rate measured in July was 9.6 posts a week. Volume is not the constraint. Aim is.

Two standing quotas ride on top of the daily post, both aimed at the beachhead:

  • At least four of every five posts in a week aim at recruiting firms and the time it takes to fill a role.
  • Comments move from a flat daily count to depth in recruiting-firm feeds specifically. The September 1 engagement diagnosis already found the mechanism: reciprocal engagement is what distributes your posts, and a reaction is invisible inside a target’s audience where a comment is not.

The pre-publish checklist

This extends an existing gate. It does not replace one. There is already a documented pre-publish chain: the Hook Quality Gate (four rules), then the voice gate, then the two-pass Council review, then the bookmarkability rubric, then the 90-composite bar. All of that stands unchanged.

What was missing is Jordan’s test, and the gap is real. Every existing gate measures how a post is written. None asks who it is for and whether the reader comes out ahead. A post can score 94, clear every ban, and still be aimed at nobody.

The Trade Test. Four questions, added as the FIRST gate, before the Hook Quality Gate. A draft that cannot answer all four does not enter the board.

  1. Who is the one person reading this? Not “recruiters.” One named role in one named situation. An agency owner whose last three searches each ran past six weeks is a person. “Leaders” is not.
  2. What do they hope for and what keeps them up? One line each, in their words, not yours. If you cannot write the fear, you do not know the reader well enough to write the post yet, and the honest move is to go ask one of them.
  3. Does line one stop the doom-scroll? Read line one alone, with nothing under it. If a reader would not stop, the post does not go out, no matter how good paragraph three is.
  4. What is the trade? You are asking for their attention. Name precisely what they get for it, and confirm that what they get is worth more to them than what you are asking. If your side of the trade is bigger, the post is a withdrawal, not a deposit.

Jordan’s framing, kept because the mechanism is in it: “If the thing that they’re getting in their eyes is more valuable than the thing that I’m getting, they will take the next step and the next step and the next step all the way to revenue. If they don’t, if the funnel breaks... I have to reexamine either I’m not giving them enough, or I don’t understand them well enough.”

Where it applies beyond posts. The trade is a funnel rule, not a content rule. Run it at every step where you ask for something: the profile itself, the DM, the workshop registration, the email capture, the call. Any step where your side of the trade is bigger than theirs is where the funnel is leaking.

The Open-to-Work conflict

What the cold read found: “‘Open to work, Recruiters only’ banner sits directly under a page that’s clearly pitching him as a consultant/business owner. Those two signals visibly conflict on the same screen.”

The critical fact: a blind reader saw it. That means the badge is set to all LinkedIn members, not recruiters only, whatever the label says. It is public. Everyone you are selling to can see it.

Why this is not small. The July audit scored this dimension 2 out of 10 and made it Priority 0, ahead of every word of copy. Its finding was that the platform’s own classifier has you flagged as interested in a new job, which means the algorithm distributing your consultant content believes you are a candidate. You pay for that flag twice: once in distribution, once in the impression on every buyer who lands on the page. Argued cold, it reads worse than a mixed signal. It reads as a man who would rather have your job than your business.

The complication, and it is genuine. You are mid-process on an HR and AI role, and your own intent is to convert it into a retainer rather than take a full-time seat. You are also genuinely open to the right role. Killing the recruiter signal entirely is not free.

Recommendation: switch the badge to recruiters-only, today, and never public.

  • The public green frame comes off. That is the entire fix and it takes under five minutes.
  • The private recruiters-only setting stays on. Recruiters still find you. Buyers do not see it.
  • You lose nothing on the live opportunity. That process came from you applying, not from a recruiter finding a badge. The badge has no role in it.
  • Stop submitting applications from this account. The classifier reads application history, and per the July audit that history is still aging out from July 2025.
  • Any backstop job search runs off-platform or through recruiters-only. This was already decided in the July plan’s red-team pass and it was right.

The one question only you can answer, and everything above depends on it: is the HR and AI role a job you would actually take, or a client you are pitching? If it is a client, the badge is pure cost and comes off today with nothing lost. If you would genuinely take the job, that is a different plan than this one, and it should be said out loud rather than hedged by leaving a badge on.

Council verdict

Five-voice adversarial pass, convergence approximately 92 percent. Verdict: CHANGE, with the scope tightened.

Convergence, all five voices:

  1. The niche was decided in August. Frame this as propagation, not as a new strategy.
  2. The Open-to-Work badge is the highest-value, lowest-cost fix and it outranks every copy change.
  3. A second website or brand is premature and goes to PARK.
  4. The missing asset is PROOF, not clarity. The free-client offer is the named mechanism for producing it.
  5. The July plan failed on execution, not on analysis, so this plan has to be small enough to actually run.

Divergence, named rather than smoothed: the width of the profile line, recruiting-only versus AI-and-HR. Resolved as profile broad by one notch, content narrow, and recorded rather than resolved away.

Six things the Council changed in this plan:

  1. Section A was rewritten from “choose a niche” to “propagate a decision already made.” The original framing would have re-opened a settled question.
  2. The order of operations was inverted. Settings first, copy second. A plan whose first step is “paste this” has already been run once and did not take.
  3. The second brand and site moved from “later” to an explicit PARK with a named trigger.
  4. The free-client livestream offer was added as a first-class step, because proof is the real gap and clarity is not.
  5. “Kaizen” was cut from the public About copy. Insider vocabulary, invisible to the buyer.
  6. A propagation fix was added, because the underlying failure is that positioning decisions do not reach live surfaces.

The Contrarian’s finding, kept unresolved on purpose: narrowing makes the missing proof more conspicuous, not less. A generalist is forgiven for having no case study in your vertical. A specialist is not. That is an argument for moving fast on the free-client offer, not an argument against narrowing.
