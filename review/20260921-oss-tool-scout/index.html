What else is out there

Open source tool scout, published 2026-09-21
"There are a lot of open source tools and resources out there we can use to generate revenue... a lot of that stuff is open source, MIT, that other businesses use as their backbone and they make money off of it. Why can't we do the same thing?"

TL;DR

You already have five repos on file (Dify, Open SEO, Open Shorts, Presenton, plus a scraper repo, from the last video). This is the rest of the search: ten more, ranked, each one currently maintained, each one with a real monetization pattern, none of them duplicating what you already have.

Test first: Uptime Kuma (monitor our own sites this week, screenshot becomes the proof-first cold-email asset) and Activepieces (MIT license, the one that's actually safe to resell white-label, unlike n8n). Invoice Ninja is worth the $40 look because it is the only entry with a vendor-confirmed resale program, not an inferred one.

The ranked list
1

Activepieces — automation you can actually resell

MIT license, built for white-label. n8n's license blocks reselling a hosted product; this one doesn't.

White-label automation retainer 24.6K stars MIT pushed today
▸
What it is
An open source Zapier/n8n alternative. One Docker container, Postgres backend (we already run one).
Why it beats n8n for this specific job
n8n's license is source-available with use restrictions that block standing up a competing hosted product. Activepieces community edition is plain MIT, which is why agencies pick it specifically when they want to rebrand and resell a hosted instance. We keep n8n for our own internal work and use this for anything we sell to a client.
The money
An automation retainer, wire a client's booking form to their CRM to their invoicing tool, is a $500 to $1,000 a month repeatable service for HVAC, property management, and other local-service businesses already complaining about manual double-entry.
Test this week
Stand up one instance, wire a trivial real workflow (lead form to CRM to a Telegram alert), use it as the demo build for pitching automation retainers.
2

Uptime Kuma — the simplest recurring dollar on this list

One container, no external database, watches a client's site or payment link and pages you before they find out from a customer.

Monitoring-as-a-service, $20-50/mo 91.6K stars (highest of all ten) MIT pushed today
▸
What it is
A self-hosted status monitor. HTTP, TCP, DNS, and ping checks, alerts over 90+ channels including Telegram. SQLite by default, no extra database to run.
Why it fits the boring-business mandate
This is the exact shape asked for: unsexy, mechanical, recurring. We already root-caused our own dead-pay-link problem this way. Selling "we watch your site so you don't find out it's down from a customer" is the same fix, sold outward.
The money
A branded status page per client, billed flat monthly. Near zero build, near zero ongoing labor once it's running.
Test this week
Point one instance at our own live surfaces (boubacarbarry.com, review pages, payment links) and use the resulting status page screenshot as the proof-first asset for cold outreach, the exact missing piece the last leadgen absorb flagged in Signal Works.
3

Twenty CRM — the CRM setup a spreadsheet business needs

A real, fast-growing Salesforce/HubSpot alternative, backed by Y Combinator, actively maintained.

CRM setup + management retainer 57.2K stars AGPL-3.0 pushed today
▸
What it is
An open source CRM, modern stack, 300+ contributors, Docker Compose deploy on Postgres plus Redis.
The money
"Get your deals out of your texts and into a real CRM" is a direct $500 to $1,000 setup-plus-retainer sale for the exact ICP we already position to.
License note, said plainly
AGPL-3.0 is fine for a per-client self-hosted install, each client gets their own unmodified instance. It only becomes a real constraint if we ever wrap it into one multi-tenant SaaS product of our own.
4

Chatwoot — one inbox instead of five ignored ones

The most-starred open source Intercom/Zendesk alternative. Live chat, email, and social messages in one place.

Support desk retainer 37K stars pushed today
▸
What it is
A self-hosted customer support desk. More moving parts than Uptime Kuma, Postgres plus Redis plus Sidekiq, but heavily documented and commonly self-hosted.
The money
A lot of small businesses run customer messages through Facebook DMs or a personal cell nobody watches. "We set up and monitor one unified inbox for your business" is a direct sale to that exact pain.
License note
Core is MIT-style; the repo mixes in gated enterprise-only features, so GitHub's automatic license detector could not confirm a single license. Read the actual LICENSE file before any client build, do not take this page's word for it.
5

Invoice Ninja — the only vendor-confirmed resale program here

The one entry where "other businesses make money off it" is not inferred, the vendor sells a white-label license outright.

Vendor-confirmed white label, $40/yr 10.1K stars pushed today
▸
What it is
Self-hosted invoicing, quoting, and time tracking. Lighter infrastructure than the CRM or support desk options above.
Why it ranks this high
Invoice Ninja itself sells a $40 a year white-label license so resellers can strip its own branding off client-facing invoices and portals. That is the vendor's own stated business model for exactly the pattern you described, not something we're inferring from a forum post.
Honest caveat
It is source-available with a paid white-label tier, not pure MIT. Still fits what was actually asked for: open source software other businesses build a backbone on and monetize.
Test before building
Worth the $40 just to see the actual reseller flow before committing to build anything on it.
6

Crawl4AI — the engine under the SEO audit offer

The highest-starred, most current web crawler in open source. Not a product on its own, the thing that powers one.

Powers the Open SEO pipeline 84K stars Apache-2.0
▸
What it is
Turns any page into clean, LLM-ready markdown. Ships with Docker and its own API server. Renders JavaScript-heavy pages by wrapping a real browser.
Where it fits
It has no revenue shape by itself, it strengthens the already-found Open SEO audit offer and any future lead or competitor monitoring, by turning any page into something an LLM can grade for the "3 worst findings" cold email opener.
Duplicate check
Not the same as the bot-wall scraper already found for Shopify price monitoring. That one is e-commerce-price-specific, this is general-purpose and newer. They stack, they don't replace each other.
7

Metabase — "we already build this for ourselves" as a client pitch

Open source BI. Point it at a client's numbers, ship a dashboard, no data team required.

Reporting-dashboard retainer 49.3K stars pushed today
▸
What it is
Open core BI tool, GUI or SQL questions, one Docker container, points at any Postgres or MySQL source.
The money
Point it at a client's bookings, leads, and revenue, sell a monthly "your numbers, one dashboard" retainer. We already build this shape of thing for ourselves internally, this is the same skill sold outward.
8

Postiz — a Buffer/Hootsuite you can bundle in

Multi-platform social scheduling, for a THIRD PARTY's own accounts, not our own content engine.

Content-retainer add-on, $300-500/mo 36.1K stars AGPL-3.0
▸
What it is
Open source social post scheduling and analytics across platforms.
The money
Bundle into a content-service retainer for businesses that post inconsistently, we schedule their Google Business, Facebook, and Instagram posts.
Duplicate check
Different job from our own publishing pipeline, which runs OUR brands. This would run a client's own accounts as a sold service.
9

Whishper — 100% local transcription for the privacy-sensitive verticals

Runs fully offline. The privacy pitch is real leverage with legal, medical, and HR clients.

Transcription/captioning service 3.1K stars 7 weeks stale, weakest signal of the ten
▸
What it is
A local Whisper-based transcription, translation, and subtitle editor with a web UI. CPU-viable.
The money
Podcast or sales-call transcription, sold as "100% local, your audio never leaves our server," a real differentiator for verticals that would balk at a cloud API.
Honest flag
Last push was 7 weeks before this scout, the weakest maintenance signal on this list. Still within normal open source norms, just worth a fresh check before any client commitment.
10

Dograh — a voice agent backbone we would actually own

Self-hosted alternative to Vapi and Retell. Fork it and own it instead of paying a per-minute middleman.

Voice-agent client work 5.7K stars BSD-2-Clause
▸
What it is
Visual workflow builder, built-in telephony (Twilio, Vonage, Telnyx, Asterisk), runs on-prem, bring your own LLM/speech providers.
Where it fits our own standing rule
Our own voice work runs on our homegrown structure, and a client-work-only vendor stays available as a back pocket. This does not change that rule, it is not that vendor, it is a self-hosted option we would own, which is a stronger position than the current fallback for the day a client specifically asks for voice.
Why it ranks last
Least mature entry on this list, and voice-agent client work is not an active near-term lane. Flagged as a real option, not a near-term build.

Test these two first
  1. Uptime Kuma. Cheapest possible pilot: monitor our own live surfaces this week, screenshot the status page, use it as the proof-first cold email asset.
  2. Activepieces. Stand up one instance, wire one real trivial workflow, that becomes the reference build for pitching automation retainers.
  3. Invoice Ninja's white-label program is worth evaluating for $40 before building anything, since it is the one confirmed-by-the-vendor resale mechanism on this list.

What got cut, and why
  • n8n, Supabase, Plausible, Cal.com, Documenso, Coolify, NocoDB, PostHog, Listmonk, Ollama, Penpot, AppFlowy — already decided in July. Not re-litigated here.
  • Crawl4AI vs. the scraper already found — kept, not a duplicate. Different scope (general crawling vs. e-commerce price watching), stacks under the same SEO audit offer.
  • Postiz vs. our own publishing pipeline — kept, different audience. Ours runs our brands, this would run a client's.
  • Metabase vs. our own internal dashboards — kept as a new offer, even though the underlying skill overlaps what we already build for ourselves.
  • Dograh vs. our own voice system — kept, different job. Ours is production, this is a candidate for client work only.
  • Docmost, Formbricks, FOSSBilling, InvoicePlane — real, cut to keep this list ruthless. Docmost overlapped too closely with the already-found handbook chatbot; the other billing tools are redundant with Invoice Ninja's stronger vendor-confirmed story.
All ten are pulled from live GitHub data this session, not a blog post's word for it. Stars, license, and last-push date are stated per tool above so the maintenance signal is checkable, not assumed.
