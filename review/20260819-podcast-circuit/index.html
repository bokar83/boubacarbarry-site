Council verdict: CHANGE

Five angles, run before this page was rewritten. Not keep, and specifically not kill.

Why not keep. The list was ranked by how impressive the show is, not by how likely it is to happen. Everyday AI sits at 5% acceptance on roughly 1,000 pitches a year, and the price of admission is that the guest promotes the episode. You said promoting to your own network is not what you are looking for right now. So the best case outcome of the top item on the old board was an obligation you had already declined. That contradiction was sitting in the plan unremarked.

Why not kill. Your guest profile is genuinely scarce. Most people in this space are HR people who talk about AI in the abstract, or AI people who have never sat in an HR seat. You are neither, and shows compete for that. A recorded episode is also durable proof that makes every other channel convert better. That is real value, it is just value as sales collateral rather than as lead generation, and that reframe changes how much it is worth rather than whether it is worth anything.

The reframe that reranked the board. The host is the asset. The audience is the byproduct. A podcast host in HR or AI is, by definition, someone who spends their week talking to operators and heads of people. Every host is a hub who makes referrals. The audience does not. That is why one warm host beats ten cold shows, and it also dissolves the promotion problem entirely, because a relationship with a host carries no promotion tax.

The honest part about conversion. The path from "was a guest" to "somebody hires you fractionally" has at least four unstated hops: they hear it, they remember your name, they have a live AI problem with budget, they find you. None of those are instrumented today. There is no offer named in an episode and no capture surface. Until there is, an appearance leaks every listener it earns. That is a defect by your own standing rule, not a nice-to-have.

The strongest dissent, and it survives. One angle held that even the warm version of this is a distraction from asking named buyers directly, and that a warm podcast list will quietly eat the same hours the cold list would have, just with better feelings about it. That risk is real. The two-hour cap below is the only thing holding it in check.

The unexplored move nobody has pitched. Stop asking for a guest slot. Offer a recurring monthly segment instead. A host's real problem is filling next month's calendar, and a standing segment solves it for them, which makes it an easier yes than a one-off appearance, not a harder one. A one-off guest slot is a depreciating asset. A recurring segment compounds.

Hours per week: two, hard capped. That buys roughly three warm approaches, or one warm approach plus one diagnosed follow-up. Above two hours it starts eating the direct-ask time that produces this quarter's revenue. Below two, the relationships never compound. It comes out of the visibility budget and never out of direct outreach.

The one question only you can answer, and it changes the size of the list. You said you do not want to promote an episode to your own network. Is that a hard no on all reciprocal promotion, which permanently removes several shows from the board, or is it a no to carrying the promotion burden while a show that accepts 5% of pitchers takes the credit? Your answer is the difference between a target list of about a dozen and one of about four. There is a note field on the first tracker row for it.
