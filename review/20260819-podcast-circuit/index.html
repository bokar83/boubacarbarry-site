The standing procedure. Four rules, built around the two a week that are actually worth your time.

  1. Do not read the daily firehose. Filter the notifications out of the inbox. You are hunting two emails a week, not sixty.
  2. Twice a week, Monday and Thursday, ten minutes. Open only the hand picked "good fit" notes and anything whose subject contains the word podcast. Pitch at most two. Anything you would not want to be on air talking about, skip without opening.
  3. Every pitch gets a calendar reminder at day seven. This is the entire fix. Two hosts read your pitches and both went cold because nothing came after.
  4. On a read receipt, follow up once at day seven and once at day twenty one, then stop. Two follow ups, then it is closed and you move on.

The reusable response skeleton. Five lines. It works because it answers their brief instead of describing you.

LINE 1, their brief in your words. "Your brief asks for X." Name the specific thing they asked for. Do not restate the title. LINE 2, the claim. Say plainly why you are that person, in one sentence, with the unusual half in it. The HR seat plus the system you built and operate. Not a resume line. LINE 3, the proof. One concrete thing. A hiring process I helped fix went from roughly 60 days to under 30. Or: I run my own business on a system I built and maintain. LINE 4, the take they can use. Give them the actual angle, not a promise of one. Most of what gets sold as an AI problem is a process problem. One sentence, quotable as it stands. LINE 5, the close. Availability and format. No pitch language, no links, no ask for a call.

The reason the skeleton leads with their brief is that a host reads twenty of these and every one opens with the sender. The one that opens with the request stands out on line one.
