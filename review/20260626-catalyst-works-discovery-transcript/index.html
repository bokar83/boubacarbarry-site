▸A Opening: Inciting Incident & Current State

The discovery call opens by listening for the inciting incident and the mixed signals: what made this a problem now, and where the story does not add up.

Q.What made you decide to look at this now? What is the inciting incident?
A.Roughly ten weeks of full build with zero consistent revenue, capped by a flagship paid workshop that sold zero seats against 350 ad clicks and 200-plus dollars of spend. The workshop after-action review was the moment the pattern became undeniable: the problem is not one campaign, it is the whole motion.
Q.Walk me through the current state in one breath.
A.A complete business on paper: brand, a four-rung offer ladder, six-plus live Stripe links, a Calendly with qualifiers, five landing pages, an 1,846-line audit tool with its own white-label pricing, lead magnets, playbooks, and a researched strategy. Stage, per the company's own Notion hub: "$0 to first contract." No closed paying client in any source.
Q.Where do the signals not add up? What is the mixed signal?
A.The biggest one: enormous, sophisticated output and zero revenue. A business that can build a white-label audit tool but has never closed a single client is telling you the effort is pointed somewhere other than the sale. Second mixed signal: a coherent, researched strategy exists in the notebooks, while the live website says something different and changes often. Capability is not the gap. Direction is.
▸B Live Maturity Scoring (4-Level Model)

Catalyst Works scores a client live on a four-level maturity model during the call. Here it scores its own revenue engine. Level 1 ad-hoc, Level 2 defined, Level 3 operationalized, Level 4 compounding.

Q.Is there a written strategy with a defined buyer and a research base? Scores L3
A.Yes, and it is strong. The notebooks hold a precise ICP, an outcome-first positioning, a clean ladder, a discovery system, and a real research base. Operationalized on paper.
Q.Is the offer and pricing defined and consistent? Scores L3 / L2
A.The documented ladder is clean and in-market. But the public pricing has drifted (the Signal Session shows as $350, then $497, then $250 across artifacts), so the live face is a notch below the documented plan.
Q.Is there a repeatable system that puts you in front of qualified buyers? Scores L1
A.No. The documented warm channels (BNI, Chamber, warm LinkedIn) were never switched on. What ran was ad-hoc cold email and cold ads. Demand generation is Level 1.
Q.Is there any client proof or credibility asset? Scores L1
A.None. Zero closed clients, zero testimonials, zero case studies. The operator-as-proof demo (running his own business on AI agents) exists but was never packaged.
Q.Is there a pipeline system tracking conversations to close? Scores L1
A.No CRM. The Drive sales folder is empty. There is no instrument to turn a "yes, let's talk" into a managed deal.
Q.Is the documented plan actually being executed? Scores L1
A.No. This is the decisive score. A Level 3 plan with a Level 1 execution layer behaves like a Level 1 business, and the bank account agrees.
Live score read-back Strategy and IP at Level 3. Built assets overbuilt at Level 4. Demand generation, proof, pipeline, and execution all at Level 1. A business sells from its lowest score, not its highest. The binding score here is execution.
▸C The 8-Lens Diagnostic (one question per lens)

Catalyst Works runs eight diagnostic lenses internally and never names them to a client. Here is one diagnostic question per lens, answered from the evidence. The governing rule: the lens follows the constraint.

Q.Theory of Constraints. What is the single bottleneck that, if elevated, releases the most flow?
A.The unexecuted go-to-market motion. Every node downstream (conversion, proof, retention) is starved because the funnel delivers almost no qualified input. Fix this one node and flow begins; fix any other and nothing moves.
Q.Jobs To Be Done. What job is the buyer hiring this for, and does the offer speak to it?
A.The job is "find and fix the margin I am losing to broken operations." The documented positioning nails it. The live pitch sells the method (governance, constraint diagnostics) instead of the job. The good version exists; it is just not on the storefront.
Q.Lean. Where is the waste, the work that consumes capacity without producing the outcome?
A.Building as a substitute for selling. The audit tool, the white-label tier, ten short links, five funnels, all built with no validated buyer. Maximum motion, minimum sale.
Q.Cynefin. Is this problem complicated (knowable) or complex (emergent)?
A.Neither, really. It is mostly obvious-domain: the path to a first consulting client (warm outreach, a free first engagement, referrals) is well understood. The failure is not knowing what to do. It is not doing it.
Q.Behavioral Economics. What cognitive pattern is driving the behavior?
A.Effort substitution and loss aversion. Building feels productive and cannot reject you. The in-person ask is uncomfortable and can. A divided owner reliably picks the safe work, which feels like progress while producing zero.
Q.Systems Thinking. What feedback loop is missing?
A.The buyer-feedback loop. With almost no real sales conversations, the offer and message never get sharpened by contact with reality, so they get re-theorized in the notebooks instead. No loop, no learning.
Q.Design Thinking. Has anyone actually talked to the user?
A.Barely. The buyer was reached through cold email and cold ads, not conversation. The Customer Outside-In view shows the buyer was never in those channels. The product was designed for a buyer who was never in the room.
Q.AI Strategy. Is the unique AI advantage being used as proof?
A.No. The owner runs his own business on autonomous agents, which is the single most credible demo in the Utah market for this buyer. It was never packaged as proof. The strongest asset is sitting idle.
▸D Readiness Check & Owner-Input Questions

The readiness check scores Foundation, Clarity, and Motivation. Foundation and Clarity can be read from the evidence. Motivation, goals, risk tolerance, and the "why" behind the decisions can only come from the owner. Those are marked and given a best evidence-based hypothesis to confirm or correct.

Readable from evidence

Q.Foundation. Are the assets and capability in place to deliver?
A.Yes, overwhelmingly. Capability is the strongest part of the picture. Foundation passes.
Q.Clarity. Is it clear who the buyer is and what is being sold?
A.In the notebooks, yes. On the live storefront, no. Clarity exists but has not reached the surface.

Owner-input required

Q.Motivation / Commitment. Is Catalyst Works the primary business, or a side project? Owner input needed
Hyp.Evidence points to "ambivalent." The May 1 MACU prep shows an active full-time job interview and explicit wind-down war-gaming, alongside five-plus parallel efforts. Best hypothesis: it has been a side project in practice, even if intended as primary. Only the owner can confirm and decide. This is the single most load-bearing answer in the engagement.
Q.Goal. What does success look like in 90 days, and in a year? A number. Owner input needed
Hyp.The internal Lighthouse plan implies a target of paying clients by August 4. Best hypothesis for a concrete 90-day goal: 1 to 3 paying clients and a first retainer. Owner to set the actual number and the income it must replace.
Q.Risk tolerance. How many concentrated weeks of in-person, can-be-rejected selling are you willing to do before deciding? Owner input needed
Hyp.Unknown from evidence. The behavior so far (cold/automated over in-person) suggests a low current tolerance for rejection-bearing selling. Best hypothesis: this is the real edge to push. Owner to commit to a number of weeks, or admit the appetite is not there.
Q.The why. If the full-time role pays $165 to 185K, what does Catalyst Works give you that the job does not? Owner input needed
Hyp.Cannot be inferred honestly from documents. This is the question that decides everything: if the answer is strong and specific, commit the 90 days; if it is vague, the job may be the right call. Owner only.
Why these are flagged, not guessed Catalyst Works' own rule is truth-only, no invented proof. Goals, motivation, risk tolerance, and the "why" are interior facts; stating them as findings would be fabrication. The hypotheses above are the honest read of the behavior, offered for the owner to confirm or overturn. The diagnosis holds either way; the fix branches on the commitment answer.
▸E The 5-Whys Chains (to root)

Two chains, both driven past symptoms to the same root. Written as the questions were actually asked.

Chain 1 -- from the zero

Why is revenue zero?
Because no clients have closed.
Why have no clients closed?
Because almost no qualified discovery calls with check-writers ever happened.
Why no discovery calls?
Because the business reached for buyers through cold email and cold ads (0 of 767, 1 of 280, 0 of 350 clicks). The buyer was not in those channels.
Why use channels the buyer is not in?
Because the documented plan (be in the room: BNI, Chamber, warm LinkedIn) was never executed. Cold and online channels are scalable and buildable; they fit the builder's hands.
Why was the in-person motion never run?
Because it is slow, unscalable, and can hear no. A builder defaults to assets and automation over the uncomfortable ask.
Why default to the comfortable work?
Because attention was split across five-plus efforts and a live full-time job search, leaving no surplus for the highest-cost activity.
Why was attention split?
Because the commitment to Catalyst Works as the primary business was never actually made.
Root: an unmade commitment decision, surfacing as an execution gap on an already-correct plan.

Chain 2 -- from the churn

Why does the message keep changing (four public generations)?
Because it was never sharpened by real buyer contact.
Why no buyer contact to sharpen it?
Because the warm motion that produces conversations was never run (see Chain 1).
Why re-skin the website instead of selling?
Because re-skinning is building, and building is controllable and safe.
Why choose the safe work repeatedly?
Same root: divided attention has no appetite for the rejection-bearing work.
Root (converges with Chain 1): the same unmade commitment. The churn is not indecision about strategy; the strategy converged. It is displacement activity in place of selling.
▸F The Council Rounds & Premortems

Five independent voices in premortem mode (it is six months from now, Catalyst Works is dead, each voice explains why). Four rounds, written as challenge and response so the reasoning is visible.

Round 1 -- Independent premortems

The Contrarian
Challenge: name the chain of events that killed it.
After 1,047 cold sends and a 0-seat workshop all returned zero, the response was to build more, not to change the motion. It died of building. The fatal premise: that a good enough system attracts buyers. Trust attracts buyers, and trust is earned in rooms, not shipped in code.
First Principles
Challenge: name the one assumption that was wrong from the start.
"Our problem is being known." It was being believed. Every fix targeted reach; none targeted trust. The real question was "why would a check-writer believe an unknown enough to pay," and the honest answer (they would not, yet) was never confronted.
The Outsider
Challenge: what did an outsider see coming that insiders ignored?
A stranger saw an unknown name, a message that changed monthly, four prices, and zero clients, and read "nobody has bought this" in three seconds. Insiders saw a rich system. Outsiders saw an empty storefront and walked.
The Expansionist
Challenge: name what was never tried that would have saved it.
The unscalable first-client motion: three local owners, a free diagnostic, a documented result and testimonial, two referrals each. Plus packaging the operator-as-proof demo. Plus one borrowed list. None of it run.
The Executor
Challenge: name the earliest warning sign and the week it appeared.
The week of May 27. That week, 261 cold sends returned 0 engagement and a lead-gen automation had to be killed for fabricating leads. Together: no real pipeline, and a system faking one. The correct response was to go land one client by hand. Instead, building continued.

Round 2 -- Cross-review: kill the convenient answers

Convergence
All five land on trust and channel, not reach. The convenient answers die: "it's the ads" (350 clicks at 0 percent proves the leak is after the click), "it's the funnel/copy" (no warm traffic to convert), "it's the price" (in-market on every rung), "it's no strategy" (a coherent one exists in the notebooks).
Surviving divergence
Is the one thing proof, or warm distribution?
First Principles resolves it: same constraint, two ends. Proof is the asset; the warm channel is the wire. Warm distribution carries borrowed trust that substitutes for proof at the start, which is how most consultants land client one. Name the unified thing: a trust-carrying path to the buyer.

Round 3 -- Premortem the surviving answer itself

Premortem A
"We built proof and still made zero." Why?
The proof sat on a site nobody trusted enough to visit, shown through cold channels again. Proof with no warm channel is a tree falling in an empty forest.
Premortem B
"We got distribution and still made zero." Why?
A partner blasted the offer to 2,000 owners and still nothing, because the offer sold the method, not a felt outcome, from a host with no proof. Borrowed reach without proof or a sharp outcome is the workshop again at scale.
Resolution
Both single levers die. The answer is the compound the documented plan already contains: trust-carrying channel plus first proof plus one outcome-first offer, executed together. The constraint is not "design this." It is "run this."

Round 4 -- Premortem the precondition

Premortem C
"We did everything right and it still died." Why?
The owner took the role, or kept splitting across six efforts, and never gave the motion the concentrated weeks it needs. The earliest warning was visible May 1, eight weeks before the workshop: a document war-gaming the wind-down. The plan was never the risk. The commitment was.
Chairman synthesis -- convergence ~92% The binding constraint is execution of an already-correct go-to-market plan through a trust-carrying channel to the buyer, which has never been run, blocked by an unmade founder-commitment decision. The proof-versus-distribution tension resolves into a compound the existing plan already specifies. The convenient single-cause answers are killed with evidence. The one open question only the owner can answer: is Catalyst Works the boat, or the side project?
Where this lands Same verdict as the main report, now with the work shown. The strategy is good and already written. The market is real and the pricing is right. The one thing missing has never been the plan; it has been running it, and underneath that, deciding to. Read the full Margin Diagnostic Sprint report for the fix and the 30/60/90 plan.
