What you missed: the three verticals where your single unfair advantage actually compounds are Recruiting & Staffing Consulting Firms Financial / Professional Services. Higher ticket ($8K to $60K vs $5K to $20K local), the buyer is a Managing Partner or Head of Ops (your peer, not a $47 price-shopper), and no other "AI consultant" can say "I ran talent inside the Fortune 100."

Recommendation — ADD, do not replace: keep the local-trade spray running as cashflow (it is live, geolisted fits it). But open one narrow premium lane into recruiting + boutique professional-services firms where your HR/operator moat is the whole pitch. The guide's own line proves the point: "One industry understood deeply beats five understood vaguely."

Wedge note: geolisted's visibility scan is a fine cold opener for these firms, but visibility is not their real pain. Pair it with an operator "where are your hours leaking" audit that lands on the same buyer. That is your locked pitch exactly.

What we missed — concrete

Every vertical your cold engine currently touches sits in the same box: local-search commodity. Home Services, Dental, Healthcare, Real Estate. Strong funnel fit, easy reach, and zero moat leverage. You are selling "you are invisible to AI search" to buyers who cannot tell you apart from any other automation shop, so it becomes a price fight.

The miss is the cluster where your one unfair advantage does the selling for you:

Recruiting & Staffing — the sharpest miss

  • The guide's top pains: resume screening + candidate ranking, job-board distribution, interview scheduling, pipeline tracking, placement reporting. This is the exact machinery of the function you ran for 15 years.
  • Its positioning tip: "Recruiting firms live and die by speed. Frame everything around time-to-fill." You do not have to learn that language. You lived it inside the Fortune 100.
  • You also built a working multi-agent system yourself. Screening/ranking/scheduling is the single most natural demo you could show, and you can show it from the buyer's side of the table.
  • Honest framing: you were the corporate talent insider, not a staffing-desk owner. That is still the strongest credibility any outside "AI consultant" can bring to a firm that sells talent.

Consulting + Professional Services (Consulting, Financial, Law, Accounting, A&E)

  • Consulting firms: proposals, SOWs, utilization tracking. You are a consulting firm. The buyer (Managing Partner / COO) is your peer and speaks operator, not "AI magic."
  • Financial services: highest ticket on the entire board ($15K–$60K+). The guide says to "position yourself as someone who understands regulated industries." You spent a decade inside the Fortune 100 — that is not a claim, it is your resume.
  • Higher ticket, stickier retainers, referral-rich. The opposite of the $5K–$20K local-trade grind.

The AI tools in the guide are not the miss — most are generic. The miss is which buyer you point them at. You have been pointing at the one segment where your moat is worth nothing.

Pivot vs Focus — recommendation

Do not pivot. Add a focused second lane. Keep the engine running.

KEEP — local-trade spray as the cashflow front door

It is live, automated, and the visibility funnel fits it perfectly. It funds the month while the premium lane's longer sales cycle matures. Killing it to chase premium would be a cashflow mistake. This is the reason the recommendation is ADD, not REPLACE.

FOCUS — one premium lane: Recruiting/Staffing + boutique professional-services

This is where the guide's own thesis and your moats agree: "One industry understood deeply beats five understood vaguely." Pick the one nobody else can copy — the HR seat — and go deep. Higher ticket, peer buyer, defensible, referral-driven.

Does the geolisted visibility wedge still work for these? Partly. Recruiting firms and boutique consultancies do want to be found (inbound candidates and clients), so the visibility scan still opens the cold door. But visibility is not their deepest pain — operational drag is (time-to-fill, utilization, intake and proposal cycle). So the scan is the opener, not the offer.

The companion wedge you need

Add an operator "where is your firm leaking hours" audit that lands on the same Managing Partner / Head of Ops. It maps the hours lost in the workflows the guide already ranked (screening, scheduling, proposals, utilization), priced at your existing $250 tier, funneling to the $597 fix and then the retainer. This is your locked pitch — find the constraint, fix it with the right tool when it fits, stay on to run it. Constraint-first, no AI-hammer.

Top 3 moves — prioritized

Move 1. Open the Recruiting & Staffing lane moat: 15yr HR / F100

Why it fits you specifically: the pains are the function you ran; the buyer speaks a language you lived; you can demo from the buyer's seat.

First action: build a 25-target list of boutique / independent recruiting + staffing firms (owner or Head-of-Ops reachable, not enterprise). Run the visibility scan as the cold opener, but lead the email with the insider angle — "I spent 10 years inside GE, a Fortune 100 company; I know exactly where time-to-fill leaks." Funnel: scan → operator hours-leak audit → retainer.

Move 2. Build the operator "hours-leak" companion audit moat: operator + MBA

Why it fits you specifically: professional-services buyers do not buy "AI," they buy utilization and fewer dropped balls. Your operator/MBA lens is the read they trust.

First action: draft the audit spec — a short diagnostic that maps hours lost across intake, proposal/SOW, scheduling, and utilization, priced at the $250 tier, funneling to the $597 fix. Point it at Consulting, Law, Accounting, Financial, A&E. Keep it constraint-first, tool-agnostic. No AI-hammer.

Move 3. Segment the current cold list into two tracks moat: keep cashflow

Why it fits you specifically: you do not lose the working engine; you route the right message to the right buyer off one list.

First action: tag your existing contacts. Local trades stay on the pure visibility offer (cashflow track). Any recruiting / professional-services / healthcare-admin contacts get routed to the HR/operator messaging (premium track). Time accounting-firm outreach for Oct/Nov (source tip); run recruiting year-round on the time-to-fill hook.

Hype vs high-leverage on the page

What to ignore (violates your positioning):

  • The core promise: "sound like the expert even if you've never worked in the vertical." That is the exact opposite of your moat. Your entire edge is that you did sit in the seat. Do not adopt fake-expertise-in-five-verticals.
  • The build-and-bounce project-fee model ($5K–$60K one-off). You stay on to run the system. Different business, different retention. Take the vertical intel, leave the model.
  • The ROI calculator's "close the deal with math, not hype." Useful as a supporting artifact, but it is a spray-agency closing tool. Your close is trust and the seat, not a spreadsheet ambush.

What is genuinely high-leverage (use it):

  • The per-vertical pain taxonomy and "who writes the check" buyer map — real, specific, ROI-ranked. Good raw material for your outreach and audit spec.
  • The line "One industry understood deeply beats five understood vaguely" — it argues against your current spray and for the focused lane. Use it as your own north star.
  • The recruiting "time-to-fill = revenue" and consulting "utilization = revenue" framings — native to you, ready to lift straight into copy.
  • The healthcare tip: "Don't lead with AI. Lead with workflow automation." That is your no-hammer rule, confirmed by a practitioner with 80+ engagements.
