What is at stake if this stays undecided one more week

Eleven routes currently cap at a reply he cannot answer. That is the whole cost, and it is worse than it sounds, because the failure lands at the exact moment a stranger finally responds. A warm reply met with "let me get back to you on pricing" spends the scarcest thing in the pipeline, which is a person's willingness to have the conversation at all.

The second cost is quieter. Every day without a price, the system produces assets instead of asks, because assets are the thing it can finish. That is precisely the failure the value-add test names: a thousand posts that lead to zero dollars are not value add.

The third is arithmetic. Forty days remain to 2026-09-20 and $0 is collected. A yes takes a reply, a conversation, and a decision. Compress that to its floor and it is still two to three weeks from first contact. The offer has to exist before the first reply arrives, not after.

What is irreversible here, and what is not

Irreversible

  • A price quoted to a specific human. Once he tells someone $3,000, that person's anchor is set permanently. He can raise it for the next buyer, never for that one. That asymmetry is exactly why the decided number is the higher one: nobody has been quoted anything yet, so the first quote should be the one he wants to live with.
  • A contact burned by a message sent before the offer existed. The reachable pool is one verified prospect. Spending it on a conversation he cannot finish costs more than the delay.
  • A public price on a live page. Nothing is public today. That is an advantage worth keeping. Recommend the price stays spoken and in email until two people have paid it, because a page makes it a commitment to strangers he has not met.

Fully reversible

  • The number itself, for every future buyer. Nothing has been published, so replacing all four legacy ranges breaks no promise to anyone.
  • The continuation price. Nobody reaches the end of a 21-day term for at least three weeks after a first yes.
  • The capacity cap, upward. Saying three and later taking a fourth is fine. Saying three and having five is the thing that is not.

The sentence he can say out loud tomorrow, and it is Option B he is describing

This is the spoken version of Option B and only Option B. If he picks A or C instead, this block does not transfer and has to be rewritten, because the price, the period and the ending are all different.

"Here is how I work. I sit with you for an hour and record it, then I write three pieces in your voice over the next three weeks. You keep the part that matters after I leave: a guide to how you actually sound, a set of prompts your own team can run to draft in that voice, and a one page process for keeping it going. Three thousand, once. You read the first piece before you pay me anything. When the three weeks are done, you either run it yourself with what you have, or I stay on and run it with you. I will tell you which one I think fits."

Ran through boub_voice_mastery. Scored 74 on the first pass and was blocked, then rewritten and re-scored at 93. Re-scored on 2026-08-11 after the price swap to $3,000: 93, held. The gate noted one thing and did not block on it: at the higher number the price arrives with no anchor beside it, which the block partly answers on its own because the hour is named in its first line. First person, no em-dashes, no hype pattern, no guarantee or refund language, no unverified numbers, no internal names.

What the voice gate caught, and it binds the whole package The first draft of that block ended "when the three weeks are up, most people want the same thing", struck here so nobody lifts it back out by accident. He has zero ghostwriting clients. A sentence about what past clients wanted is invented proof spoken to a buyer, and it was cut rather than softened, because hedging it to "some people" or "in my experience" keeps the fabrication at a lower volume. This holds everywhere, not just in that one block. Nothing in this offer, in any message built from it, or in any page that ever carries it may imply a track record, a typical client, a usual outcome, or a past result. The honest assets he has are the free five-day course, the systems he has actually built, and the piece he writes for a specific person before asking them for anything.

What he delivers, exactly

  • One 60-minute recorded working session. He runs it. This is where the thinking comes from and it is the part nobody else can do.
  • Three published pieces in the founder's voice, over 21 days, delivered ready to post. Two revision rounds each, capped.
  • A written voice guide. How this person actually sounds, with the rules, in a form someone else can follow.
  • A prompt pack the founder's own team can run to draft in that voice without him in the room.
  • A one-page weekly process for keeping the cadence alive after the engagement ends.

What is explicitly not included

Naming the exclusions is what makes it read as someone who has done this before, and it prevents the scope creep that kills a first engagement. Not included: running his accounts, replying as him, design, video, ads, distribution beyond where he already posts, and anything past two revision rounds per piece.

What happens at the end of the term

The engagement ends on delivery of the third piece. Nothing renews, so there is nothing to cancel and no notice period to negotiate. Three doors, and he recommends one rather than handing over a menu:

  • (a) Done. The client runs it with the pack. This is a real, honest ending and offering it is what makes the other two credible.
  • (b) He keeps writing. $2,500 per month, month to month.
  • (c) He comes in as the fractional AI operator. $2,500 per month, month to month, where the writing engine is one of several systems he builds and runs inside the business. This is the recommended door and the reason the offer is shaped this way.
What the decided price changed, and what it did not As originally proposed at $1,500, Option B gave up $1,000 per deal against Option A. At the decided $3,000 that trade is gone: it clears Option A per deal while still ending on delivery instead of asking for a retainer. What did not change is the continuation, which stays at $2,500 per month rather than $5,000. That is a real reduction and it is deliberate. The $5,000 figure is his own untested target with nothing external behind it (docs/plans/2026-08-10-pga-closeout.md §0.1), and asking a stranger for it with no case study, from a pool of one verified reachable prospect, is a bet on a number nobody has validated. Two clients at $2,500 per month is $5,000 per month recurring, which is the same business, reached by a shorter first step.

04  The why

Five reasons, then the pressure test that tried to break them.

  1. It is the only one of the three whose ending is a fractional conversation. Option A's best case is a bigger writing invoice. Option C has no ending at all. Fractional engagements are the north star and everything else is a means to it (memory/feedback_fractional_ai_engagements_are_the_north_star_2026_08_10.md, 2026-08-10). An offer whose success state is "more ghostwriting" is scoring the wrong objective.
  2. It is pack-shaped, which is the standing product rule as of today. The voice guide and prompt pack are built once and reused on client two and three. A service asks a buyer to trust him before anything happens. A pack can be bought without that trust, and the relationship is what it produces rather than what it requires (memory/feedback_pack_before_service_2026_08_11.md, 2026-08-11).
  3. It reaches the September numbers on the fewest yeses. At the decided $3,000, one buyer clears the $3,000 target and a second makes it two named clients. As originally proposed at $1,500 it took two buyers for the money alone. Option C needs six. When reach is the binding constraint, cutting the number of required yeses is worth more than cutting the price.
  4. It assumes no cold trust, which is what the evidence demands. 767 sends produced zero replies and zero clients, root-caused as a missing trust anchor and not the copy (docs/LIVE_STATE.md line 211, set 2026-06-09). The first piece is written and delivered before money moves, so the proof arrives before the ask does.
  5. It differentiates on the thing that is actually true about him. Ten years inside a Fortune 100 in corporate HR plus a communications degree, and he builds the systems himself. Most ghostwriters cannot build. Most builders cannot write. Handing over a working engine is the only version of this offer that uses both.
Council: five angles, run against the recommendation before it was written up

1. The skeptical buyer

Does $1,500 read as cheap, and therefore amateur, to a funded founder? At the top of the market, yes, it can. The mitigation is that it is framed as a fixed-scope first engagement with a named end date, not a discount off something bigger, and no comparison price is ever mentioned. The real cost is that a founder who would have paid $2,500 now pays $1,500. With zero closed deals in this lane, that is the right cost to pay. Held as written.

Overturned by the decision, and this is the one angle the decision reverses. The wiring pass found the discount buys nothing, because the free first piece already pays for the missing case study, and paying for one credibility gap twice is waste rather than caution. At the decided $3,000 this angle inverts: the risk is no longer reading as amateur, it is a founder who would have paid $1,500 declining at $3,000. Nothing that exists today is data on that, because nobody has been quoted anything. The first real quote that dies at $3,000 is what would settle it.

2. The cash angle

Forty days, $0 collected. Both A and B need two humans to say yes. B needs the smaller yes, so it wins on probability at equal effort. Timing is a tie: both take payment after the first piece, so cash lands roughly a week after a yes in either. C needs six yeses from a pool with one verified prospect, which is not a plan. B on probability, tie on speed.

3. The north star

This is the decisive angle. A's default ending is more writing at a higher rate. B's recommended ending is the fractional engagement, and the handed-over pack is what makes that door obvious rather than a pitch. C has no ending that forces the question. B, clearly.

4. Delivery reality

Three pieces plus a voice guide plus a prompt pack in 21 days, potentially three clients at once, alongside two workshop sessions in the same window. That is tight and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Two mitigations: the cap is three and it is real, and the pack scaffolding is built once so clients two and three cost materially less than client one. Held at cap three, with the tightness named rather than hidden.

5. Premortem: it is 2026-09-20 and this failed

The most likely cause is not the price. It is that he never got in front of enough humans, because the verified reachable pool was one person and nothing fixed that. Second most likely: the voice guide and prompt pack never got built, so the offer quietly collapsed back into hours and the differentiator vanished. Third: the offer leaked into coaching-program correspondence, breaking the standalone rule that keeps those messages about the ghostwriting journey only. All three are named in the section below so none of them gets to be a surprise.
