Logging into a KSL account from the automated side would NOT raise the ceiling, because the automated side isn't blocked for lack of credentials — it's blocked for looking like a script.

What would actually help: not a KSL login, but a genuine browser origin — the same Chrome-over-CDP bridge pattern already used elsewhere this session (pointing the tool at your own real Chrome, launched with --remote-debugging-port=9222, so requests carry your real browser's fingerprint and your home/laptop IP instead of the automation server's). That sidesteps the wall because it looks like an actual person browsing, not because of any account. A saved-Playwright-login-session (the pattern used for Skool) is the wrong tool here specifically, since KSL doesn't gate content behind a login to begin with — that pattern solves a different problem (authenticated content, not bot detection).

Bottom line: no need to log into KSL for this. The fastest real path is still you (or a real browser session) opening the listing links above directly — see Search It Yourself below.

Financing — Cash vs. Securities-Backed Loan vs. Auto Loan

This only matters if a dealer listing ends up being the real path (the current shortlist's dealer candidates range $2,500-$4,995). Context: 18-month runway, no current income, cash preservation is the priority — same constraint used for the earlier bridge-computer research.

✓ Recommended: Pay Cash

Cost$2,500-$4,995 one-time, zero interest, zero risk
WhyA bridge car is a guaranteed-depreciating asset at a low dollar amount relative to the portfolio. There's no leveraged-risk case for it — the "keep the stock invested" argument only pays off if the market outperforms the loan rate, and that upside isn't worth putting any part of an 18-month, no-income runway at risk for a car being replaced later anyway.

Securities-Backed Line of Credit (SBLOC / margin-adjacent)

Rate~6.7%-7.15% currently at major brokers (Schwab Pledged Asset Line ≈ SOFR + 2.4%; Fidelity's SBLOC is similarly SOFR-indexed), variable, resets monthly or daily depending on the broker
Real riskIf the pledged portfolio drops in value, the broker can issue a maintenance/margin call demanding more collateral or forcing a stock sale — often exactly when the market (and the portfolio) is already down. That's the wrong moment to be forced to sell.
VerdictAvailable at most major brokerages (Schwab, Fidelity, IBKR, and others all offer some version), but wrong tool for this. Using it here means carrying open-ended market risk on the exact capital that funds an income-free runway, to finance an asset that's losing value every month. Not recommended for a purchase this small.

Conventional Auto Loan

RealityMost lenders won't originate an auto loan under roughly $5,000-$7,500 at all — confirmed in this research, not assumed. A sub-$5K car often isn't loan-eligible on its own; some lenders only get there if fees/add-ons push the financed amount up, which defeats the point of buying cheap.
VerdictNot a realistic path at this price point. If a lender is found, expect a subprime-tier rate and a required down payment — no real advantage over paying cash outright.

The risk being flagged plainly

Borrowing against a stock portfolio is not free money — it's leverage. If the market drops while the loan is open, the required minimum collateral value doesn't change, but the portfolio's actual value does, which can trigger a forced sale at a loss. That risk is real and worth taking seriously for a large purchase (a house, a business investment). For a $2,500-$5,000 bridge car, it's a mismatch: real downside risk taken on to finance an asset with no upside. Cash is the right call here.
