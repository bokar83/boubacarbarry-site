TL;DR

A tool that decides whether a liquidation pallet auction is worth bidding on, before any money moves. Upload the manifest CSV (or type the numbers by hand), answer a few questions, and it hands back a GREEN/YELLOW/RED verdict plus two bid numbers: the conservative one you should actually use, and the optimistic ceiling you should not aim for.

1. What it does

Liquidation pallet auctions (B-Stock and similar) are binding: once a bid wins, there is no backing out. The scorer exists to move all of the arithmetic — landed cost, breakeven, freight, margin — in front of the bid, so a decision that currently happens on a gut feeling in the last minute of an auction instead happens on paper, calmly, before the auction ever opens.

  • Reads a manifest CSV straight off a marketplace listing's "Download CSV" button — retail value, unit count, condition mix, and category breakdown fill in automatically. No manifest for the lot? Every one of those fields can be typed in by hand instead; the tool works identically either way.
  • Prices freight by origin, not by guesswork. Freight scales with distance and weight, not with what is on the pallet, so where a lot ships from is treated as a buying criterion. Lanes are tiered on road miles to the Salt Lake valley (local / west / mid / cross-country), and an estimated freight figure gets marked up by tier because the estimator is one straight line through a single real quote and its error grows with distance.
  • Asks for your own predicted recovery BEFORE showing the result. That ordering is deliberate — it is how you find out whether you personally tend to run optimistic, rather than anchoring on the tool's own number first.
  • Outputs a strict, deliberately rare GREEN verdict. The bar is set high on purpose so STRONG VALUE means something; see Section 4.

3. How to use it on a real B-Stock lot
  1. Open the listing detail page (not the browse tile) with your ship-to address set — the browse tile's "% of MSRP" excludes shipping and understated true landed cost by ~6.7x on the one lot measured.
  2. Click Download CSV on the manifest and upload it. Retail, units, condition and category mix fill in automatically.
  3. Copy the real Shipping & Other Charges figure into "Shipping, real quote," and set the shipping terms. A blank figure falls back to an estimator, and an estimated lot cannot reach STRONG VALUE. Free or flat-rate shipping is not an estimate — it carries no freight risk, so it can reach STRONG VALUE from any origin, but a flat-rate box needs the real amount typed in.
  4. Answer the judgement questions before looking at the result — your own predicted recovery is recorded first, on purpose.
  5. Read the breakeven percentage first. It is arithmetic. Everything below it is a guess.

4. What the colours mean

This is a VALUE ranking — it tells you which lots are worth chasing, not which ones are "safe." Nothing about a binding, uninspected, non-refundable bid is safe.

  • GREEN — STRONG VALUE. The math clearly works: strong margin, strong return. Go after it, and stop at the maximum bid.
  • YELLOW — MARGINAL. Only pays at the optimistic end of a range built on guesses. Worth a second look, never worth chasing.
  • RED — PASS. Bad math. Avoid entirely.

The bar is deliberately strict, so STRONG VALUE is meant to be rare. Lower your targets if thinner margins are acceptable — never lower them to make a particular lot pass.

The two bid numbers, and the one that leads is the conservative one: "Bid up to this and you are still happy" (the walk-away number, hits your targets even at the LOW end of the resale band — this is the number to use) versus "Above this it stops paying at all" (the mid-case ceiling, assumes every guess lands exactly where expected — do not aim for it). B-Stock bids by proxy, so whatever ceiling you type is spent automatically; enter the lower of the two, and never revise it upward mid-auction.

5. The ledger — tracking what actually happened

Every score is stored automatically. Open http://127.0.0.1:8899/ledger at any time to see every lot scored: the tool's prediction, your own sealed prediction, and (once resold) the real outcome.

Every recovery percentage the tool shows is a stated prior with n=0 — a starting guess, not a track record. The ledger is how that guess gets replaced with real numbers over time. Log what each lot actually sold for, or the priors stay guesses forever.

Storage today: a local SQLite file (ledger.py) plus, on an opt-in checkbox at scoring time, a Postgres-backed running record on orc-postgres (reports.py, table pallet_scorer_reports). Both are real, live write paths from the same form as of the most recent build. The ledger view itself is read-only today; an editable ledger is a planned follow-up, not built yet.

6. Where it fits in the Cowrie / pallet-liquidation workflow

This tool answers exactly one question: is this specific lot, at this specific price, worth bidding on? That is the pre-bid step of the pallet liquidation resale lane — specifically the moment described there as "compute landed cost before the first bid" and "write the ceiling down before the auction opens." The scorer is what actually runs that arithmetic instead of doing it by hand under time pressure during a live auction.

It is deliberately upstream of, and separate from, manifest-to-market, which starts once a lot has already been won and turns the manifest into actual resale listings. Pre-bid scoring decides whether to spend the money at all; manifest-to-market handles what happens after the money is already spent.
