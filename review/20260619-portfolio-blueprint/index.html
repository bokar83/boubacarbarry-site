0 · The core strategic call

An operator who built and runs a real multi-agent system, not a dev, not an academic

Most people who call themselves AI experts describe. Almost none can show a living system they built, maintain, and teach. That gap IS the page.

The whole page is one argument: proof you can touch beats claims you have to trust. Every section converts a claim into a thing the buyer can see running or operate themselves.

Truth-only spine: every interactive element runs on real, pre-baked or backend-proxied examples of actual work. Zero invented metrics, zero fake clients, zero placeholder logos. If a number is not verified and permanent, it does not appear. Internal codenames never appear; everything is described by function.

1 · Narrative spine

The order a buyer should feel it

A buyer moves through a feeling, not a feature list.
  1. Hook: this is different.
    One specific claim plus one thing already moving on screen. No tagline, no buzzword hero.
  2. The living system: he actually built this.
    The interactive map of his real multi-agent system. This is the "THE expert" moment.
  3. How he thinks: judgment, not just tools.
    The idea-filter, made interactive. Shows he decides what NOT to build.
  4. Proof it ships: and it works.
    Before and after on a real workflow plus the prompt-to-result walkthrough. Time and mess turned into a clean result, live.
  5. He teaches it: he can hand it to my people.
    The live workshop plus plain-language teaching beat. De-risks adoption.
  6. The operator behind it: I would trust him in the room.
    The human and trust beat: HR-to-executives, honest-advisor stance, "I will tell you when AI is not the answer."
  7. The evidence wall: here is where it all lives.
    Real deployed sites and dashboards, sanitized. Quiet, dense, undeniable. No CTA. The page ends by letting the proof stand.
The "THE expert" moment is the system map. The instant a buyer hovers a node and watches a real agent flow animate (lead comes in, research agent, draft in his voice, log, his 20-second review), they understand he does not read about agents, he runs them. That is the screenshot they send to whoever else is deciding.
2 · Interactive elements, ranked by wow per effort

Build the top three for launch, stage four and five for v2

Every one is safe: no client-side keys or secrets, pre-baked real data or backend proxy only.
1 Interactive multi-agent system map (THE hero) BUILD FIRST

What: a clickable, hoverable diagram of his real operating system. Nodes are the actual agents he runs (lead research, outreach drafting, email triage, content scheduling, research, morning dashboards). Hover a node and it lights, the data-flow edges animate, and a plain-language card explains what it does and what it replaced. Click "run the lead flow" and a token of light travels the path: new lead, research agent, personal first-message draft, log, his quick review-and-send.

Why it wins: the artifact almost no applicant has. Shows architecture judgment, real integrations, and that it runs daily. The "doctoral thesis, but fun" centerpiece.

Skill shown: multi-agent design, orchestration, integrationEffort: medium-highWow: highest
2 Watch an idea go through the filter (highest wow per effort) BUILD

What: the buyer types an idea or picks from three or four real seed examples, presses "filter," and it animates through his one real question (does this move revenue or the main goal?) and lands in BUILD NOW or PARK with a one-line reason.

Why it wins: tiny to build, demonstrates the judgment buyers fear AI consultants lack, and it is genuinely his framework from the proposals. Makes "I decide what not to build" tangible and playful.

Skill shown: his idea-filter, operator judgmentEffort: low-mediumWow: high
3 Before and after slider on a real workflow (hours to minutes) BUILD

What: a draggable slider. Left is the manual reality (a morning of researching one lead, writing a personal note, logging it). Right is the same job after his build (a short review-and-send). Dragging reveals the collapse of steps and time. Time figures shown only if he confirms a real, honest number; otherwise it shows step-collapse and "most of a morning to a quick review," no fabricated metric.

Why it wins: the universal buyer emotion, "you give me my time back," made physical. Pairs naturally under the system map.

Skill shown: workflow mapping, automation ROIEffort: lowWow: high
4 Prompt to structured result walkthrough (real pre-baked examples) STAGE FOR V2

What: pick a real task, click, and watch a messy input transform into a clean structured result, step-stepped. Uses two or three real pre-baked input and output pairs from his actual work, sanitized.

Why it wins: shows the actual craft, prompt and extraction quality, without exposing how. Feels live without any live call.

Effort: mediumWow: medium-highpromote if a clean real example is ready
5 Scrollytelling teardown of one real system he built STAGE FOR V2

What: as the buyer scrolls, one real system (the lead pipeline) assembles itself piece by piece with short plain-language captions, the judgment calls, not just the tools. Ends on "and it runs every morning."

Why it wins: teaching plus judgment plus maintenance in one scroll. But it overlaps the system map; risk of redundancy. Only if it teaches something the map cannot.

Cut for now (Karpathy): the interactive build-vs-buy decision tool. Good idea, but it risks feeling like a lead-magnet quiz and competes with the idea-filter for the judgment slot. The honest-advisor line ("sometimes the right answer is NOT more AI") carries this beat in copy. Park.
Launch set: 1 + 2 + 3. Three live, touchable proofs across hero, judgment, and ROI. That trio manufactures the reaction without bloat.

5 · Interactive safety rule (all elements)

No API keys or secrets client-side. Ever. The launch set (1, 2, 3) uses pre-baked real data plus client-side logic only, so zero key exposure by construction.

If any element later goes truly live (a prompt-to-result via a real model call), it must route through a server-side backend proxy with the key held server-side and rate-limited. Default to pre-baked for launch.

6 · Premium-unique design direction

Evolved CW brand, editorial thesis that breathes

PALETTE (evolved, not the old teal and navy)

Ink #0A0E14 Cream paper Clay #B47C57 One warm glow accent, 10% surface max

Type: Spectral for display and headline (the editorial thesis gravity) plus Public Sans for body and UI (the clean operator voice), per the current typography standard.

Signature motif: subtle West African mudcloth and Adinkra geometry as the through-line: section seams as torn-paper SVG edges, accent lines as irregular hand-inked ribbons (not CSS gradient bars, not a plain border), the system-map node network echoing organic Adinkra symmetry. Organic-geometry first pass: no rectangle tells, no visible grid. Volta-grade polish.

Feel: an editorial thesis that breathes and moves: dark, warm, confident, alive. The motion is purposeful (the system actually flowing), never decorative.

Karpathy anti-slop, cut hard
  • No buzzword hero ("AI-powered transformation"). Lead with one specific claim.
  • No skill bars or percentage meters. They scream junior. Proof replaces self-rating.
  • No stock-icon grid of generic AI, automation, strategy tiles.
  • No logo soup or fake client wall. Show his real deployed sites, not borrowed logos.
  • No fabricated metrics, no "10x," no countdown or hype.
  • No second judgment widget competing with the idea-filter.
  • No contact form, no Calendly, no off-platform CTA. Observe and be impressed.
