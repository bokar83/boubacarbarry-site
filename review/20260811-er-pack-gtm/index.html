00 / THE VERDICTRead this part if you read nothing else

The finding that changes the decision

This plan will probably not produce 10 buyers in 14 days, and the reason is distribution, not the product. If we judge the pack on that bar, we risk killing something good for the wrong reason.

I worked the arithmetic against the audiences that actually exist in the system rather than the ones the spec assumed. The honest read is below in section 02. Short version: the reachable HR audience today is roughly one hundred to two hundred people, almost all of them inside one asset, and that asset is a list of named humans he is already connected to. Everything else rounds to zero.

So the plan has one spine and three supports. The spine is direct messages to about twenty named HR practitioners he already knows. The supports are a free-first content sequence that warms those messages, a referral play aimed at people who read investigation files professionally, and a trust artifact that lets a stranger verify the product without trusting him.

The three moves, in order
  1. Direct messages to about twenty named HR connections. Owner: the system drafts every one, personalised, ready to send. Boubacar presses send. Nearest thing to a paid conversation we have.
  2. A ten piece free-first content sequence over two weeks. Owner: the system drafts and schedules to the content board as drafts. Boubacar posts. Seven of the ten ask for nothing.
  3. Ten free copies to certified practitioners, reframed as a reaction request rather than a testimonial request. Owner: the system builds the list and drafts. Boubacar sends.
The reframe worth more than the $190

Ten sales at $19 is $190. That number is not the point and treating it as the point is how this gets misread. The pack is a legitimate, non-salesy reason to open about twenty conversations with senior HR people, several of them chief people officers and HR directors at real companies. Those same people buy AI governance help. The $19 is a qualification device, not the revenue.

Multiple domains pointing at the same page: what that actually buys

Close to nothing for conversion, and it can cost in search. The same page served at two addresses is duplicate content, and search engines have to guess which one is canonical. That splits rather than adds.

The only correct form is one canonical URL plus 301 redirects from any other domain. A 301 passes traffic and authority to the canonical page instead of competing with it. That is worth doing if he ever wants a short memorable address to say out loud in a talk or on a call. It is not worth doing now, because nobody is typing any of these addresses yet.

Does a domain touch the real constraint

No. Not at all. The binding constraint on record is 767 cold sends producing zero replies and zero clients, root caused as a missing trust anchor rather than the copy. Nobody in that failure ever saw a domain. They saw a from line and a subject line and did not open or did not reply. A URL cannot supply a trust anchor. A named human who vouches can, and a free artifact that works before you pay can. That is what section 04 is for.

LinkedInno ask Day 1, opens the arc

Nobody has ever asked an AI tool to explain a finding in an appeal hearing. They ask you. I spent about fifteen years working in HR at large companies, several of them in the Fortune 100. The part of that job nobody outside it understands is that a workplace investigation is not a report. It is a document that gets read later, by people who were not in the room, looking for the place where your reasoning breaks. Now a lot of the profession is drafting those documents with AI. I understand why. The writing is the slowest part of the work. But the tool does not sit in the appeal hearing. The tool does not answer to the tribunal, the arbitrator, outside counsel, or the person whose job your file ended. You do. The investigation file has your name on it. Not the AI's. That is not an argument against using AI in employee relations. I build AI systems for a living now, and I would use them for this. It is an argument for knowing exactly what the tool wrote, why it chose those words, and what breaks if you leave them in.

Xno ask Day 2

An AI summary of an investigation will hand you: "The respondent was dismissive of the complainant's concerns." Ask which line of the transcript proves "dismissive." There isn't one. That is a conclusion formatted like evidence, sitting in your file, under your name.

LinkedInno ask Day 4, the craft post

"The respondent was unprofessional." I read that sentence, or a cousin of it, for years inside HR at large companies. It is not a finding. It is an opinion wearing a finding's clothes. Here is the test. Take it, it is yours. Take any sentence in your draft and ask which of four categories it belongs to. Fact: a witness saw or heard it, or a document in the file shows it. "He raised his voice." Asserted without evidence: it reads like a fact, and nothing in the file supports it. "He had done this before." Inference: a reasonable step from a fact, but still a step. "He was trying to intimidate her." Opinion: yours. "He was unprofessional." Then hunt the adjectives. Aggressive. Hostile. Dismissive. Unprofessional. Every one of those is doing evidentiary work it was never handed evidence for. Either cut it, or put the evidence directly behind it. A file survives on the first category and dies on the second. The reason this matters more than it did five years ago is that a generic AI prompt produces category four at speed. Fluent, confident, unsupported. Which adjective is the one you keep having to defend?

Xno ask Day 5, contrarian

Most AI productivity advice is: get to a good first draft faster, then edit. That breaks in an employee relations investigation, because the draft is the artifact. There is no separate final. Whatever the model's wording talked you into is what the tribunal reads.

LinkedInno ask Day 7, the regulation

On 2 August 2026 the EU AI Act became applicable. Here is the part that lands on employee relations, in plain language. Annex III, point 4(b) classifies AI used for decisions on promotion, termination and performance monitoring as high risk. That is not a category about chatbots. That is a category about the work employee relations does every week. If you deploy a system in that category: Article 26(2) requires human oversight by people with the competence and the authority to actually exercise it. Not a named reviewer on an org chart. Someone who can overrule the output. Article 26(6) requires the logs to be kept for at least six months. Article 26(7) requires workers to be informed before the system is used on them. Article 99(4) sets deployer fines up to 15,000,000 euro or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. Two honest caveats. This binds you only if you have people in the EU, so a purely domestic US employer sits outside it today. And I am not a lawyer, this is not legal advice, and your counsel gets the final read. The part that will outlive the regulation is the shape of it. Competent human oversight, a log, and notice. That is good investigation practice with a citation attached.

06 / THE TRUST ANCHORWhy a cold HR buyer believes him at all

What the spec proposed, and what is wrong with it

The spec proposes ten free review copies to named certified holders in exchange for on record quotes. The instinct is right. The execution has three problems.

  1. The list does not exist. Three certified holders have been identified, not ten. The move is not executable on day one as written.
  2. The ask is too big for the value. Requesting an on record quote for a $19 product asks a busy professional to lend her professional name, which is a much larger favour than the product is worth. Response rates on that ask are low and the refusals are awkward.
  3. A stranger's quote is weak anyway. Testimonials are the softest form of proof, and a buyer who has been marketed to knows a solicited quote when she reads one.

The improvement

Ask for a reaction, not a quote. Ask her to run the free prompt on a file she has already closed and tell him what it caught, and explicitly invite her to tell him it is useless. The ask becomes small, specific and genuinely interesting to a professional. It starts a conversation instead of requesting a favour. And if the reaction is good, it is the quote, offered rather than extracted, which is the only kind worth having.

The stronger anchor is already on the page

The real trust anchor here is not borrowed credibility. It is two things he already has.

  • The free prompt. A buyer can run it against her own closed file and see it work before she spends anything. Proof by demonstration beats every testimonial ever written, and it is the single strongest asset the sales page has. The plan should lead with it everywhere, which is why seven of ten posts and the opening message all give it away.
  • He sat in HR at large companies for about fifteen years. Not as a claim to authority over her file, which the day 14 post explicitly disclaims, but as evidence he has read a lot of these documents. Peer credibility, stated small.

10 / COUNCIL AND KARPATHYThe pressure test

Council, five angles. The hammer: is this distribution or production in costume

Angle 1, the arithmetic

Ran the real audience numbers rather than the assumed ones. Produced the central finding: the 10 in 14 days bar measures distribution, which we already know is weak, rather than demand, which is the unknown. Recorded in section 02 with a recommendation to change the bar.

Angle 2, production in a distribution costume

Audited every item by whether it puts the pack in front of a named human. The ten social pieces failed this test hardest. They are content, and content is only distribution when an audience exists. His organic reach is genuinely small, and the temptation is to over invest in the posts precisely because they are the easiest thing for a system to produce at volume. Verdict: the posts stay because they are free and they make the messages land, but they were demoted below the direct messages and the plan says plainly that if only one thing happens it is the messages.

Angle 3, false negative risk

Three buyers from 25 engaged practitioners is a strong signal to build more. Three buyers from 5,000 impressions means nothing. Same numerator, opposite decisions. Forced the measurement section to instrument the denominator, and surfaced that the strongest leading indicator, the free prompt copy event, is not built.

Angle 4, collision and reputation

Caught two real collisions. The hrexposed.net audience collision, which would have been an own goal, and the roughly ten HR names who already have live unanswered outreach on other offers and must be dropped from this send. Also flagged a rule violation in live copy on hrexposed.net.

Angle 5, opportunity cost

Asked whether $190 justifies two weeks of attention when the goal is larger clients. It does not, on its own terms. That produced the reframe in section 00 and cross-domain move 2: the pack is a qualification device that opens senior conversations, and the AI governance seam is where the money actually is. This was the single most useful thing the Council pass produced.
