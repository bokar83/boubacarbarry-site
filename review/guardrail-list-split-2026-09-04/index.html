Guardrail List Split — Spec and Measurement

Self-modification gate (Tier-C) narrowing · 2026-09-04 · decision D-20260904-f5a226

TL;DR

TL;DR — I specced it, measured it, and it does not earn the change. Recommendation: do not narrow the gate.

You approved specifying and implementing a narrowing of what counts as self-modification. I built the spec, then replayed it against 400 real merges before touching any code. It removes 7 cards out of 102. That is a permanent narrowing of a hard rule for a 7% win, and Council converged against it at roughly 85-90%, below the 95% bar you set. Implementation is also independently blocked: the rejection-recording fix has not landed on main.

What the data says instead: 36 of the cards fire solely because of one-off scripts in the scripts/ root — which is the other lane's graduation, already in flight. The volume problem is theirs to solve, not this one's. The piece of my work worth keeping is the enumerated guardrail list itself, handed to that lane as the floor their change must not cross.

1. What the gate actually does today

The decision lives in one function, orchestrator/gate_agent.py::_tier_c_hits(). For this repo it holds a branch on a directory test:

TIER_C_DIRS = ("orchestrator/", "scripts/")
TIER_C_DIR_DOC_EXCLUDE_SUFFIXES = (".md",)

Any non-markdown file under those two directories cards for your Telegram tap. The gate is keyed on where a file lives, not on what it can do. That is the whole finding from the earlier review, and it is accurate.

2. The design I specced

Four ordered layers replacing the single directory test. Order is load-bearing, and every layer defaults to holding.

Why an allowlist, never a blocklist

A blocklist fails open: a guardrail file nobody remembered to list auto-merges silently. The allowlist fails closed: anything unlisted cards. Layers 1 and 2 then override the allowlist, so a guardrail dropped inside a graduated directory still cards. And because gate_agent.py is itself in layer 1, widening the graduation list is itself a carded change requiring your tap. The list cannot be quietly grown by the agents it governs.

5. Council

The Contrarian

Spending a hard-rule narrowing to buy 7 branches out of 102 is not a trade, it is a donation. And the layered defence collapses under the one attack it exists to stop: a new guardrail module named policy_router.py inside a graduated directory matches no explicit prefix and trips no keyword. Layer 2 is a hope that guardrails keep being named like guardrails.

The First Principles Thinker

Wrong variable. The gate is not too wide, the tap is doing two jobs. 128 taps with zero rejects means the tap is a notification wearing a safety interlock's clothes. The reframe that makes the whole question moot: keep the gate exactly as wide as it is, and change what a card does — a guardrail hit blocks, everything else merges immediately and posts a notice with one-tap revert. No narrowing at all, and the entire tap burden goes, not 7% of it.

The Expansionist

The enumerated guardrail list is worth far more as a shared primitive than as an input to one predicate. "What is a guardrail" is currently re-derived in at least four places — the gate's tiers, the write-boundary allowlist, the config-write hook, the pre-commit checks. One canonical list all of them read makes every future graduation safe by construction instead of each one re-arguing the boundary. That is the durable asset here. The 7% is noise.

The Outsider

Reading this cold: a person was asked to approve 128 things and approved 128 of them. In any other industry that is not a control, it is a rubber stamp, and the honest reading is that the control was already gone — you have just now measured it. Also striking from outside: 102 of 400 merges touched the machinery that governs the machines. The interesting question is not how to reduce the approvals.

The Executor

Monday reality: the prerequisite has not landed, so implementation is blocked regardless of the verdict. And the single biggest lever, 36 branches, belongs to a lane already mid-flight on the same function. Shipping a 7% change on top of their edit is how two lanes collide. The deliverable this week is the measurement and the guardrail list handed over, not code.

Chairman synthesis

Convergence (four of five voices): do not narrow a hard rule for a 7% win. The measurement kills the design's own justification.

Divergence, named not averaged: the Expansionist argues the enumerated guardrail list should be built anyway as a shared primitive, decoupled from this narrowing. That tension is real and I think he is right — the list is the asset, the narrowing was the excuse to write it.

What all five missed: gate_agent.py is itself layer 1, so the graduation list is self-governing — any widening needs your tap. That is a genuine point in the design's favour that the 7% framing buries. It does not rescue the change.

Convergence: roughly 85–90%. Below the 95% bar you set for this class of change. The gate does not clear.

Recommendation: kill the narrowing. Keep the guardrail list.

6. What I did not do, and why
  • No code written. Both gates must clear before implementation and neither did.
  • The prerequisite has not landed. I checked git log origin/main and every remote branch on 2026-09-04. There is no branch or commit touching gate rejection recording or approval persistence. Implementation was blocked on this independently of the Council verdict.
  • I did not touch the other lane's scope — single-use scripts, absorb decisions, or the rejection-recording fix.

7. Your decision

Three options, and my recommendation is the second.

  1. Implement as specced anyway. Narrows the hard rule, removes 7 cards. I do not recommend this and neither did the gates.
  2. Land the guardrail list without the narrowing. Add the enumerated guardrail surface to the code as an unconditional hold that every current and future graduation must pass through, and change nothing about what graduates today. Nothing gets narrowed, the other lane's graduation gets a floor it cannot cross by accident, and the boundary stops being implicit. This is a rule addition, so it does not need a separate approval from you.
  3. Take the reframe. Stop narrowing the gate at all and change what a card does: guardrail hits block, everything else merges with a notice and a one-tap revert. This is the bigger win and the bigger change, and it is a new decision rather than the one you already made — so it needs your call before anyone specs it.

The open question only you can answer: is your actual complaint the number of taps, or the risk of what merges without one? If it is the taps, option 3 is the real answer and option 2 is the safe step toward it. If it is the risk, the gate is already doing its job and none of this needs to change.
