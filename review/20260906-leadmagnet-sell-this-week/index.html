The lead magnet that starts a conversation

TL;DR

The magnet was never the missing piece. Seven finished lead magnets already exist, and one of them is live right now at a working URL with a working PDF and a free booking link behind it. Nobody has ever been sent to any of them on purpose. So the constraint is not what to offer. It is that a stranger has never once been asked for anything by hand: eight connection requests in the entire history of the account.

What changes this week is the shape of the magnet and the direction of the ask. Stop offering information to download. Start delivering a finished piece of work, unrequested, to five named owners, with no ask attached. The artifact is a one page AI policy written for their specific company, and it goes in a direct message, which means the delivery is the conversation.

Five, not twenty. The public post runs as the second channel, not the thing the week depends on.

What I need from you: five names, and the go-ahead Monday night on the five specific drafts once you have read them. About 65 minutes of your week in total. Everything else is built and waiting.

What the video actually teaches

Paraphrased throughout, nothing quoted.

The traditional lead magnet is dead because information stopped being scarce. A 46 page ultimate guide worked when downloading it from you was the only way to get the answer. Now the answer is one search or one question to an AI assistant away. Nobody on a feed is looking for more information to read.

So stop offering information and start offering a result. His image is a splinter in someone's hand. The magnet that works is not seven steps to remove your splinter. It is the tool that pulls the splinter out. The recipient does not have to learn anything or understand anything. Like a light switch: you do not need to know how electrons work to get light.

The hot versus easy grid is the useful part. Every magnet sits somewhere on two axes: how desirable it is, and how much effort it costs to get the value out.

QuadrantExampleVerdict
Desirable, high effortA five day challenge, a video courseThe juice is not worth the squeeze
Easy, not desirableAn email newsletterNobody wants it
Not desirable, high effortA free strategy sessionThe worst quadrant. He is blunt that a free consultation reads as time spent with someone who wants your money
Desirable and easyA finished result handed overThe only one that works

The title test. You can tell the quadrant from the title alone. "How to write emails that sell" against "the nine word email that revives dead leads." The second promises an instant portable result. The first promises homework.

The hand raiser post is the distribution half and is inseparable from the magnet. His worked example: an operator pair spotted a small problem, built a useful list in about fifteen minutes, posted it, and got over a thousand people to hand over an email inside 24 hours at roughly 91 cents each.

The part most people miss. Getting the hand up is not the point. Redirecting that attention to the next easy yes is the point. He is explicit that this is what a funnel actually is.

What survives contact with our numbers, and what does not

His thousand leads in a day ran on paid ads against an existing audience. Ours does not. Applying his cadence unmodified to an account averaging 4.8 likes with genuinely unknown reach would produce a handful of comments and a conclusion that the mechanism failed, when the sample was simply too small to say anything.

What transfers completely: stop offering information, offer a finished result, and make the value cost the recipient nothing to obtain. What does not transfer: the assumption of distribution.

The magnet

Named as the outcome, not the format

Where the Line Is

A one page AI policy written for one specific company.

The framework pass caught the naming failure directly. "AI use policy" is a format, and naming the format rather than the outcome is a named anti pattern. It is also the exact naming failure of the checklist already sitting dead on the archived site. "Where the Line Is" names what the reader gets, and it echoes the language of the post itself, where the problem is that nobody ever drew their people a line.

What it is

One page. Their industry, the work their people actually do, the record types they actually touch. Under 500 words. Written so an HR or operations lead could hand it to staff on Monday with light edits, rather than a framework they have to translate first. It is not a checklist about policies. It is the policy.

How someone gets it

Primary channel: the policy is written first and sent unrequested. Five named owners a week, chosen from the 524 founders, CEOs and owners already connected. An agent drafts overnight from public information, you review against a fact ledger, and you send it in a direct message.

Two things this buys, and both are load bearing:

  • It removes the dependency on post reach, which is the one number that is genuinely unknown. A direct message does not need an algorithm.
  • It removes the email problem entirely. Only 2.2% of your connections expose an address, so any email first plan aimed at your own network fails structurally. This one does not need an address.

Second channel: the hand raiser post. It runs, and it costs nothing, but the week does not depend on it. At 4.8 average likes the honest expected yield is low single digits.

What happens immediately after

The delivery message contains no ask of any kind. The page closes on one real question, which is the enforcement gap: a rule does not tell you what your people are actually using, and at their size nobody has a way to see it. Every competent owner arrives at that thought within a sentence of reading the policy. Their reply to that question is the sales conversation, and it is where the next rung enters. Not before.

Where it plugs into the ladder

The next rung is not the free 45 minute audit call and not a 20 minute walkthrough with their leadership. Both were tested against the video's own grid and both land in the worst quadrant. A free consultation reads as time spent with someone who wants your money, and a meeting with their leadership is a bigger commitment than the gift justifies. The adversarial review reached the same conclusion independently, from the recipient's side.

The right next rung is the $19 Employee Relations Investigation Pack. It is live, Stripe works, it is instantly legible to exactly this buyer with no education required, and it turns a stranger into a customer for nineteen dollars. The cheapest available transfer of trust is a small paid transaction, not another free one. That pack has had zero distribution in 18 days and its own pass criterion is ten buyers. This gives it its first real distribution at zero cost. The $497 session and the September 24 seat sit above it and are reached through conversation, not through the artifact.

The free audit booking stays live because it costs nothing. It is a destination for someone who asks for it, never the next step we push.

The tension I am not averaging away

One voice holds that an agent drafted document going out under your name is disqualifying at volume. Another holds that this exact artifact is the productisable core and the whole point. Both are right, and it resolves on volume rather than on principle. At five it is a craftsman's gift you genuinely review. At twenty it is a mail merge with your name on it. The cap is the safety mechanism, which is why it is five.

Premortem: it is Friday and there were zero conversations

Working backward, most likely first.

  1. The post never went out on Tuesday because it needed approval Monday night and Monday's messages ate the slot. This has precedent: a traffic strategy written 6 May had two next actions at the top and neither was ever done. Highest probability cause by a wide margin.
  2. The overnight run had no trigger and nobody ran it, so delivery slipped three days and the moment died.
  3. The policies read generic. Research was thin, the agent padded rather than narrowing, and recipients recognised a mail merge. This is the one that costs something real.
  4. The gift landed and the ask was too big. People took the free thing and left, exactly as 195 landing page visitors did.
  5. Emails were collected and vanished into an endpoint that writes no row, so conversations existed but nothing downstream could follow up.
  6. Five went out, all five landed, and nobody replied by Friday. Five is a small sample and four days is a short window. This is not failure, it is impatience, and calling it failure on Friday is how a working mechanism gets killed in week two.

That last one is why the success floor below is stated in messages sent, not replies received.

What the batteries caught

  • A credentials line, cut. Your own standing instruction from yesterday: credentials do not go inside post copy, your profile carries them. A year count tied to a field and a named employer tier are both explicitly banned there. The practitioner anchor replaced it, and it is stronger because it is unavailable to anyone else.
  • An unsourced first person anecdote, cut rather than softened. A specific claim about something that happened to you that is not on file anywhere.
  • "Five this week, because I write them myself", rewritten. An agent drafts and you review, so sole authorship is an overclaim, and it sat on the exact line carrying the offer's credibility. "That is as many as I can do properly" is true and the scarcity survives intact.
  • The offer of labour was buried at line five. An offer to do work is rare in a feed. Information is not. Moved up.
  • The call to action had two doors, halving both. One door now.
