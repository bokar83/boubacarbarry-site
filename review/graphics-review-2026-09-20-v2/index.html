Rebuilt as diagrams, not text cards · 20 September 2026

TL;DR

Three graphics, rebuilt from nothing. Each one is now an actual diagram you can read by its shape before you read a word.

  • A loop is drawn as a loop, a pipeline as a flow with a wall across it, and a comparison as a matrix whose marks form a staircase. The shape carries the idea; the words are short labels hung off it.
  • The old pattern is gone. No numbered list of sentences in coloured circles, no terminal-style quote box, no pull quote with your name under it.
  • All three were opened and looked at at full size, not just rendered. Every connector, arrow and label was checked on the image itself.
  • Honest read: the loop is the strongest of the three. The pipeline and the matrix are good and clearly not text cards, but they are the kind of thing a person in this world nods at rather than forwards.

What I need from you: which of the three is closest to right, and whether the look itself is what you meant by cool and high-tech. If it is, the other two get pushed to match it rather than being tweaked in place.

The three graphics

What changed against the version you rejected

Side by side with what you rejected, in plain terms.

The version you rejectedThis version
Four numbered circles down a straight line, each with a full sentence next to it. The line was decoration; it carried nothing. A closed ring with arrows running the whole way round and back to the start. The ring is the argument. Remove it and the graphic stops making sense.
No icons. The coloured circles held numbers, not pictures. Every node and every step has its own drawn glyph: a correction mark, a filed document, a load arrow, stacked bars, a padlock, an eye, a bell, a barrier.
Nothing was measured or plotted. Claims sat in prose. Real small charts: a step line climbing while a second line falls to zero, a heartbeat that flatlines into a page going off, a grid of filled marks that climbs.
A terminal-style quote box and an italic pull quote with your name under it. Both removed. A stranger reading a tagline with a name under it hears advertising. The closing line now just states the point and stops.
Roughly half the canvas was text, and the bottom third was empty. The diagram owns the middle of the frame. Longest run of prose anywhere on any of the three is nine words.

Built as hand written inline SVG rather than shapes in a design tool, so the lines, nodes and icons are real geometry and stay sharp at any size. Rendered at 2160 by 2700, which is social native portrait at double resolution.

Honest verdict

Two separate bars, scored honestly. A flattering pass here would waste another round.

Would a stranger stop, screenshot and forward it?

  • 01 The correction loop: PASS. The ring plus the climbing step line in the middle is the kind of thing people screenshot to explain an idea to someone else. It survives being seen small.
  • 02 The build pipeline: CONDITIONAL. The wall with a slot in it, and the reasoning visibly stopped dead against it, is the original idea on the page and it is strong. The three boxes around it are competent but ordinary. People who care about review process will forward it; nobody else will.
  • 03 Four kinds of helper: CONDITIONAL. The staircase reads instantly and the empty last column is a genuine hook. Against that, a comparison grid is a familiar shape, so it gets recognition rather than surprise.

Does it look cool and high-tech, or is it a text card with icons stuck on?

All three clear this one, which was the thing that failed last time. Every one of them has geometry doing real work: a closed ring, a barrier with a slot, a staircase of marks. Covering the words on any of them still leaves something you can read.

The one caveat I will not talk around: this dark ground with cyan and amber is the standard palette for this subject. It looks right and it is also guessable from the topic alone. If you want these to look like nobody else's, the palette is the lever, and that is a decision for you rather than something I should quietly pick.

Voice and social quality gates

Every label on all three went through your voice gate and the social quality gate, scored by a reviewer that had not seen the designs.

  • 01 the correction loop: 96 / 100, pass.
  • 02 the build pipeline: 94 / 100, pass. The decision shape is labelled "CLEAN PASS?", so no internal name reaches the image.
  • 03 four kinds of helper: 87 / 100, rewrite, and it was rewritten. The headline used to read "Four things get called a worker. One is.", which scans as an unfinished sentence. It now reads "Call it all a worker. Only one earns it."

No em-dashes, no internal names, no disclaimers about what the thing is not, no numbers that cannot be stood behind, nothing claiming you ran HR or operations.

What it caught, and what was then fixed:

  • Blue on dark, 43 failures. Every muted grey had drifted into the banned blue band. All sixteen offending colours were rotated out by calculation, not by eye. The checker now exits clean on all three files.
  • Banned accent stripes down the left edge of four cards, replaced with drawn corner brackets.
  • Small text that vanished at feed size. Every load bearing label went up a step or two.
  • Graphic 02 still read as a text card to that reviewer. The inspection step now shows a spec being ticked line by line instead of describing it, and the wall was made heavier so it dominates the frame.

What is still open
  • The palette. A dark ground, cyan and amber is the safe read for this subject. A deliberate break from it is the single biggest change available, and it is your call.
  • Graphic 02 wants to be about the wall. The wall is the idea nobody else is drawing. A version where the wall is the whole graphic, and the four steps shrink to a strip along the edge, would almost certainly be stronger.
  • Graphic 03 is the most conventional of the three. A grid is a grid. If it needs to be the hook rather than the reference, it needs a different shape.
  • One word I did not change, and you should decide on. Graphic 01 says "It never comes back" and "the floor never drops back". The voice gate passed both but flagged "never" as an absolute. It is the honest promise of the mechanism, and softening it costs the line its punch, so I left it. Say the word and it becomes "It stops repeating".
  • Not yet done: no re-score after the fixes, and none of these have been seen by anyone other than me and the two reviewers.
