1. Executive verdict

The sourcing side of this is real: Costco runs its liquidation channel through B-Stock at institutional scale, a paid $5/month community (Shannon Jean, shannonjean.com/mastermind) exists and gets paid monthly to teach exactly this model, and independent Reddit accounts confirm an active community of people actually transacting on this channel. Nothing about the sourcing mechanism is speculative.

What's not yet real: any number describing how fast a stranger in Salt Lake City will actually pay for a used appliance out of a garage. Every dollar figure in this plan, no matter how many decimal points it carries, is a model until the first pallet sells through. This version of the plan is built around that fact instead of around it.

The core finding, and it survives every stress test run on it: the category Boubacar is least comfortable with (cautious mixed home-goods/small-appliance) outperforms the category he's most comfortable with (appliances/furniture) by roughly 2x on effective hourly return, because appliances/furniture carries lower sell-through and mandatory handling/repair overhead that home-goods doesn't. That comparison is trustworthy even though the absolute dollar figures underneath it are not, because both categories share the same optimistic input bias in the same direction, the relative gap holds even if the absolute numbers move.

2. The recommended path

Week 1 (parallel, $0 spent): free-listing timing test + B-Stock account + SLC business license + pickup windows locked + real KSL/Facebook comps pulled, all at once, none gating the others.

Then Pallet 1 ($1,100-1,300, cautious mixed, home-goods/small-appliance).

Then the go/no-go gate (30 days): net profit ≥$400 AND sell-through ≥70% AND average landed cost ≤$250/unit.

  • Gate passes → Pallet 2, step into appliances/furniture, comfort-zone, filtered per Section 6. Then Pallet 3, rebalance to the faster-moving category, full $3-5k deployed.
  • Gate fails → halt. Diagnose pricing/category/comps before spending another dollar. Re-run a Pallet 1 variant instead of scaling up blind.

This is not the fastest path to spending the full budget. It is the fastest path to knowing whether the budget is worth spending at all.

3B. Utah business registration steps — confirmed live, free

This whole process is free except an optional $22 DBA filing. Start it this week regardless of which pallet category is chosen first, it gates Costco access on B-Stock.

  1. Optional, only if wanting a business name other than his own. Register a DBA at commerce.utah.gov/corporations, $22, run a name availability search first. Not required for the sales tax license itself.
  2. Go to tap.utah.gov and create a TAP login.
  3. Complete Form TC-69, "Utah State Business and Tax Registration," online through TAP-BR (tap.tax.utah.gov/TaxExpress/_/?Link=TapBr). Free. Needs SSN, business name, address. No LLC required, a sole proprietorship works.
  4. Receive a Sales Tax License Number. Processing timeline is not published online, call the Utah Tax Commission directly at 801-297-2200 to confirm current turnaround before planning around a specific date.
  5. This Sales Tax License Number is what goes into B-Stock's "reseller tax ID" field for buyer registration approval. Confirmed: B-Stock requires this for general registration, and Costco specifically re-checks it as a separate marketplace approval step (roughly 2 business days once submitted).
  6. Optionally fill out Form TC-721, "Exemption Certificate," with that license number for records or other suppliers. This is not filed with the state, he keeps it himself.

3C. Buying safely — scam & pitfall checklist

Sourced from real r/Flipping threads plus B-Stock's own published policy pages.

Cherry-picked or repacked pallets. A real, well-documented pattern (r/Flipping calls it "cherry picking"): local or Facebook Marketplace secondary liquidators buy a truckload, pull the high-value items to sell individually, then resell the picked-over remainder labeled as "raw" or "unsorted." This risk concentrates almost entirely in local or FB Marketplace secondary sellers, not in B-Stock's official Costco-branded auctions directly, that volume is too high for hand-picking to be profitable there. Rule: treat "raw," "unsorted," or "untouched" claims from any non-B-Stock-direct seller as a red flag, not a selling point. Source exclusively from B-Stock's official channel for primary supply.

Critical: bids are final, no buyer cancellation ever. Confirmed directly from B-Stock's own Buyer FAQ (Disputes page). Once a bid wins, there is no backing out for any reason, including "the manifest looks wrong now that I see it." All due diligence (manifest review, condition grade check) has to happen before bidding, not after winning. The only recourse is a narrow 5-business-day dispute window after delivery, evidence-based only (photos, signed Bill of Lading), and it hard-closes 6 weeks after payment regardless of shipping delays. Salvage-grade lots get zero condition-dispute protection by design, never bid salvage expecting any safety net.

Manifest reality. Manifests generally exist on B-Stock and should be reviewed before bidding, but some large-batch lots (certain Amazon shipments, for example) structurally have no per-pallet manifest. That is a known limitation on B-Stock specifically, not automatically a scam signal. It IS a red flag if a local or secondary seller who should have a manifest refuses to produce one.

Fake liquidation "broker" scams exist. One documented case: a scammer impersonated a real liquidation company via Instagram DM, and gave a victim the real company's actual address to build false credibility. Not a risk from B-Stock-direct sourcing, but relevant if ever approached by DM or text with an off-platform "Costco pallet deal." Verify independently before ever sending money, never wire funds pre-inspection.

Freight and shipping. Prefer local pickup over LTL freight where possible, it is both cheaper and lets him inspect before the deal is final. If freight is required, confirm who arranged the carrier (seller vs. B-Stock) before paying.

Structural condition gap, not fraud. Even fully honest "customer returns" grade lots will contain a real minority of worn or incomplete items, because original retailers' generous return policies accept items back without deep inspection. Budget a shrinkage buffer into every profit estimate, assume some percentage of any lot is unsellable. That is normal, not a sign something went wrong.

Eyes-wide-open checklist before first purchase

  • Source exclusively from B-Stock's official Costco-branded auctions for primary supply.
  • Pull and review the manifest before bidding, every time.
  • Never bid salvage-grade expecting condition protection.
  • Start with one small, cheap test lot before committing real capital.
  • Prefer local pickup over LTL freight.
  • Understand: winning a bid is final, all due diligence happens pre-bid.
  • Set a same-day reminder to inspect and photograph everything the moment a lot arrives, the dispute clock is only 5 business days.
  • Treat "raw" or "unsorted" claims from non-B-Stock sellers as a red flag to verify.
  • Never wire money to an unverified off-platform broker.
  • Budget a shrinkage buffer into every profit model.

5. Sourcing & grading discipline

Platform: B-Stock (bstock.com), Costco's official liquidation auction channel.

Grading tiers, in order of trust

  • New / Like New — safest, lowest defect risk, typically highest price competition.
  • Used Good — the sweet spot. Usually customer returns, usually functional.
  • Used Fair — avoid.
  • Mixed/Salvage — avoid.

Manifest-screening discipline (do this before bidding, not after winning): the grading label alone is not reliable at scale. Two real cautionary accounts already surfaced prove this: a truckload disclosed as "used/fair to salvage" arrived floor-loaded (not palletized), 20,000 lbs, no guaranteed liftgate, non-cancelable, including non-working haul-away units; a power-tools lot returned a majority of burned-out motors. Read the actual manifest line items, not just the tier label, and apply pattern-recognition the same way you'd screen for a recurring complaint pattern in any other domain, the skill transfers directly.

Return-reason risk by category (from B-Stock's own guidance + real Reddit accounts)

  • Apparel/shoes — largely "bracketing" (size-driven), lower real defect risk than the grading label suggests.
  • Home/garden, general home goods — usually "didn't fit the space," not broken. Lower risk.
  • Large appliances/furniture — real risk at scale (see manifest-screening filter above). Not banned, filtered.
  • Power tools — avoid unless repair-capable; one real account of majority burned-out motors.

$225/unit landed is a bid ceiling, not a guaranteed cost. B-Stock is a competitive auction, three other people watching the same video means the actual clearing price can run above target. Every scenario in Section 4 models what happens when it does.

6. Category guidance — reconciled

What Boubacar said he's comfortable with: appliances, lawn/patio furniture, furniture in general, and repairs "if worth the time and good enough margins." Uncertain on clothes specifically due to margin concerns.

What the risk data actually says: the comfort-zone category (appliances/furniture) has lower sell-through, mandatory handling/repair overhead, and a real (documented) tail-risk of bulk-lot logistics disasters, while the category with the best $/hour (cautious mixed home-goods/small-appliance) is the one he's less familiar with.

The reconciliation is not "avoid his comfort zone," it's "filter it before running it." Section 5's manifest-screening discipline plus this section's five-point filter is the concrete answer that makes appliances/furniture safe to include as Pallet 2, once Pallet 1 has proven the process on the faster-moving category first.

The five-point filter that makes appliances/furniture safe to include

  • Used-Good grade only. Never bid on Used-Fair, Salvage, or Mixed lots regardless of price.
  • One-person-liftable only. No floor-loaded truckloads, no lots requiring a liftgate or two-person lift as standard handling.
  • Manifest-screened before bidding, not just grading-label-trusted (Section 5).
  • Repair-if-profitable, with a real time cap: repair only if diagnosis (capped at 20 minutes) shows it clears $25/hour effective margin. If diagnosis alone eats more than 20 minutes without a clear answer, treat the unit as sell-as-is or discount, not a repair candidate.
  • Never bid on a lot disclosing non-cancelable floor-load terms — the exact structure the Reddit truckload account describes (20,000 lbs, no guaranteed liftgate, non-cancelable, condition disclosed as unreliable).

7. GTM & exit channels

Two-channel exit, not one (missing from earlier scenario math):

  1. Head of the pallet (20-40% of items) — individually worth a KSL/Facebook Marketplace listing, sold at full modeled margin.
  2. Tail of the pallet (60-80% of items) — the portion that's individually unsellable at good margin or slow to move. Instead of letting it become garage dead stock (the "$0 unsold" worst case baked into every scenario above), book one swap-meet table synced to each pallet's 2-3 week sell window and clear the tail in bulk, for cash, in hours instead of weeks. This directly converts the model's conservative $0 floor into real partial recovery and removes the garage-space bottleneck that otherwise limits how fast Pallet 2 can start.

Primary channel: KSL Classifieds. Structural pair is a swap-meet booth, not Facebook Marketplace, KSL is where the head-of-pallet items move.

Secondary channel (week 3+): Facebook Marketplace. Real ban risk exists and is unappealable, treat as channel #2, not primary.

The single most important operational decision: two fixed weekly pickup windows, published on every listing. This bounds an otherwise unbounded interruption stream and must be locked in Week 1 (Section 3).

Zero-cost referral channel found in this plan's own prior research and not yet used: Public Storage, Extra Space, and CubeSmart facilities in SLC see movers and downsizers daily who need cheap appliances/furniture fast. These same three operators were already researched for this plan (to confirm storage units can't be workshops), a flyer or simple relationship with facility staff costs nothing and directly targets the exact buyer profile this plan needs, patching the one real evidence gap in the whole plan (fresh local demand data).
