Feynman Method Absorb: Active Recall with AI

Structured absorb of Nav Toor's Feynman Method framework. Council + multi-lens. 5 identified uses.

What It Is

The Feynman Method applied to AI-assisted learning. Original insight from Nav Toor: most people use AI to consume (summaries, explanations) = fluency illusion. A few use AI for active recall = actual understanding. 20 minutes. One topic. Four prompts.

The Fluency Illusion

Reading feels like learning. It's not. You recognize ideas you've seen but can't explain them from scratch. AI summarization accelerates fluency illusion, not understanding.

The fix: switch from consume mode to recall mode. Recognition and recall are different cognitive outputs. AI makes recognition effortless — which makes the gap with actual understanding harder to notice.

The 4 Prompts (Core System)

Prompt 1

Concept Map

For [topic], give me: 5 core ideas, why each matters, one test question per idea.

Output: your learning scaffold. What to actually study. Forces explicit boundary-setting before passive reading begins.

Prompt 2

Model + Your Version

Claude writes plain-language model answers. Then you write your own version without looking. Writing it yourself is where encoding happens. Reading Claude's answer = fluency. Writing your own = understanding.

Prompt 3

Gap Finder

Paste your explanations. Claude grades each: STRONG / WEAK / WRONG. One idea to restudy surfaces immediately. No guessing where the gaps are.

Prompt 4

Analogy Lock

Two analogies per idea. Where each works, where each breaks. One sentence per idea to re-read the next morning. Spaced repetition hook. What sticks overnight is what you actually own.

Carousel Draft (ship-ready except Slide 6)

Slide 1 — Hook

You buy the books. You save the threads. You watch the videos. And 31 days later, you can't explain any of it. That's not a motivation problem. That's a method problem.

Slide 2 — The Illusion

Reading feels like learning. It's not. There's a name for it: the fluency illusion. You recognize the idea when you see it. That's different from owning it. The people using AI to summarize are building fluency. The people using AI for active recall are building understanding. Different outputs. By a lot.

Slide 3 — The Gap

Most people are still using AI to consume. Ask it to summarize. Ask it to explain. Read the answer. Fast. Forgettable. A few switched to using it for recall instead. The outputs are not close. That window is still open. Not for long.

Slide 4 — The Method

He didn't highlight. He didn't take notes. He explained. If he couldn't explain it like a 12-year-old could follow, he didn't know it. That test surfaces what you actually missed. Faster than any review system I've used. That's the whole method.

Slide 5 — The System

20 minutes. One topic. Four prompts. Prompt 1: Build your concept map. Five ideas, why each matters, one test question per idea. Prompt 2: Claude writes model answers in plain words. Then YOU write your own version. Writing it yourself is where learning happens. Prompt 3: Paste your explanations. Claude grades them STRONG / WEAK / WRONG. One idea to restudy. Prompt 4: Two analogies per idea. Where they work. Where they break. One sentence you reread the next morning so it sticks instead of fading like everything else you read this week.
