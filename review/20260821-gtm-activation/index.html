The arithmetic, with every assumption labelled

Your account has no measured reach, so the percentages below are published benchmarks for a personal account of roughly this size, not facts about you. I would rather show the working than hand you a number you cannot check.

Posts. Three a week from Aug 25 to Sep 20 is about 12 posts. A reactivating personal account at ~4,500 connections typically sees 2% to 8% of the network per post at first, climbing with consistency. Call it 6,000 to 12,000 total impressions across the month. Because the rule is no link in the post, conversion runs through your profile: roughly 1% to 2% of impressions visit the profile, and a single-field front door converts a warm visitor at maybe 25% to 40%.
That is roughly 15 to 95 signups from posting.

Comments. This is the bigger lever, and it is already running. Three to five a day for 26 days is 80 to 130 comments. A good comment on a well-followed post earns a handful of profile visits each. At 2 to 8 visits per comment and the same front door conversion, that is roughly 50 to 400 signups. Wide, because it depends entirely on whose posts you land on.

Everything else, honestly, is close to zero inside 30 days. Search and the sitemap repair take 2 to 4 weeks just to index before they return anything. The cross-site footer sits on low-traffic pages. X at 21 impressions per post is a rounding error. The newsletter at 43 is a destination, not a source.

Realistic full-effort organic total: roughly 150 to 350, centred near 250. The target is 1,000. Organic alone gets to about a quarter of it.

The newsletter target, 43 to 1,000, taken separately

This is a harder target than the signup one, and it has a structural problem underneath it that has to be fixed before the number can move at all.

The structural problem, and it is the important part of this section. "Email signups" and "newsletter subscribers" are currently two different numbers with no reliable bridge between them. The workshop and front-door style captures write rows to our own database. The newsletter lives on a separate platform. Several of the magnet pages subscribe to the newsletter and write no row we can read; others write a row and never subscribe anyone. So a signup does not become a subscriber today unless it happens to hit the right form. If the goal is newsletter subscribers specifically, then every capture has to also enrol on the newsletter platform. That is a small build and it is the first item in week one, because without it this target cannot move no matter how much traffic arrives.

Is 43 to 1,000 in 30 days achievable?

No. Not through content, and I do not think it is achievable at all inside 30 days without a budget considerably larger than the one for the signup goal.

It is a 23x jump. A newsletter growing on the back of a reactivating personal account grows in the low hundreds a month at best when things go well. The honest ceiling here is the same ceiling as the signup goal, because after the bridge above is built they are largely the same people: roughly 300 to 400 subscribers by Sep 20, which would be a 7x to 9x on 43.

What actually gets a newsletter to four digits

Steady content is not on this list, which is the point.

  • Gate the magnets behind the newsletter instead of leaving them ungated. This is the highest-leverage free move available and it is currently not done. Six finished magnets exist. Every one of them should enrol the reader on the list. Nothing to buy, nothing to negotiate.
  • Paid list-build. Roughly $2 to $6 per subscriber. Getting to 1,000 means buying about 600 of them even after everything else works, so $1,500 to $3,500 on top of the signup budget, or shared with it.
  • Cross-promo swaps. The genuinely free lever, and the one that compounds. Two to three swaps with newsletters in the same audience are worth more than a month of posting. Needs to be started this week to land inside the window.
  • Giveaway or contest mechanics. These do produce four-digit jumps. I am recommending against it: the list you get is people who wanted the prize, the open rate collapses, and a collapsed open rate on a 43-subscriber base you are trying to establish is worse than a smaller real list.

The recommendation. Commit to 400 subscribers by Sep 20. Get there by fixing the bridge, gating the six magnets, and landing two cross-promo swaps. Hold 1,000 as a target for late November, or bring it inside 30 days only if you are willing to spend on the order of $4,000 to $5,000 across both goals.

The 30 days, week by week

Ordered so the free structural work lands before anything that needs your attention, and so the measurement exists before the traffic does. Anything unmarked is agent work and costs you nothing.

Week 1, Aug 21 to Aug 27. Make it measurable and make it connect.

  • Bridge the capture to the newsletter. Every capture surface also enrols the reader on the list. Without this the newsletter target cannot move at all. Proven by reading a row back, not by a green response.
  • Fix the pages that write no row. The newsletter-only magnet pages currently leave no record we can read, so road attribution is impossible on them. They get a row as well as a subscription.
  • Wire the fulfilment email that is missing on the starter magnet. A capture with no delivery is a defect, not a lead.
  • Stand up the metrics table and log all three numbers with today's readings: 43, 769 flagged stale, and 4,511.
  • Close the personal-account posting risk. Make the guard technical rather than a convention, so nothing can publish in your name unread.
  • Sitemap, noindex and internal links for the six magnets, carried over from the 8/19 plan and still the cheapest permanent win here.
  • You, 15 min Approve the front door copy. Its name and promise are words in your name, so they are your call.
  • You, 5 min Point your LinkedIn website field and featured section at the front door. This is what turns every comment into a road. Nothing else in the plan works as well without it.

Week 2, Aug 28 to Sep 3. Turn on the roads and cut your per-day cost.

  • Build the comment-target harvester. Surfaces three to five posts a day worth answering, with a drafted comment for each. You still read, edit and post every one by hand. This is the biggest safe automation available and it does not exist today.
  • Road tagging live on every capture surface, so a signup says whether it came from a comment, a post, the footer, search or the newsletter.
  • Ship the cross-site footer pointing at the front door.
  • Start the cross-promo outreach. Two to three newsletters in the same audience. This has the longest lead time of anything here, which is why it starts now rather than at the halfway point.
  • You, 20 min Monday Approve the fortnight of posts in one batch. Posting restarts Monday, Wednesday, Friday.
  • You, ~20 min daily The comment rhythm, now fed by the harvester instead of assembled by hand.

Week 3, Sep 4 to Sep 10. First real read, and the decision point.

  • First full week of attributed data. Signups by road, in the weekly digest.
  • Record the actual reach numbers from the first two weeks of posts. This replaces every benchmark in the math section above with your real figures, and the forecast gets rebuilt on them.
  • You, day 15 or so The paid decision. See the pivot section. This is the one call that determines whether the stretch target is live.
  • Pitch the podcast circuit with a clean vanity URL, accepting that most of it lands after day 30 and counts toward the 90-day number.

Week 4, Sep 11 to Sep 20. Compound or correct.

  • If tracking on pace: keep the rhythm, add nothing new, let the roads compound.
  • If tracking under: the pre-decided pivot fires rather than being debated.
  • Sep 20 close-out: total signups, split by road; newsletter number against the 400 commitment; cost per signup on anything paid; and a plain verdict on which roads were real and which were theatre.

Your total time: about 45 minutes in week one, then roughly 20 minutes a day on comments and posts, which is already a standing rule and not new, plus 20 minutes on Mondays. This plan does not ask for the selling hours.

The pivot point, decided now instead of on day 30

The failure mode worth designing against is discovering on Sep 20 that it did not work. So the checkpoint is day 15, roughly Sep 4, and the thresholds are set today while nobody is invested in the answer.

The distinction that matters in that table: a low number can mean the roads are wrong, or it can mean the capture is broken. Those look identical from a dashboard and call for opposite responses. That is why road tagging is week one work and not week three work.
