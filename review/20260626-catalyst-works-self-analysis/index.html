▸00 The Verdict -- One Sentence
The Binding Constraint -- One Sentence
Catalyst Works does not have a strategy problem, a proof problem, or a pricing problem. It has an execution problem: a coherent, well-researched go-to-market plan already exists on paper and has never been run, and until the owner commits one concentrated block of weeks to running that plan as written, revenue stays at zero.
The diagnostic surfaced something the scattered public artifacts hide: a sharp, documented strategy sits in the NotebookLM notebooks. A precise buyer (professional-services owner-operators, 5 to 200 people, check-writers who are found at BNI and the Chamber, not on X). An outcome-first positioning. A clean offer ladder. A discovery-call system. A revenue-sprint plan with conversion math. None of it was executed. In its place the owner built landing pages and tools and ran the cold, online, trustless channels the plan itself had already ruled out. That substitution, building instead of selling, cold instead of warm, is the constraint. The root underneath it is an unmade commitment: the owner was war-gaming a full-time job exit at the same time. You cannot run a relationship-led, in-person sales motion on the side of five other things.

An audit tool with white-label pricing. Ten short links. Five funnels. A scanner. Building is controllable, scalable, and safe. The in-person relationship work the plan calls for is none of those. ▸02 The Nine-Agent Audit, Run on Catalyst Works

Catalyst Works' own audit system points its diagnostic agents at a client's operations. Here it is pointed at Catalyst Works. Five of the nine agents are named in the documented system (Hidden Factory, Systems Friction, Financial Reality, Customer Outside-In, Culture and Energy). We run those five here and fold the remaining lenses into the issue tree in Section 4, rather than invent agent names that are not in the evidence.

Hidden Factory AgentCritical

Finds the shadow work that consumes capacity without producing the outcome. At Catalyst Works the hidden factory is building as a substitute for selling. A 1,846-line audit tool with founder and pro pricing tiers, ten channel short links, five funnels, a scanner, a white-label productization, all built with no validated buyer in front of any of them. This is enormous output pointed away from the one thing that produces revenue: a conversation with a check-writer. The factory runs hot and ships nothing that sells.

Customer Outside-In AgentCritical

Looks at the business through the buyer's eyes. The documented buyer is a professional-services owner-operator who is found in a room (BNI, Chamber, association luncheon), not online. Catalyst Works reached for that buyer almost entirely through cold email and cold Meta ads. The buyer was never in the channel. A stranger who did land on the site saw an unknown name, a message that read differently month to month, four prices, and zero clients, and concluded, correctly, "no one has bought this yet."

Systems Friction AgentHigh

Hunts the friction between intent and result. The largest friction point is the gap between the documented plan and the live execution: a coherent strategy in NotebookLM, a drifting set of public artifacts, and no pipeline system (no CRM, the Drive sales folder is empty) to convert intent into a tracked conversation. There is no instrument that turns a "yes, let's talk" into a managed deal, so even a warm signal would leak.

Financial Reality AgentCritical

Strips the story to the cash. Inflows: roughly zero. Outflows tracked: about 63 dollars on Apollo with near-zero surviving leads, 200-plus on workshop ads at 0 percent conversion, plus the unmeasured cost of build hours. The unit economics never get a chance to exist because the funnel produces no qualified conversation to price. Revenue equals leads times conversion times price times retention, and the first term is effectively zero, which zeroes the whole product regardless of how good the later terms are.

Culture & Energy AgentCritical

Reads where the owner's energy actually goes, versus where it is claimed to go. The May 1 MACU prep document shows the owner interviewing for a full-time Director role (145 to 185 thousand plus) and explicitly war-gaming pausing or winding down Catalyst Works. Energy is split across the consulting arm, a sibling brand, a workshop line, faceless content channels, a ghostwriting track, and a job search. The relationship-led sales motion the plan requires is the single highest-energy-cost activity in the portfolio, and it lost the energy auction to lower-cost building. This is the root the other four agents keep pointing back to.

Audit convergence Four of five agents flag Critical, and every one of them, traced back, lands on the same place: the documented plan was never executed because the owner's committed attention was never there to execute it. Hidden Factory, Customer Outside-In, and Financial Reality describe the same substitution from three angles. Culture and Energy names why the substitution happened.

5 Whys -- driven to the root

  1. Revenue is zero. Why? No closed clients.
  2. No closed clients. Why? Almost no qualified discovery calls with check-writers ever happened.
  3. No discovery calls. Why? The business reached for buyers through cold email and cold ads. Buyers got 0 of 767, 1 reply of 280, 0 of a 350-click workshop. The buyer was not in those channels.
  4. Why use channels the buyer is not in? Because the documented plan (be in the room: BNI, Chamber, warm LinkedIn) was never executed. Cold/online channels are scalable, buildable, and automatable. They fit the builder's hands. The in-person motion does not.
  5. Why was the in-person motion never run? It is slow, unscalable, and personally costly. It can hear no. A builder defaults to assets and automation over the uncomfortable relationship ask.
  6. Why default to the comfortable work? Because attention was divided across five-plus efforts and an active full-time job search, and a divided owner has no surplus for the highest-cost activity in the stack.
  7. Why was attention divided? Because the underlying commitment to Catalyst Works as the primary business was never actually made. The May 1 document shows the owner modeling winding it down. Root: an unmade commitment decision, showing up as an execution gap on an already-correct plan.

Value-driver tree -- exactly where the math breaks

Revenue = Qualified Leads x Conversion x Price x Repeat. Plug in the real numbers:

Qualified Leads

Cold email: 0 of 767 and 1 of 280. Workshop: 0 paid of 350 clicks. Apollo: ~0 surviving of ~$63. Effective qualified-lead count: approximately zero. The product zeroes here.

Conversion

Indeterminate. With ~0 qualified conversations as input, conversion has never been tested. Not proven bad, just starved.

Price

In-market and healthy. $2 to 5K diagnostic, $3 to 5K/mo retainer match 2026 benchmarks. Not the problem.

Repeat / Retention

Not yet in play. No first client to retain.

A zero in the first term makes the other three irrelevant. This is why "fix the funnel copy," "lower the price," or "build a better tool" all return zero: they optimize terms two through four while term one stays at zero. The only move that changes the outcome is the one that turns the first term positive: get in front of qualified buyers through a channel they trust.

Outside view -- the market, the pricing, the wedge

Real 2026 benchmarks, to check whether Catalyst Works is fighting the market or itself:

  • Pricing is in-market, not the problem. Fractional AI officer engagements run $2 to 8K a month (light) up to $5 to 30K (full); SMB AI readiness audits run $2 to 8K narrow, $5 to 15K full; fixed-fee diagnostic sprints of 2 to 4 weeks are the recommended structure for founder-led firms. Catalyst Works' ladder lands inside every one of these ranges. The $8,500 Margin Diagnostic Sprint and $1.5 to 5K/mo retainer are textbook-correct.
  • The market is real and growing. AI consulting is compounding around 29% a year. Andrew Ng's "long tail" framing (1.7M US businesses at $500K to $10M) is the exact underserved segment the notebooks target. The demand is not in question.
  • The wedge is plausible but not unique. "Fractional AI" is filling fast (fractional roles jumped from ~2,000 in 2022 to 110,000-plus by early 2024). "Vendor-neutral judgment" is becoming table stakes as the fractional-CTO and AI-consultant lines converge, not a standalone moat. What is differentiable: a sharp niche (professional services), an outcome-first promise (margin), and operator-as-proof (he runs his own business on AI agents, a demo most competitors cannot show). Those three together are a real wedge. Vendor-neutrality alone is not.
  • Why consultants fail to land clients (market-wide) matches this file exactly. The dominant buyer question is "can this person actually deliver?" and the top trust-builder is social proof from prior clients. Consultants without shipped, verifiable outcomes stall. Catalyst Works has zero client proof and is selling into the single most trust-sensitive purchase category. That is not a Catalyst Works quirk; it is the known failure mode, and the known fix is a first proven engagement plus warm referral, not more cold reach.

Founder-focus lens (named honestly, because a real advisor must)

This is the part that stings, and it is the most important part. As of May 1, the owner was interviewing for a full-time Director of Employee Experience Technology role at a ~$20B credit union, comp anchored at $145K floor, $165 to 185K target plus bonus, $216K stretch, and the prep document explicitly war-games the question "wouldn't you keep building Catalyst Works?" with the answer: would pause, low-maintenance, or evolve it for the right offer. At the same time, attention is spread across the consulting arm, a sibling visibility brand, a workshop line, a faceless content studio family, and a ghostwriting track. The documented sales plan needs roughly 8 focused hours a week of in-person relationship work, sustained for weeks, the highest-discomfort, lowest-leverage-feeling activity in the entire portfolio. It never got the hours because the hours were never truly committed. This is not laziness; the build output proves enormous effort. It is divided direction. And it is upstream of every other finding: a fully committed owner does the warm asks, runs the BNI motion, lands a first client, and manufactures proof, because that is what a person does when the business is the only boat.

▸05 The Sankofa Council -- Four Premortem Rounds

Five independent voices, run in premortem mode (it is six months from now and Catalyst Works is dead; each voice explains why). Four rounds. The job: kill the convenient answers and converge on the one binding constraint. No voice softens. No em dashes.

Round 1 -- Independent premortems

C
The Contrarian -- the chain that killed it

It became unfixable the moment that, after 1,047 cold sends and a 0-seat workshop all returned zero, the response was to build more (a white-label audit tier, more short links) instead of changing the motion. Catalyst Works died of building, not of bad luck. The fatal premise: that a good enough system attracts buyers. It does not. Trust does, and trust is earned in rooms, not shipped in code.

F
First Principles -- the one wrong assumption nobody wrote down

"Our problem is being known." It was never being known. It was being believed. Every fix targeted reach (more sends, more ads, more pages). None targeted trust. The real question was never "how do we reach more buyers," it was "why would a check-writer believe an unknown enough to pay," and the honest answer (they would not, yet) was never confronted.

O
The Outsider -- what was obvious from outside

A stranger saw an unknown person, a message that changed every month, four prices, and not one named client. The market read "nobody has bought this" in three seconds. Insiders saw a rich, sophisticated system. Outsiders saw a storefront with no customers and kept walking.

E
The Expansionist -- what was never tried that would have saved it

The unscalable first-client motion. Pick three real local owner-operators, run the diagnostic free or cheap, capture a documented result and a testimonial, ask each for two referrals. Plus operator-as-proof: he runs his own business on autonomous agents, and that demo was never packaged as the case study it already is. Plus one borrowed list (SBDC, Chamber). None of it run.

X
The Executor -- the earliest warning sign, and the week it appeared

The week of May 27. That week, 261 cold sends returned 0 engagement AND a lead-gen automation had to be killed for fabricating leads. Together they screamed: there is no real pipeline, and the system is faking one. The correct response was to go land one real client by hand. Instead cold was paused and the building continued.

Round 2 -- Cross-review: kill the convenient answers

The voices read each other. Convergence is immediate on the target: this is about trust and channel, not reach. The convenient answers die here:

  • "It's the ads." Dead. 350 clicks at 0 percent with in-spec targeting proves the leak is after the click, not at it. Better ads buy more cold strangers for the same trustless page.
  • "It's the funnel / the copy." Dead as primary. There is no warm traffic to convert; fixing copy on a page no one trusts returns zero, which is exactly what happened.
  • "It's the price." Dead. The ladder is in-market on every rung.
  • "It's no strategy / unstable positioning." Demoted. A coherent strategy exists in the notebooks. The instability is in the storefront, which is a symptom of building-instead-of-selling, not the root.

One real divergence survives: is the one thing proof (Contrarian) or warm distribution (Expansionist)? First Principles resolves it: they are the same constraint from two ends. Proof is the asset; the warm channel is the wire. Warm distribution (a partner intro, a referral, a room) carries borrowed trust that substitutes for proof at the start, which is exactly how almost every consultant lands client one. You need both ends, or no current flows. Name the unified thing: a trust-carrying path to the buyer.

Round 3 -- Premortem the surviving answer itself

Pressure-test the single-lever versions so we do not ship a convenient compound either:

Premortem A -- "We built proof and still made zero"

We finally produced a case study and a slick operator demo, and revenue was still zero. Why? The proof sat on a site nobody trusted enough to visit, shown through cold channels again. Proof with no warm channel is a tree falling in an empty forest.

Premortem B -- "We got distribution and still made zero"

A partner blasted the offer to 2,000 owner-operators and we still got nothing. Why? The offer still sold the how (a constraint diagnostic) instead of a felt outcome, from a host with no proof. Borrowed reach without proof or a sharp outcome is just the workshop again at a bigger scale.

Both single levers die. The surviving answer must be the compound that the documented Gen-5 plan already contains: a trust-carrying path (warm channel) + first proof + one outcome-first offer, executed together. And critically, the Gen-5 plan already specifies all three. So the constraint is not "design this." It is "run this."

Round 4 -- Premortem the precondition (the one that stings)

Premortem C -- "We did everything right and it still died"

We had the proof, the warm channel, the sharp offer, and Catalyst Works still died. Why? The owner took the full-time role, or kept splitting across six efforts, and never gave the motion the concentrated weeks it needs. The earliest warning was visible on May 1, eight weeks before the workshop: a document war-gaming the wind-down. The plan was never the risk. The commitment was.

This is why the binding constraint has two layers, and the report states both: the operational constraint is the unexecuted trust-carrying motion; the binding precondition is the commitment decision that has never been made. You fix the second before the first, or the first never gets fixed.

Chairman synthesis -- convergence ~92% The Council converges: the binding constraint is execution of an already-correct go-to-market plan through a trust-carrying channel to the buyer, which has never been run, blocked by an unmade founder-commitment decision. The tension between proof-first and distribution-first is named and resolved as a compound (both, executed together) that the existing plan already specifies. The convenient single-cause answers (ads, funnel, price, "no strategy") are all killed with evidence. Open question only the owner can answer: is Catalyst Works the boat, or the side project? Everything downstream depends on that one word.

The "margin you're losing" promise needs a dollar number. This report names the constraint but does not size the cost of the constraint in dollars. A paid Margin Diagnostic must put a figure on the leak.

No Catalyst Works logo, cover page, or contents page. No PDF export. The maturity model and Nine-Agent system should be visually templated and reusable, not hand-built per report.

Built bespoke. To sell at volume, the Nine-Agent audit and maturity scoring need a templated intake-to-report pipeline so each one is not a from-scratch build (which is, fittingly, the exact constraint this report diagnoses).
