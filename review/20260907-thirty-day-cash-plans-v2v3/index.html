Step 1. Premortem of V1 (Council, five voices, six months out, past tense)

Frame: it is March 2027. The V1 plan is dead. Each voice explains why, from its own mandate. Evidence read for this: skills/cold-outreach/SKILL.md, skills/hormozi-lead-gen/, docs/LIVE_STATE.md, docs/decisions/log/2026-09.md, docs/roadmap/lighthouse.md, the July 30 check-in provenance, and a live read of the leads table on orc-postgres.

The Contrarian: the chain of events that killed it

It became unfixable on Monday 9/08, the day every hour of outbound was tied to two dates nobody had asked for. Friday 9/12's scoreboard read 150 emails, 0 replies, 25 calls, 0 seats. The plan had no rule for that day, only one on 9/18, so nothing changed. By 9/18 there were two seats, both from the July free-session returners who were never strangers. The room ran with three people. The $250 audit was offered once, plainly, to three people who had paid $50 to learn a tool, and none bought, because a person who came for a class is not a person who came to hire. The window closed at $100 collected against the room fee and twelve working days of the only outbound capacity that exists. The number that should have stopped it was already on the record: three paid workshop attempts in history (June 25, Aug 19, Aug 27) and zero seats, the last one cancelled at zero after personal asks to warm contacts. V1 quoted the July free room as proof and did not mention the three paid rooms.

Second flaw, in the mechanism itself. V1 assumed 1.5 percent of cold, personalised emails and calls would buy a seat. The skill's own benchmark for cold email is 1 to 3 percent replies, of which 10 to 30 percent are positive, so a paid conversion of 1.5 percent is roughly ten times the benchmark, on a channel that has produced zero positive replies across 331 humans. And the phone channel, which V1 leaned on as "the one touch a stranger cannot ignore," has no measured result anywhere in the repo; the cold-outreach skill added phone coverage on 9/06 and has never logged a call. Two of V1's three touches were untested and the third was tested and dead.

The First Principles Thinker: the assumption nobody wrote down

"A stranger will pay to learn AI on a Thursday night." Every measured signal said the opposite: 195 landing views and no paid seat in August, 1,308 ad clicks to a free room, 16 free sign-ups and 4 people in chairs. The room fee being sunk made a curiosity product feel like a demand product, and the plan optimised the wrong variable (seats) instead of the right one (a buyer who already has a budget line and a deadline of their own). The reframe that makes V1's question irrelevant: stop selling something people might want and start selling something people already buy under pressure from a credentialed stranger. Two such things exist in this inventory. A trades owner whose phone rang at 5:50pm and went to voicemail loses a job he can name in dollars. An owner with 30 employees who got an HR complaint this morning needs someone who has run investigations, this week, and will pay a deposit to make it go away.

The Outsider: what an outside observer saw coming

Read the two offers as a person with no context. "A man I have never heard of wants $50 for an AI class in Sandy next Thursday." Against: "The roofing company's phone went to voicemail at 6pm; a man says he can answer it for $197 a month and I can hear what it sounds like by calling a number right now." Or: "An HR person with ten years in a Fortune 100 will handle the employee complaint I got this morning, scoped by Wednesday, deposit up front." One is a want, two are needs. The insiders could not see it because "AI" was the whole brand and the whole slide deck. The buyer never cared what the tool was called.

Step 5. Premortem of V2, and what it forced into V3

Same frame, same five voices, past tense, aimed at V2.

The Contrarian

V2 died of division. Two engines split the only outbound capacity that exists, so by Oct 7 neither had reached the 250-prospect floor the cold-outreach skill itself says you need before you may judge a channel, and both were declared dead on noise. Worse, the receptionist took its first yes on 9/15 into a per-client config that had never handled a real caller. The forwarding code was wrong for the owner's carrier, the SMS summary went to the wrong number, and the first paying client spent his founding month as a beta tester. The plan had a delivery contract on paper and no dress rehearsal.

The First Principles Thinker

The wrong variable was "two engines." The question was never "which two offers," it was "which single motion does he control end to end, and which one is repeatable in any city." Only the phone test is both. The referral desk depends on an attorney's calendar and a client's emergency arriving inside a 30-day window; it is the right Year One target and the wrong 30-day engine. It belongs in the plan as the thing that compounds, at a fixed daily dose, not as a co-primary.

The Outsider

From outside, V2 looked like three businesses run by one man: an HR investigator, an answering-service vendor, and a guy selling pallet goods on KSL. Any buyer who searched his name found all three and trusted none. The pallet handle was already neutral; the other two needed separate front doors and V2 had not drawn the line.

The Expansionist

Never tried: making him physically present where both buyers and referrers already gather, which needs no listing, no editor, and no reply. Salt Lake SHRM's monthly chapter meeting is 40 to 100 HR people who hire investigators and refer employee-situation work; a guest ticket is a two-digit number. The South Valley Chamber's luncheon is Sandy's owner-operators in a room with a name tag. He had four facilitation certifications and the only "AI and HR" story in the room, and V2 kept him at a desk.

The Executor

Tuesday 9/09. V2 asked for a 60-firm node list, a 286-row call sheet, three drafts, and the first test sweep to all exist by then, on the day after a 2pm recruiter call. The list slipped a day, the sweep happened, the node emails did not, and Engine B started week two already behind its own scoreboard. Week one should have had one engine and one list, and the second list built while the first was running.

Chairman synthesis

Convergence (about 91 percent): one primary engine, the after-hours phone test, because it is the only motion he controls end to end and the only one that passes the parachute test cleanly. The referral desk becomes a fixed daily dose that compounds, not a co-primary. A dress rehearsal on his own number precedes any client money. Physical presence enters as a weekly, controllable act. Brands separate at the front door. Divergence: the Expansionist would put the SHRM room first; the Contrarian says a stranger at a chapter meeting is a business card, not cash, in 30 days. V3 keeps it weekly and does not count it in the estimate. Recommendation: CHANGE, not kill. V3 is V2 with the engines re-ranked, a rehearsal inserted, the calendar re-sequenced, and control moved to his hands.
