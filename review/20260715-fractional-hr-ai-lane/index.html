Fractional HR + AI

Designing the fractional-HR space into a new, additive lane for Catalyst Works — by marrying the one credential room this audience can't argue with: an HR insider who actually runs his business on AI.

SHAPE-FIRST → GO A gated fast-follow, not a new build and not a July-30 distraction.
Not a pass — the moat fit is the deepest in your whole portfolio and every asset it needs already exists. Not an unconditional go — the naive version (sell high-ticket consulting to pre-revenue solo HR folks, or build a rival community, or start today and rob the July 30 workshop of attention) fails. The win is to borrow the room, re-skin what you already have, and treat the community as a stage, not a client.

The Thesis

Fractional HR leaders are being told to "leverage AI to do more with their time" and don't know how. You are the rare one who already crossed that bridge: an HR insider who built a real AI operating system to run his own business. Sell them that crossing — with the workshop and products you already own.

The Convergence Thesis — why this isn't a distraction

"They all lead to the same place, my moats. They think I'm distracted but they converge. That's the move others aren't seeing."

This is the organizing principle for the whole portfolio. From outside it looks like five directions at once. It's the opposite: five doors into one house, and the house is the moats. Each lane is a different way in, and each one thickens the moats that make every other lane easier to win. The HR-AI lane belongs because it routes straight back to the two deepest moats.

Council — 5-angle pressure test

1. Real demand, or a shiny distraction? ›

Real for authority; thinner for high-ticket services from that exact room. A funded community that names "leverage AI to do more with your time" as a core gap is third-party proof of the wedge. But the room is HR people going solo: price-sensitive, often pre-revenue. They want to become you, not pay a consultant. So monetize the room as a stage, not as your client base.

2. Moat fit, or spread thin? ›

The deepest moat fit in the portfolio. To HR leaders, "10 years inside a Fortune 100 in HR, an MBA, and a live AI system I built to run my own business" is peer-credibility plus rare proof, not a claim. That advantage does not transfer to the cold-SMB lane. Spread-thin risk only appears if you build new — which the design forbids.

3. Can he actually win it? ›

High win-probability on visibility, medium on direct revenue. You are a cheap, obvious yes for the podcast; summit entry is winnable. Converting the room to paid is harder. But the authority spills into your other lanes — content credibility, warm HR intros, people in the room who do have budget or who refer. That spill is the real payoff.

4. Distract from IC + CW, or feed them? ›

Feeds — if and only if it is gated and reuse-only. The July 30 workshop is days out; the Denver summit is late September. Reuse plus correct sequencing makes this additive (same machinery, new audience, authority reinforcing CW). Building new now, or robbing July 30 of focus, makes it a distraction. This is the whole ballgame: fast-follow, never a parallel new build.

5. Ethical + revenue-real? ›

Ethically clean; authority-fast, cash-slower, repeatably strong. HR education, real credentials, nothing banned. Lead with "keep the human in AI"; never imply AI replaces HR people — this audience protects the human craft. The real prize: "plug into a niche community and re-skin the workshop" is a pattern you can repeat in any vertical. Fractional People People becomes the template for how CW enters any concentrated professional room.

Premortem — it's 6-12 months out and this failed. Why?

Two ways this dies. Each one is designed against below. Some counters are already baked into the design (sequence lock, reuse-only, room-as-stage); the premortem adds three NEW guardrails, flagged.

Premortem 1 — it quietly died as a distraction ›

The story: by early 2027 the lane produced a few nice podcast hits and a summit talk, but zero dollars, and it siphoned focus from the paying lanes. What killed it: he treated the room as a client base and kept nurturing pre-revenue solo HR folks who were never going to pay a fractional operator; the workshop re-skin quietly became a new build (rebuilt deck, funnel, a separate brand) instead of a fork; it ran in parallel with July 30 so everything got 60 percent; and because "authority" is unfalsifiable, the lane never got killed even though it wasn't paying.

Designed against:

  • Room is a stage, not a client (already baked). Cap time in the community to a fixed weekly budget; measure outbound (intros, pipeline, seats), not vanity.
  • Reuse-only gate (already baked): if a move needs a new deck, brand, or funnel, it fails. Fork, don't rebuild.
  • Sequence lock (already baked + in the Money Map as "hold-until-after-jul30"): nothing new until July 30 closes.
  • NEW guardrail — one kill-metric: by the Denver summit + 30 days, has the lane produced at least one booked discovery call OR a defined number of workshop seats? If zero, pause it. Make authority falsifiable via a downstream number.
Premortem 2 — brand misfire or single-channel bet ›

The story: it "worked" for attention but hurt him. He became "the AI guy" to HR people in a way that muddied his Catalyst Works operator positioning, or spread his personal brand across so many audiences that nobody knew what he did. Worse: in a room that protects the human craft of HR, an "AI to do more with less" message landed as "AI replaces HR headcount," and one misfire damaged his standing with the exact community. Or the whole lane was single-threaded on one small founder-led community that didn't convert, and the summit slot never materialized.

Designed against:

  • Lead with human-in-AI, always; never "replace" (already baked). Pre-clear the podcast and summit angle against the community's values.
  • Keep the brands clean (already baked): Human at Work is the HR-audience magnet, Catalyst Works is the operator offer. Don't muddy the CW positioning with HR-consultant messaging on the same surface.
  • NEW guardrail — bet on the pattern, not one room: the community is the PILOT of a repeatable "plug into a niche community" motion. If it underperforms, the pattern ports to the next HR or adjacent community. Never single-thread the lane on one room.
  • NEW guardrail — the podcast is the 30-day signal before summit spend: the podcast pitch is the cheap, fast test. If it gets no traction within 30 days, that's the early signal to reallocate before committing money or travel to Denver.

The Lane Design

  • Core: fractional / solo HR consultants building their own practice (the "HRpreneur" starting out).
  • Named pain (their words): building a sales cycle, articulating a value prop, building client trust fast, and above all learning to leverage AI to do more with their time.

Offers — reuse first, in wallet order ›
  • Free front door: an HR-to-AI give from the existing catalog (a solo-HR "start with AI" resource). Captures the contact, proves the crossing.

  • High-ticket (for the ones who have budget): a done-with-you AI setup for a solo HR practice, delivered as your fractional-operator engagement. Not pushed at the room; offered to the few who qualify.

  • Denver summit, Sep 28-30: aim for a speaking slot on AI for solo HR; sponsor or attend as the fallback. 120+ exact-fit people in one room after July 30 is fully closed out.
